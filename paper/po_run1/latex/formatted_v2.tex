\begin{abstract}
%%%%%%%%% ABSTRACT %%%%%%%%%
Controlled comparisons of designs turning papers into cited text concern question answering and summarisation, not writing what experts wrote about each paper. We build one from 100 OMIM phenotype entries, each drafted from exactly its cited papers by one model under one claim-level audit and scoring; the primary endpoint, OMIM facts conveyed, is coverage of fixed fact lists split from curator-written per-paper text. Hiding the full text of the same papers lowers mean coverage from 79.8\% to 36.1\%. Reading strategies convey over nine in ten facts the supplied text states and under 7\% of those it does not. Three reading strategies differ by under a point, with intervals that include or touch zero; our case-table-first implementation trails direct reading by a mean 1.9 points (interval excluding zero; sign test $p=0.071$); drafting from memory conveys far less. On the same papers and model, PaperQA2's document-query pipeline and GraphRAG with local search conveyed fewer facts than direct reading at higher cost (10 and 4 entries); STORM could not run with this model. Handling stopped replies asymmetrically produced a spurious difference larger than those between reading strategies. One model family wrote, audited and judged everything, and no human validated any judgement.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%%%%%%%%% INTRODUCTION %%%%%%%%%
Curated knowledge bases are written by experts who read papers and record, entry by entry, what each paper showed. Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man (OMIM), the reference catalogue of human genes and genetic disorders \citep{Amberger2014OmimorgOnline,amberger2019omim,hamosh2021online}, is built this way, and so, in part, are the resources that record variant interpretations \citep{Landrum2017ClinvarImproving,landrum2025clinvar}, phenotype annotations \citep{robinson2008human,gargano2024human} and the validity of gene--disease associations \citep{strande2017evaluating}. Language models can now read the papers, and many designs have been proposed for turning documents into structured, cited text: retrieval-augmented or long-context reading \citep{Lewis2020RetrievalaugmentedGeneration,li2024retrieval}, extraction into a graph or a table before writing \citep{edge2024local,joshi2026sliders}, agents that decide what to read \citep{Nakano2021WebgptBrowserassisted,Lla2023PaperqaRetrievalaugmented,skarlinski2024language}, drafting, then verifying \citep{Gao2022RarrResearching,dhuliawala2024chainofverification}, and prompts optimised from examples \citep{Khattab2023DspyCompiling,agrawal2026gepa}.

Comparisons between such designs exist, several with the model and settings held fixed \citep{li2024retrieval,Li2025LaraBenchmarking,han2025rag}, but they concern question answering and query-based summarisation on public benchmarks. The closest work in genetics and biology searches a corpus for its sources, measures memory alone, compares models rather than strategies, or scores against something other than what curators wrote about each paper (Section~\ref{sec:related_work}). The curator's task itself, writing an entry from exactly the cited papers and scoring it per paper against what the curators wrote, has not been used to compare strategies. Three questions are open. With the model, the sources, the guards and the scoring held fixed, how much does the generation strategy matter? What limits agreement with what experts wrote: the strategy, or what the strategy was given to read? And which errors does a claim-level audit against the cited source catch, and which can it not see? This paper is an evaluation and benchmark contribution: it proposes no new method, but compares system designs for evidence-grounded generation under one control.

We answer the questions with a controlled benchmark in which the generation strategy is the only variable (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). Each of 100 OMIM phenotype entries with 2--12 references is written from its header and exactly the papers its curators cited, with the causal gene withheld; only 19\% of the cited papers are readable in full through open access. The strategies form three paradigms (\emph{read and write}; \emph{extract, then write}; \emph{memory first}), one control (a memory-only draft with no papers) and three add-ons, each tested against its parent. Each strategy writes a whole entry, but the primary endpoint, \emph{OMIM facts conveyed}, scores only what the draft says about each cited paper against curator-written per-paper text: OMIM's allelic-variant descriptions as deposited in ClinVar, each split once, without sight of any draft, into a fixed list of facts (6,652 over 476 paragraphs).

\textbf{In the regime tested, what limits agreement is what the strategy was given to read, not how it reads.} The direction is expected, since curators wrote from full papers; we add the size of the effect within the same papers and an attribution of what the drafts lack. Hiding the full text of papers that have one lowers coverage of the same fixed facts from 79.8\% to 36.1\% (means over 46 entries, no arm repaired). On facts the supplied text is judged to state, direct reading, the agentic curator and the evidence graph convey 93.6--94.4\%, differing by under a point; on facts it does not state, 5.5--6.9\%. The memory-based strategies are far behind; of the reading strategies only our case-table-first implementation trails direct reading (an interval that excludes zero, a sign test that does not reach 0.05), most on facts stated in full-text papers; a matched ablation suggests, as an inference, that part of this reflects its extraction effort. These results hold for one model and for entries with 2--12 references whose papers fit one prompt, a regime in which direct reading is expected to do well; padding with unrelated papers tests size alone, and large sets of relevant papers remain untested.

Our contributions are: (1) a benchmark and harness for evidence-grounded knowledge-base writing against curator-written references, built only from public resources, with a claim-level audit, fixed fact lists and per-entry release of every draft, verdict and score; (2) a controlled comparison of three paradigms, a control and three add-ons under one model, with completion as an outcome, run-to-run variation, a matched ablation, and PaperQA2's document-query pipeline and GraphRAG with local search; (3) a within-paper measurement of how much access bounds agreement, with missed and conveyed facts attributed and strategies compared on the facts the supplied text states; (4) what the audit can and cannot see, with six classes of planted error; (5) a memory-only control by creation date, and null or negative results at this scale for recall-then-verify, reconciliation and a small learned guide; and (6) measurement lessons (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}). One model family wrote, audited and judged everything, no curator has read any draft, every strategy was given the curators' reference list, and the entries are drafts for expert review, not clinical guidance.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}
%%%%%%%%% RELATED WORK %%%%%%%%%
\paragraph{Language models for literature curation in genetics and biology.}
Structured prompting and assistant tools have been proposed for populating and maintaining knowledge bases \citep{caufield2024structured,caufield2024curategpt,toro2024dynamic,niyonkuru2025leveraging}. In clinical genetics, language models extract variant, gene--disease and gene--phenotype evidence from papers \citep{li2025autopm3,furlan2025eagleai,wang2026pubmind,yin2026benchmarking}. FlyAOC scores memorisation, pipeline and agent harnesses that search a paper corpus against FlyBase curators' annotations \citep{zhang2026flyaoc}; GEN-KnowRD generates schema-guided rare-disease profiles, assesses their quality and builds a knowledge base from them, evaluated by disease ranking on patient benchmarks \citep{yan2026genknowrd}; PaperQA2 writes cited Wikipedia-style summaries of scientific topics, compared against existing Wikipedia articles \citep{skarlinski2024language}; and \citet{groza2026systematic} measure model memory of rare-disease genes and phenotypes. CGBench, built from ClinGen's expert-curated literature interpretations, scores model explanations against human ones but compares models, not generation strategies \citep{queen2025cgbench}. In our benchmark the cited papers are fixed, the reference is the curators' own per-paper text and structured fields, and the generation strategy is the only variable.

\paragraph{Intermediate representations versus long-context reading.}
Retrieval augmentation supplies a model with selected passages \citep{Lewis2020RetrievalaugmentedGeneration}. Whether this beats placing all documents in a long context is contested: one re-evaluation finds long context generally ahead on question answering \citep{Li2024LongContext}; others argue that very long contexts dilute focus on relevant information \citep{Yu2024DefenseRag}, propose hybrids \citep{li2024retrieval}, or report no single winner \citep{Li2025LaraBenchmarking}. Graph intermediates are used for query-focused summarisation \citep{edge2024local}, and systematic comparisons ask when graphs help \citep{han2025rag,xiang2025use}. Database-centred systems reconcile extracted databases for systematic reviews \citep{joshi2026sliders}. Agentic and retrieval-augmented systems write cited answers or articles from the scientific literature \citep{Lla2023PaperqaRetrievalaugmented,Asai2024OpenscholarSynthesizing,singh2025ai2,skarlinski2024language}, or Wikipedia-like articles from Internet sources \citep{shao2024assisting}. Here the papers are fixed to the curators' reference list, the reference is their own text, all paradigms share one audit and scoring, and access is measured and manipulated.

\paragraph{Memory, verification and contamination.}
Draft-then-verify methods plan verification questions \citep{dhuliawala2024chainofverification}, post-edit unsupported content \citep{Gao2022RarrResearching}, or check against domain databases \citep{wang2025geneagent}. Because curated knowledge bases are public, memory is also a confound. Methods detect pretraining data or contamination from black-box access \citep{Shi2023DetectingPretraining,oren2024proving,golchin2024time,Golchin2025DataContamination}, exploit training cut-offs \citep{Roberts2023DataContamination}, or collect new test items over time \citep{Jain2024LivecodebenchHolistic,White2024LivebenchChallenging}. We adopt the time-based view: a memory-only control analysed by entry creation date, and entries created in 2026.

\paragraph{Supervision by prompt optimisation.}
Instructions can be optimised by language models \citep{Zhou2022LargeLanguage,Pryzant2023AutomaticPrompt,Yang2023LargeLanguage}, evolved \citep{Guo2023EvopromptConnecting,Fernando2023PromptbreederSelfreferential,agrawal2026gepa}, or compiled in multi-stage programs \citep{Khattab2023DspyCompiling,Opsahlong2024OptimizingInstructions}. Our learned guide is a deliberately small instance.

\paragraph{Evaluating cited long-form text.}
Factuality metrics split long-form text into atomic claims and verify each against a knowledge source \citep{min2023factscore,wei2024longform}. Citation quality has been measured for text generated with citations \citep{gao2023enabling} and for medical references \citep{wu2025automated}. Work studies model judges against human preferences \citep{zheng2023judging,shankar2024who} and reports that evaluators recognise and favour their own generations \citep{panickssery2024llm}; our judges share the writer's model family, with no human validation. Text mining yields more from full text than from abstracts \citep{Westergaard2018ComprehensiveQuantitative}, whose structure and content differ \citep{Cohen2010StructuralContent}; so drafts from abstracts should convey less; we measure how much.

\section{Benchmark and Strategies}
\label{sec:method}
%%%%%%%%% METHOD %%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Task, entries and reference}
\label{sec:task}
An entry is $e=(h,R)$ with header $h$ (number, title and alternative titles) and references $R=\{d_1,\dots,d_n\}$, the papers its curators cited, each with an access level $a(d)\in\{\text{full text},\text{abstract},\text{title}\}$ determined by open access. A strategy $S$ maps $(h,R)$ to a draft $D$: paragraphs, each citing a non-empty subset of $R$, and sets of genes, inheritance modes, ontology terms and allelic variants. The causal gene is withheld, because naming it from the papers is part of the curator's task.

\textbf{Entries and papers.} We draw 100 OMIM phenotype entries at random with a fixed seed, 20 from each of five bands of entry number that track creation date (Figure~\ref{fig:memory}). Eligible entries have 2--12 cited references, a causal gene in the gene map, at least five phenotype annotations and at least one curator paragraph in scope, so the sample selects well-annotated, molecularly characterised entries. Of the 515 cited papers, 96 (19\%) are readable in full through PubMed Central or Europe PMC \citep{rosonovski2024europe}, 345 as abstract only and 74 as title only; supplements were not retrieved.

\textbf{Reference.} The \emph{curator-written per-paper text} is OMIM's allelic-variant descriptions as deposited by OMIM in ClinVar \citep{landrum2025clinvar}: variant-centred prose from OMIM's gene entries about one or more cited papers. A paragraph is in scope for an entry when it is about a paper the entry cites; 476 are in scope. The narrative sections of the phenotype entries are not available publicly and are not compared. The clinical-synopsis reference is the Human Phenotype Ontology's disease annotations \citep{gargano2024human}: 2,311 annotations, of which 1,835 are the HPO team's own curation from papers and 476 are taken from OMIM. The causal gene comes from OMIM's gene map via MedGen \citep{Louden2020MedgenNcbis} and mim2gene; inheritance comes from HPO and MedGen modes, compared as coarse classes that must be equal as sets. The allelic-variant reference is the entry's 427 in-scope OMIM-submitted ClinVar records.

\subsection{Strategies}
\label{sec:strategies}
The strategies are three paradigms, one control and three add-ons (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_strategies}). \emph{Read and write}: \textbf{direct reading} puts all papers in one prompt and is the baseline; the \textbf{agentic curator} (a term also used by \citealp{balhoff2026frontier}) works in a folder of the papers with read and search tools and must quote every paragraph's source verbatim. \emph{Extract, then write}: the \textbf{evidence graph} extracts quote-backed findings per paper into a graph of papers, genes, variants, phenotypes and inheritance; \textbf{case table first} extracts one record per reported patient or family per paper and pools them; in both, the writer sees only the intermediate, synopsis terms are computed by rule, and so is inheritance for the table. \emph{Memory first}: \textbf{recall, then verify} writes from memory, audits the draft against the papers and rewrites it from what survives; it may not add content from the papers. The \textbf{memory-only control} is the memory draft with no papers, audited but not repaired. The add-ons are \textbf{second-hand evidence} on direct reading (passages from later open-access papers describing a paper not readable in full), the \textbf{reconciled case database} on case table first (merging the same patient, family or variant across papers), and a \textbf{learned curation guide} on direct reading (rules learned in two reflection rounds from 27 training entries). These are our own implementations (released systems: Section~\ref{sec:released}). The four strategies that read the papers are the \emph{reading strategies}.

\textbf{Guards and model.} For every strategy, ontology identifiers are chosen by the model only from candidates returned by an ontology search, gene symbols are normalised against HGNC \citep{seal2023genenames}, entry cross-reference numbers recalled from memory are removed, and every paragraph must cite a supplied paper. One model, Claude Opus 5.5, does everything, called through a command-line interface in headless mode with schema-constrained output and no temperature or seed; reasoning effort is high for writers and medium otherwise (Appendix~\ref{app:settings}), no strategy has web access, and replies are cached. A reply \emph{stopped by the model's safety filter} comes back empty, is recorded as such and is never retried with a changed prompt; the reasons are not visible.

\subsection{Audit, scoring and statistics}
\label{sec:scoring}
\textbf{Completion.} $S$ completes $e$ when its draft exists and no per-paper call failed. A stopped reply costs a one-call strategy the entry, a per-paper strategy only a paper; scoring the second and excluding the first would bias comparisons, so completion is reported as an outcome, comparisons use only entries every strategy completed, and a sensitivity analysis counts a missing draft as zero.

\textbf{Audit.} Each paragraph is split into atomic claims $c$ attributed to the papers $R(c)\subseteq R$ it cites. Each cited paper returns a verdict $v(c,d)\in\{$supported, partly supported, unsupported, contradicted$\}$ with a quote; a ``supported'' verdict whose quote is not verbatim in the paper is downgraded. A claim's verdict is the best over $R(c)$. Flagged (unsupported or contradicted) claims get one repair round.

\textbf{Definitions used throughout.} A \emph{curator paragraph} $g$ is an in-scope OMIM allelic-variant description. Its \emph{fixed fact list} $\Phi(g)$ was produced once, without sight of any draft, and is reused for every strategy; splitting afresh for each draft would let the split adapt to the draft and give strategies different denominators. Each fact is marked \emph{present}, \emph{partly present} (stated less precisely), \emph{absent} or \emph{contradicted} in what $D$ says about the paragraph's papers, and $\text{coverage}(D)=(\#\text{present}+\tfrac12\,\#\text{partly present})/\#\text{facts judged}$. \emph{OMIM facts conveyed}, the primary endpoint, is this coverage averaged over entries (the \emph{mean over entries}); paired differences are means over entries of per-entry differences. Levels in the main text are means over entries unless marked \emph{pooled} (the same ratio over all facts in a group, the only form of the attributions of missed and conveyed facts). A claim is \emph{judged supported} when the audit's final verdict is ``supported''; this is a judgement of fidelity to the cited text by the model family that wrote the draft, not of correctness, and \emph{unsupported} is not \emph{wrong}. Finally, each fixed fact carries a judgement of whether the text available for its paper \emph{states}, \emph{partly states} or does \emph{not state} it (a ``stated'' verdict needs a quote verified in that text). It is a property of the supplied text, the same for every strategy, and a model judgement. The reference covers a minority of the task: on the 68 held-out entries of the guide experiment, 24\% of a direct-reading draft's statements have a counterpart in the paragraph (Appendix~\ref{app:addons}).

\textbf{Secondary measures.} Synopsis terms are scored against the HPO annotations by precision, recall and F1 (exact, hierarchical and by Lin similarity), allelic variants by recall and precision, the causal gene by F1 and inheritance by agreement (Appendix~\ref{app:settings}). A strategy that predicts nothing where the reference is not empty scores zero.

\textbf{Statistics.} Paired differences from direct reading are means over entries with a bootstrap 95\% interval over entries (10,000 resamples; \citealp{Efron1979BootstrapMethods}) and entries better/worse/tied. The main comparison adds an exact two-sided sign test, ties dropped \citep{Dixon1946StatisticalSign}, Holm-adjusted \citep{holm1979simple} across the eight secondary headline measures within each strategy; the primary endpoint is not adjusted. The interval and the sign test are the tests of a difference. The main comparison is one run per entry; replicate noise from three replicates on 30 entries is a yardstick for how much a difference moves when generation, audit and scoring are repeated. ``Points'' are percentage points.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
%%%%%%%%% EXPERIMENTS %%%%%%%%%
\textbf{Takeaways.} (1) Access bounds agreement: hiding full text lowers coverage sharply, and reading strategies convey more than nine in ten of the facts the supplied text states. (2) Three reading strategies differ by under a point; our case-table-first implementation trails, most where full text is readable. (3) On these entries and settings, PaperQA2's document-query pipeline and GraphRAG with local search conveyed fewer facts than direct reading at higher cost; STORM could not run with this model. (4) Unrelated padding costs no strategy more than about a point (6 entries, losses of added papers allowed); direct reading rose at both sizes against an earlier-generation baseline (not read as a gain), so the others lost ground to it at the first size; on large real entries, stopped replies decide which drafts exist. (5) Memory alone conveys far less, and little for the newest entries. (6) The audit catches swapped citations and moved sentences, not over-citation, most broadened claims or omissions, and checks fidelity, not correctness. (3), (4) and the new classes of (6) rest on 4 to 10 entries per experiment and 30 items per class: directions and large effects only. Every result rests on judgements by the writer's model family, none validated by a person (entry sets: Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_experiments}).

\subsection{What limits agreement: access to the source}
\label{sec:access}
\textbf{Hiding the full text of the same papers.} In 49 entries the 91 papers readable in full were hidden and each entry was rewritten by direct reading from their abstracts, with and without second-hand passages from later open-access papers, and scored on the same fixed facts of the 46 entries with a full-text draft. The full-text arm is the main run's direct-reading draft before the repair round, so no arm is repaired. Coverage is 79.8\% with the full text and 36.1\% from the abstracts, higher with the full text in every entry but one, which tied (Figure~\ref{fig:hidden}); scoring the full-text arm after repair gives $+43.9$ points (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_hidden}).

\textbf{Where the missed facts went.} Of the facts direct reading did not fully convey, 49\% were stated in the available text for full-text papers, 2\% for abstract-only and none for title-only papers (Figure~\ref{fig:missed}; pooled); over all facts, only 4\% were omitted although stated (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_missed}).

\textbf{Where the conveyed facts came from.} Of 1,420 facts direct reading conveyed in full from abstract-only papers, 74.7\% are stated in the paper's own abstract, 15.6\% partly and 7.2\% only in another supplied paper; only 24 (1.7\%) are stated in no supplied text (recalled, inferred or misjudged). Facts conveyed in part are more often outside the text (10.0\%; pooled, Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_conveyed}).

\textbf{Three reading strategies differ by under a point; case table first trails them.} On the primary endpoint, direct reading, the evidence graph and the agentic curator lie within 0.7 points of one another (Table~\ref{tab:main}; completion in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_completion}); the evidence graph's interval includes zero and the agentic curator's touches it. Case table first's excludes zero, but its sign test does not reach 0.05 ($p=0.071$; evidence graph 0.213, agentic curator 0.242). Without partial credit the levels are 42.4\% (direct reading), 42.0\% (evidence graph), 41.9\% (agentic curator) and 40.8\% (case table first), in the same order (levels only; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_full}). After Holm adjustment no secondary headline measure reaches $p<0.05$ for these three, though some intervals exclude zero, whereas the memory-based strategies differ on the primary endpoint ($p<0.001$) and four or five secondary measures each (Appendix Tables~\ref{tab:app_paired}--\ref{tab:app_sign}). Recall, then verify may not add content from the papers, so its low coverage is partly by construction. As a yardstick, not a test, the replicate-noise 95\% half-width is 1.0 point on a difference over 93 entries (Appendix~\ref{app:stability}). On entries created in 2026, not used in developing the strategies, the ordering is the same, though stopped replies were frequent (20 of 30 compared; five strategies run; Appendix~\ref{app:new}).

\textbf{Matched ablation.}\label{sec:ablation} On 38 entries weighted towards full text, high-effort extraction with writer-chosen synopsis terms (\emph{matched}) moves case table first from $-2.3$ to $-0.2$ points against direct reading, a step of $+2.1$ ($+1.1$ to $+3.2$), and the evidence graph from $+1.0$ to $+1.8$ ($+0.4$ to $+3.3$), a step of $+0.8$ ($-0.5$ to $+2.1$) (Appendix Tables~\ref{tab:app_abl_means}--\ref{tab:app_abl_diff}). The two changes were made together, but choosing synopsis terms cannot change which facts are extracted, so we attribute the gain to extraction effort, as an inference.

\textbf{Measurement choices move differences of this size.} Excluding entries whose writer was stopped while scoring entries with a stopped per-paper call placed the evidence graph 2.1 points below direct reading, a difference not distinguishable from zero under a symmetric rule. Splitting facts afresh for each draft gives levels 2--5 points higher, with the same ordering; counting missing drafts as zero gives intervals that include zero for all three other reading strategies (Appendix Tables~\ref{tab:app_full} and~\ref{tab:app_itt}).

\textbf{Contamination check.}\label{sec:memory} The memory-only control conveys only 5.7\% of facts for the newest band and 7.0\% on the 2026 entries (Figure~\ref{fig:memory}). Reading is not shown free of memorisation: within abstract-only papers, direct-reading coverage (pooled) falls from 49.8\% in the second-oldest band to 37.9\% in the newest (classic band 33.0\%), consistent with memory helping on older entries.

\textbf{Add-ons.}\label{sec:addons} Second-hand evidence raises coverage by $+1.8$ points ($+1.0$ to $+2.8$) on 96 entries, but also unsupported claims before repair and contradictions with the reference (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_secondhand}). The learned guide did not improve its own training entries, and its held-out gain, $+1.0$ points ($+0.0$ to $+2.0$), is indistinguishable from zero (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_guide}). Reconciliation moved no score by over 0.1 point, and the case database was not validated against per-patient ground truth (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_recon}).

\subsection{More full text, larger inputs and large entries}
\label{sec:strata}
\textbf{Entries with more full text (observational).} Grouping the 93 entries by the share of references readable in full gives 46 with none, 25 with under half and 21 with half or more (Figure~\ref{fig:strata}; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_strata}): across entries, few per group, confounded with creation date and journal. Case table first consistently trails in both groups with full text. The evidence graph does not: $-1.8$ points in the top group, but $+1.7$ on facts stated in full-text papers (Table~\ref{tab:stated}); on the ablation entries, whose drafts are the main run's, it is at $-1.2$ where half or more is full text and $+3.1$ elsewhere (Appendix~\ref{app:ablation}). The agentic curator trails in the top group but not on facts stated in full-text papers. We therefore do not claim that reading strategies in general separate where more full text is readable.

\textbf{Padding the same entries with unrelated papers.}\label{sec:size} Six ablation entries with at least half their papers readable in full were padded with unrelated full-text papers cited by other entries, from a median of 39,450 characters to about 500,000 and about 1,200,000 (direct reading then needs three prompts and a merge), with fact lists and task unchanged and no audit or repair (Appendix~\ref{app:r2_pad}). No strategy lost more than about a point at either size (case table first $-1.0$, $-2.1$ to $+0.0$, and $-0.9$, $-2.1$ to $+0.1$; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_pad_cov}). Direct reading rose by $+3.6$ points ($+1.0$ to $+5.9$) and $+3.2$ ($-0.7$ to $+6.8$), better on 5 of 6 entries at each size (sign test $p=0.219$); the unpadded drafts are an earlier generation with no replicate, so we do not read the rise as a gain. The others therefore lost ground to it at the first size, through its rise rather than their own fall (at most 1.0 point): their differences from it changed from no padding by $-2.8$ ($-4.3$ to $-1.1$; agentic curator), $-3.8$ ($-7.5$ to $-0.3$; evidence graph) and $-4.6$ points ($-7.2$ to $-1.5$; case table first); at the largest size these intervals include zero. The added papers were ignored (no draft cited one; the agentic curator opened few or none), so this tests input size, not crowding by relevant papers. The per-paper strategies lost added papers to stopped extraction calls (each complete on 3 of 6 entries at the largest size); these figures allow such losses, and under the symmetric completion rule only 2 entries remain (changes from $-3.0$ to $+4.7$ points; the same order at every size).

\textbf{Large real entries.} On four new entries with 76--148 references, 12--33 readable in full (375,649--998,176 characters), run without audit or repair, the safety filter stopped at least one reading strategy on every entry, so no new entry enters the comparison (Appendix~\ref{app:r2_large}). This is a finding about completion: one-call strategies lose whole entries, per-paper strategies lose papers. The comparison on large sets of relevant papers remains untested; the scored curator paragraphs cover only 5--10\% of these entries' references.

\subsection{What the audit can and cannot see}
\label{sec:audit}
\textbf{What it removes.} Unrepaired, the memory-only control has 22.1\% of claims unsupported and 0.6\% contradicted; after repair the other strategies keep 94.2--96.5\% judged supported (Table~\ref{tab:main}). A single value altered by rule (count, zygosity, residue or mode of inheritance) was flagged in all 60 altered paragraphs, on a claim with the altered value; this is not a sensitivity estimate: it shows that blunt single-value substitutions in paragraphs the audit had passed are caught. Re-audited unaltered with fresh calls, the 60 drew no flag (0 of 361 claims), the rate at which a re-audit flags a paragraph that had passed once, not a false-flag rate on arbitrary paragraphs.

\textbf{Six further classes of planted error} (30 items each, in evidence-graph paragraphs the audit had passed; Appendix~\ref{app:r2_audit}). The audit flagged the planted claim for every citation swapped to a paper of another entry (30 of 30) or of the same entry (26 of 26 with known truth) and every variant-naming sentence moved into another paper's paragraph (25 of 25 with known truth), easy cases, as the swapped paper is usually about something else. It caught every deleted negation (8 of 8), which reverses the claim and so plants a contradiction, not a broadening, but rarely a deleted hedge (2 of 8) or count, zygosity or ``one family'' (1 of 14); each rule has 2 to 8 items, and not every such edit broadens the claim (on reading, 2 hedge and about 3 zygosity edits are nearly meaning-preserving and 1 hedge edit is degenerate). It cannot see an added irrelevant citation (0 of 30), because a claim takes the best verdict over its cited papers, nor omissions (0 of 30), by construction; no unaltered control was flagged (0 of 113). In the stored audits, 4--7\% of (claim, cited paper) pairs in the reading strategies' multi-citation paragraphs are judged not to support the claim (memory-only control 27\%; excluding entries the repair round changed). A separate completeness check, run and judged by the same model, listed the deleted finding in full for 16 of 29 planted deletions (one stopped by the safety filter) and in full or in part for 24 (unaltered texts: 0 and 2 of 29), and 24 of 60 curator facts a direct-reading draft omitted although its text stated them, among about ten listed omissions per paper, a fifth matching any curator fact.

\textbf{What it cannot see.} Of 112 claims about hidden papers supported only by a later paper's passage, the full text does not support 11 (an upper bound, as supplements are excluded) and contradicts 3 (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_secondhand_claims}); an audit against what the writer saw passes them. It checks fidelity to the cited text, not correctness: a draft that copies a source's error passes.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
For this model and in the regime tested (2--12 references, 19\% of papers readable in full, inputs that fit one prompt), access to the source limits agreement with curator-written text far more than how the strategy reads; unrelated padding did not change this, nor did released retrieval-augmented and graph-based pipelines do better at the settings tested. \textbf{Measurement lessons:} handle stopped replies symmetrically; fix fact lists before scoring; compare strategies on the facts the supplied text states; report run-to-run noise beside intervals over entries; match settings unrelated to the representation before blaming it; keep per-paper audit verdicts, since a claim's best verdict hides over-citation; and check that a released pipeline runs with the model it will be used with, since pipelines depend on the model behind them in ways a model-agnostic description hides (STORM could not run here).

\textbf{Limitations.} (i) One model family wrote, audited and judged everything, including what the supplied text states; no human has read any draft, verdict or fact judgement; other model families are untested. (ii) The reference is indirect and narrow (variant-centred paragraphs, two-source ontology annotations, gene and inheritance tables; no narrative sections); other draft content is checked only by the audit. (iii) Every strategy is given the curators' reference list (retrieval was tested on ten entries). (iv) Only open-access text without supplements was available; reading and recall of facts the abstract states cannot be separated. (v) Variant names are not validated against sequences; inheritance is compared as coarse classes. (vi) Strategies were developed on the main entries; the 2026 set has 20 compared entries, five strategies and frequent stopped replies. (vii) Stopped replies remove entries unevenly. (viii) Entries with few references dominate; input size alone was tested by padding with unrelated text, and large sets of relevant papers could not be compared because of stopped replies. (ix) The audit does not catch over-citation, most broadened claims or omissions, and its stress test is small. (x) Released systems were run at one setting each, PaperQA2 without its agent and STORM not at all. (xi) Cost is at list price for one model.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%%%%%%%%% CONCLUSION %%%%%%%%%
For this model and in the regime tested, access to the source limits agreement with curator-written text: hiding the full text of the same papers lowers coverage from 79.8\% to 36.1\%, and three reading strategies convey 93.6--94.4\% of stated facts. Our case-table-first implementation trails where full text is readable; on the same papers and model, PaperQA2's document-query pipeline (without its agent) and GraphRAG with local search conveyed fewer facts at higher cost (10 and 4 entries), and STORM could not run with this model. No judgement was validated by a person.

\section{Strategies and settings}
\label{app:settings}
Table~\ref{tab:app_strategies} describes each strategy. Settings: one model for everything, Claude Opus 5.5 (\texttt{claude-opus-5-5}), called through the Claude Code command-line interface in headless mode with schema-constrained output; no temperature or seed is exposed. Reasoning effort is high for writers (direct reading, the graph and table writers, the agent, the memory draft) and medium for per-paper extraction in the evidence graph and case table first, for the audit and for all judging; the matched ablation raises per-paper extraction to high. The agentic strategy has read and search tools over a folder of the papers and nothing else. The agentic curator reports which papers it opened (self-reported; tool calls are not logged): in the 98 entries it wrote it reported opening every paper, and all 2,600 quotes it gave were found verbatim in the papers. The memory drafts that recall, then verify starts from are the memory-only control's drafts. The evidence graph discards any finding whose quote is not in the paper (none was). Synopsis terms are assigned by a lexical search over the ontology's labels and synonyms that returns candidates, from which the model chooses. Secondary measures: synopsis terms are compared with the HPO annotations exactly, hierarchically (both sets expanded with all ancestors) and by Lin semantic similarity, an ontology-aware comparison in the spirit of \citet{Khler2009ClinicalDiagnostics}; Lin similarity uses information content computed from the ontology's disease annotations, averaged in each direction to give precision and recall. Allelic variants are matched on normalised protein or nucleotide change, with a model judge for names that do not normalise; precision counts a variant OMIM does not list as a miss whether or not the paper reports it. No fact went unjudged in any run.

\section{Ablation of the extract-then-write strategies}
\label{app:ablation}
The 40-entry subset contains every entry with at least half its papers readable in full that all reading strategies completed, filled to 40 with entries drawn at random to bring each creation-era band to 8 entries; 22 of the 40 have at least half their papers readable in full and 29 have at least one, so absolute levels are higher than in the main comparison. 38 were completed by all seven strategies. The two changes in \emph{matched} (high-effort extraction; writer-chosen synopsis terms and, for the table, inheritance) were made together; since choosing synopsis terms cannot change which facts or variants are extracted, attributing the change in facts, claims and variants to extraction effort is an inference. Also giving the writer the papers' text (\emph{plus text}) raises claims judged supported, not facts (Table~\ref{tab:app_abl_diff}).

\textbf{The ablation set and the main comparison.} The ablation reuses the main run's drafts for direct reading, the evidence graph and case table first: on all 38 entries the drafts and fact marks are identical. By the main comparison's access groups the 38 entries comprise 11 with no full text, 7 with under half, 19 with half or more and 1 with all papers in full text; all are among the main comparison's 93 entries. Main run on the 38 ablation entries: evidence graph $+1.0$ ($-0.8$ to $+2.9$; better/worse/tied 19/15/4), case table first $-2.3$ ($-4.5$ to $+0.0$; 13/24/1). Main run on its 21 entries with half or more full text: evidence graph $-1.8$ ($-4.4$ to $+0.6$; 7/12/2), case table first $-6.1$ ($-8.3$ to $-3.7$; 3/18/0); 19 of them are in the ablation set (the other two are among the entries a matched or plus-text variant did not complete), and on those 19 the evidence graph is at $-1.2$ ($-3.8$ to $+1.3$; 7/10/2) and case table first at $-6.2$ ($-8.6$ to $-3.5$; 3/16/0). On the ablation's other 19 entries (no full text, under half, or all full text): evidence graph $+3.1$ ($+1.0$ to $+5.6$; 12/5/2), case table first $+1.5$ ($-1.1$ to $+4.7$; 10/8/1). Because the drafts are the same, the estimates differ only in the entries they average over; intervals here come from a separate bootstrap and can differ slightly from those of Table~\ref{tab:app_abl_diff} and Table~\ref{tab:app_strata}.

Completion: direct reading, the evidence graph and case table first completed all 40; the matched and plus-text graph variants each lost one entry and the matched and plus-text table variants two, because the safety filter stopped a high-effort extraction call for a paper that had gone through at medium effort. Generation cost per entry at list price: direct reading \$0.32, evidence graph \$0.65, matched \$0.72, plus text \$0.88, case table first \$0.72, matched \$0.84, plus text \$0.97; with audit and scoring: \$1.59, \$2.00, \$2.14, \$2.25, \$2.07, \$2.26, \$2.29. \section{Reconciled case database and learned curation guide}
\label{app:addons}
\textbf{Reconciliation.} Over the 100 entries, reconciliation proposed 57 merges of patient or family records and accepted 40 (17 rejected by the verifier) in 27 entries; it merged 11 variant names and found 57 conflicts between papers. Counted individuals went from 2,580 to 2,413 (net; counts rose in a few entries where a re-report gave a larger family size) and families from 1,126 to 1,092. A planted test on six entries merged 10 of 10 copied reports with their originals and merged no unrelated records; this easy case shows only that a stated re-report is caught. The accepted merges were not checked by a person. Cost: one or two extra calls per entry. None of the entry-level measures uses patient counts, so no change in them is expected. Agreement of the table's patient-count fractions with those in the HPO annotations (about 560 terms) was 48\% before and after, with a mean absolute difference of 0.26; each reference fraction comes from one source while the table pools all papers.

\textbf{Learned guide.} Training (27 of 30 training entries had a direct-reading draft; scored without the audit, with the earlier per-draft fact splitting): baseline OMIM facts conveyed 53.8\% and synopsis exact F1 36.6\%. Round 1 changed facts by $+0.14$ points and synopsis F1 by $+1.29$; round 2 by $-1.41$ and $+3.57$. The text rules of round 1 and the synopsis rules of round 2 were selected by a rule fixed in advance. The final guide, run once more on the training entries: facts $+0.06$ points, synopsis F1 $+1.28$ points. A first guide, learned before stopped replies were recorded, had treated three empty drafts as omissions and was discarded. On the held-out entries, a draft makes about twice as many statements about a paper as the OMIM paragraph has facts (direct reading 24.0 statements per paper, with the guide 22.2, against 11.9 facts), and 24\% (direct) and 25\% (guide) of a draft's statements have a counterpart in that paragraph; the paragraph is variant-centred, so this is a lower bound on agreement with the entry.

\subsection{Updates, retrieval without a reference list, and draft--reference disagreements}
\label{app:updates}
\textbf{Disagreements.} Of 37 facts on which a direct-reading draft contradicted the reference, the same model, re-examining them against the paper, judged the paper to support the draft in 22, the reference in 1 and both in 1; 13 could not be decided from the readable text; these are unconfirmed leads (no curator has checked them, many rest on abstracts only, and the judge had already passed the same claims). \subsection{Analyses that were corrected}
The first analysis excluded entries whose writer was stopped but scored entries in which a per-paper call was stopped; under it the evidence graph appeared 2.1 points below direct reading on OMIM facts conveyed, and with completion treated the same way for every strategy the difference is not distinguishable from zero. \section{Second round: released systems, input size and audit coverage}
\label{app:round2}
These experiments were run on 20--21 November 2026 with the same model, reply cache and scoring as the rest of the study; a reply stopped by the model's safety filter was recorded and never retried with a changed prompt. Before any run finished, their sizes were cut to about a third of the design (for example 10 instead of 24 entries for PaperQA2 and STORM, and 30 instead of about 60 items per planted class), and some ended below even the reduced size because entries cost more model calls than projected. Every result in this section is exploratory and shows directions and large effects only. No person checked any judgement.

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Released systems}
\label{app:r2_ext}
\textbf{How the systems were run.} Each system was installed from the package index at its latest released version (Table~\ref{tab:app_ext_run}) and run on exactly the entry's supplied papers, at the access level each paper has, through a local adapter in front of the one model; no system was told how it would be scored. STORM was given the entry title as its topic. PaperQA2 and GraphRAG, both question-answering systems, were asked six fixed section questions per entry (description; clinical features; inheritance; molecular genetics and the variants each paper reported; genotype--phenotype relationships; pathogenesis and functional evidence), and their answers were concatenated; the strategies write an OMIM-style entry in one pass. GraphRAG's answers include headings and framing lines as separate blocks; all text counts for crediting anywhere in the draft and for the share without a mapped citation. Citations were mapped back to the supplied papers without editing the text: PaperQA2 names papers; GraphRAG's local search cites text units, entities and relationships (a block's papers are the union over every record it cites, counting only records the query showed the model); global search cites community reports only, each mapped to the papers of the text units its community's entities came from, a looser mapping. Facts are credited to paragraphs citing the paper, as in the main comparison, and, as a check that does not depend on the mapping, anywhere in the draft (Tables~\ref{tab:app_ext_own} and~\ref{tab:app_ext_strat}). The two are separate judge passes, so the second can fall below the first; it did on 5 system entries, by differences within the judge's noise. The claim-level audit without repair was run on every paragraph with mapped citations of the 4 entries both systems completed; the strategies' figures there are their stored audits before repair (Table~\ref{tab:app_ext_audit}).

\textbf{Deviations from the design.} (1) Sizes: PaperQA2 and STORM on the first 10 entries of the seeded order instead of 24; GraphRAG within 1,000 model calls (floor 6 entries) instead of 3,000 (floor 12). GraphRAG stopped at 4 entries when the fifth did not fit what was left of the cap (698 calls were made in all, including the global-search queries and a test run). (2) PaperQA2 ran without its agent, as the document-query pipeline (the package's manual, no-agent usage): its default agent selects tools by function calling, and its alternative agent ends with a function-calling step, which the adapter cannot serve. Retrieval, contextual summaries and the answer step are PaperQA2's own; the agent's iterative search and its decision to stop are not exercised. (3) The shared local embedding model truncates its input at 512 tokens, so the first PaperQA2 run embedded only the first about 510 tokens of each about 5,000-character chunk. PaperQA2 was run again with whole-chunk embeddings (windows of 510 tokens, averaged), which is the main result; the first run is kept as a sensitivity result. Requests identical between the two runs were served from the cache in the second, so its fresh calls understate a stand-alone run; calls, tokens and cost count each distinct request once, with the figures it had when made. (4) GraphRAG's global search was added on the same indexes; local search, the command-line default, is the main GraphRAG result. (5) STORM completed no entry (below). (6) The strategies' drafts are the published drafts, after their audit-and-repair round; the systems' drafts had none. On these 10 entries the repair round revised 3, 2, 5 and 1 paragraphs (direct reading, agentic curator, evidence graph, case table first) of 234, 240, 317 and 291 in the final drafts and deleted none; in the hidden-full-text experiment repair moved coverage by 0.2 points (Table~\ref{tab:app_hidden}), so this favours the strategies very slightly at most. (7) Wall-clock time is not recorded per strategy in the main run; for them, time is the sum of model-call seconds, which is also given for the systems (Table~\ref{tab:app_ext_cost}). The systems ran under the adapter's concurrency cap of 3.

\textbf{STORM.} STORM completed 0 of 10 entries. The model's safety filter stopped every chain-of-thought prompt of its research stage (30 of 30 fresh requests), while the 20 plain outline prompts were answered, so no article was produced; nothing was retried or reworded, and there is nothing to score. It made 5.0 distinct requests per entry (50 fresh in all), \$0.07 at list price.

\subsection{Controlled padding of the input}
\label{app:r2_pad}
\textbf{Design.} The entries are the ablation entries that every ablation variant completed and that have at least half their papers readable in full, taken in entry-number order; all four strategies had completed them in the main run. The added papers are full-text papers already fetched for other entries, excluding the entry's own papers, papers cited by any entry sharing a causal gene with it, papers whose text names one of its genes, papers whose citing entry has no reference record (gene unknown), and papers over 180,000 characters (the extraction cap). They are drawn in a seeded order per entry; the additions of level P1 (about 500,000 characters) are a prefix of those of P2 (about 1,200,000), and each level stops at the paper that brings the total nearest its target (sizes in Table~\ref{tab:app_pad_size}). The supplied order is shuffled with the entry's seed, but direct reading and the agent's index sort papers by year and the extract-then-write strategies read each paper separately, so order has little effect. The task prompt is unchanged. Level P0 (no padding) uses the main run's drafts before repair, rebuilt from the model cache without new generation (replaying the cached audit and repair on them reproduces the stored final drafts exactly); P1 and P2 are fresh generations. No level was audited or repaired. Coverage is that of the entry's own fixed facts, credited to paragraphs citing the paper (present plus half of partly present, over judged facts). Completion follows the main rule, so a per-paper extraction failure makes a draft incomplete; every such failure here was a safety-filter stop on an added paper, so the main analysis allows failures on added papers, and under the symmetric rule 2 entries remain (Table~\ref{tab:app_pad_cov2}; completion in Table~\ref{tab:app_pad_completion}). The unpadded drafts are an earlier generation with no replicate, so a change from P0 mixes input size with run-to-run variation (within-entry standard deviation of single-entry coverage in the replicate runs: 2.5 points for direct reading, 4.0 for the evidence graph; Table~\ref{tab:app_stability}).

\textbf{What the strategies did with the added papers.} No paragraph of any padded draft cited an added paper (0 of 115--140 paragraphs per strategy and level), and none attributed a statement about the disorder to one. The agentic curator opened every one of the entry's own papers at every level, 5 of 63 added papers at P1 and none of 174 at P2. The extract-then-write strategies marked 59 papers not relevant at P1 and 159 (evidence graph) and 163 (case table first) at P2. Direct reading's prompt capped each paper at 75,000--300,000 characters at P0 (one own paper cut) and at 33,333--66,666 at P1 (two own papers, both in one entry, and 24 added papers cut); at P2 it was split into three prompts and a merge, with no paper cut and none lost in the merge. Extraction calls were stopped for 1 (evidence graph) and 3 (case table first) papers at P1 and for 3 and 5 at P2.

\textbf{Deviations.} The design planned 12 entries and about 1,800,000 characters at P2. Before any run finished this was cut to 8 entries, about 1,200,000 characters and at most 800 new model calls; the pilot (about 125 new calls per entry) projected about 1,000 calls for 8 entries, so the entries were reduced in order to 6. The run made 683 new model calls (Table~\ref{tab:app_pad_cost}). Wall-clock time per task includes waiting for one of at most 4 concurrent model calls.

\subsection{Large entries}
\label{app:r2_large}
\textbf{Selection and deviations.} Eligible phenotype entries (the main set's rules) with 30--150 references in no existing evaluation set were sampled with a fixed seed, and the 8 with the most references readable in full (at least 5; ties by entry number) were selected; the design called for a sample of 400 and 16 entries, and the sample was up to 200. Only the first 4 in rule order were run: an entry cost about 250--330 new model calls, most of them per-paper extraction for the evidence graph and the case table, and the cap of 1,500 new calls allowed no more (1,338 were made). The selection was resolved in three passes across machine restarts, and the first three entries were run once their place among the 8 was certain. The comparison on facts the supplied text states was not made. Direct reading holds 600,000 characters in one prompt, cutting each paper to the larger of 20,000 characters and 600,000 divided by the number of papers; above that, papers are split into batches of that size and the partial drafts merged, and only a paper longer than a whole batch is cut. Each per-paper extraction reads at most 180,000 characters; no paper of these entries exceeded it. The new entries were generated and scored without audit or repair, whereas the 12 earlier entries (Appendix~\ref{app:large}) were scored after repair.

\textbf{Outcome.} No new entry was completed by every strategy, so the tier's comparison rests on the 10 earlier entries, with the figures of Table~\ref{tab:app_large}. Table~\ref{tab:app_r2_large} gives the new entries; its coverage figures describe drafts written without the papers whose extraction was stopped and are not a comparison. On the one new entry where every strategy wrote a draft (152700), direct reading conveyed 52.3\%, the agentic curator 51.5\%, case table first 49.6\% (2 papers lost) and the evidence graph 39.8\% (3 papers lost): one entry, not a result.

\subsection{Audit coverage: planted errors beyond single values}
\label{app:r2_audit}
\textbf{Construction.} Items were planted in the main run's evidence-graph drafts, as in the single-value test, in paragraphs the audit had passed as fully supported that cite one paper and are longer than 150 characters (a pool of 1,494), at most 3 items per entry per class, in one seeded order shared by all classes (Table~\ref{tab:app_audit_classes}). Citation swaps and added citations change the in-text author--year label as well as the cited paper; the new paper is a random readable paper of another entry (swap to another entry, added citation) or another readable paper of the same entry (swap within the entry). A moved sentence names a variant, carries no author citation and is inserted at a seeded sentence boundary after the first sentence of the target paragraph, whose paper must not already name that variant. Truth is decided mechanically from the text the auditor reads (title, abstract and full text cut at 180,000 characters): a variant occurs in a paper if any of its protein or nucleotide forms, its residue or nucleotide position, or its raw name does. A same-entry swap is truth-known when the paragraph names at least one variant absent from the new paper, a moved sentence when none of its variants occurs in the target paper. Qualifiers are deleted by five rules (count, hedge, negation, single family or patient, zygosity; Table~\ref{tab:app_audit_rules}). All 30 qualifier edits were read before the run: 5 of 8 hedge edits change the claim, 2 are nearly meaning-preserving and 1 is degenerate; all 8 negation edits reverse the claim (so they plant contradictions, not broadening); about 3 of 8 zygosity edits are nearly meaning-preserving where de novo or dominant inheritance implies heterozygosity; 2 of 4 count edits are clean, 1 ungrammatical and 1 awkward; both single-family edits are clean. No rule mostly produced ungrammatical or meaning-preserving edits, so all five were kept. The \emph{planted claim} is every claim for swaps to another entry and added citations, claims naming the absent variant for same-entry swaps and moved sentences, and claims taken from the altered sentence for deleted qualifiers; an item counts as flagged when any planted claim is unsupported or contradicted. The unaltered paragraphs of all classes (113) were audited once, fresh, as the shared control. The audit used its own prompts and best-verdict rule, on items in chunks of 5 paragraphs (the single-value test audited its 60 together).

\textbf{Completeness check.} A separate check lists findings a cited paper states that the draft's text about that paper omits, each with a quote that is verified against the paper. It was run on 30 planted deletions (a sentence other than the first, carrying a variant or a number, years and entry numbers aside, that appears nowhere else in the draft's text about that paper), on the same texts unaltered, and on natural omissions: 60 curator facts (a seeded sample, facts of papers readable in full first, of 130 eligible) that direct-reading drafts omitted although the text the writer had states them, on 28 papers. A listed omission matches a deleted sentence mechanically when it names one of the sentence's variants or carries all its numbers, and by judgement when the model judge says it does (Table~\ref{tab:app_audit_complete}).

\textbf{Citation precision.} The main run's entry files keep only each claim's best verdict. Verdicts per cited paper were recovered without new calls by replaying the stored audit from the model-reply cache, which is possible only for entries whose paragraphs the repair round left unchanged (about 80--88\% of entries per strategy, and every memory-only entry, which has no repair); a paragraph was kept only if the replayed best verdict of every claim equals the stored one (Table~\ref{tab:app_audit_precision}).

\textbf{Deviations and cost.} 30 items per class instead of about 60, and at most 600 new model calls instead of 1,200, pilot included; natural omissions capped at 60 facts; the audit run on chunks of 5 paragraphs rather than on all items together; one control audit for all classes. The qualifier rules were tightened once before the pilot after reading the edits they produced, and after the main run the rule that picks the planted claims for deleted qualifiers was widened (a claim also counts when it contains two of the content words next to the deletion point), which recovered two flagged negation items, with no new calls. The mechanical matching of the completeness check was corrected after the pilot (number words and thousands separators read as digits), with no new calls. The run made 540 new model calls (cap 600) at a list price of \$43.78; 2 replies were stopped by the safety filter, both on the completeness check of one item (its altered and unaltered text), so the completeness figures are over 29 items.

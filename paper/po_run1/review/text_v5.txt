WHAT LIMITS EVIDENCE-GROUNDED KNOWLEDGE-
BASE WRITING?
A CONTROLLED COMPARISON
OF GENERATION STRATEGIES AGAINST CURATOR-
WRITTEN TEXT
Anonymous authors
Paper under double-blind review
ABSTRACT
Many system designs turn papers into cited, structured text, but comparisons hold-
ing papers, model and scoring fixed concern question answering and summarisa-
tion, not writing what experts wrote about each paper. We build such a comparison
from 100 OMIM phenotype entries, each drafted from exactly the papers its cu-
rators cited by one model under one claim-level audit and scoring; the primary
endpoint, OMIM facts conveyed, is coverage of fixed fact lists split from curator-
written per-paper text. Hiding the full text of the same papers lowers coverage
from 79.8% to 36.1% (means over entries). On facts the supplied text states, the
reading strategies convey more than nine in ten; on facts it does not state, under
7%. Three reading strategies are within about a point of one another, our case-
table-first implementation trails them, and drafting from memory conveys far less.
On the same papers and model, released PaperQA2 and GraphRAG pipelines con-
veyed fewer facts than direct reading at higher cost (10 and 4 entries), and STORM
could not run with this model. Handling stopped replies asymmetrically produced
a spurious difference larger than those between reading strategies. One model
family wrote, audited and judged everything, and no human validated any judge-
ment.

INTRODUCTION
Curated knowledge bases are written by experts who read papers and record, entry by entry, what
each paper showed. Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man (OMIM), the reference catalogue of
human genes and genetic disorders (Amberger et al., 2014; 2019; Hamosh et al., 2021), is built this
way, and so, in part, are the resources that record variant interpretations (Landrum et al., 2017; 2025),
phenotype annotations (Robinson et al., 2008; Gargano et al., 2024) and the validity of gene–disease
associations (Strande et al., 2017). Language models can now read the papers, and many system
designs have been proposed for turning a set of documents into structured, cited text: retrieval-
augmented or long-context reading (Lewis et al., 2020; Li et al., 2024), extraction into a graph or a
table before writing (Edge et al., 2024; Joshi et al., 2026), agents that decide what to read (Nakano
et al., 2021; L´ala et al., 2023; Skarlinski et al., 2024), drafting first and verifying afterwards (Gao
et al., 2023a; Dhuliawala et al., 2024), and prompts optimised from examples (Khattab et al., 2023;
Agrawal et al., 2026).
Comparisons between such designs exist, several with the model and settings held fixed (Li et al.,
2024; 2025a; Han et al., 2025), but they concern question answering and query-based summarisation
on public benchmarks. The closest work in genetics and biology searches a corpus for its sources,
measures memory alone, compares models rather than strategies, or scores against something other
than what curators wrote about each paper (Section 2). The curator’s task itself, writing an entry
from exactly the cited papers and scoring it per paper against what the curators wrote, has not been
used to compare strategies. Three questions are open. With the model, the sources, the guards
and the scoring held fixed, how much does the generation strategy matter? What limits agreement
with what experts wrote: the strategy, or what the strategy was given to read? And which errors
does a claim-level audit against the cited source catch, and which can it not see? This paper is an

We answer the questions with a controlled benchmark in which the generation strategy is the only
variable (Figure 1). Each of 100 OMIM phenotype entries with 2–12 references is written from
its header and exactly the papers its curators cited, with the causal gene withheld; only 19% of the
cited papers are readable in full through open access. The strategies form three paradigms (read and
write; extract, then write; memory first), one control (a memory-only draft with no papers) and three
add-ons, each tested against its parent. Each strategy writes a whole entry, but the primary endpoint,
OMIM facts conveyed, scores only what the draft says about each cited paper against curator-written
per-paper text: OMIM’s allelic-variant descriptions as deposited in ClinVar, each split once, without
sight of any draft, into a fixed list of facts (6,652 over 476 paragraphs).
In the regime tested, what limits agreement is what the strategy was given to read, not how it
reads. The direction is expected, since curators wrote from full papers; we add the size of the effect
within the same papers and an attribution of what the drafts lack. Hiding the full text of papers that
have one lowers coverage of the same fixed facts from 79.8% to 36.1% (means over 46 entries, no
arm repaired). On facts the supplied text is judged to state, direct reading, the agentic curator and
the evidence graph convey 93.6–94.4%, within a point of one another; on facts it does not state, 5.5–
6.9%. The memory-based strategies are far behind; of the reading strategies only our case-table-first
implementation trails direct reading (an interval that excludes zero, a sign test that does not reach
0.05), most on facts stated in full-text papers; a matched ablation suggests, as an inference, that
part of this reflects its extraction effort. These results hold for one model and for entries with 2–12
references whose papers fit one prompt, a regime in which direct reading is expected to do well;
padding with unrelated papers tests size alone, and large sets of relevant papers remain untested.
Our contributions are: (1) a benchmark and harness for evidence-grounded knowledge-base writing
against curator-written references, built only from public resources, with a claim-level audit, fixed
fact lists and per-entry release of every draft, verdict and score; (2) a controlled comparison of three
paradigms, a control and three add-ons under one model, with completion as an outcome, run-to-run
variation, a matched ablation and released PaperQA2 and GraphRAG pipelines; (3) a within-paper
measurement of how much access bounds agreement, with missed and conveyed facts attributed and
strategies compared on the facts the supplied text states; (4) a characterisation of what the audit can
and cannot see, including six classes of planted error; (5) a memory-only control by creation date,
and null or negative results at this scale for recall-then-verify, reconciliation and a small learned

RELATED WORK
Language models for literature curation in genetics and biology.
Structured prompting and as-
sistant tools have been proposed for populating and maintaining knowledge bases (Caufield et al.,
2024b;a; Toro et al., 2024; Niyonkuru et al., 2025). In clinical genetics, language models have been
applied to extracting variant, gene–disease and gene–phenotype evidence from papers (Li et al.,
2025b; Furlan et al., 2025; Wang & Wang, 2026; Yin et al., 2026). FlyAOC scores memorisa-
tion, pipeline and agent harnesses that search a paper corpus against FlyBase curators’ annotations
(Zhang et al., 2026); GEN-KnowRD generates schema-guided rare-disease profiles, assesses their
quality and builds a knowledge base from them, evaluated by disease ranking on patient benchmarks
(Yan et al., 2026); PaperQA2 writes cited Wikipedia-style summaries of scientific topics, compared
against existing Wikipedia articles (Skarlinski et al., 2024); and Groza et al. (2026) measure model
memory of rare-disease genes and phenotypes. CGBench, built from ClinGen’s expert-curated liter-
ature interpretations, scores model explanations against human explanations, but compares models
rather than generation strategies (Queen et al., 2025). In our benchmark the cited papers are fixed,
the reference is the curators’ own per-paper text and structured fields, and the generation strategy is
the only variable.
Intermediate representations versus long-context reading.
Retrieval augmentation supplies a
model with selected passages (Lewis et al., 2020). Whether this beats placing all documents in a
long context is contested: one re-evaluation finds long context generally ahead on question answer-
ing (Li et al., 2025c); others argue that very long contexts dilute focus on relevant information (Yu
et al., 2024), propose hybrids (Li et al., 2024), or report no single winner (Li et al., 2025a). Graph
intermediates are used for query-focused summarisation (Edge et al., 2024), and systematic com-
parisons ask when graphs help (Han et al., 2025; Xiang et al., 2025). Database-centred systems
reconcile extracted databases for systematic reviews (Joshi et al., 2026). Agentic and retrieval-
augmented systems write cited answers or articles from the scientific literature (L´ala et al., 2023;
Asai et al., 2024; Singh et al., 2025; Skarlinski et al., 2024), or Wikipedia-like articles from Internet
sources (Shao et al., 2024). Here, by contrast, the papers are fixed to the curators’ reference list,
the reference is the curators’ own text, all paradigms share one audit and one scoring, and access is
measured and manipulated.
Memory, verification and contamination.
Draft-then-verify methods plan verification questions
(Dhuliawala et al., 2024), post-edit unsupported content (Gao et al., 2023a), or check against domain
databases (Wang et al., 2025). Because curated knowledge bases are public, memory is also a
confound. Methods detect pretraining data or contamination from black-box access (Shi et al., 2024;
Oren et al., 2024; Golchin & Surdeanu, 2024; 2025), exploit training cut-offs (Roberts et al., 2023),
or collect new test items over time (Jain et al., 2025; White et al., 2024). We adopt the time-based
view: a memory-only control analysed by entry creation date, and entries created in 2026.
Supervision by prompt optimisation.
Instructions can be optimised by language models (Zhou
et al., 2022; Pryzant et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023), evolved (Guo et al., 2023; Fernando et al., 2024;
Agrawal et al., 2026), or compiled in multi-stage programs (Khattab et al., 2023; Opsahl-Ong et al.,
2024). Our learned guide is a deliberately small instance.
Evaluating cited long-form text.
Factuality metrics decompose long-form text into atomic claims
and verify each against a knowledge source (Min et al., 2023; Wei et al., 2024). Citation quality has
been measured for models that generate text with citations (Gao et al., 2023b) and for medical
references (Wu et al., 2025). Work studies model judges against human preferences (Zheng et al.,
2023; Shankar et al., 2024) and reports that evaluators recognise and favour their own generations
(Panickssery et al., 2024); our judges share the writer’s model family, with no human validation. Text
mining yields more from full text than from abstracts (Westergaard et al., 2018), whose structure and
content differ (Cohen et al., 2010); that drafts from abstracts convey less is therefore expected, and
we measure how much.

BENCHMARK AND STRATEGIES
3.1
TASK, ENTRIES AND REFERENCE
An entry is e = (h, R) with header h (number, title and alternative titles) and references R =
{d1, . . . , dn}, the papers its curators cited, each with an access level a(d) ∈{full text, abstract, title}
determined by open access. A strategy S maps (h, R) to a draft D: paragraphs, each citing a non-
empty subset of R, and sets of genes, inheritance modes, ontology terms and allelic variants. The
causal gene is withheld, because naming it from the papers is part of the curator’s task.
Entries and papers. We draw 100 OMIM phenotype entries at random with a fixed seed, 20 from
each of five bands of entry number that track when the entry was created (Figure 5). Eligible entries
have 2–12 cited references, a causal gene in the gene map, at least five phenotype annotations and at
least one curator paragraph in scope, so the sample selects well-annotated, molecularly characterised
entries. Of the 515 cited papers, 96 (19%) are readable in full through PubMed Central or Europe
PMC (Rosonovski et al., 2024), 345 as an abstract only and 74 as a title only; supplementary material
was not retrieved.
Reference. The curator-written per-paper text is OMIM’s allelic-variant descriptions as deposited
by OMIM in ClinVar (Landrum et al., 2025): variant-centred prose from OMIM’s gene entries about
one or more cited papers. A paragraph is in scope for an entry when it is about a paper the entry
cites; 476 are in scope. The narrative sections of the phenotype entries are not available publicly
and are not compared. The clinical-synopsis reference is the Human Phenotype Ontology’s disease
annotations (Gargano et al., 2024): 2,311 annotations, of which 1,835 are the HPO team’s own
curation from papers and 476 are taken from OMIM. The causal gene comes from OMIM’s gene
map via MedGen (Louden, 2020) and mim2gene; inheritance comes from HPO and MedGen modes,
compared as coarse classes that must be equal as sets. The allelic-variant reference is the entry’s 427
in-scope OMIM-submitted ClinVar records.
3.2
STRATEGIES
The strategies are three paradigms, one control and three add-ons (Figure 1; Appendix Table 5).
Read and write: direct reading puts all papers in one prompt and is the baseline; the agentic curator
(a term also used by Balhoff & Lapp, 2026, for agents working in a workspace that supplies the
source publication) works in a folder of the papers with read and search tools and must quote every
paragraph’s source verbatim. Extract, then write: the evidence graph extracts quote-backed findings
per paper into a graph of papers, genes, variants, phenotypes and inheritance; case table first extracts
one record per reported patient or family per paper and pools them; in both, the writer sees only the
intermediate, synopsis terms are computed by rule, and so is inheritance for the table. Memory first:
recall, then verify writes from memory, audits the draft against the papers and rewrites it from what
survives; it may not add content from the papers. The memory-only control is the memory draft
with no papers, audited but not repaired. The add-ons are second-hand evidence on direct reading
(passages from later open-access papers describing a paper not readable in full), the reconciled
case database on case table first (merging the same patient, family or variant across papers), and a
learned curation guide on direct reading (rules learned in two reflection rounds from 27 training
entries). These are our own implementations (released systems: Section 4.4). The four strategies
that read the papers are the reading strategies.
Guards and model. For every strategy, ontology identifiers are chosen by the model only from
candidates returned by an ontology search, gene symbols are normalised against HGNC (Seal et al.,
2023), entry cross-reference numbers recalled from memory are removed, and every paragraph must
cite a supplied paper. One model, Claude Opus 5.5, does everything, called through a command-line
interface in headless mode with schema-constrained output and no temperature or seed; reasoning
effort is high for writers and medium otherwise (Appendix B), no strategy has web access, and
replies are cached. A reply stopped by the model’s safety filter comes back empty, is recorded as
such and is never retried with a changed prompt; the reasons are not visible.

3.3
AUDIT, SCORING AND STATISTICS
Completion. S completes e when its draft exists and no per-paper call failed. A one-call strategy
whose reply is stopped loses the entry, a per-paper strategy only a paper; scoring the second and
excluding the first would bias the comparison, so completion is reported as an outcome, strategies
are compared only on entries every one of them completed, and a sensitivity analysis counts a
missing draft as zero.
Audit. Each paragraph is split into atomic claims c attributed to the papers R(c) ⊆R it cites. Each
paper receives its claims and returns a verdict v(c, d) ∈{supported, partly supported, unsupported,
contradicted} with a quote; a “supported” verdict whose quote is not found verbatim in the paper is
downgraded. A claim’s verdict is the best over R(c). Flagged (unsupported or contradicted) claims
get one repair round.
Definitions used throughout. A curator paragraph g is an in-scope OMIM allelic-variant de-
scription. Its fixed fact list Φ(g) was produced once, without sight of any draft, and is reused
for every strategy; splitting afresh for each draft would let the split adapt to the draft and give
strategies different denominators.
Each fact is marked present, partly present (stated less pre-
cisely), absent or contradicted in what D says about the paragraph’s papers, and coverage(D) =
(#present+ 1
2 #partly present)/#facts judged. OMIM facts conveyed, the primary endpoint, is this
coverage averaged over entries (the mean over entries); paired differences are means over entries of
per-entry differences. Levels in the main text are means over entries unless marked pooled (the same
ratio over all facts in a group, the only form of the attributions of missed and conveyed facts). A
claim is judged supported when the audit’s final verdict is “supported”; this is a judgement of fidelity
to the cited text by the model family that wrote the draft, not of correctness, and unsupported is not
wrong. Finally, each fixed fact carries a judgement of whether the text available for its paper states,
partly states or does not state it (a “stated” verdict needs a quote verified in that text). It is a property
of the supplied text, the same for every strategy, and a model judgement. The reference covers a
minority of the task: on the 68 held-out entries of the guide experiment, 24% of a direct-reading
draft’s statements have a counterpart in the paragraph (Appendix H).
Secondary measures. Synopsis terms are scored against the HPO annotations by precision, recall
and F1 (exact, hierarchical and by Lin similarity), allelic variants by recall and precision, the causal
gene by F1 and inheritance by agreement (Appendix B). A strategy that predicts nothing where the
reference is not empty scores zero.
Statistics. Paired differences from direct reading are means over entries with a bootstrap 95% inter-
val over entries (10,000 resamples; Efron, 1979) and entries better/worse/tied. The main comparison
adds an exact two-sided sign test, ties dropped (Dixon & Mood, 1946), Holm-adjusted (Holm, 1979)
across the eight secondary headline measures within each strategy; the primary endpoint is not ad-
justed. The interval and the sign test are the tests of a difference. The main comparison is one run
per entry; replicate noise from three replicates on 30 entries is a yardstick for how much a difference
moves when generation, audit and scoring are repeated. “Points” are percentage points.

EXPERIMENTS
Takeaways. (1) Access bounds agreement: hiding full text lowers coverage sharply, and reading
strategies convey more than nine in ten of the facts the supplied text states. (2) Three reading
strategies are close; our case-table-first implementation trails, most where full text is readable. (3)
Released PaperQA2 and GraphRAG pipelines convey fewer facts than direct reading at higher cost;
STORM could not run with this model. (4) Unrelated padding costs no strategy more than about a
point; on large real entries, stopped replies decide which drafts exist. (5) Memory alone conveys far
less, and little for the newest entries. (6) The audit catches swapped citations and moved sentences,
not over-citation, most broadened claims or omissions, and checks fidelity, not correctness. (3), (4)
and the new classes of (6) rest on 4 to 10 entries per experiment and 30 items per class: directions
and large effects only. Every result rests on judgements by the writer’s model family, none validated
by a person (entry sets: Appendix Table 4).

4.1
WHAT LIMITS AGREEMENT: ACCESS TO THE SOURCE
Hiding the full text of the same papers. In 49 entries the 91 papers readable in full were hidden
and each entry was rewritten by direct reading from their abstracts, with and without second-hand
passages from later open-access papers, and scored on the same fixed facts of the 46 entries with
a full-text draft. The full-text arm is the main run’s direct-reading draft before the repair round, so
no arm is repaired. Coverage is 79.8% with the full text and 36.1% from the abstracts, higher with
the full text in every entry but one, which tied (Figure 2); scoring the full-text arm after repair gives
+43.9 points (Appendix Table 24).
Where the missed facts went. Of the facts direct reading did not fully convey, 49% were stated in
the available text for full-text papers, 2% for abstract-only and none for title-only papers (Figure 3;
pooled); over all facts, only 4% were omitted although stated (Appendix Table 18).
Where the conveyed facts came from. Of 1,420 facts direct reading conveyed in full from abstract-
only papers, 74.7% are stated in the paper’s own abstract, 15.6% partly and 7.2% only in another
supplied paper; only 24 (1.7%) are stated in no supplied text (recalled, inferred or misjudged). Facts
conveyed in part are more often outside the text (10.0%; pooled, Appendix Table 19).
4.2
CONTROLLED COMPARISON OF THE STRATEGIES
Three reading strategies are close; case table first trails them. On the primary endpoint, direct
reading, the evidence graph and the agentic curator lie within 0.7 points of one another (Table 1;
completion in Appendix Table 6); the evidence graph’s interval includes zero and the agentic cura-
tor’s touches it. Case table first’s excludes zero, but its sign test does not reach 0.05 (p = 0.071; ev-
idence graph 0.213, agentic curator 0.242). After Holm adjustment no secondary headline measure
reaches p < 0.05 for these three, though some intervals exclude zero, whereas the memory-based
strategies differ on the primary endpoint (p < 0.001) and four or five secondary measures each (Ap-
pendix Tables 8–9). Recall, then verify may not add content from the papers, so its low coverage is
partly by construction.
Matched ablation. On 38 entries weighted towards full text, high-effort extraction with writer-
chosen synopsis terms (matched) moves case table first from −2.3 to −0.2 points against direct
reading, a step of +2.1 (+1.1 to +3.2); the evidence graph’s step is not distinguishable from zero
(Appendix F). The two changes were made together, but choosing synopsis terms cannot change
which facts are extracted, so we attribute the gain to extraction effort, as an inference.

As a yardstick, not a test, three replicates of generation, audit and scoring on 30 entries give a
replicate-noise 95% half-width of 1.0 point on a difference over 93 entries (Appendix E). On entries
created in 2026, not used in developing the strategies, the ordering is the same, though stopped
replies were frequent (20 of 30 compared; five strategies run; Appendix I.2).
Measurement choices move differences of this size. Excluding entries whose writer was stopped
while scoring entries with a stopped per-paper call placed the evidence graph 2.1 points below direct
reading, a difference not distinguishable from zero under a symmetric rule. Splitting facts afresh for
each draft gives levels 2–5 points higher, with the same ordering; counting missing drafts as zero
gives intervals that include zero for all three other reading strategies (Appendix Tables 7 and 12).
Contamination check. The memory-only control conveys 5.7% of facts for the newest band and
7.0% on the 2026 entries (Figure 5), so memory alone contributes little there. Reading is not
shown free of memorisation: within abstract-only papers, direct-reading coverage (pooled) falls
from 49.8% in the second-oldest band to 37.9% in the newest (classic band 33.0%), consistent with
memory helping on older entries.
Add-ons. Second-hand evidence raises OMIM facts conveyed by +1.8 points on 96 entries, but also
unsupported claims before repair and contradictions with the reference (Appendix Table 26). The
learned curation guide did not improve its own training entries, and its held-out gain, +1.0 points
(+0.0 to +2.0), is not distinguishable from zero (Appendix Table 28). Reconciliation changed no
score by more than 0.1 point, and the case database was not validated against per-patient ground
truth (Appendix Table 27).
4.3
STRATEGIES COMPARED ON THE FACTS THE SUPPLIED TEXT STATES
On stated facts (Table 2), direct reading, the evidence graph and the agentic curator differ by un-
der a point, with intervals that include zero; on facts the text does not state, the reading strategies
convey no more than the memory-only control. This contrast, with the within-paper experiment,
is the evidence that access bounds agreement. Two caveats apply. The same model family judged
both what the text states and what the draft conveys, so this analysis supports the hidden-full-text
experiment and does not stand alone. And the restriction has a ceiling of its own: on facts stated in
abstract-only papers the reading strategies convey 95.5–97.1% and most entries tie. Case table first
is the exception: its deficit is larger on stated facts, most of all in full-text papers.

WHAT THE AUDIT CAN AND CANNOT SEE
What it removes. Unrepaired, the memory-only control has 22.1% of claims unsupported and 0.6%
contradicted; after repair the other strategies keep 94.2–96.5% judged supported (Table 1). A single
value altered by rule (count, zygosity, residue or mode of inheritance) in 60 paragraphs the audit had
passed was flagged in all 60, on a claim with the altered value; this is not a sensitivity estimate: it
shows that blunt single-value substitutions in paragraphs the audit had passed are caught. Re-audited
unaltered with fresh calls, the 60 drew no flag (0 of 361 claims), the rate at which a re-audit flags a
paragraph that had passed once, not a false-flag rate on arbitrary paragraphs.

5
DISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS
For this model and in the regime tested (2–12 references, 19% of papers readable in full, inputs
that fit one prompt), access to the source limits agreement with curator-written text far more than
how the strategy reads; unrelated padding did not change this, nor did released pipelines of two
of these designs do better at the settings tested. Measurement lessons: handle stopped replies
symmetrically; fix fact lists before scoring; compare strategies on the facts the supplied text states;
report run-to-run noise beside intervals over entries; match settings unrelated to the representation
before blaming it; keep per-paper audit verdicts, since a claim’s best verdict hides over-citation;
and test a released pipeline with its intended model, on which it depends in ways a model-agnostic
description hides (STORM could not run here).
Limitations. (i) One model family wrote, audited and judged everything, including what the sup-
plied text states; no human has read any draft, verdict or fact judgement; other model families are
untested. (ii) The reference is indirect and narrow (variant-centred paragraphs, two-source ontol-
ogy annotations, gene and inheritance tables; no narrative sections); other draft content is checked
only by the audit. (iii) Every strategy is given the curators’ reference list (retrieval was tested on
ten entries). (iv) Only open-access text without supplements was available; reading and recall of
facts the abstract states cannot be separated. (v) Variant names are not validated against sequences;
inheritance is compared as coarse classes. (vi) Strategies were developed on the main entries; the
2026 set has 20 compared entries, five strategies and frequent stopped replies. (vii) Stopped replies
remove entries unevenly. (viii) Entries with few references dominate; input size alone was tested
by padding with unrelated text, and large sets of relevant papers could not be compared because of
stopped replies. (ix) The audit does not catch over-citation, most broadened claims or omissions,
and its stress test is small. (x) Released systems were run at one setting each, PaperQA2 without its
agent and STORM not at all. (xi) Cost is at list price for one model.
6
CONCLUSION
For this model and in the regime tested, access to the source limits agreement with curator-written
text: hiding the full text of the same papers lowers coverage from 79.8% to 36.1%, and three read-
ing strategies convey 93.6–94.4% of stated facts. Our case-table-first implementation trails where
full text is readable; on the same papers and model, released PaperQA2 and GraphRAG pipelines
conveyed fewer facts at higher cost (10 and 4 entries), and STORM could not run with this model.

ETHICS STATEMENT
The benchmark is built from public resources (NCBI, ClinVar, HPO, MedGen, Europe PMC,
HGNC). Curator-written text is used for scoring and is not redistributed; released score files carry
fact identifiers and statuses, not wording, and rights holders’ terms of use for curated text apply to
anyone rebuilding the reference. No human subjects were involved, and no patient-level data beyond
what published papers report were used. Generated entries are drafts for expert review and are not
clinical guidance; they must be marked as machine generated and not as OMIM content. No curator
has checked any draft, and the disagreements between drafts and the reference reported here are
unconfirmed.
REPRODUCIBILITY STATEMENT
Section 3 specifies the entry sampling (fixed seed, five creation-era bands, eligibility rules), the
reference sources, the strategies, guards, audit, scoring and statistics; Appendix B lists the model
settings and Appendix Table 4 the entry sets of every experiment. One model (Claude Opus 5.5) was
used through a command-line interface in headless mode with schema-constrained output; it exposes
no temperature or seed, so exact regeneration is not guaranteed, and we instead report replicate and
judge variation (Appendix E). Replies are cached, so each call was made once. All experiments,
pilots and repeated scoring used 43,096 cached model calls, 314 million input and 75 million output
tokens, about $3,294 at list price. Appendix J.1 gives the released systems’ versions, entry points
and every setting changed from their defaults. The curator-written text is not redistributed, but every
part of the reference comes from public resources (ClinVar, HPO, MedGen), from which it can
be rebuilt under the rights holders’ terms of use; the released score files carry fact identifiers and
statuses, not wording. The harness and per-entry drafts, verdicts and scores are released with the

STRATEGIES AND SETTINGS
Table 5 describes each strategy.
Settings:
one model for everything, Claude Opus 5.5
(claude-opus-5-5), called through the Claude Code command-line interface in headless mode
with schema-constrained output; no temperature or seed is exposed. Reasoning effort is high for
writers (direct reading, the graph and table writers, the agent, the memory draft) and medium for
per-paper extraction in the evidence graph and case table first, for the audit and for all judging; the
matched ablation raises per-paper extraction to high. The agentic strategy has read and search tools
over a folder of the papers and nothing else. The agentic curator reports which papers it opened
(self-reported; tool calls are not logged): in the 98 entries it wrote it reported opening every paper,
and all 2,600 quotes it gave were found verbatim in the papers. The memory drafts that recall, then
verify starts from are the memory-only control’s drafts. The evidence graph discards any finding
whose quote is not in the paper (none was). Synopsis terms are assigned by a lexical search over the
ontology’s labels and synonyms that returns candidates, from which the model chooses. Secondary
measures: synopsis terms are compared with the HPO annotations exactly, hierarchically (both sets
expanded with all ancestors) and by Lin semantic similarity, an ontology-aware comparison in the
spirit of K¨ohler et al. (2009); Lin similarity uses information content computed from the ontology’s
disease annotations, averaged in each direction to give precision and recall. Allelic variants are
matched on normalised protein or nucleotide change, with a model judge for names that do not nor-
malise; precision counts a variant OMIM does not list as a miss whether or not the paper reports it.
No fact went unjudged in any run.

Paradigm
What it does

Read and write
All papers in one prompt; the model writes the entry. Baseline.

Read and write

Memory only
Control
The memory draft, with no papers. Audited, not repaired.

Add-on to direct reading
For a paper not readable in full, passages from later open-access papers that

Reconciled case

Judge variation: the same drafts and fact lists judged three times (top). Evidence graph minus direct
reading in the three judgings: +0.7/+0.5/+1.3 points; per-fact agreement between two judgings of
the same draft: 3,498 of 3,654 statuses (95.7%). Run-to-run variation: generation, audit and scoring
repeated three times on 30 entries (bottom). Evidence graph minus direct reading in replicates 1/2/3:
+0.7/+1.9/+0.5 points; within-entry SD of that paired difference 5.0 points, giving a replicate-noise
95% half-width of 1.0 point over 93 entries and 1.8 points over 30. One replicate is one sample; the
main comparison is a single run per entry. The half-width is a yardstick for how much a difference of
this kind moves when generation, audit and scoring are repeated; the bootstrap interval over entries
is the test of a difference. On these 30 entries the evidence graph is ahead of direct reading in all three
replicates and all three judgings, whereas on the 93 entries of the main comparison its difference is
−0.5 (−1.5 to +0.5). Replicate 1 reproduces the main run on these entries, so the difference in sign
comes from which entries are averaged, not from replicate noise; the replicates themselves range
from +0.5 to +1.9. Crediting a fact anywhere in the draft rather than only in text citing its paper
(same 30 entries): direct reading 46% against 47%, evidence graph 46% against 46%, case table

RECONCILED CASE DATABASE AND LEARNED CURATION GUIDE
Reconciliation. Over the 100 entries, reconciliation proposed 57 merges of patient or family records
and accepted 40 (17 rejected by the verifier) in 27 entries; it merged 11 variant names and found 57
conflicts between papers. Counted individuals went from 2,580 to 2,413 (net; counts rose in a few
entries where a re-report gave a larger family size) and families from 1,126 to 1,092. A planted test
on six entries merged 10 of 10 copied reports with their originals and merged no unrelated records;
this easy case shows only that a stated re-report is caught. The accepted merges were not checked
by a person. Cost: one or two extra calls per entry. None of the entry-level measures uses patient
counts, so no change in them is expected. Agreement of the table’s patient-count fractions with
those in the HPO annotations (about 560 terms) was 48% before and after, with a mean absolute
difference of 0.26; each reference fraction comes from one source while the table pools all papers.
Learned guide. Training (27 of 30 training entries had a direct-reading draft; scored without the
audit, with the earlier per-draft fact splitting): baseline OMIM facts conveyed 53.8% and synopsis
exact F1 36.6%. Round 1 changed facts by +0.14 points and synopsis F1 by +1.29; round 2 by
−1.41 and +3.57. The text rules of round 1 and the synopsis rules of round 2 were selected by a rule
fixed in advance. The final guide, run once more on the training entries: facts +0.06 points, synopsis
F1 +1.28 points. A first guide, learned before stopped replies were recorded, had treated three empty
drafts as omissions and was discarded. On the held-out entries, a draft makes about twice as many
statements about a paper as the OMIM paragraph has facts (direct reading 24.0 statements per paper,
with the guide 22.2, against 11.9 facts), and 24% (direct) and 25% (guide) of a draft’s statements
have a counterpart in that paragraph; the paragraph is variant-centred, so this is a lower bound on
agreement with the entry.

I.3
UPDATES, RETRIEVAL WITHOUT A REFERENCE LIST, AND DRAFT–REFERENCE
DISAGREEMENTS
Disagreements. Of 37 facts on which a direct-reading draft contradicted the reference, the same
model, re-examining them against the paper, judged the paper to support the draft in 22, the reference
in 1 and both in 1; 13 could not be decided from the readable text. These are unconfirmed leads: no
curator has checked them, many rest on abstracts only, and the judge had already passed the same
claims. The two analyses that follow use the evidence-graph strategy only. Updates. Ten entries
were rolled back to an earlier reference list. A literature search found all 28 references OMIM later
added; all passed screening and 21 (75%) became update proposals. The same run proposed 33
papers OMIM does not cite. Retrieval. Given a title and the causal gene, a search capped at 12
papers selected 56 of the 85 references OMIM cites for ten entries (66%); 47% of what it selected
is in OMIM’s list (entry mean).
I.4
ANALYSES THAT WERE CORRECTED
The first analysis excluded entries whose writer was stopped but scored entries in which a per-paper
call was stopped; under it the evidence graph appeared 2.1 points below direct reading on OMIM
facts conveyed, and with completion treated the same way for every strategy the difference is not
distinguishable from zero. A bug in variant naming wrote frameshifts as missense changes in the
evidence-graph and case-table-first drafts (39 and 44 variant headings in 19 entries); it was fixed and
the drafts regenerated before the results reported here.
J
SECOND ROUND: RELEASED SYSTEMS, INPUT SIZE AND AUDIT COVERAGE
These experiments were run on 20–21 November 2026 with the same model, reply cache and scoring
as the rest of the study; a reply stopped by the model’s safety filter was recorded and never retried
with a changed prompt. Before any run finished, their sizes were cut to about a third of the design
(for example 10 instead of 24 entries for PaperQA2 and STORM, and 30 instead of about 60 items
per planted class), and some ended below even the reduced size because entries cost more model
calls than projected. Every result in this section is exploratory and shows directions and large effects
only. No person checked any judgement.
J.1
RELEASED SYSTEMS
How the systems were run. Each system was installed from the package index at its latest released
version (Table 31) and run on exactly the entry’s supplied papers, at the access level each paper
has, through a local adapter in front of the one model; no system was told how it would be scored.
STORM was given the entry title as its topic. PaperQA2 and GraphRAG, both question-answering
systems, were asked six fixed section questions per entry (description; clinical features; inheri-
tance; molecular genetics and the variants each paper reported; genotype–phenotype relationships;
pathogenesis and functional evidence), and their answers were concatenated; the strategies write an
OMIM-style entry in one pass. GraphRAG’s answers include headings and framing lines as sepa-
rate blocks; all text counts for crediting anywhere in the draft and for the share without a mapped
citation. Citations were mapped back to the supplied papers without editing the text: PaperQA2
names papers; GraphRAG’s local search cites text units, entities and relationships (a block’s papers
are the union over every record it cites, counting only records the query showed the model); global
search cites community reports only, each mapped to the papers of the text units its community’s
entities came from, a looser mapping. Facts are credited to paragraphs citing the paper, as in the
main comparison, and, as a check that does not depend on the mapping, anywhere in the draft. The
two are separate judge passes, so the second can fall below the first; it did on 5 system entries, by
differences within the judge’s noise. The claim-level audit without repair was run on every para-
graph with mapped citations of the 4 entries both systems completed; the strategies’ figures there
are their stored audits before repair.

J.2
CONTROLLED PADDING OF THE INPUT
Design. The entries are the ablation entries that every ablation variant completed and that have at
least half their papers readable in full, taken in entry-number order; all four strategies had completed
them in the main run. The added papers are full-text papers already fetched for other entries, exclud-
ing the entry’s own papers, papers cited by any entry sharing a causal gene with it, papers whose
text names one of its genes, papers whose citing entry has no reference record (gene unknown), and
papers over 180,000 characters (the extraction cap). They are drawn in a seeded order per entry; the
additions of level P1 (about 500,000 characters) are a prefix of those of P2 (about 1,200,000), and
each level stops at the paper that brings the total nearest its target. The supplied order is shuffled with
the entry’s seed, but direct reading and the agent’s index sort papers by year and the extract-then-
write strategies read each paper separately, so order has little effect. The task prompt is unchanged.
Level P0 (no padding) uses the main run’s drafts before repair, rebuilt from the model cache without
new generation (replaying the cached audit and repair on them reproduces the stored final drafts
exactly); P1 and P2 are fresh generations. No level was audited or repaired. Coverage is that of the
entry’s own fixed facts, credited to paragraphs citing the paper (present plus half of partly present,
over judged facts). Completion follows the main rule, so a per-paper extraction failure makes a draft
incomplete; every such failure here was a safety-filter stop on an added paper, so the main analysis
allows failures on added papers, and under the symmetric rule 2 entries remain (Table 41). Run-
to-run variation of single-entry coverage is about 4 points (within-entry standard deviation in the
replicate runs), so changes of a few points over 6 entries are within noise.
What the strategies did with the added papers. No paragraph of any padded draft cited an added
paper (0 of 115–140 paragraphs per strategy and level), and none attributed a statement about the
disorder to one. The agentic curator opened every one of the entry’s own papers at every level, 5 of
63 added papers at P1 and none of 174 at P2. The extract-then-write strategies marked 59 papers not
relevant at P1 and 159 (evidence graph) and 163 (case table first) at P2. Direct reading’s prompt cut
each paper to 75,000–300,000 characters at P0 (one own paper cut) and to 33,333–66,666 at P1 (two
own papers, both in one entry, and 24 added papers cut); at P2 it was split into three prompts and a
merge, with no paper cut and none lost in the merge. Extraction calls were stopped for 1 (evidence
graph) and 3 (case table first) papers at P1 and for 3 and 5 at P2.
Deviations. The design planned 12 entries and about 1,800,000 characters at P2. Before any run
finished this was cut to 8 entries, about 1,200,000 characters and at most 800 new model calls; the
pilot (about 125 new calls per entry) projected about 1,000 calls for 8 entries, so the entries were
reduced in order to 6. The run made 683 new model calls. Wall-clock time per task includes waiting
for one of at most 4 concurrent model calls.

LARGE ENTRIES
Selection and deviations. Eligible phenotype entries (the main set’s rules) with 30–150 references
in no existing evaluation set were sampled with a fixed seed, and the 8 with the most references
readable in full (at least 5; ties by entry number) were selected; the design called for a sample of
400 and 16 entries, and the sample was up to 200. Only the first 4 in rule order were run: an entry
cost about 250–330 new model calls, most of them per-paper extraction for the evidence graph and
the case table, and the cap of 1,500 new calls allowed no more (1,338 were made). The selection
was resolved in three passes across machine restarts, and the first three entries were run once their
place among the 8 was certain. The comparison on facts the supplied text states was not made. Direct
reading holds 600,000 characters in one prompt, cutting each paper to the larger of 20,000 characters
and 600,000 divided by the number of papers; above that, papers are split into batches of that size
and the partial drafts merged, and only a paper longer than a whole batch is cut. Each per-paper
extraction reads at most 180,000 characters; no paper of these entries exceeded it. The new entries
were generated and scored without audit or repair, whereas the 12 earlier entries (Appendix I.1)
were scored after repair.
Outcome. No new entry was completed by every strategy, so the tier’s comparison rests on the 10
earlier entries, with the figures of Table 29. Table 43 gives the new entries; its coverage figures
describe drafts written without the papers whose extraction was stopped and are not a comparison.
On the one new entry where every strategy wrote a draft (152700), direct reading conveyed 52.3%,
the agentic curator 51.5%, case table first 49.6% (2 papers lost) and the evidence graph 39.8% (3
papers lost): one entry, not a result.

AUDIT COVERAGE: PLANTED ERRORS BEYOND SINGLE VALUES
Construction. Items were planted in the main run’s evidence-graph drafts, as in the single-value
test, in paragraphs the audit had passed as fully supported that cite one paper and are longer than
150 characters (a pool of 1,494), at most 3 items per entry per class, in one seeded order shared
by all classes (Table 44). Citation swaps and added citations change the in-text author–year label
as well as the cited paper; the new paper is a random readable paper of another entry (swap to
another entry, added citation) or another readable paper of the same entry (swap within the entry).
A moved sentence names a variant, carries no author citation and is inserted at a seeded sentence
boundary after the first sentence of the target paragraph, whose paper must not already name that
variant. Truth is decided mechanically from the text the auditor reads (title, abstract and full text
cut at 180,000 characters): a variant occurs in a paper if any of its protein or nucleotide forms, its
residue or nucleotide position, or its raw name does. A same-entry swap is truth-known when the
paragraph names at least one variant absent from the new paper, a moved sentence when none of
its variants occurs in the target paper. Qualifiers are deleted by five rules (count, hedge, negation,
single family or patient, zygosity). All 30 qualifier edits were read before the run: 5 of 8 hedge
edits change the claim, 2 are nearly meaning-preserving and 1 is degenerate; all 8 negation edits
reverse the claim (so they plant contradictions, not broadening); about 3 of 8 zygosity edits are
nearly meaning-preserving where de novo or dominant inheritance implies heterozygosity; 2 of 4
count edits are clean, 1 ungrammatical and 1 awkward; both single-family edits are clean. No
rule mostly produced ungrammatical or meaning-preserving edits, so all five were kept. The planted
claim is every claim for swaps to another entry and added citations, claims naming the absent variant
for same-entry swaps and moved sentences, and claims taken from the altered sentence for deleted
qualifiers; an item counts as flagged when any planted claim is unsupported or contradicted. The
unaltered paragraphs of all classes (113) were audited once, fresh, as the shared control. The audit
used its own prompts and best-verdict rule, on items in chunks of 5 paragraphs (the single-value test
audited its 60 together).
Completeness check. A separate check lists findings a cited paper states that the draft’s text about
that paper omits, each with a quote that is verified against the paper. It was run on 30 planted
deletions (a sentence other than the first, carrying a variant or a number, years and entry numbers
aside, that appears nowhere else in the draft’s text about that paper), on the same texts unaltered,
and on natural omissions: 60 curator facts (a seeded sample, facts of papers readable in full first,
of 130 eligible) that direct-reading drafts omitted although the text the writer had states them, on
28 papers. A listed omission matches a deleted sentence mechanically when it names one of the
sentence’s variants or carries all its numbers, and by judgement when the model judge says it does.

Citation precision. The main run’s entry files keep only each claim’s best verdict. Verdicts per cited
paper were recovered without new calls by replaying the stored audit from the model-reply cache,

only if the replayed best verdict of every claim equals the stored one.
Deviations and cost. 30 items per class instead of about 60, and at most 600 new model calls instead
of 1,200, pilot included; natural omissions capped at 60 facts; the audit run in chunks rather than
in one holder; one control audit for all classes. The qualifier rules were tightened once before the
pilot after reading the built edits, and after the main run the selector of planted claims for deleted
qualifiers was widened (a claim also counts when it contains two of the content words next to the
deletion point), which recovered two flagged negation items, with no new calls. The mechanical
matching of the completeness check was corrected after the pilot (number words and thousands
separators read as digits), with no new calls. The run made 540 new model calls (cap 600) at a list
price of $43.7849; 2 replies were stopped by the safety filter, both on the completeness check of one

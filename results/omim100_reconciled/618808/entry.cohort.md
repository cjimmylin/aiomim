### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that intellectual developmental disorder with poor growth and with or without seizures or ataxia (IDPOGSA) is caused by recessively inherited mutation in the ABCA2 gene (600047) on chromosome 9q34.3. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Aslam and Naz (2019) studied a consanguineous Pakistani family with a movement disorder affecting 2 sibs. Both affected individuals had symptoms consistent with cerebellar ataxia with dysarthria. Brain MRI showed no abnormalities. Both affected individuals had elevated low density lipoprotein levels in their blood. The authors described their report as an extension of the phenotypic spectrum associated with ABCA2.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern in the consanguineous Pakistani family reported by Aslam and Naz (2019) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In 2 sibs from a consanguineous Pakistani family with cerebellar ataxia and dysarthria, Aslam and Naz (2019) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a frameshift mutation in the ABCA2 gene (c.4993delG; Val1665TyrfsTer36; NM_212533.2). Sanger sequencing showed that the mutation segregated with the disorder in the family. It was absent from all publicly available databases and from ethnically matched controls.

### Pathogenesis

Aslam and Naz (2019) suggested that their findings further delineate the importance of low density lipid metabolism and intracellular sterol trafficking in brain function. They also suggested that ABCA2 variants have pleiotropic effects.

### Animal Model

Aslam and Naz (2019) noted that the phenotype in their patients with an ABCA2 frameshift mutation closely matched the phenotype seen in Abca2 knockout mice.

In 2 sibs from a consanguineous Pakistani family with cerebellar ataxia, dysarthria and elevated low density lipoprotein levels, Aslam and Naz (2019) identified a 1-bp deletion in the ABCA2 gene (c.4993delG, NM_212533.2). The deletion causes a frameshift and a premature termination codon (Val1665TyrfsTer36). Whole-exome sequencing identified the mutation, and Sanger sequencing showed that it segregated with the disorder in the family. It was absent from all publicly available databases and from ethnically matched controls. ⚠︎

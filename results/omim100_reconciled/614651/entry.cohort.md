### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that primary coenzyme Q10 deficiency-2 (COQ10D2) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the PDSS1 gene (607429) on chromosome 10p12.1.

### Description

Primary coenzyme Q10 deficiency-2 (COQ10D2) is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by a defect in coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10) biosynthesis. It reduces quinone-dependent oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) activities. Reported clinical features include sensorineural hearing loss, optic atrophy, intellectual disability, muscle weakness, and cardiac valve involvement. One reported patient had a severe infantile-onset progressive multisystem disorder that was fatal in early childhood. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Mollet et al. (2007) studied a family (family 1) with CoQ10 deficiency and an oxidative phosphorylation disorder. Enzymologic studies showed that quinone-dependent OXPHOS activities were in the range of the lowest control values, while OXPHOS enzyme activities were normal.

Nardecchia et al. (2021) reported 2 sisters born to consanguineous Egyptian parents. The older sister, a 16-year-old girl, was born after an uneventful pregnancy. At age 4 years she was found to have profound bilateral sensorineural hearing loss, which was treated with an externally powered prosthesis. She had severe language delay and mild intellectual disability. She also had progressive visual loss with red-green dyschromatopsia, optic atrophy, abnormal visual evoked potentials, and reduced thickness of the retinal nerve fiber layer. Other findings were diffuse mild muscle weakness and a mild postural tremor of the upper limbs. Her brain MRI was normal. Cardiac evaluation showed a dysplastic mitral valve with moderate insufficiency, tricuspid insufficiency, and a dilated left atrium. She did not have lactic acidosis, nephrotic syndrome, peripheral neuropathy, or macrocephaly. Her 6-year-old sister had bilateral sensorineural hearing loss. At age 6 years she developed decreased visual acuity, and ophthalmologic examination showed optic atrophy, with bilateral pallor of the temporal sector of the optic nerve head, and divergent strabismus. She also had mild intellectual disability and muscle weakness with exercise intolerance. She had no cardiac valvulopathy, lactic acidosis, or nephrotic syndrome. Nardecchia et al. (2021) noted that in PDSS1 deficiency, unlike OPA1 and OPA3 defects, sensorineural deafness seemed to precede optic atrophy. They proposed that PDSS1 deficiency, a potentially treatable condition, be included in the differential diagnosis of optic atrophy with sensorineural deafness. ⚠︎

Bellusci et al. (2021) reported a boy who presented at 5 months of age with failure to thrive, hypotonia, feeding difficulties, bilateral hearing impairment, pulmonary hypertension, livedo reticularis, and distal phalangeal erythema. Laboratory tests showed anemia, lactic acidosis, 3-methylglutaconic and tricarboxylic aciduria, and decreased CoQ10 levels in lymphocytes. He developed a progressive multisystem disorder that included severe developmental delay, pyramidal dysfunction, seizures, tremor, optic atrophy, nystagmus, severe visual and hearing impairment, cystic white matter changes, mild cerebral atrophy, and chronic heart failure. He died at 3 years of age.

### Biochemical Features

In family 1, Mollet et al. (2007) found that quinone-dependent OXPHOS activities were in the range of the lowest control values, while OXPHOS enzyme activities were normal. They confirmed the CoQ10 deficiency by showing that normal OXPHOS activities were restored when quinone was added.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of COQ10D2 is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Mollet et al. (2007) identified a homozygous PDSS1 mutation in family 1. In the consanguineous Egyptian family reported by Nardecchia et al. (2021), both affected sisters were homozygous for a PDSS1 mutation and their parents were heterozygous carriers. Bellusci et al. (2021) reported a patient with biallelic PDSS1 mutations. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Mollet et al. (2007) carried out a genome-wide search for homozygosity in family 1 and identified a region of chromosome 10 that contained the PDSS1 gene.

### Heterogeneity

Mollet et al. (2007) studied 2 families with CoQ10 deficiency. In the first family they found a homozygous mutation in PDSS1. In the second family, direct sequencing of the COQ2 gene (609825) identified a single-basepair frameshift deletion (c.1198delT, N401fsX415) predicted to cause a premature stop codon.

### Molecular Genetics

Mollet et al. (2007) studied family 1 (see Mapping). Sequencing of the PDSS1 gene in this family identified a homozygous substitution that changed a conserved amino acid (D308E). When a yeast Delta-coq1 strain was transformed with a mutant yeast COQ1 corresponding to the D308E mutation, the strain grew poorly on respiratory medium. This finding indicated that the mutation causes the OXPHOS deficiency.

Vasta et al. (2012) performed targeted next-generation sequencing of the exons of 908 known and candidate nuclear genes in 26 patients with known or suspected mitochondrial disorders. Among the variants they identified in known pathogenic genes were variants in PDSS1.

In 2 sisters from a consanguineous Egyptian family with COQ10D2, Nardecchia et al. (2021) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a homozygous c.735G-T transversion in the PDSS1 gene, resulting in a gln245-to-his (Q245H) substitution. The variant affected a highly conserved residue (98.5% conservation across 184 eukaryotic sequences). It was absent from gnomAD and lay within a 12-Mb region of homozygosity. Both parents were heterozygous for the mutation. Of the unaffected sibs, a sister was homozygous wildtype and a brother was heterozygous. ⚠︎

In a boy with severe infantile-onset COQ10D2, Bellusci et al. (2021) identified biallelic mutations in the PDSS1 gene: a c.716T-G transversion resulting in a val239-to-gly (V239G) substitution, and a c.1183C-T transition resulting in an arg395-to-ter (R395X) substitution.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Nardecchia et al. (2021) noted that visual involvement in CoQ10 deficiencies had been reported only with defects in the first steps of CoQ10 synthesis. These were optic atrophy in PDSS1 deficiency, optic atrophy with retinitis pigmentosa in PDSS2 deficiency, and retinitis pigmentosa alone in COQ2 deficiency.

### Pathogenesis

Bellusci et al. (2021) suggested that the combination of distal phalangeal erythema, livedo reticularis, and pulmonary hypertension in their patient with PDSS1 mutations pointed to microvascular dysfunction as a possible underlying mechanism.

### Clinical Management

In the patient reported by Bellusci et al. (2021), oral CoQ10 (ubidecarenone) supplementation, increased from 15 to 30 mg/kg/day, did not control disease progression. The patient died at 3 years of age.

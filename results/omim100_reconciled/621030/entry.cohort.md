### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal dominant autoinflammation, panniculitis, and dermatosis syndrome (AIPDSA) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the OTULIN gene (615712) on chromosome 5p15.2.

### Description

Autosomal dominant autoinflammation, panniculitis, and dermatosis syndrome (AIPDSA) is a severe autoinflammatory disorder with onset in the neonatal period or infancy. Its features include fever, raised C-reactive protein (CRP), leukocytosis with neutrophilia, and generalized edema. Patients also have inflammatory skin disease, including panniculitis, sterile pustular or necrotizing neutrophilic lesions, and pathergy. Takeda et al. (2024) noted that OTULIN-related autoinflammatory syndrome (ORAS), which had previously been attributed to homozygous or compound heterozygous OTULIN variants, is characterized by fever and autoinflammation, sterile neutrophilic dermatitis, and failure to thrive, and that anti-TNF therapy is a viable treatment. Davidson et al. (2024) and Takeda et al. (2024) showed that the same picture can result from a single heterozygous OTULIN variant that acts in a dominant-negative manner. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Davidson et al. (2024) reported a 22-month-old boy (P1) born to nonconsanguineous Caucasian parents. He was born prematurely at 31 weeks, and the authors attributed the prematurity to another cause. Disease began at 4 days of age with peri-umbilical erythema and a presumed abscess. He later had a nonhealing abdominal wound after surgical dehiscence, acute respiratory deterioration with presumed pulmonary edema, extensive body edema, and hypotension. He also developed a spontaneous cutaneous duodenal stoma, and an echogenic focus on the tricuspid valve was treated as infective endocarditis. He had panniculitis and pathergy, with sterile pustular abscesses at sites of trauma. A skin biopsy showed acute inflammatory exudate, suppuration, and necrosis. Investigations showed raised CRP (100-200 mg/L), neutrophilia (30-50 x 10(9)/L), monocytosis (2-10 x 10(9)/L), eosinophilia (1-3 x 10(9)/L), and persistent thrombocytopenia (below 100 x 10(9)/L). He also had hypogammaglobulinemia, which was possibly related to prematurity. Lymphocyte subsets were normal. Transcription of IFNB1 and IL6 was elevated in his peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs). ⚠︎

Davidson et al. (2024) also reported a girl (P2) born at full term to nonconsanguineous Saudi parents after an insignificant antenatal history. She was asymptomatic until 7 months of age, when she presented with multiple tender skin lesions over the upper and lower limbs bilaterally, fever (38-39 degrees C), and generalized edema. She had leukocytosis, neutrophilia, and high CRP. Some of her lesions progressed to tender cellulitis-like plaques that evolved into necrotizing fasciitis with ulceration. Skin biopsy showed panniculitis, with infiltration by neutrophils and plasma cells and necrosis. She also had recurrent chest infections. She was 3 years old at last report. ⚠︎

Takeda et al. (2024) reported a 9-year-old Japanese girl, born to nonconsanguineous parents, who had neonatal-onset severe autoinflammation, neutrophilic dermatitis, respiratory distress, and growth failure. She was born at 33 weeks 1 day of gestation and weighed 1,436 g. Shortly after birth she developed meningitis with polynucleocytosis in the cerebrospinal fluid and negative cultures. She also developed antibiotic-refractory omphalitis that extended over the abdominal wall, and methicillin-susceptible Staphylococcus aureus was cultured from it. She had persistent fever with leukocytosis (predominantly neutrophils), elevated serum CRP, sterile pustulosis, erythema nodosum, and impaired physical and psychological development. Skin histology showed massive neutrophil infiltration and inflammatory granuloma, and the dermis was infiltrated by neutrophils and mononuclear cells that produced TNF. TUNEL and cleaved caspase-3 staining showed increased cell death in lesional tissue. She had 3 episodes of acute respiratory distress syndrome with pneumonia, during which she had thrombocytopenia. During febrile episodes she had thrombocytosis of up to 1 x 10(9)/microliter. Serum IL1-beta and IL6 were elevated. Antinuclear antibodies and rheumatoid factor were absent.

### Biochemical Features

In the patient reported by Takeda et al. (2024), linear ubiquitin was increased in PBMCs and EBV-transformed B cells, although the amount of OTULIN protein was normal. IL1-beta-stimulated PBMCs and CD14-positive macrophages from the patient produced more IL6 than controls.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous OTULIN mutations in the patients reported by Davidson et al. (2024) and Takeda et al. (2024) occurred de novo, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. Davidson et al. (2024) described this as the first ORAS-like disease caused by a heterozygous, dominant-negative OTULIN variant, in contrast to the biallelic loss-of-function variants reported previously. Takeda et al. (2024) noted that OTULIN-related autoinflammatory syndrome had previously been known to follow autosomal recessive inheritance, with homozygous or compound heterozygous variants. They concluded that it can also arise in an autosomal dominant fashion from the dominant-negative R306Q variant acting alone.

### Molecular Genetics

Davidson et al. (2024) performed trio exome sequencing in P1 and his parents. They identified a de novo heterozygous c.386G-C transversion in the OTULIN gene, resulting in a cys129-to-ser (C129S) substitution. Whole-genome sequencing in P2 identified a different nucleotide change, c.385T-A, that caused the same C129S substitution, and parental testing confirmed that it had arisen de novo. The C129S variant was absent from gnomAD (v2.1.1 and v3.1.2). Cys129 lies in the catalytic domain and is essential for enzymatic activity. C129S OTULIN was as stable as wildtype protein but did not hydrolyze M1-linked diubiquitin. Patient fibroblasts expressed active wildtype and inactive C129S OTULIN in seemingly equal amounts and accumulated linear ubiquitin chains. Transfection of C129S OTULIN into HEK293T cells, which express endogenous OTULIN, caused M1-ubiquitin chains to accumulate and activated NF-kappa-B in a dose-dependent manner. The loss-of-function variants Y244C and N341D did not have these effects. The authors concluded that C129S exerts a dominant-negative effect over wildtype OTULIN. ⚠︎

Takeda et al. (2024) performed whole-exome sequencing in a Japanese girl with ORAS-like symptoms and her parents. They identified 2 rare heterozygous OTULIN variants on different alleles (NM_138348.6). The first, c.455C-T, resulting in a pro152-to-leu (P152L) substitution, was inherited from her healthy mother and was also present in a healthy sister. It was listed in gnomAD (51 of 1,611,754 alleles) and classified in ClinVar as of uncertain significance. The second, a de novo c.917G-A transition resulting in an arg306-to-gln (R306Q) substitution, was extremely rare. It appeared only once in gnomAD, in an adult aged 50 to 55 years. Because a somatic heterozygous R306Q variant had been identified by whole-exome sequencing in a cancer specimen, the authors suspected that the gnomAD occurrence was also somatic. They concluded that a germline R306Q variant was unlikely to have been reported before. Recombinant R306Q OTULIN had markedly impaired deubiquitinating activity against linear diubiquitin (kcat/KM 8.92 +/- 1.61 x 10(3) M-1 sec-1, compared with 300.82 +/- 97.49 for wildtype). P152L protein digested linear diubiquitin as effectively as wildtype (294.79 +/- 74.01). The authors considered R306Q to be the causal variant and showed that it acts in a dominant-negative manner (see PATHOGENESIS).

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Takeda et al. (2024) compared the residual enzymatic activity of OTULIN mutants. R306Q was less active than Y244C but more active than L272P and G281R. They concluded that the severity of ORAS symptoms appears to correlate with residual OTULIN function rather than with the mode of inheritance.

### Pathogenesis

Davidson et al. (2024) found that fibroblasts from patients heterozygous for C129S showed reduced recruitment of LUBAC to the TNFR1 signaling complex and reduced modification of RIPK1 there. The fibroblasts were as sensitive to TNF-induced cell death, mainly apoptosis, as fibroblasts from patients with biallelic ORAS, and more sensitive than OTULIN-haploinsufficient fibroblasts. TNF-induced NF-kappa-B and MAPK signaling was largely unchanged. In patient fibroblasts, catalytically inactive OTULIN bound more strongly to LUBAC and promoted LUBAC autoubiquitination. The authors proposed that this autoubiquitination reduces LUBAC recruitment to the TNFR1 complex and promotes TNF-induced cell death and tissue necrosis. In a cell-free assay, C129S did not directly protect diubiquitin from cleavage by wildtype OTULIN. PBMCs from P1 showed increased IFNB1 and IL6 transcription. Patient fibroblasts had an elevated interferon-stimulated gene score, similar to biallelic ORAS cell lines. THP-1 cells stably expressing C129S OTULIN showed a similar inflammatory gene signature. ⚠︎

Takeda et al. (2024) used structural analysis and surface plasmon resonance to show that arg306 links the distal ubiquitin-binding site of OTULIN to its catalytic core. Catalytically inactive OTULIN carrying R306Q failed to bind linear diubiquitin, to a similar extent as the known ORAS mutant L272P, whereas P152L caused only minor structural changes. The authors reconstituted OTULIN-knockout HeLa cells with OTULIN constructs. R306Q OTULIN was expressed at wildtype levels and bound LUBAC normally. However, it increased TNF-induced auto-linear ubiquitination of HOIL-1L and enhanced cell death induced by TNF plus cycloheximide, measured by caspase-8 and caspase-3 cleavage, LDH release, and annexin-V positivity. P152L OTULIN behaved like wildtype, and neither variant clearly affected IkappaB-alpha phosphorylation or degradation. In NF-kappa-B reporter assays and in OTULIN-null HEK293 cells, R306Q counteracted the suppression of LUBAC-mediated NF-kappa-B activation by wildtype OTULIN in a dose-dependent manner. It also reversed the reduction of linear chains and the rescue from TNF-induced death conferred by wildtype or P152L OTULIN, demonstrating a dominant-negative function. ⚠︎

Takeda et al. (2024) differentiated patient-derived iPS cells into mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) and used base editing to correct one variant at a time to wildtype. Cells retaining R306Q with a wildtype allele (Edit-L152) accumulated linear ubiquitin on TNF treatment. These cells were as sensitive to TNF-mediated death as the original patient cells and more sensitive than control cells or Edit-Q306 (wildtype/P152L) cells. Fibroblasts from patients with autosomal recessive ORAS have reduced NF-kappa-B activation, but the patient's MSCs showed normal TNF-mediated NF-kappa-B activation and normal amounts of LUBAC subunits in unstimulated cells. The authors suggested that reduced NF-kappa-B activation is not a common feature of ORAS. They speculated that enhanced cell death caused by reduced OTULIN activity plays a crucial role in its pathogenesis.

### Clinical Management

Davidson et al. (2024) reported that both of their patients responded to corticosteroids and that TNF blockade allowed steroids to be reduced or stopped. Prophylactic antibiotics appeared to help wound healing and reduce infections. P2 remained in remission on infliximab with methotrexate. The JAK inhibitor ruxolitinib was tried in P1, but its benefit was unclear.

Takeda et al. (2024) reported that their patient's inflammatory episodes responded to high-dose dexamethasone, prednisolone, or methylprednisolone pulse therapy but relapsed when steroids were tapered. Etanercept twice weekly, started at 2 years of age, completely controlled the inflammation and allowed steroid-free remission, with catch-up of physical and psychological development.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 AUTOINFLAMMATION, PANNICULITIS, AND DERMATOSIS SYNDROME, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
OTULIN, CYS129SER [c.385T>A; c.386G>C; p.C129S]

Davidson et al. (2024) identified a de novo heterozygous cys129-to-ser (C129S) mutation in the OTULIN gene in 2 unrelated patients with AIPDSA. One was a Caucasian boy (P1) whose disease began at 4 days of age, and the other a Saudi girl (P2) whose disease began at 7 months. The mutation resulted from a c.386G-C transversion in P1, found by trio exome sequencing, and from a c.385T-A transversion in P2, found by whole-genome sequencing. In both patients, parental testing showed that the mutation was absent from the parents. The variant was absent from gnomAD, and cys129 in the catalytic domain is essential for enzymatic activity. C129S OTULIN was stable but could not hydrolyze M1-linked diubiquitin, and patient fibroblasts accumulated linear ubiquitin chains. When expressed in HEK293T cells alongside endogenous OTULIN, C129S caused M1-ubiquitin chains to accumulate and activated NF-kappa-B in a dose-dependent manner, consistent with a dominant-negative effect. ⚠︎

**.0002 AUTOINFLAMMATION, PANNICULITIS, AND DERMATOSIS SYNDROME, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
OTULIN, ARG306GLN [c.917G>A; p.R306Q]

Takeda et al. (2024) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a de novo heterozygous c.917G-A transition in the OTULIN gene, resulting in an arg306-to-gln (R306Q) substitution, in a 9-year-old Japanese girl with neonatal-onset AIPDSA. On the other allele, she also carried a P152L variant inherited from her healthy mother and present in a healthy sister. The authors considered P152L not to be causal because recombinant P152L protein had wildtype deubiquitinating activity. R306Q appeared only once in gnomAD, possibly as a somatic event. Recombinant R306Q OTULIN had markedly reduced activity against linear diubiquitin. Arg306 links the distal ubiquitin-binding site of OTULIN to its catalytic core, and the R306Q mutant failed to bind linear diubiquitin. R306Q counteracted the effects of wildtype OTULIN on LUBAC-mediated NF-kappa-B activation, on linear ubiquitin chains, and on protection from TNF-induced death, demonstrating a dominant-negative effect. Patient-derived mesenchymal stem cells that had been base-edited to carry R306Q with a wildtype allele accumulated linear ubiquitin on TNF treatment and were as sensitive to TNF-induced death as the original patient cells.

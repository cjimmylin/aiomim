### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome (ABERS) is caused by mutation in the TBX22 gene (300307) on chromosome Xq21.1.

### Description

Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome is a syndrome characterized by cleft palate, coloboma, hypospadias, deafness, short stature, and radial synostosis (Abruzzo and Erickson, 1977). The deafness is sensorineural (Pauws et al., 2013). Although the disorder has been described as CHARGE-like, Abruzzo and Erickson (1989) concluded that their patients did not have familial CHARGE syndrome.

### Clinical Features

Abruzzo and Erickson (1977) described a family with a new syndrome characterized by cleft palate, coloboma, hypospadias, deafness, short stature, and radial synostosis. ⚠︎

Abruzzo and Erickson (1989) did a follow-up study of the family they had reported in 1977. This family had been cited as an example of familial CHARGE syndrome, but after re-evaluation the authors concluded that the patients did not have familial CHARGE syndrome. They referred to the disorder as a new X-linked syndrome. ⚠︎

Pauws et al. (2013) studied the family with X-linked, CHARGE-like Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome and noted that the phenotype, compared with classic X-linked cleft palate, extended to additional features including sensorineural deafness and coloboma.

### Inheritance

Abruzzo and Erickson (1977) concluded that the family history suggested either autosomal dominant inheritance with limited expression in females or X-linked inheritance. Abruzzo and Erickson (1989) later referred to the disorder as X-linked. Pauws et al. (2013) identified a mutation in the X-linked TBX22 gene that tracked with the phenotype in the family, consistent with X-linked inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Hypothesizing a possible involvement of TBX22 in the X-linked, CHARGE-like Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome family of unknown etiology originally reported by Abruzzo and Erickson (1977), Pauws et al. (2013) identified a novel splice acceptor mutation in the TBX22 gene (c.593-5T-A) that tracked with the phenotype in the family. In patients with classic X-linked cleft palate phenotypes, they found a novel TBX22 splice donor variant (c.767+5G-A) and a known canonical splice donor mutation (c.767+1G-A), affecting the same exon as the Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome mutation. In silico and in vitro splicing studies predicted that all 3 variants abolished normal mRNA splicing, and an in vitro assay indicated that use of alternative splice sites was a likely outcome. Pauws et al. (2013) concluded that TBX22 is the gene underlying Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome, expanding the phenotypic spectrum of TBX22 mutations. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Pauws et al. (2013) noted that the developmental expression pattern of Tbx22 suggests the additional features of sensorineural deafness and coloboma seen in Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome, although these features had not previously been associated with X-linked cleft palate.

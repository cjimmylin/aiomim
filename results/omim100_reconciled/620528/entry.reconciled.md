### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal dominant distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-11 (HMND11) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the SPTAN1 gene (182810) on chromosome 9q34.

### Description

Distal hereditary motor neuropathies are a rare subgroup of inherited peripheral neuropathies. They are marked by length-dependent axonal degeneration of lower motor neurons without significant sensory involvement (Beijer et al., 2019). HMND11 is a form with juvenile onset, and disease severity varies greatly between patients.

### Clinical Features

Beijer et al. (2019) reported patients from 3 unrelated families with autosomal dominant distal hereditary motor neuropathy that began in juvenile life. Penetrance was variable in 2 of the 3 families, and phenotype severity differed greatly between patients.

Dong et al. (2021) reported a Chinese family with hereditary motor neuropathy.

### Inheritance

Beijer et al. (2019) found that HMND11 segregated as an autosomal dominant trait in 3 families, with variable penetrance in 2 of them. Dong et al. (2021) reported a de novo SPTAN1 nonsense variant in a Chinese family.

### Molecular Genetics

Using next-generation sequencing, Beijer et al. (2019) identified heterozygous nonsense mutations in the SPTAN1 gene in affected members of 3 unrelated families with autosomal dominant juvenile-onset distal hereditary motor neuropathy. Penetrance of the mutations was variable in 2 of the 3 families. Mutant SPTAN1 mRNA was degraded by nonsense-mediated decay, which reduced alpha-II spectrin protein levels in patient cells.

Dong et al. (2021) identified a novel de novo nonsense variant in the SPTAN1 gene in a Chinese family with hereditary motor neuropathy.

Ylikallio et al. (2020) reported a de novo SPTAN1 mutation in a phenotype of axonal sensorimotor neuropathy and developmental disorder. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Beijer et al. (2019) found that mutant SPTAN1 mRNA carrying the nonsense mutations was degraded by nonsense-mediated decay, which reduced alpha-II spectrin protein levels in patient cells. This finding pointed to haploinsufficiency as the disease mechanism, in contrast to the dominant-negative SPTAN1 mutations previously reported in epilepsy phenotypes. ⚠︎

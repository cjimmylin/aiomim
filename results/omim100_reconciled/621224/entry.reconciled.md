### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that ectodermal dysplasia-17 with or without limb malformations (ECTD17) is caused by heterozygous or homozygous mutation in the LEF1 gene (153245) on chromosome 4q25. ⚠︎

### Description

Ectodermal dysplasia-17 with or without limb malformations (ECTD17) is a disorder of ectodermal development. Its features include severe oligodontia, hypotrichosis, and hypohidrosis (Levy et al., 2020). Some patients also have limb malformations such as radial ray defects, polydactyly, or split hand/foot (Dufour et al., 2022). Severity ranges from mild to moderate with monoallelic LEF1 variants to severe with biallelic variants (Dufour et al., 2022).

### Clinical Features

Levy et al. (2020) reported 2 unrelated patients with severe oligodontia of the primary and permanent dentition, hypotrichosis, and hypohidrosis, which together were compatible with hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia. Both patients also had taurodontism and a particular alveolar bone defect.

Dufour et al. (2022) described 11 affected individuals from 4 unrelated families with monoallelic LEF1 variants and 1 affected individual from a consanguineous family with a biallelic LEF1 variant. The phenotypic spectrum included ectodermal dysplasia and various limb malformations, such as radial ray defects, polydactyly, or split hand/foot. The authors found that the biallelic variant was associated with a severe phenotype.

Hassan et al. (2024) reported a family with hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia and described it as expanding the spectrum of LEF1-related disorders.

### Inheritance

The 4q25 deletions that encompassed LEF1 in the 2 patients reported by Levy et al. (2020) occurred de novo, which fits autosomal dominant inheritance. Dufour et al. (2022) found monoallelic LEF1 variants in 11 affected individuals from 4 families and a biallelic variant in an affected individual from a consanguineous family. They stated that inheritance can be either autosomal dominant or autosomal recessive, depending on the type and location of the LEF1 variant. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In 2 unrelated patients with hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia, Levy et al. (2020) identified de novo heterozygous deletions at 4q25 that encompassed the LEF1 gene. The authors said their findings gave further evidence that LEF1 haploinsufficiency plays a role in ectodermal dysplasia. They noted that no pathogenic variants involving LEF1 had previously been reported in humans.

Dufour et al. (2022) detected monoallelic (heterozygous) variants in the LEF1 gene in 11 affected individuals from 4 unrelated families with ectodermal dysplasia and/or limb malformations. In an affected individual from a consanguineous family, they detected a biallelic (homozygous) LEF1 variant. The authors used immunofluorescence, immunoblotting, pulldown assays, and RNA sequencing to study the variants. Transcriptomic studies showed altered WNT signaling associated with the LEF1 variants.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Dufour et al. (2022) found that LEF1 haploinsufficiency or loss of LEF1 DNA binding was associated with a mild to moderate phenotype. In contrast, loss of beta-catenin binding caused by biallelic variants was associated with a severe phenotype.

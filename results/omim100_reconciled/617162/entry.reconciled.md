### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-46 (DEE46) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GRIN2D gene (602717) on chromosome 19q13.33.

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-46 (DEE46) is a neurologic disorder with epileptic encephalopathy, intractable seizures that do not respond to conventional antiepileptic medications, and severe developmental delay (Li et al., 2016; Tsuchida et al., 2018).

### Clinical Features

Li et al. (2016) reported 2 unrelated children with epileptic encephalopathy. Conventional antiepileptic medications had failed to control the seizures in both children. The older proband later developed refractory status epilepticus.

Tsuchida et al. (2018) reported 3 unrelated patients with severe developmental delay and intractable epilepsy. They compared their patients with the 2 patients reported by Li et al. (2016) and found that all 5 had similar clinical features.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous GRIN2D mutation identified in 2 unrelated children with DEE46 by Li et al. (2016) occurred de novo in both, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Li et al. (2016) used exome and candidate panel sequencing in 2 unrelated children with epileptic encephalopathy. Both children carried the same de novo heterozygous missense mutation in the GRIN2D gene, c.1999G-A, which causes a val667-to-ile (V667I) substitution. The substituted residue lies in the M3 transmembrane domain, which is involved in channel gating. Functional studies showed that the mutation caused a gain of function. It doubled glutamate and glycine potency and raised channel open probability 6-fold. It also reduced the receptor's sensitivity to negative modulators such as extracellular protons and prolonged deactivation after glutamate was removed.

Tsuchida et al. (2018) used whole-exome sequencing in 3 unrelated patients with severe developmental delay and intractable epilepsy. Each patient carried a novel heterozygous missense variant in the GRIN2D gene. The affected residues were highly conserved across vertebrates and among the 4 GluN2 subunits. From the protein structure, the authors predicted that all 3 variants probably impair GluN2D function, either by affecting how the subunits interact or by altering channel gating.

### Pathogenesis

Li et al. (2016) transfected cultured neurons with human GRIN2D cDNA carrying the c.1999G-A mutation, which caused dendritic swelling and neuronal cell death. The authors took this as suggestive of excitotoxicity from NMDA receptor overactivation.

### Clinical Management

Li et al. (2016) tested the mutant receptors in vitro for sensitivity to FDA-approved NMDA receptor antagonists. They then gave oral memantine to both of their patients, and seizure burden and development improved mildly to moderately. The older proband later developed refractory status epilepticus, which improved dramatically on ketamine and magnesium. The authors suggested that NMDA receptor antagonists could be useful as adjuvant therapy in patients with GRIN2D gain-of-function mutations.

Tsuchida et al. (2018) suggested that a genetic diagnosis of GluN2-related disorders may be clinically useful when considering drug therapy that targets NMDA receptors.

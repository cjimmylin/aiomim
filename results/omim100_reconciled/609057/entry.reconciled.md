### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that epidermolysis bullosa simplex-7 with nephropathy and deafness (EBS7) is caused by homozygous mutation in the CD151 gene (602243) on chromosome 11p15. ⚠︎

### Description

Epidermolysis bullosa simplex-7 with nephropathy and deafness (EBS7) is an autosomal recessive disorder characterized by hereditary nephritis progressing to end-stage renal disease, pretibial epidermolysis bullosa, and sensorineural deafness. Affected individuals lack the MER2 blood group antigen on their red cells. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Kagan et al. (1988) reported 2 sibs with end-stage renal disease who had hereditary nephritis, pretibial epidermolysis bullosa, and beta-thalassemia minor.

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) studied 3 MER2-negative patients of Indian Jewish origin with end-stage kidney disease, 2 of whom were sibs. Along with hereditary nephritis, the sibs had sensorineural deafness, pretibial epidermolysis bullosa, and beta-thalassemia minor. The only features given for the third patient were end-stage kidney disease and the MER2-negative blood group. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of EBS7 is autosomal recessive. Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) found homozygous CD151 mutations in 3 patients, including 2 affected sibs. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) showed that CD151 carries the MER2 blood group antigen and is present on erythrocytes. It was the first member of the tetraspanin superfamily found on red cells. They examined the CD151 gene in 3 MER2-negative patients of Indian Jewish origin who had end-stage kidney disease, 2 of whom were sibs. All 3 patients were homozygous for a 1-nucleotide insertion (G383) in exon 5 of CD151. The insertion caused a frameshift and a premature stop at codon 140, and was predicted to produce a truncated protein lacking the integrin-binding domain. The authors concluded that CD151 is essential for proper assembly of the glomerular and tubular basement membranes in the kidney and is functionally significant in skin. They also suggested that CD151 is probably a component of the inner ear and could play a role in erythropoiesis.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the DNAAF3 gene (614566) on chromosome 19q13.42. ⚠︎

### Description

Primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD) is an autosomal recessive condition with chronic sinusitis, bronchiectasis, and subfertility. About 50% of cases have situs inversus (Kartagener syndrome). The phenotype results from defective ciliary function and is associated with ultrastructural abnormalities that include absent dynein arms, absent radial spokes, and disturbed ciliary orientation (Meeks et al., 2000). In CILD2, the respiratory cilia are immotile and have reduced or absent inner and outer dynein arms (Mitchison et al., 2012).

### Clinical Features

Meeks et al. (2000) studied 5 Arabic families with primary ciliary dyskinesia.

Mitchison et al. (2012) reported 10 affected individuals with DNAAF3 mutations. They had classic PCD symptoms, and 4 of the 10 had situs inversus. Their respiratory cilia were immotile, with reduced or absent inner and outer dynein arms. The patients came from 3 consanguineous families. In family UCL89, of Arabic origin, patients had neonatal respiratory distress, recurrent chest infections, sinusitis, otitis media, and bronchiectasis. Transmission electron microscopy showed loss of both outer and inner dynein arms in the affected individuals, and their respiratory cilia were immotile. Affected members of a second Arabic family, UCL66/67, had recurrent chest infections, bronchiectasis, immotile respiratory cilia, and absent outer dynein arms. The parents of all the affected individuals were first cousins, except for UCL66/67 individuals III.1 and III.2. Affected members of family UCL71, a UK-Pakistani family from a first-cousin union, had recurrent chest infections, bronchiectasis, and immotile respiratory cilia. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Meeks et al. (2000) described primary ciliary dyskinesia as an autosomal recessive condition. In the families reported by Mitchison et al. (2012), all affected individuals were homozygous for DNAAF3 mutations and their unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous, which is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Mapping

Meeks et al. (2000) performed a genome scan in 5 Arabic families with primary ciliary dyskinesia. Using GENEHUNTER, they obtained a maximum multipoint lod score (HLOD) of 4.4 on chromosome 19q13.3-qter, at a proportion of linked families (alpha) of 0.7. Recombinations at D19S572 and D19S218 defined a 15-cM critical region.

Mitchison et al. (2012) noted that the DNAAF3 gene (C19orf51) on chromosome 19q13 lies within the CILD2 locus. This locus had been mapped in consanguineous PCD families of Arabic origin with absent outer dynein arms. Two of the 3 originally linked families showed large regions of marker homozygosity across the locus.

### Heterogeneity

Meeks et al. (2000) concluded that their data provided significant evidence for a PCD locus on chromosome 19q and confirmed that PCD shows locus heterogeneity.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of 2 of the 3 consanguineous Arabic families originally linked to the CILD2 locus on chromosome 19q, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified homozygous mutations in the DNAAF3 gene. Family UCL89 carried a missense mutation in exon 3, L108P. Family UCL66/67 carried a nonsense mutation in exon 4, R136X. Mitchison et al. (2012) then sequenced DNAAF3 in 112 additional PCD families, 107 of which had outer dynein arm defects. Only one further family, UCL71 (UK-Pakistani), had a homozygous pathogenic mutation: a 1-bp insertion in exon 6 that is predicted to cause a frameshift (V255CfsX12). This variant was absent from 172 control UK-Pakistani chromosomes. For all 3 mutations, every affected individual was homozygous and the unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous. None of the mutations were present in dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, or NHLBI-ESP. A homozygous A325T missense variant found in a Belgian patient (OP-549) affected a residue that is not conserved, and the authors considered it likely to be a Caucasian polymorphism.

### Pathogenesis

Mitchison et al. (2012) used immunofluorescence to show that the outer arm proteins DNAH5, DNAH9, and DNAI2 and the inner arm light chain DNALI1 were absent from the respiratory cilia of patients with DNAAF3 mutations. In some patients, residual DNAH5 and DNALI1 remained in the apical cytoplasm. These findings suggested that DNAAF3 is needed for cytoplasmic preassembly of both outer and inner dynein arms. The Chlamydomonas ortholog, PF22, was found only in the cytoplasm. The pf22 null mutant (Trp79X) had paralyzed, half-length flagella that lacked outer dynein arms and inner dynein arms b and c. Biochemical studies suggested that PF22 acts at a later step of dynein preassembly than PF13 and ODA7, for example in the association of heavy chains with smaller subunits. ⚠︎

### Population Genetics

Meeks et al. (2000) stated that the estimated incidence of primary ciliary dyskinesia is 1 in 20,000 live births.

### Animal Model

Mitchison et al. (2012) found that morpholino knockdown of dnaaf3 in zebrafish caused immotile olfactory placode cilia with reduced or missing dynein arms. Morphant embryos also showed body curvature, hydrocephalus, pronephric cysts, otolith defects, and laterality defects. About half of dnaaf3MOex8 embryos had reversed heart looping or no heart looping.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, LEU108PRO [c.323T>C; p.L108P]

In affected members of a consanguineous Arabic family (UCL89) with primary ciliary dyskinesia-2, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous c.323T-C transition in exon 3 of the DNAAF3 gene, resulting in a leu108-to-pro (L108P) substitution. All affected individuals were homozygous, and the unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous. The mutation was not present in dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, or NHLBI-ESP. The patients had neonatal respiratory distress, recurrent chest infections, sinusitis, otitis media, bronchiectasis, and immotile respiratory cilia that lacked both outer and inner dynein arms. ⚠︎

**.0002 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, ARG136TER [c.406C>T; p.R136*]

In affected members of an Arabic family (UCL66/67) with primary ciliary dyskinesia-2, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous c.406C-T transition in exon 4 of the DNAAF3 gene, resulting in an arg136-to-ter (R136X) substitution. All affected individuals were homozygous, and the unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous. The mutation was not present in dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, or NHLBI-ESP. The patients had recurrent chest infections, bronchiectasis, and immotile respiratory cilia with absent outer dynein arms. ⚠︎

**.0003 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, 1-BP INS, 762T [c.762_763inst; p.V255C]

In affected members of a UK-Pakistani family from a first-cousin union (UCL71) with primary ciliary dyskinesia-2, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous 1-bp insertion (c.762_763insT) in exon 6 of the DNAAF3 gene. The insertion is predicted to cause a frameshift that introduces a val255-to-cys change followed by 11 novel amino acids and a premature stop codon (V255CfsX12). This family showed evidence of linkage to the locus. All affected individuals were homozygous, and the unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous. The variant was absent from 172 control UK-Pakistani chromosomes. The patients had recurrent chest infections, bronchiectasis, and immotile respiratory cilia. ⚠︎

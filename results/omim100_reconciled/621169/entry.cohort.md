### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that monilethrix-2 (MNLIX2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the type II hair cortex keratin gene hHb1 (HHB1). ⚠︎

### Description

Monilethrix-2 is an inherited hair disorder characterized by beaded (moniliform) hair. Its severity can vary widely within one family, from dystrophic alopecia with follicular keratosis to moniliform hair that can be seen only by electron microscopy (Winter et al., 1998).

### Clinical Features

Winter et al. (1997) studied a monilethrix family in which affected individuals had beaded (moniliform) hair. ⚠︎

Winter et al. (1998) reported a French monilethrix family with a particularly variable phenotype. The pedigree included 2 infants. One had pronounced dystrophic alopecia, follicular keratosis and clear-cut moniliform hair. The other had no hair loss at all, and the moniliform hair could be detected only by electron microscopy. An adult family member had no clinical or electron microscopic signs of the disorder but had clear historical proof of the disease.

### Inheritance

In the families reported by Winter et al. (1997) and Winter et al. (1998), monilethrix-2 was inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Winter et al. (1997) studied 2 unrelated monilethrix families and a single case. In affected members of one family and in the single patient, they found the prevalent glu410-to-lys (E410K) mutation in the type II hair keratin hHb6 rather than a mutation in hHb1.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected individuals from a monilethrix family, Winter et al. (1997) identified a glu403-to-lys (E403K) substitution in the type II hair keratin hHb1. This glutamic acid residue corresponds to glu410 in hHb6, which lies in the helix termination motif. Because the hHb6 E410K mutation had been found in other monilethrix patients, the authors suggested that this site is a mutational hotspot in these closely related type II hair keratins.

Winter et al. (1998) noted that the monilethrix mutations reported so far in the type II hair cortex keratins hHb6 and hHb1 were restricted to glu413 (glu117 of the 2B subdomain) of the helix termination motif. In a French monilethrix family, they identified a new lysine substitution of another highly conserved glutamic acid residue in hHb1. This change, glu402-to-lys (E402K), affects glu106 of the 2B subdomain in the EIATYRRLLEGEE motif. It was present in 2 infants, one severely and one mildly affected, and in an adult with a history of the disease but no current symptoms.

### Pathogenesis

Winter et al. (1997) noted that hHb1 and hHb6 are largely coexpressed in the cortical trichocytes of the hair shaft. They concluded that monilethrix is a disease of the hair cortex.

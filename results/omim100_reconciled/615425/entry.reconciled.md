### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that epidermolysis bullosa simplex-3, localized or generalized intermediate, with BP230 deficiency (EBS3) is caused by homozygous or other biallelic mutation in the DST gene (113810) on chromosome 6p12.1. ⚠︎

### Description

EBS3 is an autosomal recessive inherited skin fragility disorder caused by intraepidermal separation. It is caused by loss of the epithelial isoform of bullous pemphigoid antigen-1 (BPAG1-e, BP230), which is encoded by the DST gene. Affected individuals have trauma-induced blisters and erosions. In the skin, hemidesmosomal inner plaques are lost and BPAG1-e is absent on immunostaining.

### Clinical Features

Groves et al. (2010) reported a 38-year-old man who had had generalized, trauma-induced spontaneous blisters and erosions all his life, particularly around the ankles. From age 37 years he also had episodic numbness in his limbs. The authors suggested that these neurologic symptoms might also be due to the DST mutation, but noted that he had a concomitant diagnosis of CADASIL (cerebral arteriopathy, autosomal dominant, with subcortical infarcts and leukoencephalopathy).

Ganani et al. (2021) reported 5 patients from 3 families who had epidermolysis bullosa simplex, with skin fragility due to intraepidermal separation.

### Inheritance

Groves et al. (2010) found a homozygous mutation in their patient and defined the disorder as a new form of autosomal recessive epidermolysis bullosa simplex. Ganani et al. (2021) stated that DST-associated epidermolysis bullosa simplex is transmitted as an autosomal recessive trait.

### Molecular Genetics

In a 38-year-old man with an inherited skin fragility disorder, Groves et al. (2010) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation (Q1124X) in the DST gene. The mutation lies in the region that encodes the coiled-coil domain of the epithelial isoform BPAG1-e (BP230). It caused loss of hemidesmosomal inner plaques and complete absence of BPAG1-e immunostaining in the patient's skin. The authors defined this as a new subtype of autosomal recessive epidermolysis bullosa simplex.

Liu et al. (2012) reported autosomal recessive epidermolysis bullosa simplex due to loss of BPAG1-e expression.

Ganani et al. (2021) found biallelic DST mutations in 5 patients with epidermolysis bullosa simplex from 3 families. Two of the variants had not been reported before: a 1-bp duplication (c.7097dupA), which results in a tyr2366-to-ter (Y2366X) substitution, and a 1-bp deletion (c.7429delC), which results in a frameshift and premature termination (Leu2477SerfsTer13).

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Ganani et al. (2021) reviewed 6 previously reported cases, bringing the total of individuals with EBS due to DST variants to 11. They found that the patients had distinctive phenotypes regardless of the causative variant.

### Pathogenesis

In the skin of their patient, Groves et al. (2010) found loss of hemidesmosomal inner plaques and complete absence of BPAG1-e immunostaining. Immunolabeling for plectin, the beta-4 integrin subunit, and type XVII collagen was also reduced.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 EPIDERMOLYSIS BULLOSA SIMPLEX 3, LOCALIZED OR GENERALIZED INTERMEDIATE, WITH BP230 DEFICIENCY**  
DST, GLN1124TER [p.Q1124*]

In a 38-year-old man with EBS3, Groves et al. (2010) identified a homozygous gln1124-to-ter (Q1124X) mutation in the DST gene. The mutation lies in the region that encodes the coiled-coil domain of the epithelial isoform BPAG1-e. The patient had lifelong generalized, trauma-induced blisters and erosions. His skin showed loss of hemidesmosomal inner plaques and complete absence of BPAG1-e immunostaining, as well as reduced labeling for plectin, beta-4 integrin, and type XVII collagen.

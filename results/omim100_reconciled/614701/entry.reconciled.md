### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Cornelia de Lange syndrome-4 with or without midline brain defects (CDLS4) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the RAD21 gene (606462) on chromosome 8q24.

### Description

CDLS4 is a cohesinopathy caused by heterozygous variants in RAD21. Affected individuals have growth retardation, minor skeletal anomalies, and facial features that overlap Cornelia de Lange syndrome (CdLS). Compared with CdLS caused by NIPBL, SMC1A, or SMC3 mutations, the phenotype is attenuated, and cognitive impairment is usually much milder (Deardorff et al., 2012; Krab et al., 2020). Some individuals with monoallelic RAD21 variants have holoprosencephaly, with or without CdLS characteristics (Krab et al., 2020). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

McBrien et al. (2008) reported a boy who had some features of Langer-Giedion syndrome, which suggested a functional disturbance of TRPS1. Oligo array-CGH had characterized his microdeletion as small and involving EXT1 alone. In addition to a mild Langer-Giedion-like phenotype, he had unusual features: prominent toe pads and fat pads on the soles of his feet, similar to those described in Pierpont syndrome. Krab et al. (2020) later listed this boy (family F21) as patient 2 of Deardorff et al. (2012) and as the patient of McBrien et al. (2008). They reported that he carried a de novo 1.7-Mb deletion at 8q23.3q24.12 that included the whole RAD21 gene.

Deardorff et al. (2012) reported children with RAD21 mutations who had growth retardation, minor skeletal anomalies, and facial features overlapping those of individuals with CdLS. Unlike children with mutations in NIPBL, SMC1A, or SMC3, these individuals had much milder cognitive impairment than those with classic CdLS.

Minor et al. (2014) reported 2 patients with a mild CdLS-like presentation. The first patient had developmental delay, hypospadias, inguinal hernia, and dysmorphic features. The second had developmental delay, characteristic facial features, hirsutism, and anomalies of the hands and feet. The first patient was more mildly affected than the second. The first patient's mutation was inherited from his mother, who had a history of melanoma and other unspecified medical problems; this was the first familial case. ⚠︎

Boyle et al. (2017) reported a family with intrafamilial phenotypic variation. The index patient (IV.16) was classified as having mild CdLS. The patient's mother and 2 maternal aunts carried the same RAD21 variant but did not fulfill the diagnostic criteria for CdLS, and none of them had been suspected of having CdLS or a cohesinopathy before the study. ⚠︎

Gudmundsson et al. (2019) reported a 15-month-old boy with developmental delay, distinct CdLS-like facial features, gastroesophageal reflux in early infancy, testis retention, prominent digit pads, and diaphragmatic hernia.

Kruszka et al. (2019) reported holoprosencephaly in individuals with variants in cohesin complex genes. Among them were 4 individuals with variants in the non-X-linked cohesin genes SMC3 and RAD21.

Dorval et al. (2020) reported a boy with a mild CdLS presentation and a molecularly confirmed RAD21 diagnosis. They compared his clinical features with those of previously described patients carrying other RAD21 intragenic mutations.

Krab et al. (2020) reported 49 individuals from 33 families with RAD21 alterations, 24 of whom were previously unpublished. The 29 patients with full clinical information (cohort A) had an attenuated CdLS phenotype compared with patients with NIPBL or SMC1A variants, particularly for facial morphology, limb anomalies, cognition, and behavior. In cohort A, they found postnatal microcephaly in 16 of 28, postnatal short stature in 10 of 27, synophrys in 19 of 28, a long and/or smooth philtrum in 26 of 29, a short fifth finger in 23 of 28, cleft palate in 6 of 25, heart anomalies in 9 of 23, hearing loss in 8 of 24, and gastroesophageal reflux in 13 of 25. None of the 29 had major limb malformations. Short stature and microcephaly typically developed postnatally. Unusual anomalies included vertebral anomalies (clefts and hemivertebrae) and relatively frequent malformations of foregut-derived structures. Sixteen of 29 patients (55%) had normal or mildly impaired cognition, and all patients aged 3 years or older used some words. Fourteen of 25 had behavioral problems, mainly anxiety, ADHD, autism spectrum disorder, and obsessive-compulsive behavior; aggression and self-injurious behavior each occurred in only 1 patient.

Goel and Parasivam (2020) reported another case of holoprosencephaly associated with a RAD21 loss-of-function variant.

### Inheritance

Transmission of CDLS4 in the reported families is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. Minor et al. (2014) reported a mutation inherited from a mother. Boyle et al. (2017) found the mutation in a patient's mother and 2 aunts, none of whom fulfilled CdLS diagnostic criteria; they suggested that RAD21 variants are associated with incomplete penetrance and that such individuals may be underdiagnosed. Krab et al. (2020) found that variants were frequently familial (9 of 21 index cases in whom inheritance could be established). Their cohort included several affected parent-child pairs carrying the same heterozygous variant (families F11, F16, F25, F27, and F28). ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Krab et al. (2020) suggested that biallelic RAD21 variants can cause Mungan syndrome and that monoallelic variants can cause holoprosencephaly, with or without CdLS characteristics. Whether RAD21 variants are involved in sclerocornea and schizophrenia was uncertain. In a family (F30) previously reported by Bonora et al. (2015), 3 members homozygous for an ala622-to-thr (A622T) variant had Mungan syndrome with chronic intestinal pseudo-obstruction. Three heterozygous members had some clinical features of the CdLS spectrum, but further clinical data could not be retrieved, so it remained uncertain whether they had a full CdLS phenotype. Molecular modeling predicted no structural alteration from A622T.

Kruszka et al. (2019) reported holoprosencephaly associated with variants in the 2 X-linked cohesin complex genes STAG2 and SMC1A. Ten individuals had loss-of-function variants, and 1 had a missense variant.

### Molecular Genetics

Deardorff et al. (2012) identified dominant missense and loss-of-function mutations in the RAD21 gene in children with features overlapping CdLS. The mutations affected the interface between RAD21 and the cohesin proteins STAG2 and SMC1A and impaired the cellular DNA damage response. Krab et al. (2020) provided data on patients originally reported by Deardorff et al. (2012): a de novo P376R missense variant (patient 5; F8), a de novo C585R missense variant (patient 6; F13), a 2-Mb deletion including the whole gene (patient 4; F19), and a de novo 1.7-Mb deletion including the whole gene (patient 2; F21).

Minor et al. (2014) reported the first intragenic deletion and frameshift mutations in RAD21 in patients with atypical CdLS. One patient had a heterozygous in-frame deletion of exon 13, inherited from his mother. The second had a heterozygous c.592_593dup frameshift mutation. Krab et al. (2020) further characterized the exon 13 deletion as a 665-bp deletion (c.1621-388_1704+193del) that predicted an in-frame deletion of asp541 to gln568. ⚠︎

In a patient with mild CdLS, Boyle et al. (2017) identified a heterozygous 1-bp deletion (c.704delG) in the RAD21 gene. The deletion was also found in the patient's mother and 2 aunts, none of whom fulfilled the diagnostic criteria for CdLS. ⚠︎

In a 15-month-old boy with CDLS4, Gudmundsson et al. (2019) used exome sequencing to identify a heterozygous in-frame deletion in the RAD21 gene (c.1774_1776del; Gln592del). Testing of the 2 healthy parents confirmed that the variant was de novo. In silico structural modeling predicted that the variant disrupts contact between RAD21 lys591 and SMC1A residues glu1191 and glu1192, altering the RAD21-SMC1A interface. ⚠︎

Kruszka et al. (2019) reported 4 individuals with holoprosencephaly and variants in SMC3 and RAD21. Krab et al. (2020) listed 2 of these RAD21 patients: one (patient 14; F9) had a de novo 8-bp deletion (c.1217_1224del), and the other (patient 12; F28) had a 1-bp duplication (c.1550dupC) inherited from his or her father.

Dorval et al. (2020) reported a novel RAD21 mutation in a boy with a mild CdLS presentation. Krab et al. (2020) listed this patient (F7) as carrying a de novo heterozygous 4-bp deletion (c.943_946del) in exon 9, predicted to cause a frameshift (Glu315GlnfsTer9). ⚠︎

Krab et al. (2020) reported 49 individuals from 33 families with RAD21 alterations: 24 intragenic variants and 7 microdeletions. Thirteen of the 24 sequence variants were predicted to be truncating and were scattered throughout the gene, whereas missense variants tended to cluster at protein-binding domains. Six of the 7 deletions included other genes in addition to RAD21. These ranged from a de novo 2.3-Mb deletion at 8q23.3q24.11 (F20) to deletions of 2 Mb, 880 kb, and 1.4 Mb recorded in ClinVar (F31-F33). A 27-kb deletion removed exons 1 to 9 (F22). The phenotype associated with the Phe600del variant (F18) was classic CdLS, and no variants were found in other known CdLS genes on a targeted gene panel; although the variant did not seem to affect RAD21 structure, the authors considered it likely pathogenic.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Deardorff et al. (2012) suggested that, compared with loss-of-function mutations in RAD21, dominant missense mutations caused more severe functional defects and worse structural and cognitive clinical findings.

Minor et al. (2014) found that their patient with the in-frame exon 13 deletion was more mildly affected than their patient with the frameshift mutation. They discussed variable clinical presentation within families and low penetrance of the mutations.

Dorval et al. (2020) noted that, among the genes causing CdLS, RAD21 is associated with a milder phenotype, and that very few RAD21 intragenic mutations had been identified.

Krab et al. (2020) observed a trend toward higher CdLS scores and more impaired growth in patients with microdeletions than in those with intragenic variants. They found no difference between truncating and nontruncating variants. In most families, mosaicism did not explain the striking interfamilial and intrafamilial variability, particularly in cognition and behavior. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Deardorff et al. (2012) found that the RAD21 mutations acted at the interface between RAD21 and the cohesin proteins STAG2 and SMC1A, and that they impaired the cellular DNA damage response.

Kruszka et al. (2019) found that knockdown of STAG2 and SMC1A by shRNA in human neural stem cells altered the expression of the holoprosencephaly-associated genes ZIC2, GLI2, SMAD3, and FGFR1. By whole-mount in situ hybridization in mouse embryos, Stag2 and Smc1a were expressed in the prosencephalic neural folds during primary neurulation, an expression pattern consistent with a role in forebrain morphogenesis. The authors concluded that the cohesin complex is an important regulator of median forebrain development.

Krab et al. (2020) performed protein modeling and molecular dynamics on 12 RAD21 variants. They predicted that S345P impairs and P376R over-stabilizes the RAD21-STAG2 interaction. Cys585Arg and Arg586Gln were predicted to destabilize the RAD21-SMC1A domain, and Gln592del and Leu603Pro to displace lys605, affecting SMC1A ATPase site 1. Arg65Gln, Phe600del, Ser618Gly, and Ala622Thr caused no structural alteration.

### Diagnosis

Minor et al. (2014) stressed the clinical value of RAD21 mutation analysis in patients with atypical forms of CdLS.

Krab et al. (2020) found that all cohort A patients had CdLS scores of at least 5, so no RAD21 variant would have been missed by applying the CdLS consensus criteria for molecular testing. CdLS had been suspected before testing in about 60% of index cases.

### Clinical Management

Krab et al. (2020) recommended parental testing even when parents appear unaffected, as well as periodic formal neuropsychological and behavioral screening. For a RAD21 variant of uncertain significance whose pathogenicity cannot be determined, they recommended testing of other CdLS genes or exome/genome sequencing.

### Animal Model

Deardorff et al. (2012) showed that the RAD21 mutations disrupted transcription in a zebrafish model.

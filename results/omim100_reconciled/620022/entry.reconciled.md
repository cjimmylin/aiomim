### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Stickler syndrome type VI (STL6) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the COL9A3 gene (120270) on chromosome 20q13.

### Description

Stickler syndrome type VI is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic loss-of-function mutations in COL9A3. Its consistent features are moderate-to-profound progressive sensorineural hearing loss and moderate to high myopia with vitreoretinal degeneration. Retinal detachment and cataract occur occasionally. Skeletal involvement is more variable and can include spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia and early-onset arthropathy (Markova et al., 2021; Rad et al., 2022).

### Clinical Features

Faletra et al. (2014) described a family with autosomal recessive Stickler syndrome caused by a loss-of-function mutation in the COL9A3 gene. They described it as the first such family.

Hanson-Kahn et al. (2018) reported a 12-year-old boy of Indian ancestry (family R08-308) whose parents were third cousins. He failed his newborn hearing screen and had moderate to severe sensorineural hearing loss with mild enlargement of the vestibular aqueducts. His speech delay was attributed to the hearing loss. He had progressive high myopia but no vitreous abnormalities or retinal degeneration. Femoral and tibial bowing were noted at birth. Radiographs at 6 years showed slightly flattened proximal femoral epiphyses, normal knee epiphyses, and mild platyspondyly. Other radiographic findings were irregular vertebral endplates, flattened acetabula, and irregularity of the medial distal femoral epiphyses. He had midfacial hypoplasia and did not have cleft palate or pes planus. He also had autoimmune hypothyroidism. His intelligence was normal and his growth parameters were in the normal range. ⚠︎

Nixon et al. (2019) studied 6 patients from 4 families with recessive Stickler syndrome due to homozygous variants in type IX collagen genes. Two of these patients were from 1 family with a COL9A3 variant. All 6 patients were highly myopic, with congenital megalophthalmos and abnormal, hypoplastic vitreous gel, and all had sensorineural hearing loss. One of the 6 patients had severe arthropathy. Nixon et al. (2019) noted that sensorineural hearing loss is more common and more severe in recessive than in dominant Stickler syndrome.

Markova et al. (2021) reported a 3-year-old Russian boy born to nonconsanguineous parents. He had congenital high bilateral myopia, hip dysplasia, and grade 3-4 bilateral sensorineural hearing loss with delayed speech development. On ophthalmologic examination he had peripheral retinal pigment rearrangement with dystrophic foci, vitreous floaters, and a preretinal membrane. His electroretinogram was normal and he had no retinal detachment. Radiography showed mild spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia, including mild platyspondyly, kyphosis, flattened and irregular femoral heads, coxa valga, and flattened distal femoral epiphyses. He also had spina bifida occulta of the sacral vertebrae. Other findings were genu valgum, a waddling gait, early-onset arthropathy with lower limb joint pain, lumbar lordosis, limited hip abduction, prominent knee joints, long fingers, and planovalgus foot deformity. His facial features included a flattened nasal bridge, small nose, mild midface hypoplasia, and high palate, without cleft palate. His height was normal. ⚠︎

Rad et al. (2022) reported 5 patients from 3 unrelated consanguineous Iranian families. In family 1, the patient was a 28-year-old woman. She was the oldest and only affected of 3 children born to first-cousin parents. She had high myopia in both eyes, vitreoretinal degeneration with empty vitreous, multiple lattice degenerations, retinal pigmentary changes, and severe progressive sensorineural hearing loss. Her skull x-ray showed unilateral absence of the frontal sinus. She also had knee pain, pes planus, low-set ears, short metacarpals with widened epiphyses, mild platyspondyly of the thoracic spine, and genu valgum. She did not have cleft palate, micrognathia, short stature, retinal detachment, or intellectual disability. ⚠︎

Family 2 of Rad et al. (2022) included 2 affected brothers, aged 65 and 57 years, among 4 children of a first-cousin marriage. Both had high myopia and had undergone multiple vitreoretinal surgeries for recurrent rhegmatogenous retinal detachments resulting from advanced vitreoretinal degeneration. Both had cataract. Despite surgery, the older brother was blind without light perception in either eye. The younger brother had counting-finger vision in one eye and no light perception in the other. Both had severe progressive sensorineural hearing loss and a herniated cervical disc. The older brother also had muscular atrophy. Neither had short stature.

Family 3 of Rad et al. (2022) had 2 affected and 2 healthy children born to first-cousin parents. The affected children were an 11.8-year-old boy and a 3.1-year-old girl. Both had myopia and congenital moderate to severe progressive sensorineural hearing impairment. In both, x-ray and detailed examination showed spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia. Both also had pes planus, a depressed nasal bridge, and anteverted nares. Midface hypoplasia and downslanting palpebral fissures were more pronounced in the girl. The boy reported knee pain, especially when running. He also had genu valgum, bowed tibiae, short metacarpals, a broad hallux, irregular femoral epiphyses, and flat acetabular roofs with flattened femoral heads. Neither child had cleft palate, and none of the individuals in the study showed signs of intellectual disability.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of STL6 in the families reported by Faletra et al. (2014), Hanson-Kahn et al. (2018), Markova et al. (2021), and Rad et al. (2022) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. In the consanguineous family of Hanson-Kahn et al. (2018), the parents and an unaffected sister of the proband were heterozygous for the mutation. Several affected individuals were born to consanguineous parents.

Rad et al. (2022) noted that Nixon et al. had observed mild Stickler phenotypes in carrier parents. In contrast, Rad et al. (2022) did not observe mild phenotypes in heterozygous individuals.

### Heterogeneity

Faletra et al. (2014) noted that dominant Stickler syndrome is caused by mutations in COL2A1, COL11A1, and COL11A2, and recessive forms by mutations in COL9A1 and COL9A2. Before their report, only heterozygous COL9A3 mutations had been reported in humans. These had been associated with multiple epiphyseal dysplasia type 3, susceptibility to intervertebral disc disease, and hearing loss.

### Molecular Genetics

In a family with autosomal recessive Stickler syndrome, Faletra et al. (2014) identified a loss-of-function mutation in the COL9A3 gene (c.1176_1198del; p.Gln393CysfsTer25).

Hanson-Kahn et al. (2018) studied a boy with STL6 from a consanguineous family (R08-308). Using exome sequencing, they identified a homozygous 1-bp duplication (c.650dupC) in exon 13 of the COL9A3 gene. His parents (third cousins) and an unaffected sister were heterozygous for the duplication. The change was not found in public SNP databases. The authors predicted that this frameshift causes premature termination of translation and absence of the COL9A3 gene product. The proband was also heterozygous for an arg127-to-his (R127H) variant in the GJB2 gene (121011); no second GJB2 variant was found. ⚠︎

Nixon et al. (2019) studied 6 patients from 4 families with recessive Stickler syndrome. In 2 patients from 1 family, they identified a homozygous variant in the COL9A3 gene. The other families carried homozygous variants in COL9A1 (120210) or COL9A2 (120260).

Markova et al. (2021) studied a Russian boy with STL6. Using targeted sequencing of 166 genes associated with congenital skeletal disorders, they identified compound heterozygous nonsense mutations in the COL9A3 gene: R90X in exon 5 and R577X in exon 30. Sanger sequencing in the family showed that the mutations were in trans. The healthy sibs did not carry pathogenic COL9A3 variants.

Rad et al. (2022) studied 5 patients from 3 unrelated consanguineous Iranian families with STL6. Using exome sequencing, they identified 3 different novel homozygous loss-of-function variants in the COL9A3 gene. The variants were a 10-bp deletion causing a frameshift in exon 2 (c.107_116del; p.Pro36ArgfsTer49), a nonsense mutation in exon 23 (R402X), and a 1-bp deletion in exon 7 (c.355delC; p.Leu119SerfsTer9). With these families, the total number of reported families reached 7.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Hanson-Kahn et al. (2018) noted that all Stickler syndrome patients with type IX collagen defects had high myopia, midface hypoplasia, and mild to severe hearing loss. Vitreous and retinal degeneration had been reported with COL9A1 and COL9A2 mutations but not with COL9A3 mutations. They suggested that loss of the alpha-3 chain of type IX collagen produces a phenotype similar to the other recessive type IX collagen forms.

Markova et al. (2021) compared their patient with 6 previously reported patients from 3 families with homozygous COL9A3 mutations. High myopia and sensorineural hearing loss were present in all of them, and none had cleft palate. Their compound heterozygous patient had a more severe presentation, with early-onset arthropathy and vitreoretinal degeneration.

Rad et al. (2022) reviewed patients with biallelic COL9A3 loss-of-function variants. Moderate-to-profound progressive sensorineural hearing loss and moderate to high myopia with vitreoretinal degeneration were consistent features. Retinal detachment and cataract occurred occasionally, and skeletal involvement seemed more variable.

### Diagnosis

Nixon et al. (2019) suggested adding COL9A1, COL9A2, and COL9A3 to genetic screening panels for patients with congenital hearing loss. They reasoned that early diagnosis of recessive Stickler syndrome would have a high impact for children at risk of dual sensory impairment.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0002 STICKLER SYNDROME, TYPE VI**  
COL9A3, 1-BP DUP, 650C [c.650dupc]

Hanson-Kahn et al. (2018) studied a boy of Indian ancestry with STL6 from a consanguineous family. Using exome sequencing, they identified a homozygous 1-bp duplication (c.650dupC, NM_001853.3) in exon 13 of the COL9A3 gene. His parents (third cousins) and an unaffected sister were heterozygous for the duplication. The frameshift was predicted to cause premature termination of translation and absence of the gene product, and the change was not found in public SNP databases.

**.0003 STICKLER SYNDROME, TYPE VI**  
COL9A3, ARG90TER [c.268C>T; p.R90*]

Markova et al. (2021) reported a Russian boy with STL6 who was compound heterozygous for 2 mutations in the COL9A3 gene, found by targeted sequencing of 166 skeletal disorder genes. One was a c.268C-T transition in exon 5, resulting in an arg90-to-ter (R90X) substitution. The other was a c.1729C-T transition in exon 30, resulting in an arg577-to-ter (R577X; see V8) substitution. Sanger sequencing of the family showed that the mutations were in trans. The healthy sibs did not carry pathogenic COL9A3 variants.

**.0004 STICKLER SYNDROME, TYPE VI**  
COL9A3, ARG577TER [c.1729C>T; p.R577*]

In a Russian boy with STL6, Markova et al. (2021) identified a c.1729C-T transition in exon 30 of the COL9A3 gene, resulting in an arg577-to-ter (R577X) substitution. The mutation was in compound heterozygosity with R90X, and Sanger sequencing in the family showed the 2 mutations to be in trans.

**.0005 STICKLER SYNDROME, TYPE VI**  
COL9A3, 10-BP DEL, NT107 [c.107_116del; p.P36R]

Rad et al. (2022) identified a homozygous 10-bp deletion (c.107_116del, rs1470627424) in exon 2 of the COL9A3 gene by exome sequencing. The deletion causes a frameshift (p.Pro36ArgfsTer49). The patient was a 28-year-old Iranian woman with STL6 (family 1), the only affected of 3 children born to first-cousin parents.

**.0006 STICKLER SYNDROME, TYPE VI**  
COL9A3, ARG402TER [c.1204C>T; p.R402*]

Rad et al. (2022) identified a homozygous c.1204C-T transition (rs989413835) in exon 23 of the COL9A3 gene by exome sequencing, resulting in an arg402-to-ter (R402X) substitution. The carriers were 2 Iranian brothers with STL6 (family 2), aged 65 and 57 years, born to first-cousin parents. Both had recurrent rhegmatogenous retinal detachments and severe progressive sensorineural hearing loss.

**.0007 STICKLER SYNDROME, TYPE VI**  
COL9A3, 1-BP DEL, 355C [c.355delc; p.L119S]

Rad et al. (2022) identified a homozygous 1-bp deletion (c.355delC) in exon 7 of the COL9A3 gene by exome sequencing. The deletion causes a frameshift (p.Leu119SerfsTer9).

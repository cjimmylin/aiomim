### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that intermediate junctional epidermolysis bullosa-5A (JEB5A) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the ITGB4 gene (147557) on chromosome 17q25.1. ⚠︎

### Description

Intermediate junctional epidermolysis bullosa-5A (JEB5A) is a skin-fragility disorder caused by mutation in the gene encoding beta-4 integrin. It occurs without pyloric atresia. Reported features include blistering from birth, nail dystrophy and enamel hypoplasia (Inoue et al., 2000; Jonkman et al., 2002).

### Nomenclature

In a consensus expert review, Has et al. (2020) reclassified inherited epidermolysis bullosa and other disorders with skin fragility using new clinical and molecular data. They introduced the concept of genetic disorders with skin fragility, with classical epidermolysis bullosa as the prototype.

### Clinical Features

Inoue et al. (2000) reported non-Herlitz junctional epidermolysis bullosa without pyloric atresia in a patient (or patients) homozygous for a missense mutation in the cytoplasmic tail of beta-4 integrin. The available evidence describes the disorder as junctional, i.e. with cleavage in the lamina lucida. ⚠︎

Jonkman et al. (2002) described a 49-year-old woman who had had mild blistering of the hands and feet from birth, together with nail dystrophy with onychogryposis and enamel hypoplasia. She had no alopecia and no history of pyloric atresia. The blisters cleaved within the epidermis, low in the basal keratinocytes at the level of the hemidesmosome attachment plaque. The authors considered the phenotype to have predominant features of epidermolysis bullosa simplex.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of JEB5A in the reported patients is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Inoue et al. (2000) found a homozygous ITGB4 mutation. The patient of Jonkman et al. (2002) was heterozygous for an in-frame ITGB4 deletion and was expected to also carry a null allele on the other chromosome. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Pulkkinen et al. (1998) described epidermolysis bullosa with pyloric atresia, which is also caused by ITGB4 mutation, as a clinically and genetically heterogeneous autosomal recessive blistering disorder with lethal and nonlethal variants.

### Molecular Genetics

In a patient (or patients) with non-Herlitz junctional epidermolysis bullosa without pyloric atresia, Inoue et al. (2000) identified a homozygous missense mutation in the cytoplasmic tail of beta-4 integrin (ITGB4), gly931-to-asp (G931D). They proposed that the mutation disrupts hemidesmosome assembly.

In a 49-year-old woman with mild blistering from birth, Jonkman et al. (2002) identified a heterozygous 2-bp deletion in the ITGB4 gene (4733delCT). The deletion caused in-frame skipping of exon 36 and was predicted to remove 50 amino acids (residues 1450-1499) within the third fibronectin type III repeat of the beta-4 cytoplasmic domain. On immunoblot the beta-4 polypeptide was 5 kDa shorter than normal. No full-size protein was detected in vitro, and the epitope recognized by antibody 450-11A was absent in vivo, so the patient was expected to also be heterozygous for a null allele.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Mellerio et al. (1998) reported 2 unrelated patients with ITGB4 mutations who had pyloric atresia but only mild mucocutaneous fragility and survived into early childhood. Each was compound heterozygous for a nonsense or deletion mutation and either a missense or a splice site mutation. The authors suggested that the nonsense and deletion mutations would markedly reduce beta-4 integrin mRNA, whereas the missense or splice site mutation on the second allele might allow synthesis of some functional, albeit perturbed, beta-4 polypeptide. They proposed this as a possible explanation for the mild phenotype and survival of their patients. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Jonkman et al. (2002) concluded that deletion of the third fibronectin type III repeat of the beta-4 integrin cytoplasmic domain is pathogenic and results in a mild phenotype with predominant features of epidermolysis bullosa simplex. This repeat is thought to interact with BP180/type XVII collagen. In the patient's skin, immunofluorescence showed no binding of antibody 450-11A, which recognizes this repeat, and reduced binding of antibodies to the amino- and carboxy-terminal ends of beta-4.

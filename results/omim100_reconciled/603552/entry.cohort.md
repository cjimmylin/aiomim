### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis-4 (FHL4) is caused by homozygous mutation in the syntaxin-11 gene (STX11; 605014) on chromosome 6q24.2.

### Description

Familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis-4 (FHL4) is an autosomal recessive form of hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH). It presents in infancy or childhood with fever, hepatosplenomegaly, cytopenias, hypertriglyceridemia, hypofibrinogenemia, and hyperferritinemia. Patients have defective natural killer (NK) cell cytotoxicity and degranulation (Muller et al., 2014). Compared with other forms of FHL, STX11 mutations have been associated with a milder course that can include prolonged remissions without specific treatment (Rudd et al., 2006). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

zur Stadt et al. (2005) studied a large consanguineous Kurdish kindred in which 5 children had familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis. They also studied 5 further consanguineous Turkish/Kurdish FHL kindreds.

Zur Stadt et al. (2006) screened 63 unrelated patients with primary HLH. Six of the 32 patients from Turkey had STX11 mutations.

Rudd et al. (2006) described 6 children with STX11 mutations from 4 of 28 unrelated FHL families without PRF1 mutations. Three patients had long periods (1 year or more) in remission without specific treatment, which the authors noted is very uncommon in this disease. Some patients developed severe psychomotor retardation. Two of the 6 patients developed myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) or acute myelogenous leukemia (AML).

Muller et al. (2014) reported 3 children from unrelated consanguineous Pakistani families who presented with HLH. Patient A, a boy, presented at 2 months of age with fever, splenomegaly, hepatomegaly, anemia (hemoglobin 55 g/L), neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, hypertriglyceridemia, hypofibrinogenemia, and hyperferritinemia. Patient B, a boy, presented at 5 years of age with the same features (hemoglobin 71 g/L). Hemophagocytosis and neurologic manifestations were absent in both boys. Patient C, a girl, presented at 48 months of age with a hyperinflammatory syndrome. All 3 patients had deficient NK cell activity and degranulation.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of FHL4 is autosomal recessive. zur Stadt et al. (2005) found homozygous STX11 mutations in affected children from consanguineous kindreds. Muller et al. (2014) found that the parents of affected homozygous children were unaffected heterozygous carriers.

### Mapping

zur Stadt et al. (2005) performed genome-wide homozygosity mapping in a large consanguineous Kurdish kindred in which 5 children had FHL. They found linkage to a 10-cM region on chromosome 6q24 between D6S1569 and D6S960, defining a novel FHL locus.

### Heterogeneity

Zur Stadt et al. (2006) found mutations in 38 of 63 unrelated patients with primary HLH: 20 in PRF1 (170280), 12 in UNC13D (608897), and 6 in STX11. They concluded that FHL shows extensive genetic and allelic heterogeneity.

### Molecular Genetics

zur Stadt et al. (2005) screened positional candidate genes in a large consanguineous Kurdish kindred linked to 6q24. In affected children they found a homozygous 5-bp deletion in the STX11 gene. They then found homozygous STX11 mutations in 5 consanguineous Turkish/Kurdish FHL kindreds. Two families had the 5-bp deletion, 1 family had a 19.2-kb genomic deletion spanning the entire coding region of STX11 (exon 2), and 2 families had a nonsense mutation causing a premature stop codon in the C-terminal end of the protein. Syntaxin-11 protein was absent from the mononuclear cell fraction of patients homozygous for the 5-bp deletion.

Zur Stadt et al. (2006) found STX11 mutations in 6 of 32 patients with primary HLH from Turkey.

Rudd et al. (2006) sequenced STX11 in 28 FHL families without perforin mutations. They found STX11 mutations in 4 families (14% of the PRF1-negative families), affecting 6 of 34 children. Two different, previously reported mutations were identified: a nonsense mutation and a deletion.

Muller et al. (2014) sequenced the coding regions and splice sites of UNC13D, STX11, and STXBP2 in 3 children with FHL4 from unrelated consanguineous Pakistani families. All 3 were homozygous for a novel missense mutation in the Habc domain of STX11, c.173T-C, resulting in a leu58-to-pro (L58P) substitution. The parents were heterozygous for the mutation and had no recorded history of inflammatory disease. Patient A was also heterozygous for a rare UNC13D variant, c.811C-T (P271S), inherited from the father, and homozygous for an uncommon UNC13D variant, c.2782C-T (R928C). The authors suggested that these UNC13D variants might contribute to the severity of his disease. Syntaxin-11 levels were greatly reduced in peripheral blood mononuclear cells from patient C. Her lymphokine-activated killer (LAK) cells showed a selective loss of syntaxin-11 expression, whereas Munc18-2 expression was similar to that of controls.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Rudd et al. (2006) concluded that STX11 mutations were associated with a milder phenotype, with prolonged remissions without specific treatment, but that some patients developed severe psychomotor retardation. They also suggested that STX11 mutations may be associated with secondary malignancies (MDS/AML).

### Pathogenesis

Both STX11 and UNC13D are involved in vesicle trafficking and membrane fusion. On this basis, zur Stadt et al. (2005) concluded that, besides perforin mutations, defects in the endocytotic or exocytotic pathway may be a common mechanism in FHL.

Muller et al. (2014) found defective NK cell cytotoxicity and degranulation in patients homozygous for the STX11 L58P mutation, with normal expression of perforin, granzyme B, and CD107a. IL-2 stimulation partly restored both cytotoxicity and degranulation. In HEK-293T cells, the syntaxin-11 L58P mutant and an N-peptide R4A mutant were expressed at wildtype levels but did not bind Munc18-2. In contrast, the C-terminal Q268X FHL4 mutant still bound Munc18-2. The authors proposed that loss of Munc18-2 binding may destabilize syntaxin-11, which would explain its low expression in patient cells.

### Clinical Management

Locatelli et al. (2020) conducted an open-label, single-group phase 2-3 study of emapalumab, a human anti-interferon-gamma antibody, given with dexamethasone. The 34 patients were aged 18 years or younger and had primary HLH; 27 had been treated previously and 7 had not. Among previously treated patients, 63% had a response, compared with a prespecified null hypothesis of 40%. Seventy percent of these patients proceeded to transplantation, and 74% were alive at the last observation. Emapalumab was not associated with any organ toxicity. Severe infections developed in 10 patients during treatment, and emapalumab was stopped in 1 patient because of disseminated histoplasmosis.

### Population Genetics

Among 63 unrelated patients with primary HLH, Zur Stadt et al. (2006) found STX11 mutations in 6 of the 32 patients from Turkey. There were no STX11 mutations in the 23 German patients or the 8 patients of other origins. FHL2, FHL3, and FHL4 together accounted for 80% of HLH cases of Turkish origin and 30% of German cases.

Muller et al. (2014) studied 3 children with FHL4 from unrelated consanguineous Pakistani families: 2 boys who presented at 2 months and 5 years of age and a girl who presented at 48 months.

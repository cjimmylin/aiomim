### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis-4 (FHL4) is caused by homozygous mutation in the STX11 gene (605014) on chromosome 6q24.

### Description

Familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis-4 (FHL4) is an autosomal recessive hyperinflammatory disorder caused by defects in syntaxin-11. Reported patients had fever, hepatosplenomegaly, cytopenias, hypertriglyceridemia, hypofibrinogenemia and hyperferritinemia, together with deficient natural killer (NK) cell activity and degranulation (Muller et al., 2014). Rudd et al. (2006) found that some patients had long disease-free remissions without specific treatment. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

zur Stadt et al. (2005) studied a large consanguineous Kurdish kindred with 5 children affected with familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (FHL). Syntaxin-11 protein was absent from the mononuclear cells of the patients. They also studied 5 more consanguineous Turkish/Kurdish FHL kindreds. ⚠︎

Zur Stadt et al. (2006) reported 6 patients from Turkey with hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis and STX11 mutations. They were identified among 63 unrelated FHL patients.

Rudd et al. (2006) reported 6 children with FHL from 4 unrelated families without perforin mutations who carried STX11 mutations. Three patients had long periods (1 year or more) in remission without specific treatment, which the authors noted is very uncommon in this disease. Some patients developed severe psychomotor retardation. Two of the 6 patients developed myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) or acute myelogenous leukemia (AML).

Muller et al. (2014) described 3 children from unrelated consanguineous Pakistani families. The authors described them as 2 infants and a 5-year-old child who presented with HLH. Patient A, a boy, had onset at 2 months of age. He had fever, splenomegaly, hepatomegaly, anemia (hemoglobin 55 g/L), neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, hypertriglyceridemia, hypofibrinogenemia and hyperferritinemia. Patient B, a boy with onset at 5 years, had the same findings, with a hemoglobin of 71 g/L. Hemophagocytosis and neurologic manifestations were recorded as absent in both boys. Patient C, a girl whose onset was listed at 48 months, presented with a hyperinflammatory syndrome. All 3 patients had deficient NK cell activity and degranulation.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of FHL4 is autosomal recessive. zur Stadt et al. (2005) found homozygous STX11 mutations in affected children from consanguineous kindreds. Muller et al. (2014) found homozygous STX11 mutations in patients from consanguineous families, and the patients' unaffected parents were heterozygous.

### Mapping

zur Stadt et al. (2005) performed genome-wide homozygosity mapping in a large consanguineous Kurdish kindred with 5 children affected with FHL. They found linkage to a 10-cM region on chromosome 6q24 between D6S1569 and D6S960, which defined a novel FHL locus.

### Heterogeneity

Zur Stadt et al. (2006) found mutations in PRF1 (170280), UNC13D (608897) or STX11 in 38 of 63 unrelated FHL patients: 20 in PRF1, 12 in UNC13D and 6 in STX11. They concluded that FHL shows extensive genetic and allelic heterogeneity.

### Molecular Genetics

In a large consanguineous Kurdish kindred with 5 children affected with FHL, zur Stadt et al. (2005) screened positional candidate genes and identified a homozygous 5-bp deletion in the STX11 gene. They found homozygous STX11 mutations in 5 additional consanguineous Turkish/Kurdish FHL kindreds. Two families had the 5-bp deletion. One family had a 19.2-kb genomic deletion spanning the entire coding region of STX11 (exon 2). Two families had a nonsense mutation causing a premature stop codon in the C-terminal end of the protein. Syntaxin-11 protein was absent from the mononuclear cell fraction of patients homozygous for the 5-bp deletion.

Zur Stadt et al. (2006) screened 63 unrelated FHL patients and found STX11 mutations in 6 of the 32 patients from Turkey.

Rudd et al. (2006) sequenced STX11 in 28 FHL families without perforin mutations. They found STX11 mutations in 4 families (14% of the PRF1-negative families), affecting 6 of 34 children. Two different mutations were identified, a nonsense mutation and a deletion, and both had been reported previously.

Muller et al. (2014) sequenced the coding regions and splice sites of UNC13D, STX11 and STXBP2 in 3 children with FHL4 from unrelated consanguineous Pakistani families. All 3 patients were homozygous for a novel missense mutation in STX11, c.173T-C (L58P), located in the Habc domain. Their parents were heterozygous for the mutation and had no recorded history of inflammatory disease. Patient A also carried 2 UNC13D variants. He was heterozygous for a rare c.811C-T (P271S) variant, which he inherited from his father, and homozygous for an uncommon c.2782C-T (R928C) variant. The authors suggested that these UNC13D variants might contribute to the severity of his disease.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Rudd et al. (2006) concluded that STX11 mutations were associated with a milder phenotype, with prolonged remissions without specific treatment. However, some patients developed severe psychomotor retardation. The authors suggested that STX11 mutations may be associated with secondary malignancies (MDS/AML). ⚠︎

Zur Stadt et al. (2006) found that the PRF1 mutation Trp374X was present in 12 patients from Turkey and was associated with very early onset of disease, below 3 months of age, in all cases.

### Pathogenesis

Because both STX11 and UNC13D are involved in vesicle trafficking and membrane fusion, zur Stadt et al. (2005) concluded that, besides perforin mutations, defects in the endocytotic or exocytotic pathway may be a common mechanism in FHL.

Muller et al. (2014) found defective NK cell cytotoxicity and degranulation in patients homozygous for the STX11 L58P mutation, and IL-2 stimulation partly restored both. Expression of perforin, granzyme B and CD107a was normal. Syntaxin-11 levels were greatly reduced in patient PBMCs. Patient LAK cells showed a selective loss of syntaxin-11, while Munc18-2 expression was similar to that of controls. In HEK-293T cells, the syntaxin-11 L58P and N-peptide R4A mutants were expressed at wildtype levels but did not bind Munc18-2. In contrast, the C-terminal Q268X FHL4 mutant still bound Munc18-2. The authors proposed that loss of Munc18-2 binding may destabilize syntaxin-11, which would explain its low expression in patient cells. ⚠︎

### Clinical Management

Locatelli et al. (2020) conducted an open-label, single-group phase 2-3 study of emapalumab, a human anti-interferon-gamma antibody, given with dexamethasone. They treated 34 patients aged 18 years or younger with primary hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis: 27 had been treated before and 7 had not. Of the previously treated patients, 63% had a response, compared with a prespecified null hypothesis of 40%. Seventy percent of them proceeded to transplantation, and 74% were alive at the last observation. Emapalumab was not associated with any organ toxicity. Severe infections developed in 10 patients during treatment, and emapalumab was stopped in 1 patient because of disseminated histoplasmosis.

### Population Genetics

Among 63 unrelated FHL patients, Zur Stadt et al. (2006) found STX11 mutations in 6 of the 32 patients from Turkey. They found none among the 23 German patients or the 8 patients of other origins. FHL2, FHL3 and FHL4 together accounted for 80% of HLH cases of Turkish origin and 30% of German cases.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

Syntaxin-11 protein was absent from the mononuclear cell fraction of patients homozygous for the deletion.

In 2 consanguineous Turkish/Kurdish families with FHL4, zur Stadt et al. (2005) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in the STX11 gene that leads to a premature stop codon in the C-terminal end of the protein.

**.0004 HEMOPHAGOCYTIC LYMPHOHISTIOCYTOSIS, FAMILIAL, 4**  
STX11, LEU58PRO [c.173T>C; p.L58P]

In 3 children with FHL4 from unrelated consanguineous Pakistani families, Muller et al. (2014) identified a homozygous c.173T-C transition in the STX11 gene. It results in a leu58-to-pro (L58P) substitution in the Habc domain. The parents were heterozygous for the mutation and had no recorded history of inflammatory disease. The patients had deficient NK cell activity and degranulation, with greatly reduced syntaxin-11 levels in their cells. In HEK-293T cells, the L58P mutant was expressed at wildtype levels but did not bind Munc18-2. One patient was also heterozygous for an UNC13D P271S variant and homozygous for an UNC13D R928C variant, which the authors suggested might contribute to the severity of his disease. ⚠︎

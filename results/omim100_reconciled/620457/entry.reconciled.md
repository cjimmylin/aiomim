### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that auriculocondylar syndrome-4 (ARCND4) is caused by a tandem duplication on chromosome 7p21.1 that covers most of the HDAC9 gene (606543) and lies telomeric to TWIST1.

### Description

Auriculocondylar syndrome is an autosomal dominant disorder of the first and second pharyngeal arches. Its features are malformed 'question mark' ears, prominent cheeks, microstomia, an abnormal temporomandibular joint, and hypoplasia of the mandibular condyle. Penetrance appears to be complete, but the features vary widely within and between families (Masotti et al., 2008). ARCND4 is caused by a 430-kb duplication that covers most of the HDAC9 gene and includes TWIST1 regulatory elements (Romanelli Tavares et al., 2022).

### Clinical Features

Masotti et al. (2008) described a multigeneration family (F1) with 11 individuals affected by auriculocondylar syndrome. The family showed variable clinical features. Many of the patients had facial asymmetry, a feature that the authors noted had not been highlighted in other reports of the syndrome.

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) restudied the same family (F1), which was Brazilian and nonconsanguineous and included 11 members with typical features of ARCND. Among the members whose features were documented, 8 of 10 had question mark ears, 8 of 10 had microstomia, and 6 of 10 had micrognathia. Large, open fontanelles were not present. The features varied considerably within the family.

### Inheritance

Masotti et al. (2008) reported autosomal dominant inheritance of auriculocondylar syndrome in the multigeneration family F1. In the same family, Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) showed that the causal duplication segregated with the phenotype. Because all unaffected family members lacked the duplication, they suggested that penetrance was full. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Masotti et al. (2008) performed genome-wide linkage analysis in 2 large families with auriculocondylar syndrome. One family (F2) showed linkage to 1p21.1-q23.3, but the phenotype in the other family (F1) was not linked to this locus.

In the 4-generation Brazilian family F1, Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) found no pathogenic variants in PLCB4, GNAI3, or EDN1. Linkage analysis assuming autosomal dominant inheritance gave a maximum LOD score of 2.88 (maximum theoretical score, 2.93) in a 17.6-Mb region on chromosome 7. Microsatellite markers narrowed this region to chr7:14395902-28158440 (hg38).

### Heterogeneity

Masotti et al. (2008) found linkage to the 1p21.1-q23.3 locus in one family (F2) but not in a second large family (F1). They concluded that at least 2 loci are responsible for auriculocondylar syndrome.

### Molecular Genetics

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) used targeted resequencing in affected members of the Brazilian family F1 originally reported by Masotti et al. (2008). They identified a 430-kb tandem duplication (NC_000007.14:g.18437239_18867540dup) that began about 280 kb telomeric to TWIST1 and covered most of the HDAC9 gene. Several shorter HDAC9 isoforms were duplicated in full, whereas the full-length catalytically active isoforms were predicted to be disrupted. The authors amplified the breakpoint and used Sanger sequencing to show that the duplication segregated with the phenotype. No unaffected family member carried it (LOD score 2.88). ⚠︎

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) also identified 19 patients in the DECIPHER database with copy-number gains in the region. Seven of them had ear malformations and 7 had micrognathia, and most of these duplications included the TWIST1 enhancer eTW6. One patient without features of ARCND had a 179-kb duplication that lay entirely within the ARCND duplication but did not include the TWIST1 regulatory elements. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) performed Capture-C from the TWIST1 promoter in human embryonic calvarial mesenchymal cells. The most frequent cis interactions were telomeric to TWIST1, within HDAC9 and the duplicated region. This suggested that the region contains TWIST1 regulatory elements, which would give the duplication a possible pathogenic mechanism.

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) found that neural crest cells derived from patient iPSCs expressed more HDAC9 (3.15-fold) and TWIST1 (2.03-fold) than control cells. The patient cells also showed a 4.3-fold decrease in migration. During osteogenic differentiation, patient mesenchymal cells had lower alkaline phosphatase activity and less matrix mineralization, which the authors interpreted as delayed or impaired osteogenesis.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 AURICULOCONDYLAR SYNDROME 4**  
HDAC9, 430-KB DUP [g.18437239_18867540dup]

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) studied a nonconsanguineous Brazilian family (F1) with 11 members affected by auriculocondylar syndrome-4 (ARCND4). In affected members they identified a 430,302-bp tandem duplication (NC_000007.14:g.18437239_18867540dup) telomeric to TWIST1 that covered most of the HDAC9 gene. The duplication segregated with the phenotype, and all unaffected family members were negative for it (LOD score 2.88). Neural crest cells derived from patient iPSCs showed increased HDAC9 and TWIST1 expression and reduced migration. Capture-C showed that the duplicated region interacted with the TWIST1 promoter.

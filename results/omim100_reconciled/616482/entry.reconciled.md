### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that severe achondroplasia with developmental delay and acanthosis nigricans (SADDAN) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the FGFR3 gene (134934) on chromosome 4p16.3. ⚠︎

### Description

Severe achondroplasia with developmental delay and acanthosis nigricans (SADDAN) is a skeletal dysplasia caused by a lys650-to-met (K650M) mutation in FGFR3. Endochondral bone growth is severely disturbed, at times nearly as severely as in thanatophoric dysplasia type I. Patients also have progressive acanthosis nigricans, structural anomalies of the central nervous system, seizures and severe developmental delay. Unlike thanatophoric dysplasia, SADDAN is most often compatible with survival past infancy, and it is not associated with cloverleaf skull or craniosynostosis.

### Nomenclature

Tavormina et al. (1999) named the phenotype caused by the FGFR3 lys650-to-met mutation 'severe achondroplasia with developmental delay and acanthosis nigricans' (SADDAN). They gave it a separate name because it differs significantly from the phenotypes caused by other known FGFR3 mutations.

### Clinical Features

Tavormina et al. (1999) reported 4 unrelated individuals whose skeletal dysplasia approached the severity seen in thanatophoric dysplasia type I (TD1). Three of the 4 developed extensive areas of acanthosis nigricans beginning in early childhood and had severe neurologic impairment. These 3 had survived past infancy without prolonged life-support measures.

Bellus et al. (1999) gave a phenotypic analysis of the same 4 unrelated individuals. The FGFR3 lys650-to-met mutation severely disturbed endochondral bone growth, approaching and overlapping the changes of thanatophoric dysplasia type I, but was most often compatible with survival into adulthood. Some patients had other unusual bone deformities: femoral bowing with reverse (posterior apex) tibial and fibular bowing, and 'ram's horn' bowing of the clavicle. Surviving patients had progressive acanthosis nigricans, structural anomalies of the central nervous system, seizures and severe developmental delay, in addition to the skeletal dysplasia. Cloverleaf skull and craniosynostosis were not present. ⚠︎

Zankl et al. (2008) described the prenatal and postnatal features of a patient with SADDAN. The patient had severe achondroplasia, developmental delay and acanthosis nigricans. Zankl et al. (2008) reviewed the clinical and radiologic features of all reported cases of SADDAN. They noted that theirs was one of only 6 cases in the literature with molecular analysis, and that it confirmed the severe morbidity of SADDAN and added to the reports of early mortality.

### Molecular Genetics

In 4 unrelated individuals with SADDAN, Tavormina et al. (1999) identified a novel missense mutation in the FGFR3 gene, a 1949A-T transversion resulting in a lys650-to-met (K650M) substitution. The mutation lies at the nucleotide adjacent to the thanatophoric dysplasia type II (TD2) mutation (1948A-G, lys650-to-glu). It produces a different amino acid substitution at a highly conserved codon in the activation loop of the kinase domain. In transient transfection studies with mutant FGFR3 constructs, the K650M mutation caused a dramatic increase in constitutive receptor kinase activity. This increase was about 3 times greater than that seen with the TD2 mutation lys650-to-glu.

Zankl et al. (2008) confirmed the clinical diagnosis of SADDAN in their patient by mutation analysis, which detected the FGFR3 K650M mutation.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Bellus et al. (1999) pointed out that the K650M mutation lies in the same FGFR3 codon as the thanatophoric dysplasia type II mutation lys650-to-glu (K650E). The 2 mutations have a similar effect in constitutively activating the FGFR3 tyrosine kinase. Yet K650M is not associated with cloverleaf skull or craniosynostosis.

### Pathogenesis

Iwata et al. (2001) noted a paradox: in vitro, FGFR3 carrying the SADDAN K650M mutation is more highly activated than FGFR3 carrying the TDII K650E mutation, yet TDII is lethal at birth whereas most SADDAN patients survive the perinatal period.

### Animal Model

Iwata et al. (2001) introduced into the mouse Fgfr3 gene the point mutation K644M, which corresponds to human FGFR3 K650M. Heterozygous mutant mice had a phenotype similar to human SADDAN, and most survived the perinatal period. This suggested that the survival of SADDAN patients is indeed attributable to the K650M mutation. The long bone abnormalities were milder than in the TDII mouse model. Cartilaginous tissues were overgrown in the rib cartilage, trachea and nasal septum. In primary chondrocyte cultures, a low concentration of FGF ligand activated MAP kinase differently in cells from wildtype and from SADDAN mice.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that common variable immunodeficiency-15 (CVID15) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the SEC61A1 gene (609213).

### Description

Common variable immunodeficiency-15 (CVID15) is an autosomal dominant primary antibody deficiency. Patients have low levels of IgM, IgG and IgA, or more broadly hypogammaglobulinemia, and recurrent bacterial infections of the respiratory and gastrointestinal tracts. Peripheral B- and T-cell subpopulations are normal, but circulating plasmablasts are reduced and B cells do not differentiate normally into plasma cells. Severity varies, ranging from transient hypogammaglobulinemia and specific antibody deficiency to CVID (Schubert et al., 2018). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Schubert et al. (2018) studied 10 affected individuals in a family with dominantly inherited antibody deficiency (Family I). They had isotype deficiencies of IgM, IgG and IgA. They had severe recurrent bacterial infections, including tonsillitis, otitis, sinusitis, pneumonias and gastrointestinal infections, which in most cases began in the first year of life. The affected individuals did not respond to polysaccharide vaccination, and their responses to tetanus toxin vaccination varied. Their peripheral B-cell and T-cell subpopulations were normal, but they had fewer circulating plasmablasts, and their B cells showed defective plasma cell differentiation in vitro.

Schubert et al. (2018) also described a second family (Family II). The index patient (II.P3) was an 8-year-old boy who had had hypogammaglobulinemia since birth. Because he was treated with IVIG, he had not had any severe or recurrent infections. His B cells showed defective plasma cell differentiation in vitro, but his peripheral B-cell subpopulations were normal. His mother also carried the mutation but had a secondary immunodeficiency and was not included in the study. His grandmother (II.P1), another carrier, reported more frequent upper respiratory infections in childhood, with recurrent bilateral bacterial otitis media. At age 66 her serum immunoglobulin levels were normal.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CVID15 in the families reported by Schubert et al. (2018) was consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. In Family I, the heterozygous mutation segregated with the disease in 10 affected individuals. In Family II, the mutation was passed from the grandmother through the mother to the index patient. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Schubert et al. (2018) performed genome-wide linkage analysis in Family I and found linkage to chromosome 3 between markers D3S3697 and D3S3684, with a peak LOD score of 3.31.

### Molecular Genetics

In the 10 affected members of Family I, Schubert et al. (2018) identified a heterozygous c.254T-A transversion in the SEC61A1 gene, resulting in a val85-to-asp (V85D) substitution. Sanger sequencing confirmed that the mutation segregated perfectly with the disease in the family. In HeLa cells, the V85D mutation impaired cotranslational translocation of preprolactin. Overexpressing the mutant reduced the calcium load of the endoplasmic reticulum, which the authors attributed to a dominant effect of increased calcium leakage through the channel. ⚠︎

Schubert et al. (2018) carried out targeted sequencing of SEC61A1 in more than 200 additional patients with primary antibody deficiency and found a nonsense mutation in 1 of them. They identified a heterozygous c.1325G-T transversion in SEC61A1, resulting in a glu381-to-ter (E381X) substitution, in the index patient of Family II (II.P3). His mother and grandmother (II.P1) also carried the mutation. The gnomAD database listed no premature stop mutations in SEC61A1 in healthy individuals.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Schubert et al. (2018) suggested that in Family II, SEC61A1 haploinsufficiency caused hypogammaglobulinemia that was possibly transient, with variable expressivity and incomplete penetrance. By contrast, they proposed that the dominant-negative V85D mutation may underlie the fully penetrant antibody deficiency in Family I, which ranged from transient hypogammaglobulinemia and specific antibody deficiency to CVID.

### Pathogenesis

Reimold et al. (2001) found that XBP1 transcripts were rapidly upregulated in vitro by stimuli that induce plasma cell differentiation. Introducing XBP1 into B-lineage cells initiated plasma cell differentiation. The authors concluded that XBP1 is selectively and specifically required for the terminal differentiation of B lymphocytes into plasma cells. Schubert et al. (2018) noted that SEC61A1 is a target gene of XBP1s. By proteomics, they found that SEC61A protein was about 2-fold more abundant in human peripheral blood plasmablasts than in naive and memory B cells.

Schubert et al. (2018) found that purified B cells from mutation carriers in both families differentiated into plasmablasts and plasma cells significantly less well in vitro and secreted less IgG and IgA. EBV-transformed B-cell lines from patients lacked the CD38-high/CD138-positive plasma cell subpopulation and secreted less immunoglobulin. Naive B cells from carriers of the E381X mutation had reduced SEC61A1 protein, and no truncated protein was detected. The authors concluded that the nonsense mutation likely causes nonsense-mediated mRNA decay and SEC61A1 haploinsufficiency. Stable expression of the V85D mutant caused GFP-positive cells to disappear from multiple myeloma cell lines but did not affect other cell lines. In myeloma cells, V85D activated the terminal unfolded protein response, with upregulation of PERK, ATF4 and CHOP. ⚠︎

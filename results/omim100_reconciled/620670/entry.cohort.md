### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that common variable immunodeficiency-15 (CVID15) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the SEC61A1 gene (609213) on chromosome 3q21.3.

### Description

Common variable immunodeficiency-15 (CVID15) is an autosomal dominant primary antibody deficiency. It is characterized by deficiency of several antibody isotypes and recurrent bacterial infections, beginning in infancy or childhood. Peripheral B-cell and T-cell subpopulations are normal, but circulating plasmablasts are reduced and B cells differentiate poorly into plasma cells in vitro. Severity varies from transient hypogammaglobulinemia and specific antibody deficiency to CVID (Schubert et al., 2018). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Schubert et al. (2018) studied 10 affected individuals in a family (Family I). They had deficiencies of the IgM, IgG and IgA antibody isotypes and severe recurrent bacterial infections, including tonsillitis, otitis, sinusitis, pneumonia and gastrointestinal infections. Onset was mostly in the first year of life. Affected individuals did not respond to polysaccharide vaccination and responded variably to tetanus toxin vaccination. Peripheral B-cell and T-cell subpopulations were normal, but circulating plasmablasts were reduced. Plasma cell differentiation was defective in vitro. ⚠︎

In a second family (Family II), Schubert et al. (2018) described an 8-year-old boy (II.P3) who had had hypogammaglobulinemia since birth. Because he was treated with IVIG, he had not had any severe or recurrent infections. His peripheral B-cell subpopulations were normal, and his B cells showed defective plasma cell differentiation in vitro. His mother also carried the mutation but had a secondary immunodeficiency and was not included in the study. His grandmother (II.P1) also carried the mutation. She reported more frequent upper respiratory infections during childhood, with recurrent bilateral bacterial otitis media, and at age 66 years she had normal serum immunoglobulin levels.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CVID15 in the families reported by Schubert et al. (2018) was consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. In Family II, penetrance was incomplete and expressivity was variable. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Schubert et al. (2018) performed genome-wide linkage analysis in Family I. They found linkage to chromosome 3 between markers D3S3697 and D3S3684, with a peak LOD score of 3.31.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of Family I with CVID15, Schubert et al. (2018) identified a heterozygous missense mutation in the SEC61A1 gene (c.254T-A; V85D) within the linkage region on chromosome 3. Sanger sequencing confirmed that the mutation segregated perfectly with the disease in the family. In HeLa cells, the V85D mutation impaired cotranslational translocation of preprolactin. Overexpression of the mutant lowered the calcium load of the endoplasmic reticulum, which the authors interpreted as a dominant effect caused by increased calcium leakage through the channel.

In the index patient of Family II, Schubert et al. (2018) identified a heterozygous nonsense mutation in SEC61A1 (c.1325G-T; E381X). His mother and grandmother also carried the mutation. In naive B cells from carriers, SEC61A1 protein was reduced and no truncated protein was detected. The authors concluded that the mutation likely causes nonsense-mediated mRNA decay and SEC61A1 haploinsufficiency. Targeted sequencing of SEC61A1 in more than 200 additional patients with primary antibody deficiency found a nonsense mutation in 1 of them. The gnomAD database listed no premature stop mutations in SEC61A1 in healthy individuals. ⚠︎

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Schubert et al. (2018) suggested that SEC61A1 haploinsufficiency underlies the possibly transient hypogammaglobulinemia in Family II, which showed variable expressivity and incomplete penetrance. In contrast, they proposed that the dominant-negative V85D mutation may underlie the fully penetrant antibody deficiency in Family I, which ranged from transient hypogammaglobulinemia and specific antibody deficiency to CVID.

### Pathogenesis

Schubert et al. (2018) found that purified B cells from mutation carriers in both families differentiated significantly less well into plasmablasts and plasma cells in vitro and secreted less IgG and IgA. EBV-transformed B-cell lines from patients lacked the CD38-high/CD138-positive plasma cell subpopulation and secreted less immunoglobulin. Stable expression of SEC61A1 V85D caused GFP-positive cells to disappear from multiple myeloma cell lines but did not affect other cell lines. In myeloma cells, the mutant activated the terminal unfolded protein response, with upregulation of PERK, ATF4 and CHOP.

Schubert et al. (2018) noted that SEC61A1 is a target gene of XBP1s. By proteomics, SEC61A protein was about 2-fold more abundant in human peripheral blood plasmablasts than in naive and memory B cells. Earlier, Reimold et al. (2001) had found that XBP1 transcripts were rapidly upregulated in vitro by stimuli that induce plasma cell differentiation. Introducing XBP1 into B-lineage cells initiated plasma cell differentiation. Mouse lymphoid chimeras deficient in Xbp1 had normal numbers of activated B lymphocytes and formed normal germinal centers. However, they secreted very little immunoglobulin of any isotype and failed to control infection with the B-cell-dependent polyoma virus, because plasma cells were markedly absent.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 IMMUNODEFICIENCY, COMMON VARIABLE, 15**  
SEC61A1, VAL85ASP [c.254T>A; p.V85D]

In 10 affected members of a family (Family I) with CVID15, Schubert et al. (2018) identified a heterozygous c.254T-A transversion in the SEC61A1 gene, resulting in a val85-to-asp (V85D) substitution. Sanger sequencing confirmed that the mutation segregated perfectly with the disease in the family, which showed linkage to chromosome 3 with a peak LOD score of 3.31. The patients had IgM, IgG and IgA deficiency and severe recurrent bacterial infections. In HeLa cells, the mutation impaired cotranslational translocation of preprolactin and lowered the calcium load of the endoplasmic reticulum. In multiple myeloma cell lines, expression of the mutant activated the terminal unfolded protein response.

SEC61A1, GLU381TER [c.1325G>T; p.E381*]

In an 8-year-old boy (II.P3) with hypogammaglobulinemia since birth, Schubert et al.

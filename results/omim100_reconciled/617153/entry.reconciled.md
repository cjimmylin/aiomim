### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GABRB1 gene (137190) on chromosome 4p12. ⚠︎

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is a seizure disorder with onset in infancy. Reported patients have had infantile spasms or epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures. One reported patient also had severe to profound developmental impairment and hypotonia.

### Clinical Features

Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) screened for de novo mutations in patients with 2 classic epileptic encephalopathies, infantile spasms (n = 149) and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome (n = 115). One of the patients carried a de novo mutation in the GABRB1 gene. Janve et al. (2016) later identified this patient as having infantile spasms.

Lien et al. (2016) reported a second patient with epileptic encephalopathy and a de novo GABRB1 mutation.

Burgess et al. (2019) studied a cohort of patients with epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (EIMFS). One of them (patient 63) had a de novo GABRB1 variant. Seizures began at 4 months of age with focal seizures. The patient was last reported at 2 years of age and had profound to severe developmental impairment and hypotonia. Burgess et al. (2019) noted that this finding expanded the phenotypic spectrum of GABA receptor subunit genes, including GABRB1, to include EIMFS. They emphasized a role for GABA-related mechanisms in EIMFS.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous GABRB1 mutations in patients with DEE45 occurred de novo, as reported by Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013), Janve et al. (2016), and Burgess et al. (2019). This is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) performed exome sequencing on 264 trios in which the child had infantile spasms or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome. They found de novo mutations in GABA-A receptor subunit genes in 6 patients: 4 in GABRB3 and 1 each in GABRA1 and GABRB1. The GABRB1 mutation was confirmed by Sanger sequencing and was absent from both parents. The authors suggested that individual rare mutations in different genes may converge on shared pathways. They also found significant genetic heterogeneity underlying infantile spasms and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, and concluded that it may be difficult to predict the responsible gene from the clinical presentation.

Janve et al. (2016) studied the de novo heterozygous GABRB1 mutation phe246-to-ser (F246S) in the patient with infantile spasms who was originally reported by Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013). In HEK293T cells, the mutation caused only a minor reduction (about 25%) in GABA-evoked peak current in the homozygous expression condition and no change in the heterozygous condition. However, it slowed current deactivation and increased single-channel mean open time and burst duration. Receptors containing the mutant subunit opened spontaneously, with a 4-fold increase in spontaneous open probability and increased holding currents. Surface levels of the mutant beta-1 subunit were slightly reduced, whereas total levels were unchanged.

Lien et al. (2016) identified a de novo GABRB1 mutation in a second patient with epileptic encephalopathy.

In a cohort of 135 patients with EIMFS, Burgess et al. (2019) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a de novo heterozygous missense variant in the GABRB1 gene in 1 patient. The variant (Chr4:47405633T-C) results in an ile247-to-thr (I247T) substitution. It was absent from the ExAC and gnomAD databases and was classified as likely pathogenic. GABRB1 was among 9 genes newly implicated in EIMFS.

### Pathogenesis

Janve et al. (2016) suggested that the faster rise time and slower deactivation caused by the GABRB1 F246S mutation would lengthen the first inhibitory postsynaptic current (IPSC) but reduce charge transfer during later IPSCs. The result would be a net loss of GABAergic inhibition. They suggested GABRB1 as a candidate human epilepsy gene.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type Ib (CDAN1B) is caused by homozygous mutation in the CDIN1 gene (615626) on chromosome 15q14.

### Description

Congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type Ib is an autosomal recessive form of congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I. It occurs in patients who do not carry mutations in the CDAN1 gene. Babbs et al. (2013) found homozygous mutations in the C15ORF41 gene (CDIN1) in these patients. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Sabry et al. (1997) described 3 Bedouin sibs with congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type 1 and dysmorphic features. The features included growth retardation/short stature, congenital ptosis, abnormal tarsal bones, metatarsal duplication/hypoplasia, nail/phalangeal hypoplasia of the fingers and toes, Madelung deformity, syndactyly of the toes, and hallux valgus. The patients' peripheral blood lymphocyte cultures also had a very low mitotic index. The phenotype varied among the 3 sibs. Sabry et al. (1997) concluded that their report confirmed an association between a subset of congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type 1 and a specific form of distal limb anomalies. They suggested that congenital ptosis and a low mitotic index might also be part of the syndrome. ⚠︎

Babbs et al. (2013) studied patients with congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I who did not carry CDAN1 mutations. They included 1 consanguineous pedigree of Middle-Eastern origin and 2 pedigrees of SouthEast Asian origin.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CDAN1B is autosomal recessive. Babbs et al. (2013) found homozygous mutations in the C15ORF41 gene in affected individuals, including in a consanguineous pedigree. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Ahmed et al. (2006) and Ahmed et al. (2006) reported evidence of genetic heterogeneity in congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I. The second of these studies was based on linkage and mutational analysis of the CDAN1 gene. ⚠︎

Babbs et al. (2013) noted that no likely pathogenic CDAN1 mutation is detected in approximately 20% of cases of congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I, which suggested at least one other locus. They identified C15ORF41 as such a locus.

### Molecular Genetics

Babbs et al. (2013) used whole genome sequencing and segregation analysis in a consanguineous pedigree of Middle-Eastern origin with congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I. They identified a homozygous c.533T-A transversion in the C15ORF41 gene (CDIN1), a gene of unknown function. The mutation was predicted to cause a leu178-to-gln (L178Q) substitution. They then sequenced C15ORF41 in other CDAN1 mutation-negative pedigrees with the disorder. In 2 further pedigrees of SouthEast Asian origin, they found a homozygous c.281A-G transition, predicted to cause a tyr94-to-cys (Y94C) substitution. Detailed sequence similarity searches predicted that C15ORF41 encodes a novel restriction endonuclease belonging to the Holliday junction resolvase family of proteins.

### Population Genetics

Babbs et al. (2013) found that the haplotype surrounding the C15ORF41 c.281A-G mutation suggested a founder effect for this mutation in Pakistan.

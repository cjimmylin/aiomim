### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Pallister-Hall-like syndrome (PHLS) is caused by biallelic germline mutation in the SMO gene (601500) on chromosome 7q32. ⚠︎

### Description

Pallister-Hall-like syndrome is a developmental disorder in which hypothalamic hamartoma occurs with polydactyly. Other reported features include microcephaly, atrioventricular septal defect, narrow chest, shortening of the long bones, and intestinal aganglionosis (Rubino et al., 2019; Le et al., 2020). ⚠︎

Craig et al. (2008) noted that hypothalamic hamartomas are rare, benign congenital tumors associated with intractable epilepsy. Most cases are sporadic and nonsyndromic, and approximately 5% are associated with Pallister-Hall syndrome.

### Nomenclature

Encha-Razavi et al. (1992) proposed that hypothalamic hamartoma with skeletal dysplasia and oro-facial abnormalities be treated as the minimum criteria for a distinct, possibly familial entity, which they called 'congenital hypothalamic hamartoma syndrome' (CHHS). In their analysis of reported cases of Pallister-Hall syndrome, skeletal dysplasia and oro-facial abnormalities were the only features that were always present. As far as they knew, isolated skeletal dysplasia with congenital hypothalamic hamartoma had not been reported before.

### Clinical Features

Marcuse et al. (1953) reported 2 cases of hamartoma of the hypothalamus with associated developmental defects.

Encha-Razavi et al. (1992) found congenital hypothalamic hamartomas at autopsy in 3 unrelated fetuses. In the first 2 fetuses, the tumor occurred with skeletal dysplasia only. In the third fetus, the tumor was part of a nonrandom association of congenital malformations suggestive of Meckel syndrome. In one of the families, a previous boy had died soon after birth with similar craniofacial and skeletal abnormalities.

Rubino et al. (2019) described 2 brothers with a familial syndrome of hypothalamic hamartomas and polydactyly.

Le et al. (2020) reported 7 individuals from 5 independent families with a wide range of developmental anomalies. These affected the brain (hypothalamic hamartoma and microcephaly), the heart (atrioventricular septal defect), the skeleton (postaxial polydactyly, narrow chest, and shortening of the long bones), and the enteric nervous system (aganglionosis).

### Inheritance

Encha-Razavi et al. (1992) suggested that congenital hypothalamic hamartoma syndrome might be familial. In one of their families, a previous boy had died soon after birth with similar craniofacial and skeletal abnormalities.

Rubino et al. (2019) reported affected brothers with germline SMO mutations. Le et al. (2020) found biallelic SMO variants in affected individuals from 5 families, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Rubino et al. (2019) stated that their SMO-associated familial syndrome was the first familial syndrome with hypothalamic hamartomas other than Pallister-Hall syndrome, which is caused by truncation of GLI3 (165240).

Craig et al. (2008) compared paired blood and resected tumor DNA from 55 patients with sporadic, nonsyndromic hypothalamic hamartoma and intractable epilepsy. Resequencing of GLI3 found no causative germline mutations. However, 8 of the 55 (15%) hamartoma tissue samples had somatic chromosomal abnormalities that included the GLI3 locus. One sample had a 7p copy-number abnormality. Eight samples had loss of heterozygosity within GLI3: 3 were found by resequencing and 5 more by genotyping 28 SNPs. Craig et al. (2008) concluded that chromosomal abnormalities within GLI3 are associated with the pathogenesis of hypothalamic hamartoma in sporadic, nonsyndromic patients, and noted that these somatic mutations appeared to be highly variable.

### Molecular Genetics

Rubino et al. (2019) found germline mutations in the SMO gene in 2 brothers with hypothalamic hamartomas and polydactyly. They stated that this was the first report of a familial syndrome caused by germline mutations in SMO.

Le et al. (2020) found biallelic loss-of-function variants in the SMO gene in 7 individuals from 5 unrelated families with developmental anomalies that included hypothalamic hamartoma, microcephaly, atrioventricular septal defect, postaxial polydactyly, narrow chest, shortened long bones, and aganglionosis.

### Pathogenesis

In cells from individuals with biallelic SMO variants, Le et al. (2020) found that ciliogenesis was normal but hedgehog signal transduction was severely altered. This resulted either from altered trafficking in the primary cilium or from abnormal activation of the pathway downstream of SMO. GLI2 accumulated at the cilium tip independently of hedgehog, suggesting that SMO may regulate basal ciliary trafficking of GLI2 when the pathway is off.

### Clinical Management

Rubino et al. (2019) described endoscopic endonasal biopsy and subtotal resection of a large hypothalamic hamartoma in 1 of 2 brothers with germline SMO mutations.

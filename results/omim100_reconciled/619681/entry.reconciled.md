### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia (DYTSPG) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the ATP5MC3 gene (602736) on chromosome 2q31.1. ⚠︎

### Description

Early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia (DYTSPG) is an autosomal dominant neurologic disorder with variable presentation. Affected individuals may have childhood-onset, progressive generalized dystonia or milder focal dystonia, including within a single family. Some patients also have developmental delay and pyramidal signs. Patient fibroblasts show reduced activity of mitochondrial complex V (ATP synthase).

### Clinical Features

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported a large multigenerational Ohio kindred with autosomal dominant hereditary spastic paraplegia (HSP) and dystonia. Spasticity and weakness began between the first year and the sixth decade of life, and dystonia between the first and third decades. Generalized dystonia was described in 1 affected child. The family had no ataxia and no apparent cognitive involvement. Neuroimaging and peripheral neurophysiology were normal.

Neilson et al. (2022) reported a family (Family-1) with an autosomal dominant pedigree of 40 individuals. Eighteen were affected with either childhood-onset, progressive generalized dystonia or adult-onset, gradually progressive spastic paraplegia with progressive leg weakness. Some family members had less severe, later-onset focal limb and neck dystonia and spasmodic dysphonia, which the authors suggested might be related to trauma or overuse. The authors stated that dystonia could generalize when it began in childhood, showed incomplete penetrance, and responded to pallidal deep brain stimulation. The spastic paraplegia began in adulthood (after age 20 years), progressed gradually, and was not accompanied by other neurologic problems. In the proband, dystonia began in one upper limb at age 2 years and generalized to the trunk and all limbs, leading to loss of ambulation. Brain MRI and peripheral nerve physiology were normal. Blood lactate and pyruvate were normal in the 2 individuals tested.

Neilson et al. (2022) also reported a second, unrelated patient (Family-2). He was a right-handed boy born to healthy, unrelated parents of European descent with no family history of dystonia or related movement disorders. He first presented at age 11 years with a 4-year history of gradually worsening upper limb dystonia. Brain MRI showed nonspecific, mild, bilateral frontoparietal white-matter changes without other abnormalities.

Zech et al. (2022) reported 3 unrelated patients with dystonia and/or spasticity. Patient 8 was the boy previously reported by Neilson et al. (2022); at 12 years of age he had isolated upper-limb dystonia. Patient 9 was a 15-year-old girl with a history of milestone delay, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia with prominent upper-body involvement. Patient 10 was a 6-year-old French boy with delayed psychomotor development, lower-extremity spasticity, and elevated blood lactate. Zech et al. (2022) combined these patients with the multigenerational family of Neilson et al. (2022). Across these individuals, dystonia was present in 12 of 18, spasticity or pyramidal signs in 17 of 18, and developmental delay or intellectual disability in 2 of 18. ⚠︎

### Biochemical Features

Neilson et al. (2022) studied fibroblasts from the Family-1 proband, who had dystonia, and his affected father, who had HSP. Activities of complexes I through IV were normal, but complex V activity was reduced by 20%. Cellular ATP was reduced by 20 to 32% and oxygen consumption by 21 to 25%. Because oxygen consumption and ATP fell in proportion, the results did not support uncoupling of oxidative phosphorylation.

Zech et al. (2022) found a slight but significant decrease in ATPase activity, as well as a reduced oxygen consumption rate, in fibroblasts from patient 8. Fibroblasts from patient 10 had deficient ATPase activity and a moderate decrease in assembled ATPase on blue native PAGE.

### Inheritance

Gilbert et al. (2009) described autosomal dominant, highly penetrant inheritance of HSP and dystonia in a large Ohio kindred. Neilson et al. (2022) also described an autosomal dominant pedigree, in which the ATP5MC3 mutation cosegregated with the disorder and dystonia showed incomplete penetrance. The mutations in the patients reported by Zech et al. (2022) occurred de novo.

### Mapping

Gilbert et al. (2009) performed single and multipoint linkage analysis with microsatellite and SNP array genotyping in a large multigenerational Ohio kindred with HSP and dystonia. They mapped the disease locus to chromosome 2q24-q31, between markers rs1424937 and rs1559510. The region lies proximal to SPG13 and contained no known HSP or dystonia genes. In a secondary analysis looking for candidate regions that segregated with the dystonia phenotype, they found 2 regions with parametric lod scores above 2.0.

### Molecular Genetics

Neilson et al. (2022) noted that linkage analysis in their large family (Family-1) had previously mapped the disorder to chromosome 2q24-q31. Whole-exome sequencing restricted to this region identified a heterozygous missense mutation in the ATP5MC3 gene (c.318C-G; N106K) as the only shared novel coding variant. Sanger sequencing showed that only affected family members carried the variant. In a second, unrelated patient (Family-2), trio whole-exome sequencing identified the same N106K mutation in the heterozygous state, occurring de novo. Fibroblasts from Family-1 patients showed reduced complex V activity, and Drosophila carrying the equivalent mutation had reduced mobility and reduced complex V activity. Screening of ATP5MC1, ATP5MC2, and ATP5MC3 in 384 patients with dystonia (n = 362) or HSP (n = 22) found no deleterious variants.

Zech et al. (2022) identified de novo heterozygous missense mutations in the ATP5MC3 gene in 3 unrelated patients with dystonia and/or spasticity: N106K in patient 8 (the patient previously reported by Neilson et al., 2022), P107A in patient 9, and G79V in patient 10. All 3 variants affected strictly conserved residues in the C-terminal helical motifs of the protein and were absent from gnomAD. Fibroblasts from patients 8 and 10 showed reduced ATPase activity.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Zech et al. (2022) found that the ATP5MC3 G79V mutation caused a significantly greater ATPase enzyme deficit than the N106K mutation. G79V was associated with a more severe neurodevelopmental presentation with spasticity, whereas N106K was associated with isolated dystonia. The authors cautioned that further studies were needed before genotype-phenotype correlations could be clearly postulated.

### Pathogenesis

Neilson et al. (2022) noted that 2 paralogs, ATP5MC1 and ATP5MC2, also encode subunit c, so haploinsufficiency of ATP5MC3 would be unlikely to explain the severe phenotypes. They hypothesized that the mutant protein interferes with the F1-F0 interaction at a conserved loop. They suggested that relative expression of the mutant gene in different tissues may influence whether dystonia or spastic paraplegia develops.

Zech et al. (2022) suggested that dominant-negative effects could underlie the disease caused by ATP5MC3 mutations. This was supported by mild to moderate defects in ATPase assembly and/or enzyme activity in patient cell lines. Structural modeling placed G79V at an interhelical contact site and the N106 and P107 residues at the site of interaction between the FO and F1 subcomplexes.

### Clinical Management

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported that deep brain stimulation markedly improved generalized dystonia in 1 affected child.

Neilson et al. (2022) reported that bilateral pallidal deep brain stimulation, implanted at age 9 years, restored ambulation and limb use in the Family-1 proband with generalized dystonia. The response remained excellent at age 25 years.

### Animal Model

Neilson et al. (2022) expressed the Drosophila ATPsynC (CG1746) homolog carrying either the mutation equivalent to human N106K (N102K) or the mutation equivalent to an E. coli change (N102E), using the arm-GAL4 driver. Both mutant lines showed reduced mobility in geotaxis assays, with N102K flies the slowest. Complex V activity was reduced by 53% in N102K flies and by 37% in N102E flies.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, ASN106LYS [c.318C>G; g.176043127G>C; p.N106K]

In affected members of a large family (Family-1) with autosomal dominant dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia, Neilson et al. (2022) identified a heterozygous c.318C-G transversion (NM_001689.4) in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in an asn106-to-lys (N106K) substitution. The family had 18 affected members, with childhood-onset generalized dystonia, adult-onset spastic paraplegia, or milder focal dystonia. The mutation was found by whole-exome sequencing restricted to the linked region on chromosome 2q24-q31, and Sanger sequencing showed that only affected individuals carried it. Fibroblasts from 2 affected family members showed a 20% reduction in complex V activity, with reduced cellular ATP and oxygen consumption. Drosophila carrying the equivalent mutation showed reduced mobility and reduced complex V activity. In an unrelated boy of European descent (Family-2) with upper limb dystonia beginning at age 7 years, Neilson et al. (2022) identified the same N106K mutation in the heterozygous state by trio whole-exome sequencing; it had occurred de novo.

Zech et al. (2022) reported further on the Family-2 patient (patient 8), who at 12 years of age had isolated upper-limb dystonia and carried the de novo heterozygous N106K mutation. His fibroblasts showed a slight but significant decrease in ATPase activity and a reduced oxygen consumption rate. The mutation affected a strictly conserved residue and was absent from gnomAD.

**.0002 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, PRO107ALA [c.319C>G; p.P107A]

In a 15-year-old girl (patient 9) with a history of milestone delay, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia with prominent upper-body involvement, Zech et al. (2022) identified a de novo heterozygous c.319C-G transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in a pro107-to-ala (P107A) substitution. The variant affected a strictly conserved residue and was absent from gnomAD. Structural modeling placed P107 at the site of interaction between the FO and F1 subcomplexes.

**.0003 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, GLY79VAL [c.236G>T; p.G79V]

In a 6-year-old French boy (patient 10) with delayed psychomotor development, lower-extremity spasticity, and elevated blood lactate, Zech et al. (2022) identified a de novo heterozygous c.236G-T transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in a gly79-to-val (G79V) substitution. The variant affected a strictly conserved residue and was absent from gnomAD. Patient fibroblasts showed deficient ATPase activity and a moderate decrease in assembled ATPase on blue native PAGE. The ATPase deficit was significantly greater than that caused by the N106K mutation. ⚠︎

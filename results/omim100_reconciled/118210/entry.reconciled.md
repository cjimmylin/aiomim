### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2A1 (CMT2A1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the KIF1B gene (605995) on chromosome 1p36.22. ⚠︎

### Description

Xu et al. (2018) gave the prevalence of Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease (CMT) as 1:2,500. Ben Othmane et al. (1993) described CMT2 as an inherited peripheral neuropathy with a variable age of onset and normal or slightly reduced nerve conduction velocity, distinct from CMT1 both pathologically and genetically.

### Clinical Features

Saito et al. (1997) described 2 Japanese families with CMT2 linked to chromosome 1p. In both families, affected individuals had a similar degree of weakness in the posterior and anterior tibial muscles. Tendon reflexes were preserved in the upper extremities but reduced or absent in the lower extremities. The peripheral nerves were not enlarged. Only 20% of affected individuals had a sensory disturbance, and it was mild.

Xu et al. (2018) reported 2 independent pedigrees with hereditary neuropathy, diagnosed at the Neuromuscular Clinic of the Penn State Hershey Medical Center and the Hamburg Center. Patient 1 was a left-handed Caucasian man diagnosed with CMT2 at age 55 years. He had a progressive, symmetric, length-dependent, predominantly axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy with acute and chronic features. He also had intermittent cranial nerve involvement, including Bell palsy, and hearing loss. Brain MRI was normal. The right radial nerve showed a mixed axonal and demyelinating neuropathy, and the median nerve conduction velocity was 38.9 m/s. His mother, 3 brothers and 3 sisters had no symptoms. His father had died in an accident at a young age.

Patient 2 of Xu et al. (2018) was the proband of an American pedigree. He was a left-handed Caucasian man diagnosed with autosomal dominant CMT2 at age 40 years and was 43 years old at the time of the report. Electromyography and nerve conduction studies showed a predominantly length-dependent axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy. He also had mental retardation, bipolar disorder, hypertension, cystic kidney disease and a lacune in the right basal ganglia. Brain MRI showed thinning of the posterior half of the corpus callosum. His 48-year-old brother had axonal CMT disease together with mental retardation, psychiatric disease and kidney disease. Their mother had a history of CMT disease and mild cognitive dysfunction and died in her sixties. Their father was asymptomatic and healthy at age 72 years.

### Inheritance

Ben Othmane et al. (1993) mapped the CMT2A locus by studying families with autosomal dominant CMT2. In the American pedigree reported by Xu et al. (2018), the disease was transmitted in an autosomal dominant pattern: a heterozygous KIF1B mutation was found in 2 affected brothers, and their mother had clinically diagnosed CMT2.

### Mapping

Hentati et al. (1992) studied 2 CMT2 pedigrees and excluded the CMT2 locus from the CMT1A region on chromosome 17 (lod score -2.80 at theta = 0.05 for D17S58). They also excluded it from the CMT1B region on chromosome 1 (lod score -3.09 at theta = 0.05 for D1S61). In the same study, a CMT1 pedigree showed linkage to the CMT1A region (lod score +3.67 at theta = 0.00).

Ben Othmane et al. (1993) carried out linkage analysis in 6 large families with autosomal dominant CMT2. They found linkage to microsatellite markers on the distal short arm of chromosome 1 (D1S160, D1S170, D1S244, D1S228 and D1S199). Three families, DUK662, DUK1241 and 1523, had posterior probabilities of 1.0, 0.98 and 0.88, respectively, of belonging to the linked type. Multipoint analysis of the linked families placed the CMT2A gene most likely in the interval flanked by D1S244 and D1S228, with odds of about 70:1 that it lay within rather than outside this interval.

In 2 Japanese families with CMT2, Saito et al. (1997) found linkage to chromosome 1p, with a highest cumulative multipoint lod score of 3.69 at D1S244. They excluded the CMT2B and CMT2D loci using markers D3S1551, D3S1290 and D7S484.

### Heterogeneity

Hentati et al. (1992) concluded that the hypertrophic (CMT1) and neuronal (CMT2) forms of Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease are genetically heterogeneous. The CMT2 gene was not allelic to either of the CMT1 genes mapped at that time.

Using admixture analysis, Ben Othmane et al. (1993) found significant evidence for genetic heterogeneity. Only 3 of their 6 CMT2 families had high posterior probabilities of linkage to 1p, suggesting that the CMT2 phenotype is caused by at least 2 different genes. ⚠︎

Xu et al. (2018) noted that a KIF1B mutation was the first mutation identified in a pedigree with CMT2A, but only a few pedigrees with a KIF1Bbeta mutation causing CMT2A1 had been reported. In contrast, MFN2 mutations cause CMT2A2, the most common known axonal form. MFN2 and more than 30 related genes were intact in the CMT2A pedigrees that Xu et al. (2018) identified.

### Molecular Genetics

Zhao et al. (2001) described KIF1Bbeta, an isoform of the kinesin motor KIF1B whose cargo-binding domain differs from that of the mitochondria-transporting KIF1B. They found that patients with CMT2A carried a loss-of-function mutation in the motor domain of the KIF1B gene.

Xu et al. (2018) studied 2 independent pedigrees with hereditary neuropathy by exome sequencing of 33 CMT2/neuropathy-related genes, including MFN2. In affected individuals from both pedigrees, they identified a heterozygous 3260A-G transition in the KIF1B gene (chr1:10387567), resulting in a tyr1087-to-cys (Y1087C) substitution in KIF1Bbeta. According to the in silico programs PP2 and SIFT, this was the only variant in the 33 genes with the capacity to cause disease. In the American pedigree, the mutation was found in the proband and in his affected brother. The authors reported a minor allele frequency of 0.0395 for the variant in the 1000 Genomes database. In functional studies, the Y1087C mutation reduced binding of KIF1Bbeta to IGF1R. In Kif1b-null mouse neurons, the mutant protein failed to rescue axonal outgrowth or anterograde IGF1R transport (see PATHOGENESIS).

### Pathogenesis

Zhao et al. (2001) concluded that their findings clearly showed that defective axonal transport caused by a mutated motor protein can underlie human peripheral neuropathy.

Xu et al. (2018) identified the receptor tyrosine kinase IGF1R as a direct binding partner of KIF1Bbeta. The intracellular domain of IGF1Rbeta bound a minimal region of the KIF1Bbeta stalk domain spanning residues 885 to 1,410. The authors showed the interaction by yeast 2-hybrid analysis, by immunoprecipitation of vesicles from Kif1bbeta-EGFP knockin mouse brain, by Nycodenz vesicle coflotation and by GST pulldown of purified proteins. Live imaging showed the 2 proteins comigrating at about 1 micrometer/s. IGF1R did not bind the corresponding stalk region of KIF1A, which shares 65% sequence identity with this part of KIF1Bbeta. Just before the core-binding residue Tyr1087, the authors found a stretch of 20 amino acids that is not conserved between the 2 proteins. By superresolution microscopy, DENN/MADD and IGF1R scarcely colocalized, indicating that IGF1R is transported independently of synaptic vesicle precursors.

Xu et al. (2018) performed GST pulldown assays on mouse brain lysates. The Y1087C mutation lies in the middle of the minimal IGF1R-binding sequence, and it significantly reduced binding of the KIF1Bbeta 885-1,410 fragment to both IGF1R subunits. Binding to the synaptic vesicle precursor protein synaptophysin and to the adaptor DENN/MADD was unchanged. Rescue experiments used Kif1b-null mouse hippocampal neurons. Wildtype mouse KIF1Bbeta restored axonal outgrowth and axonal surface expression of IGF1R. In contrast, KIF1Bbeta-Y1087C, KIF1Bbeta-Q98L, KIF1Balpha and KIF1A significantly failed to complement. After IGF-I stimulation, neurons expressing Y1087C had significantly lower pAkt levels along the axon. Live imaging showed that wildtype KIF1Bbeta, but not KIF1Bbeta-Y1087C, rescued the number of anterogradely transported IGF1R-containing vesicles. With Y1087C, the proportion of KIF1Bbeta vesicles comigrating with IGF1R was significantly reduced, but motor velocity was unaffected. The authors concluded that the mutation at least increases susceptibility to neuropathy by reducing IGF-I signal transduction.

### Animal Model

Zhao et al. (2001) found that Kif1b knockout mice died at birth from apnea caused by defects of the nervous system. In culture, the death of knockout neurons could be rescued by expressing the beta isoform. Kif1b heterozygous mice had defective transport of synaptic vesicle precursors and developed progressive muscle weakness resembling human neuropathies.

Xu et al. (2018) found that axonal outgrowth was significantly impaired at DIV2-3 in dissociated hippocampal neurons from Kif1b-null mice. Knockdown with isoform-specific miRNA vectors showed that the beta isoform of KIF1B was specifically responsible. Overall apoptosis was higher in Kif1b-null neurons than in wildtype neurons, but TUNEL staining showed that neurite-bearing Kif1b-null neurons were mostly not apoptotic. Surface and axonal IGF1R levels were significantly reduced in Kif1b-null hippocampal and cortical neurons. Kif1b-null neurons did not elongate their axons in response to IGF-I stimulation, and IGF-I-induced increases in pAkt and pERK were impaired. Constitutively active Ras V12 significantly rescued the deficit in axonal outgrowth. In transfected hippocampal neurons, anterograde transport of IGF1R was lower in Kif1b +/- neurons than in wildtype neurons and was scarcely detectable in Kif1b -/- neurons. The authors concluded that IGF1R depends on KIF1Bbeta for transport down the axon.

**.0002 CHARCOT-MARIE-TOOTH DISEASE, AXONAL, TYPE 2A1**  
KIF1B, TYR1087CYS [c.3260A>G; p.Y1087C]

Xu et al. (2018) studied 2 independent pedigrees with hereditary neuropathy. Patient 1 was a 55-year-old Caucasian man with a predominantly axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy and cranial nerve involvement. Patient 2 was a 43-year-old American Caucasian man with an axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy, mental retardation and cystic kidney disease, and his 48-year-old brother was also affected. In these patients the authors identified a heterozygous 3260A-G transition in the KIF1B gene, resulting in a tyr1087-to-cys (Y1087C) substitution in KIF1Bbeta. The mutation was found by exome sequencing of 33 neuropathy-related genes and was the only variant that PP2 and SIFT predicted to be capable of causing disease. The authors reported a 1000 Genomes minor allele frequency of 0.0395. The mutation significantly reduced binding of KIF1Bbeta to IGF1R. In Kif1b-null mouse hippocampal neurons, the mutant protein failed to rescue axonal outgrowth, axonal surface IGF1R expression or anterograde transport of IGF1R vesicles.

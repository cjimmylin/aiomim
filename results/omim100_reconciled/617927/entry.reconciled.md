### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII (OFD18) is caused by homozygous mutation in the IFT57 gene (606621) on chromosome 3q13.

### Description

Orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII (OFD18) is a form of oral-facial-digital syndrome. Its oral, facial and digital malformations occur together with acromelic short stature and genu valgum, and some of its features overlap with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome (Phadke et al., 1999). Thevenon et al. (2016) classified it as an unclassified OFDS with skeletal dysplasia, marked by short stature, brachymesophalangia and stocky femoral necks, without rib shortening or thoracic narrowing.

### Clinical Features

Phadke et al. (1999) reported a consanguineous family with what they described as a new variety of oral-facial-digital syndrome (OFDS). The affected members had oral, facial and digital malformations together with acromelic short stature and genu valgum, features that overlap with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome. One of the sibs had urinary incontinence and growth hormone deficiency. The authors noted that neither finding had been reported before in any type of OFDS.

Thevenon et al. (2016) restudied the consanguineous Indian family first reported by Phadke et al. (1999), in which 3 sibs were affected. Subject II-1, a male, was examined at 25 years of age. He had normal intelligence. His final height was 155 cm (-3 SD), his weight 45 kg (less than -3 SD) and his occipitofrontal circumference 50 cm (mean). His facial features included a square face, short forehead, upslanting palpebral fissures, high nasal bridge, short philtrum and medial clefting of the upper lip. His oral anomalies were supernumerary frenulae and missing incisors. His digital anomalies were pronounced brachydactyly, upper-limb postaxial polydactyly and severe mesophalangeal shortening, and his feet showed preaxial polydactyly with first metatarsal duplication and a gap between the first and second toes. Bone studies showed bilateral short femoral necks with varus femurs and symmetric genu valgum. He had no thoracic deformity and no vertebral anomalies. Heart, renal and abdominal ultrasound examinations were normal. ⚠︎

Subject II-2, a female, was examined at 22 years of age (Thevenon et al., 2016). She had normal intelligence and was married with no children. Her final height was 122 cm (-5 SD), her weight 37 kg (-2 SD) and her occipitofrontal circumference 51 cm (mean). Her facial features included a square face, narrow forehead, enophthalmia, upslanting palpebral fissures, broad nasal bridge, bilateral hypopigmented pseudoclefts and medial labial clefting. She had missing incisors and supernumerary frenulae. Her digital anomalies were marked brachydactyly, four-limb postaxial polydactyly that was surgically removed in infancy, mesophalangeal shortening and bifid terminal phalanges. Her feet showed first metatarsal duplication and absent intermediate phalanges of the toes. Skeletal studies showed vertebral bodies with medial notches but no platyspondyly, bilateral cervical ribs without a narrow thorax, gracile long bones and shortened femoral necks. Puberty was not delayed, and brain MRI was normal. The third sib, subject II-3, died at 17 years of age of a complicated pulmonary infection.

Phadke et al. (1999) had regarded this family as an OFDS-Ellis-van Creveld syndrome overlap. Thevenon et al. (2016) reclassified it as an unclassified OFDS with skeletal dysplasia, because the patients had no rib shortening or thoracic narrowing but did have short stature, brachymesophalangia and stocky femoral necks.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OFD18 is autosomal recessive. Phadke et al. (1999) described the disorder in sibs born to consanguineous parents. Thevenon et al. (2016) found a homozygous IFT57 mutation in all 3 affected sibs of this family.

### Mapping

Thevenon et al. (2016) performed homozygosity mapping in affected sibs of the consanguineous Indian family first reported by Phadke et al. (1999). The sibs shared runs of homozygosity totaling 11 Mb, located on chromosome 3 (chr3:104822019-111671862) and chromosome 10 (chr10:1372501-6029726). Segregation analysis in the third sib excluded the chromosome 10 interval.

### Heterogeneity

Thevenon et al. (2016) found no causative IFT57 mutations by Sanger sequencing in 13 subjects with OFDS or in 12 subjects with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome who were negative for EVC1/EVC2 mutations. They also found no biallelic IFT57 mutations in exome or ciliome data from 172 subjects with various ciliopathies.

### Molecular Genetics

Thevenon et al. (2016) studied the consanguineous Indian family first reported by Phadke et al. (1999). In the region of homozygosity on chromosome 3, they identified a homozygous synonymous c.777G-A transition (lys259-to-lys; K259K) in the IFT57 gene, which affects the last base of exon 6. Sanger sequencing confirmed that all 3 affected sibs were homozygous for the variant. The variant was not present in the ExAC database or in 50 northern Indian controls. In patient fibroblasts, the variant produced a complex splicing pattern: the normal transcript was present, along with 3 aberrant transcripts lacking exon 6, exons 5-6 or exons 4-6, which could be detected only after inhibition of nonsense-mediated mRNA decay. IFT57 mRNA levels were also reduced. The authors considered the mutation hypomorphic.

### Pathogenesis

Thevenon et al. (2016) found that primary cilia were normal in number and length in fibroblasts from patients with the IFT57 mutation. However, IFT57 staining at the ciliary tip was reduced (35% of cilia, compared with 90% in controls) and basal body staining was increased, indicating defective anterograde ciliary transport. Stimulation with SAG induced GLI1 only poorly in patient fibroblasts, and siRNA knockdown of IFT57 in control fibroblasts abolished GLI1 induction. These results indicated impaired sonic hedgehog (SHH) signaling.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 OROFACIODIGITAL SYNDROME XVIII**  
IFT57, 777G-A [c.777G>A; p.K259K]

Thevenon et al. (2016) studied 3 affected sibs from a consanguineous Indian family with OFD18, first reported by Phadke et al. (1999). All 3 were homozygous for a c.777G-A transition in the IFT57 gene, a synonymous change (lys259-to-lys; K259K) at the last base of exon 6, and Sanger sequencing confirmed the homozygous variant in each of them. The variant was not present in ExAC or in 50 northern Indian controls. In patient fibroblasts, the variant produced normal transcript plus 3 aberrant transcripts that skipped exon 6, exons 5-6 or exons 4-6. The aberrant transcripts could be detected only after inhibition of nonsense-mediated decay, and IFT57 mRNA levels were reduced. Patient fibroblasts showed reduced IFT57 at the ciliary tip and poor GLI1 induction after SAG stimulation.

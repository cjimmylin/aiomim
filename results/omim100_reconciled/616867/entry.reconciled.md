### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spinal muscular atrophy with congenital bone fractures-2 (SMABF2) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the ASCC1 gene (614215) on chromosome 10q22.1. ⚠︎

### Description

Spinal muscular atrophy with congenital bone fractures-2 is a severe autosomal recessive neuromuscular disorder that starts before birth. Features include prenatal-onset muscle weakness, neonatal hypotonia, arthrogryposis, congenital bone fractures, and respiratory insufficiency. Oliveira et al. (2017) placed the disorder within the spectrum of spinal muscular atrophy or primary muscle disease. ASCC1 encodes a subunit of the activating signal cointegrator-1 (ASC-1) complex, a transcriptional coactivator made of 4 subunits: ASCC1, ASCC2, ASCC3, and TRIP4 (Böhm et al., 2019). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Knierim et al. (2016) studied 4 families whose affected members had prenatal-onset spinal muscular atrophy (SMA), multiple congenital contractures (arthrogryposis multiplex congenita), respiratory distress, and congenital bone fractures. Affected individuals in these families carried mutations in either TRIP4 or ASCC1.

Oliveira et al. (2017) reported a female newborn with a severe neuromuscular disorder. She had generalized hypotonia, congenital bone fractures, no spontaneous movements, and poor respiratory effort, and she died within the first days of life. Her male sib was born later with the same clinical presentation.

Böhm et al. (2019) gave a clinical, histologic, and genetic description of 3 further families with ASCC1 mutations. All patients had severe prenatal-onset muscle weakness, neonatal hypotonia, arthrogryposis, and congenital bone fractures. Muscle biopsies from affected infants showed intense oxidative rims beneath the sarcolemma and scattered sarcomere remnants with enlarged Z-bands. The authors suggested that these findings may be a histopathologic hallmark of the disorder.

Giuffrida et al. (2020) described a stillborn infant with SMABF2 and documented how the disease evolved during the prenatal period. A chromosomal microarray was requested because of arthrogryposis and hydrops.

### Inheritance

SMABF2 is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. Knierim et al. (2016) found homozygous or compound heterozygous mutations that cosegregated with the disease. Oliveira et al. (2017) and Giuffrida et al. (2020) found homozygous and compound heterozygous ASCC1 mutations, respectively, in affected individuals.

### Heterogeneity

Knierim et al. (2016) found mutations in either TRIP4 or ASCC1, both subunits of the ASC-1 complex, in families with prenatal-onset SMA, arthrogryposis, respiratory distress, and congenital bone fractures. Oliveira et al. (2017) noted that variants in TRIP4 cause a similar disorder.

### Molecular Genetics

Knierim et al. (2016) used autozygosity mapping and whole-exome sequencing in 4 families with prenatal-onset SMA and congenital bone fractures. They identified homozygous and compound heterozygous nonsense and frameshift mutations in TRIP4 (604501) or ASCC1. The mutations led to truncation or complete absence of the respective proteins and cosegregated with the disease phenotype.

Oliveira et al. (2017) performed whole-exome sequencing under a recessive model in a female newborn with SMABF2. They found no disease-causing variants in genes known to cause congenital myopathies, muscle diseases, or other neuromuscular disorders. They did identify a homozygous frameshift mutation in the ASCC1 gene (c.157dupG; Glu53GlyfsTer19), which made her the second reported case.

In 3 families with SMABF2, Böhm et al. (2019) identified recessive nonsense or frameshift mutations in the ASCC1 gene, 2 of which were novel.

In a stillborn infant with SMABF2, Giuffrida et al. (2020) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the ASCC1 gene. Microarray analysis first detected a microdeletion of about 64 kb that removed 4 exons of ASCC1. Exome analysis then found a c.1027C-T transition that produced an arg343-to-ter (R343X) substitution, introducing a premature termination codon. The authors stated that this was the first reported case of ASCC1 compound heterozygosity due to an exonic microdeletion together with a nucleotide variant. They suggested that exonic copy number variants are an underestimated cause of disease alleles.

### Pathogenesis

Knierim et al. (2016) found that ASCC1-mutant fibroblasts downregulated genes linked to neurogenesis, neuronal migration, and pathfinding (SERPINF1, DAB1, SEMA3D, and SEMA3A) and genes linked to bone development (TNFRSF11B, RASSF2, and STC1). Immunoprecipitation of the ASC-1 complex consistently copurified the transcriptional cofactor CSRP1. In 17.5-day mouse embryos, Trip4 and Ascc1 had identical expression patterns, with high expression in spinal cord, brain, paraspinal ganglia, thyroid, and submandibular glands. The authors concluded that dysfunction of a transcriptional coactivator complex can cause a syndrome affecting the neuromuscular system.

Böhm et al. (2019) concluded that their findings point to an important role for the ASC-1 complex in fetal muscle and bone development.

### Diagnosis

In the patient reported by Oliveira et al. (2017), karyotyping and screening of several neuromuscular disease genes were negative before exome sequencing. In the stillborn infant reported by Giuffrida et al. (2020), the ASCC1 deletion was first found by microarray analysis, and the second mutation was then found by exome analysis.

### Animal Model

Knierim et al. (2016) found that morpholino knockdown of either trip4 or ascc1 in zebrafish disrupted alpha-motoneuron outgrowth and the formation of myotomes and neuromuscular junctions, and caused a swimming defect in larvae.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 SPINAL MUSCULAR ATROPHY WITH CONGENITAL BONE FRACTURES 2**  
ASCC1, 1-BP DUP, 157G [c.157dupg; p.E53G]

Oliveira et al. (2017) identified a homozygous 1-bp duplication (c.157dupG) in the ASCC1 gene, causing a frameshift (Glu53GlyfsTer19), in a female newborn with SMABF2. She had generalized hypotonia, congenital bone fractures, no spontaneous movements, and poor respiratory effort, and she died within the first days of life. The variant was found by whole-exome sequencing under a recessive model, after no disease-causing variants were found in known neuromuscular disease genes.

**.0002 SPINAL MUSCULAR ATROPHY WITH CONGENITAL BONE FRACTURES 2**  
ASCC1, 64-KB DEL

In a stillborn infant with SMABF2, arthrogryposis, and hydrops, Giuffrida et al. (2020) used microarray analysis to detect a microdeletion of about 64 kb removing 4 exons of the ASCC1 gene. It was in compound heterozygosity with a nonsense mutation (R343X). The authors stated that this was the first case of ASCC1 compound heterozygosity involving an exonic microdeletion.

**.0003 SPINAL MUSCULAR ATROPHY WITH CONGENITAL BONE FRACTURES 2**  
ASCC1, ARG343TER [c.1027C>T; p.R343*]

In a stillborn infant with SMABF2, arthrogryposis, and hydrops, Giuffrida et al. (2020) used exome analysis to identify a c.1027C-T transition in the ASCC1 gene, resulting in an arg343-to-ter (R343X) substitution and a premature termination codon. It was in compound heterozygosity with a 64-kb exonic deletion of ASCC1.

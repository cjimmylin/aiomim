## NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER WITH HYPOTONIA, POOR GROWTH, DYSMORPHIC FACIES, AND AGAMMAGLOBULINEMIA; NEDHGFA

### TEXT

This entry describes neurodevelopmental disorder with hypotonia, poor growth, dysmorphic facies, and agammaglobulinemia (NEDHGFA); the evidence available here consists only of studies in mice and mouse cells and does not establish a causal gene in patients.

### Animal Model

Ji et al. (2016) found that mice with B cell-specific deletion of Sel1l (Sel1L-CD19) looked healthy and grew normally. However, they had smaller spleens and 60 to 75% fewer peripheral B cells in spleen, blood, and lymph nodes. T cells and myeloid cells were unaffected. B cell development was severely blocked at the transition from large to small pre-B cells. This block did not depend on Chop and was not explained by increased cell death. Instead, large pre-B cells kept proliferating, with more than 2.5-fold more proliferation of lambda-5-positive large pre-B cells.

Ji et al. (2016) showed that the Sel1L-Hrd1 endoplasmic reticulum-associated degradation (ERAD) complex recognized the pre-B cell receptor (pre-BCR) complex and targeted it for ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation in a BiP-dependent manner. Without Sel1L, the pre-BCR built up inside the cell and at the cell surface, and pre-BCR signaling persisted. Sel1L-Hrd1 ERAD did not degrade the mature BCR. In 70z/3 pre-B cells, loss of Sel1L or Hrd1 raised the levels of all 3 pre-BCR components and stabilized the Ig-mu heavy chain, extending its half-life from about 60 minutes to more than 120 minutes. Loss of Sel1L or Hrd1 also increased phosphorylation of Syk, a kinase downstream of the pre-BCR.

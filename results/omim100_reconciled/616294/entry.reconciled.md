### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Cole-Carpenter syndrome-2 (CLCRP2) is caused by compound heterozygous mutation in the SEC24D gene (607186) on chromosome 4q26.

### Description

Cole-Carpenter syndrome-2 is an autosomal recessive syndromic form of osteogenesis imperfecta. Its main features are bone fragility with multiple fractures, which can begin before birth, and severely disturbed ossification of the skull. Craniofacial malformations include macrocephaly, frontal bossing, midface hypoplasia and micrognathia, and patients can also have short stature (Garbes et al., 2015; Takeyari et al., 2018). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Garbes et al. (2015) reported 3 affected individuals from 2 families with a similar skeletal phenotype. One was a 7-year-old boy and the other 2 were fetuses from 1 family, initially suspected to have a severe type of osteogenesis imperfecta. All 3 had severely disturbed ossification of the skull and multiple fractures that began before birth. The boy had short stature and craniofacial malformations including macrocephaly, midface hypoplasia, micrognathia, frontal bossing, and down-slanting palpebral fissures. Garbes et al. (2015) noted that cranio-lenticulo-sutural dysplasia (CLSD) has a largely overlapping craniofacial phenotype but lacks generalized bone fragility, and that cataracts were present in the original CLSD family. ⚠︎

Zhang et al. (2017) reported 2 Chinese families with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta. The proband in family 1 was a 23-year-old man with recurrent fractures and dentinogenesis imperfecta. His anterior fontanel had not closed, and he showed facial dysmorphism. Three-dimensional computed tomography of the cranium showed skull deformities with a broad ossification defect in the frontoapical area, a widened sagittal suture, and Wormian bones. The proband in family 2 was a 7-year-old boy who also had recurrent fractures, dentinogenesis imperfecta and an unclosed anterior fontanel, but no obvious facial dysmorphism.

Takeyari et al. (2018) described a 15-year-old Japanese boy with Cole-Carpenter syndrome type 2. He had short-trunk short stature and craniofacial abnormalities including ocular proptosis, marked frontal bossing, midface hypoplasia, and micrognathia. He also had low bone mineral density and basilar impression.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of Cole-Carpenter syndrome-2 is autosomal recessive. Garbes et al. (2015), Zhang et al. (2017), and Takeyari et al. (2018) found affected individuals to be compound heterozygous for SEC24D mutations, and Moosa et al. (2016) reported that SEC24D mutations cause autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Garbes et al. (2015) used whole-exome sequencing to study 3 individuals from 2 families with a syndromic form of osteogenesis imperfecta. They identified 3 mutant alleles of the SEC24D gene: a truncating c.613C-T mutation (gln205-to-ter; Q205X); a c.3044C-T missense mutation (ser1015-to-phe; S1015F), located in a cargo-binding pocket; and a c.2933A-C missense mutation (gln978-to-pro; Q978P), located in the gelsolin-like domain. Each affected individual was compound heterozygous for 2 of these alleles. The c.3044C-T mutation lay on a 14-Mb founder haplotype shared by all 3 individuals.

Moosa et al. (2016) reported that mutations in the SEC24D gene cause autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta.

Zhang et al. (2017) studied the probands from 2 Chinese families with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta. Both carried novel compound heterozygous missense mutations in the SEC24D gene. The family 1 proband had C908Y and S948P, and the family 2 proband had R313H and P292L. ⚠︎

Takeyari et al. (2018) used whole-exome sequencing in a 15-year-old Japanese boy with Cole-Carpenter syndrome type 2. They identified compound heterozygous variants in the SEC24D gene, a nonsense mutation (R484X) and a missense mutation (R313H).

### Pathogenesis

Garbes et al. (2015) used electron and immunofluorescence microscopy to examine skin fibroblasts from an affected boy. The cells showed inefficient export of procollagen from the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and dilated ER tubules. This reproduced the cellular phenotype of cranio-lenticulo-sutural dysplasia caused by SEC23A mutations. The authors stated that the cellular and morphologic phenotypes were concordant with those of Sec24d-deficient medaka (vbi) and zebrafish (bulldog) mutants.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 COLE-CARPENTER SYNDROME 2**  
SEC24D, SER1015PHE [c.3044C>T; p.S1015F]

Garbes et al. (2015) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a c.3044C-T transition in the SEC24D gene, resulting in a ser1015-to-phe (S1015F) substitution in a cargo-binding pocket. They found it in 3 individuals from 2 families with a syndromic form of osteogenesis imperfecta: a 7-year-old boy and 2 fetuses. Each was compound heterozygous for S1015F and a second mutant SEC24D allele. The S1015F mutation lay on a 14-Mb founder haplotype shared by all 3 individuals. Skin fibroblasts from the affected boy showed inefficient export of procollagen from the endoplasmic reticulum and dilated ER tubules.

**.0002 COLE-CARPENTER SYNDROME 2**  
SEC24D, CYS908TYR [c.2723G>A; p.C908Y]

Zhang et al. (2017) studied a 23-year-old Chinese man (family 1) with osteogenesis imperfecta. He had recurrent fractures, dentinogenesis imperfecta, an unclosed anterior fontanel, facial dysmorphism, and skull ossification defects. In this patient they identified compound heterozygous missense mutations in the SEC24D gene: a c.2723G-A transition, resulting in a cys908-to-tyr (C908Y) substitution, and a c.2842T-C transition, resulting in a ser948-to-pro (S948P) substitution.

**.0003 COLE-CARPENTER SYNDROME 2**  
SEC24D, SER948PRO [c.2842T>C; p.S948P]

In a 23-year-old Chinese man with osteogenesis imperfecta, Zhang et al. (2017) identified compound heterozygous SEC24D mutations: a c.2842T-C transition, resulting in a ser948-to-pro (S948P) substitution, and C908Y.

**.0004 COLE-CARPENTER SYNDROME 2**  
SEC24D, ARG313HIS [c.938G>A; p.R313H]

Zhang et al. (2017) studied a 7-year-old Chinese boy (family 2) with osteogenesis imperfecta. He had recurrent fractures, dentinogenesis imperfecta, and an unclosed anterior fontanel. In this patient they identified compound heterozygous missense mutations in the SEC24D gene: a c.938G-A transition, resulting in an arg313-to-his (R313H) substitution, and a c.875C-T transition, resulting in a pro292-to-leu (P292L) substitution.

Takeyari et al. (2018) used whole-exome sequencing in a 15-year-old Japanese boy with Cole-Carpenter syndrome type 2. They identified compound heterozygosity for the R313H mutation and a nonsense mutation (R484X) in the SEC24D gene.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that annular epidermolytic ichthyosis-1 (AEI1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the keratin-10 gene (KRT10; 148080) on chromosome 17q21. ⚠︎

### Description

Annular epidermolytic ichthyosis is a form of epidermolytic (acanthokeratolytic) ichthyosis. Bullous ichthyosis in infancy or early childhood is followed by persistent hyperkeratotic plaques. Episodes of annular and polycyclic, erythematous, hyperkeratotic or scaly plaques on the trunk and proximal extremities come and go. Histology shows epidermolytic hyperkeratosis (Sahn et al., 1992; Suga et al., 1998).

### Nomenclature

Sahn et al. (1992) suggested that the kindred they described might represent a new phenotype of epidermolytic (acanthokeratolytic) ichthyosis. Joh et al. (1997) concluded that annular epidermolytic ichthyosis should be considered a variant of bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma. Suga et al. (1998) likewise described it as a distinct phenotypic variant of bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Sahn et al. (1992) reported a 30-year-old white woman who developed bullous and ichthyosiform skin lesions at 8 months of age. Blistering stopped at puberty, but the hyperkeratotic plaques persisted. At age 27 years she developed numerous annular and polycyclic, erythematous, hyperkeratotic plaques on the trunk and extremities. These plaques itched, enlarged slowly and then resolved. Histopathology showed epidermolytic hyperkeratosis. On electron microscopy, the annular plaques showed interrupted perinuclear tonofilament rings, which the authors considered consistent with either bullous ichthyotic erythroderma of Brocq or ichthyosis bullosa of Siemens. Two of her 5 children were affected with a bullous type of ichthyosis.

Joh et al. (1997) reported the second known occurrence of the disorder, in a family with 2 affected generations. The proband had bullous ichthyosis with bouts of disease activity. During these bouts, numerous annular and polycyclic erythematous, hyperkeratotic plaques developed on the trunk and proximal extremities. Histology showed epidermolytic hyperkeratosis, with abnormal keratin filament networks and clumping of perinuclear tonofilaments. ⚠︎

Suga et al. (1998) described a third kindred with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis. Bullous ichthyosis in early childhood was followed by hyperkeratotic lichenified plaques in the flexural areas and on the extensor surfaces. Characteristic intermittent bouts of annular and polycyclic erythematous scaly plaques occurred on the trunk and proximal extremities.

Mikilita et al. (2020) reported an 8-year-old Brazilian girl, born to nonconsanguineous parents, with a diffuse dermatosis that began in the first months of life. On examination she had erythematous, scaly, hyperkeratotic plaques with prominent borders on the mesogastrium, cubital and popliteal fossae, and inguinal and cervical regions. The plaques were migratory and polycyclic. She also had palmoplantar hyperkeratosis and yellowish hyperkeratotic plaques on the scalp and nasal introitus. She had no hair, nail or mucosal abnormalities. A skin biopsy from the extensor forearm showed acanthosis, papillomatosis and hyperkeratosis, with marked epidermolysis in the granular layer. In a review of the literature in English, Portuguese and Spanish published after the first description by Sahn et al. (1992), the authors found 19 cases of annular epidermolytic ichthyosis in 10 publications. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

In the kindred reported by Sahn et al. (1992), an affected mother had 2 affected children, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. Joh et al. (1997) found the disorder in 2 generations of a family. They stated that the pattern of inheritance of annular epidermolytic ichthyosis was consistent with autosomal dominant transmission. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In affected individuals from 2 generations of a family with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis, Joh et al. (1997) identified a heterozygous tandem 2-bp CG-to-GA mutation in the KRT10 gene. Both nucleotide changes were on the same allele. The mutation resulted in an arg-to-glu substitution at residue 83 (R83E) of the 2B helical segment. ⚠︎

In a third kindred with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis, Suga et al. (1998) identified a mutation in the KRT10 gene that resulted in an ile-to-thr substitution at residue 107 (codon 446). The change lay within the highly conserved helix termination motif at the end of the rod domain of keratin-10.

### Diagnosis

Mikilita et al. (2020) noted that the main differential diagnosis of annular epidermolytic ichthyosis is erythrokeratodermia variabilis et progressiva (EKVP). EKVP is distinguished by onset in childhood, the absence of epidermal fragility, and histology without epidermolysis. Their patient was first diagnosed with EKVP, but the diagnosis was revised after a biopsy showed epidermolytic hyperkeratosis. ⚠︎

### Clinical Management

In their literature review, Mikilita et al. (2020) found that topical treatments produced little response. These included retinoids, corticosteroids, propylene glycol, calcipotriene and keratolytics. Three reports described a good response to systemic retinoids (acitretin, or low-dose isotretinoin).

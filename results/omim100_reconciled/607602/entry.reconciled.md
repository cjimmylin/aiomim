### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that annular epidermolytic ichthyosis-1 (AEI1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the keratin-10 gene (KRT10; 148080) on chromosome 17q21.2. ⚠︎

### Description

Annular epidermolytic ichthyosis is a form of epidermolytic (acanthokeratolytic) ichthyosis. Bullous ichthyosis begins in early childhood and is followed by hyperkeratotic, lichenified plaques in flexural and extensor areas. A characteristic feature is intermittent bouts of annular and polycyclic, erythematous, scaly or hyperkeratotic plaques on the trunk and proximal extremities, which resolve. Histology shows epidermolytic hyperkeratosis (Sahn et al., 1992; Suga et al., 1998). ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

Sahn et al. (1992) suggested that annular epidermolytic ichthyosis may be a new phenotype of epidermolytic (acanthokeratolytic) ichthyosis. Joh et al. (1997) concluded that annular epidermolytic ichthyosis should be considered a variant of bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma. Suga et al. (1998) also described it as a distinct phenotypic variant of bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma.

### Clinical Features

Sahn et al. (1992) reported a 30-year-old white woman who developed bullous and ichthyosiform skin lesions at the age of 8 months. The blistering stopped at puberty, but the hyperkeratotic plaques persisted. At age 27 years, she developed numerous annular and polycyclic, erythematous, hyperkeratotic plaques on the trunk and extremities. These plaques were pruritic, enlarged slowly, and then resolved. Histopathology showed epidermolytic hyperkeratosis. On electron microscopy, the annular plaques showed interrupted perinuclear tonofilament rings, consistent with either bullous ichthyotic erythroderma of Brocq or ichthyosis bullosa of Siemens. Two of her 5 children were affected with a bullous type of ichthyosis. The authors suggested that this kindred may represent a new phenotype of epidermolytic ichthyosis.

Joh et al. (1997) reported a second occurrence of annular epidermolytic ichthyosis, in a family with 2 affected generations. The proband had bullous ichthyosis with bouts of disease activity, during which numerous annular and polycyclic, erythematous, hyperkeratotic plaques developed on the trunk and proximal extremities. Histology showed epidermolytic hyperkeratosis, with abnormal keratin filament networks and clumping of perinuclear tonofilaments. ⚠︎

Suga et al. (1998) described a third kindred with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis. Affected individuals had bullous ichthyosis in early childhood, followed by hyperkeratotic, lichenified plaques in flexural areas and on extensor surfaces. They had characteristic intermittent bouts of annular and polycyclic, erythematous, scaly plaques on the trunk and proximal extremities.

Mikilita et al. (2020) reported an 8-year-old Brazilian girl, born to nonconsanguineous parents, who had a diffuse dermatosis that began in the first months of life. On examination, she had erythematous, scaly, hyperkeratotic plaques with prominent borders. These affected the mesogastrium, cubital and popliteal fossae, and inguinal and cervical regions. The plaques were migratory and polycyclic. She also had palmoplantar hyperkeratosis and yellowish hyperkeratotic plaques on the scalp and nasal introitus. She had no hair, nail, or mucosal abnormalities. A skin biopsy from the extensor forearm showed acanthosis, papillomatosis, and hyperkeratosis, with marked epidermolysis in the granular layer. In a review of literature published in English, Portuguese, and Spanish since the first description by Sahn et al. (1992), Mikilita et al. (2020) found 19 cases of annular epidermolytic ichthyosis in 10 publications. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

In the kindred reported by Sahn et al. (1992), an affected mother had 2 affected children. Joh et al. (1997) reported a family with 2 affected generations and stated that the inheritance pattern of annular epidermolytic ichthyosis was consistent with autosomal dominant transmission.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of a family with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis spanning 2 generations, Joh et al. (1997) found a novel heterozygous mutation in the KRT10 gene. This was a tandem CG-to-GA 2-bp change in the same allele that resulted in an arg83-to-glu (R83E) substitution in the 2B helical segment. ⚠︎

In a third kindred with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis, Suga et al. (1998) identified a novel mutation in the KRT10 gene. The mutation resulted in an ile107-to-thr substitution (codon 446) within the highly conserved helix termination motif at the end of the rod domain of keratin-10.

### Diagnosis

Mikilita et al. (2020) noted that the main differential diagnosis of annular epidermolytic ichthyosis is erythrokeratodermia variabilis et progressiva (EKVP). EKVP is distinguished by onset during childhood, absence of epidermal fragility, and histology without epidermolysis. Their patient was first diagnosed with EKVP, and the diagnosis was revised after a biopsy showed epidermolytic hyperkeratosis. ⚠︎

### Clinical Management

In their literature review, Mikilita et al. (2020) found that topical treatments produced little response. These included retinoids, corticosteroids, propylene glycol, calcipotriene, and keratolytics. Three reports described a good response to systemic retinoids (acitretin or low-dose isotretinoin).

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 ICHTHYOSIS, ANNULAR EPIDERMOLYTIC, 1**  
KRT10, ARG83GLU [p.R83E]

In affected members of a family with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis spanning 2 generations, Joh et al. (1997) identified a heterozygous tandem CG-to-GA 2-bp mutation in the same allele of the KRT10 gene. It resulted in an arg83-to-glu (R83E) substitution in the 2B helical segment. The proband had bullous ichthyosis with bouts of annular and polycyclic, erythematous, hyperkeratotic plaques on the trunk and proximal extremities. ⚠︎

**.0002 ICHTHYOSIS, ANNULAR EPIDERMOLYTIC, 1**  
KRT10, ILE446THR [p.I107T]

In a kindred with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis, Suga et al. (1998) identified a mutation in the KRT10 gene that resulted in an ile107-to-thr substitution (codon 446). The change lies within the highly conserved helix termination motif at the end of the rod domain of keratin-10. Affected individuals had bullous ichthyosis in early childhood, then hyperkeratotic, lichenified plaques and intermittent annular, polycyclic, erythematous, scaly plaques on the trunk and proximal extremities.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that osteogenesis imperfecta type X (OI10) is caused by homozygous mutation in the SERPINH1 gene (600943) on chromosome 11q13.

### Description

Osteogenesis imperfecta type X is a severe autosomal recessive form of osteogenesis imperfecta caused by mutation in SERPINH1, which encodes the collagen chaperone-like protein HSP47 (Christiansen et al., 2010).

### Clinical Features

Christiansen et al. (2010) reported an individual with a severe osteogenesis imperfecta phenotype. Type I procollagen accumulated in the Golgi of fibroblasts from the affected individual.

Duran et al. (2015) studied a patient with osteogenesis imperfecta who carried a SERPINH1 mutation. ⚠︎

### Biochemical Features

Christiansen et al. (2010) found normal 3-hydroxylation of proline-986 in proalpha1(I) chains in their patient, placing HSP47 function downstream of the CRTAP/P3H1/CyPB prolyl 3-hydroxylation complex.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OI10 in the patient reported by Christiansen et al. (2010) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance; the patient was homozygous for a SERPINH1 mutation.

### Molecular Genetics

In an individual with severe autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta, Christiansen et al. (2010) identified a homozygous missense mutation (c.233T-C, leu78-to-pro; L78P) in the SERPINH1 gene, which encodes the collagen chaperone HSP47. The L78P mutation led to proteasomal degradation of endoplasmic reticulum (ER)-resident HSP47; type I procollagen accumulated in the Golgi of patient fibroblasts, and a population of the secreted type I procollagen was protease sensitive.

Duran et al. (2015) identified a new mutation in SERPINH1 in a patient with osteogenesis imperfecta. The mutation resulted in destabilization and mislocalization of HSP47 and secondarily had similar effects on FKBP65. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

On the basis of their findings in fibroblasts from a patient homozygous for the L78P mutation in SERPINH1, Christiansen et al. (2010) suggested that HSP47 monitors the integrity of the type I procollagen triple helix at the ER/cis-Golgi boundary and that, in its absence, ER-to-Golgi transit is increased and helical structure is compromised.

Duran et al. (2015) found evidence that HSP47 and FKBP65 act cooperatively during posttranslational maturation of type I procollagen and fail to interact properly in cells with mutant HSP47, suggesting a common cellular pathway in osteogenesis imperfecta caused by HSP47 and FKBP65 deficiency.

In an individual with severe autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta (OI10), Christiansen et al. Type I procollagen accumulated in the Golgi of the patient's fibroblasts, and part of the secreted type I procollagen was protease sensitive.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that epidermolysis bullosa simplex-7 with nephropathy and deafness (EBS7) is caused by homozygous mutation in the CD151 gene on chromosome 11p15.5. ⚠︎

### Description

Epidermolysis bullosa simplex-7 with nephropathy and deafness (EBS7) is a disorder in which pretibial epidermolysis bullosa occurs together with hereditary nephritis that progresses to end-stage renal disease, and with sensorineural deafness. Reported patients also had nail dystrophy and lacrimal duct stenosis (Kagan et al., 1988; Karamatic Crew et al., 2004). †

### Clinical Features

Kagan et al. (1988) described 2 sibs from an Oriental Jewish family with hereditary nephritis, pretibial epidermolysis bullosa, and beta-thalassemia minor. Both sibs had proteinuria in the nephrotic range and developed end-stage renal disease. They needed dialysis (hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis) from the ages of 14 and 16 years, respectively. They also had nasolacrimal duct stenosis, nail dystrophy, and sensorineural hearing loss. †

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) studied 3 patients of Indian Jewish origin with end-stage kidney disease, 2 of whom were sibs. As well as hereditary nephritis, the sibs had sensorineural deafness, pretibial epidermolysis bullosa, and beta-thalassemia minor. The patients also had pretibial bullous skin lesions. The red blood cells of all 3 patients were negative for the MER2 blood group antigen. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The disorder occurred in sibs (Kagan et al., 1988), and Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) found the same homozygous CD151 mutation in all 3 affected patients they studied. Both findings are consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) studied 3 MER2-negative patients of Indian Jewish origin with end-stage kidney disease, 2 of whom were sibs. They examined CD151, the gene that lies at the same position on chromosome 11p15.5 as the MER2 blood group gene. All 3 patients were homozygous for a single-nucleotide insertion (G383) in exon 5 of CD151, which caused a frameshift and a premature stop signal at codon 140. The resulting truncated protein would lack the integrin-binding domain. The authors showed that CD151 carries the MER2 blood group antigen and is present on erythrocytes. CD151 forms laminin-binding complexes with integrins alpha-3/beta-1 and alpha-6/beta-1 in kidney and with alpha-3/beta-1 and alpha-6/beta-4 in skin. The authors concluded that CD151 is essential for the proper assembly of the glomerular and tubular basement membranes in the kidney, has functional significance in the skin, and is probably a component of the inner ear.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-9 (HMNR9) is caused by homozygous or biallelic mutation in the COQ7 gene on chromosome 16.

### Description

Autosomal recessive distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-9 (HMNR9) is a length-dependent, pure motor axonal neuropathy that begins in childhood. It causes slowly progressive distal weakness and atrophy, worse in the lower limbs than the upper limbs, with foot drop, pes cavus, and walking difficulties. Sensation and cognition are preserved. Some patients have pyramidal signs (Jacquier et al., 2023; Smith et al., 2023). The disorder is associated with reduced coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10) levels and accumulation of the COQ7 substrate demethoxy-CoQ10 in patient cells (Jacquier et al., 2023; Smith et al., 2023).

### Clinical Features

Jacquier et al. (2023) reported 2 brothers and a sister, aged 42, 36, and 25 years, from a Portuguese family. The sibs were born to nonconsanguineous healthy parents and had a recessively inherited axonal distal hereditary motor neuropathy. Development was normal in all 3. Walking difficulties began at ages 10, 12, and 9 years, respectively, and included foot drop, stiffness of the lower limbs, or fatigability. Weakness of the hands followed at ages 22, 32, and 13 years. Weakness progressed with a distal predominance and mainly affected foot dorsiflexion. Heel and tiptoe walking was impossible. All 3 sibs had amyotrophy of the lower limbs and hands, and all had pes cavus. The oldest brother had complete loss of foot plantar and dorsal flexion and walked with ankle-foot orthoses. The other 2 sibs walked without aid. Sensory examination was normal in all 3, although the sister had difficulty feeling cold and warm temperatures on her feet. None had cognitive impairment, and cranial nerve examination was normal. The index patient had brisk deep tendon reflexes in all 4 limbs, except for absent Achilles reflexes, and a Hoffman sign, without Babinski sign or spasticity. The sister had normal reflexes and a Babinski sign on the left foot. The oldest brother had absent Achilles reflexes. Electrophysiologic studies in 2 patients showed a pure motor axonal neuropathy that mainly affected the distal lower limbs. Common fibular motor responses could not be obtained, sensory responses were normal, and needle examination showed fibrillations and neurogenic changes. In the index patient, brain and spinal cord MRI, echocardiography, ECG, audiometry, and ophthalmic examination were normal, apart from known myopia. His creatine phosphokinase was mildly elevated (215 U/l; normal less than 200). The sister took CoQ10 (30 mg per day) between 15 and 18 years of age and reported that her symptoms stabilized during that time. Progressive worsening resumed at age 18 years, when the supplementation was stopped. Jacquier et al. (2023) noted that the pyramidal features suggested upper motor neuron involvement. ⚠︎

Smith et al. (2023) reported 3 sibs, a 24-year-old woman and 2 males aged 23 and 12 years, born to Syrian parents who were first cousins. All 3 developed distal weakness by 10 to 14 years of age. They had muscle atrophy affecting the distal lower extremities more than the upper extremities, with preserved sensation and deep tendon reflexes. They had no developmental delay, cognitive impairment, or upper motor neuron features. Electrodiagnostic testing showed reduced distal motor amplitudes with preserved sensory responses, and needle EMG showed distal chronic reinnervation without active denervation. Whole-body muscle MRI in 2 sibs showed normal proximal muscles and distal muscle wasting, with moderate to severe fat infiltration that began at the distal third of the thigh and was most marked in the calves. Vastus lateralis biopsy in 1 sib showed neurogenic atrophy with angular atrophic fibers, group atrophy, and fiber type grouping, without ragged red or cytochrome oxidase-negative fibers. Two of the affected sibs and an unaffected brother had been deaf since childhood. The deafness did not segregate with the neuropathy and was explained by a homozygous GJB2 variant.

### Biochemical Features

In fibroblasts from a patient with HMNR9, Jacquier et al. (2023) found that CoQ10 was decreased from 71.5 pmol/mg protein in controls to 12.5 pmol/mg protein, and that 6-demethoxy-CoQ10 (6-DMQ), the COQ7 substrate, had appeared. 6-DMQ was also detected in the patient's plasma. The authors suggested that plasma 6-DMQ accumulation could be used to confirm the pathogenicity of COQ7 variants.

Smith et al. (2023) found that cultured fibroblasts from 3 affected sibs had on average 87% less CoQ10 than fibroblasts from their father and an unaffected sib, together with substantial accumulation of demethoxyubiquinone-10 (DMQ10). Muscle CoQ10 in 1 sib was 67% lower than in an unrelated control (63 vs 192.7 ng/mg protein), but no DMQ10 was detected in muscle.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of HMNR9 in the families reported by Jacquier et al. (2023) and Smith et al. (2023) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. In both families, affected sibs were homozygous for the mutation and the parents were heterozygous carriers.

### Molecular Genetics

Jacquier et al. (2023) performed whole-exome sequencing of both parents and the 3 affected sibs of a Portuguese family with HMNR9. They identified a homozygous c.3G-T transversion in the COQ7 gene, which disrupts the start codon of isoform 1 (NM_016138.5; p.Met1?). Isoform 1 is the main isoform expressed in the spinal cord. The mutation was confirmed by Sanger sequencing and segregated with the disorder; the unaffected parents were heterozygous. The variant was absent from ExAC and present once in the heterozygous state in gnomAD. In transfected SH-EP neuroblastoma cells, the mutant construct produced 85% less COQ7 protein than wildtype, with no significant change in mRNA levels. In the patient's fibroblasts, COQ7 protein was barely detectable, mRNA levels were only mildly decreased, CoQ10 levels were reduced, and 6-DMQ had accumulated. Supplementation with the CoQ10 precursor 2,4-dihydroxybenzoic acid normalized 6-DMQ levels and slightly increased CoQ10, from 12.5 to 18.8 pmol/mg. The patient's fibroblasts showed reduced mitochondrial ATP production, a compensatory increase in glycolytic ATP production, and reduced basal and maximal respiration. They also proliferated more slowly in galactose medium. CoQ10 supplementation rescued respiration and partially restored proliferation.

In 3 sibs with HMNR9 born to consanguineous Syrian parents, Smith et al. (2023) identified a homozygous c.1A-G mutation in the COQ7 gene (NM_016138.5; p.?), which abolishes the initiating methionine. The mutation was found by duo-exome sequencing of 2 sibs. The third affected sib was also homozygous, and the parents and an unaffected sib were heterozygous. The variant was absent from gnomAD. Fibroblasts from the affected sibs showed an 85% reduction in COQ7 protein, while the level of PDSS2 was unchanged. No COQ7 was detected in muscle from 1 sib. Patient cells had reduced CoQ10 levels, and fibroblasts accumulated DMQ10. Maximal mitochondrial respiration was reduced in both fibroblasts and muscle. Patient fibroblasts relied more on glycolysis but remained viable in galactose medium. Muscle from the biopsied sib showed increased H2O2 emission and reduced levels of supercomplexes containing complexes I and IV.

The title of the report by Liu et al. (2023) states that biallelic variants in the COQ7 gene cause distal hereditary motor neuropathy in 2 Chinese families.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Jacquier et al. (2023) and Smith et al. (2023) contrasted the pure distal motor neuropathy in their families with earlier reports of COQ7-related primary CoQ10 deficiency. Those earlier cases had early childhood onset, developmental delay, multiorgan involvement, sensorimotor polyneuropathy, or spastic paraplegia. Jacquier et al. (2023) noted that the previously described mutations lay in the catalytic domain and would affect all COQ7 isoforms.

Wongkittichote et al. (2023) reported 5 new patients with COQ7-related primary CoQ10 deficiency and studied the functional effects of new and previously reported COQ7 variants. They described 2 distinct presentations. The first had neonatal onset with severe neuromuscular, cardiorespiratory, and renal involvement. The second had later onset with progressive neuropathy, lower extremity weakness, abnormal gait, and variable developmental delay. In a yeast model, the equivalents of the human variants arg54-to-gln (R54Q), arg107-to-trp (R107W), and ile66-to-asn (I66N), and of the complex allele p.[Thr103Met;Leu111Pro], partially rescued the growth defect of the yeast deletion strain. The authors therefore concluded that these are hypomorphic alleles. They described an emerging genotype-phenotype correlation.

### Pathogenesis

Jacquier et al. (2023) suggested that the c.3G-T start-codon mutation impairs translation of the main isoform, isoform 1, but leaves isoforms 2 and 3 unaffected because they are translated from alternative start codons. In transfected cells, isoform 2 localized to mitochondria. The authors proposed that the alternative isoforms might maintain basal mitochondrial COQ7 levels, which could explain why the disease is milder than other COQ7-related disorders. Smith et al. (2023) predicted that the c.1A-G mutation leads to translation from methionine-39. This would remove the cleavable N-terminal presequence, which contains the mitochondrial and nuclear targeting signals. They suggested that metabolic dysfunction and mitochondrial oxidative stress may contribute to the distal motor phenotype.

### Clinical Management

Jacquier et al. (2023) found that CoQ10 supplementation of the culture medium rescued mitochondrial respiration and proliferation of patient fibroblasts in vitro. One of their patients reported that her symptoms stabilized while she took CoQ10, 30 mg daily, and worsened again after she stopped. The authors suggested that CoQ10 supplementation be considered to prevent disease progression. In patient fibroblasts, 2,4-dihydroxybenzoic acid increased CoQ10 only slightly, and the authors judged it unlikely to be clinically practical.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 NEURONOPATHY, DISTAL HEREDITARY MOTOR, AUTOSOMAL RECESSIVE 9**  
COQ7, 3G-T [NM_016138.5:c.3G>T; NP_057222.2:p.Met1?]

In 3 sibs from a Portuguese family with autosomal recessive distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-9 (HMNR9), Jacquier et al. (2023) identified a homozygous c.3G-T transversion in the COQ7 gene. The change converts the ATG start codon of isoform 1 (NM_016138.5) to ATT (p.Met1?). The mutation was found by whole-exome sequencing, and the unaffected parents were heterozygous. The variant was absent from ExAC and present once in the heterozygous state in gnomAD. In transfected cells, the mutation reduced COQ7 protein levels by 85% without affecting mRNA levels. Patient fibroblasts had barely detectable COQ7 protein, decreased CoQ10, and accumulation of 6-demethoxy-CoQ10, which was also found in plasma. They also showed reduced mitochondrial respiration and reduced growth in galactose medium, which were rescued by CoQ10 supplementation.

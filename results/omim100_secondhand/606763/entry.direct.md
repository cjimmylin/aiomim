### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the DNAAF3 gene on chromosome 19q13.

### Description

Primary ciliary dyskinesia is an autosomal recessive disorder marked by chronic sinusitis, bronchiectasis, and subfertility. Situs inversus occurs in about half of cases (Kartagener syndrome). The disorder results from defective ciliary function, which is associated with a range of ultrastructural abnormalities of the cilia (Meeks et al., 2000). In CILD2, respiratory cilia are immotile and lack both outer and inner dynein arms (Mitchison et al., 2012).

### Clinical Features

Meeks et al. (2000) studied 5 Arabic families with primary ciliary dyskinesia.

Mitchison et al. (2012) reported 10 affected individuals with DNAAF3 mutations from 3 consanguineous families. Two families were of Arabic origin and had been included in the original linkage studies. The third, UCL71, was a UK-Pakistani family from a first-cousin union. The parents of all affected individuals were first cousins except for 2 affected members of family UCL66/67. Family UCL89 included affected monozygotic twins. Patients had classic clinical features, including neonatal respiratory distress, recurrent cough and wheezing, nasal discharge, chest infections, sinusitis, otitis media, nasal polyps, and bronchiectasis. Four of the 10 affected individuals had situs inversus. Cilia in nasal brushing biopsies were immotile in all affected family members. Where electron microscopy of respiratory cilia was available, it showed a shared defect of reduced or absent inner and outer dynein arms. Mitchison et al. (2012) concluded that loss of DNAAF3 results in classic primary ciliary dyskinesia associated with defective left-right organ asymmetry and male infertility.

### Inheritance

Mitchison et al. (2012) found that, for the CILD2 mutations they identified, all affected individuals were homozygous and unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous. This pattern is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Mapping

Meeks et al. (2000) performed a genome scan in 5 Arabic families with primary ciliary dyskinesia. Using GENEHUNTER, they obtained a maximum multipoint lod score (HLOD) of 4.4 on chromosome 19q13.3-qter, with alpha (the proportion of linked families) = 0.7. Recombinations at D19S572 and D19S218 defined a 15-cM critical region.

### Heterogeneity

Because not all of the families studied were linked to chromosome 19q, Meeks et al. (2000) concluded that their data confirmed locus heterogeneity in primary ciliary dyskinesia.

### Molecular Genetics

Mitchison et al. (2012) found homozygous mutations in the DNAAF3 gene in affected members of 2 of the 3 Arabic-origin consanguineous families originally linked to the CILD2 locus on chromosome 19q13. In both families, affected individuals showed large regions of marker homozygosity across the DNAAF3 locus. Family UCL89 carried a missense mutation (L108P), and family UCL66/67 carried a nonsense mutation (R136X). Neither variant was present on 148 population-matched control Arabic chromosomes. The authors then sequenced DNAAF3 in 112 additional families with primary ciliary dyskinesia, most with outer dynein arm defects. They found a homozygous 1-bp insertion causing a frameshift in a UK-Pakistani first-cousin family (UCL71) that showed evidence of linkage to the locus. This variant was absent from 172 control UK-Pakistani chromosomes. For all 3 mutations, affected individuals were homozygous and unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous. None of the mutations were present in the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, or NHLBI-ESP databases. A homozygous ala325-to-thr (A325T) variant in a Belgian patient was considered likely to be a Caucasian polymorphism, because it was found in 1 of 192 Caucasian control chromosomes, was predicted to be benign, and affected a nonconserved residue. Immunofluorescence analysis of patient respiratory cells showed that the outer dynein arm proteins DNAH5, DNAH9, and DNAI2 and the inner dynein arm light chain DNALI1 were absent from the cilia. In some patient cells, residual DNAH5 and DNALI1 were seen in the apical cytoplasm. Electron microscopy confirmed that both outer and inner dynein arms were missing.

### Pathogenesis

Mitchison et al. (2012) showed that the Chlamydomonas ortholog of DNAAF3, PF22, is found only in the cytoplasm. A PF22-null mutant could not assemble outer dynein arms or some inner dynein arms. In mutant cytoplasm, the outer arm intermediate chain IC2 was hyperabundant and dynein heavy chains were retained at near wild-type levels. Dynein complexes formed in pf22 cytoplasm but could not go on to assemble into flagella, and an epitope on the HC-beta tail that is normally exposed was inaccessible. The authors proposed that DNAAF3 acts in the cytoplasmic preassembly of axonemal dyneins, at a later step than the folding of dynein heavy chains, which depends on PF13/DNAAF1 and ODA7/DNAAF2.

### Animal Model

Mitchison et al. (2012) knocked down dnaaf3 in zebrafish with morpholinos. Olfactory placode cilia were completely static, and electron microscopy showed reduced or missing outer and inner dynein arms. Morphant embryos showed body axis curvature, hydrocephalus, pronephric cysts, abnormal numbers of otoliths, and left-right patterning defects; about half of dnaaf3 MO ex8 embryos had reversed or absent heart looping. In Chlamydomonas, the pf22 mutant carried a nonsense mutation (trp79-to-ter) in the PF22 gene. The mutant had paralyzed half-length flagella and lacked outer dynein arms and inner dynein arms 'b' and 'c'. Transformation with the wildtype gene rescued this phenotype.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, LEU108PRO [c.323T>C; p.Leu108Pro]

In affected members of a consanguineous Arabic-origin family (UCL89) with primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2), Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous c.323T-C transition in exon 3 of the DNAAF3 gene, resulting in a leu108-to-pro (L108P) substitution at a highly conserved residue. Unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous. The variant was absent from 148 Arabic control chromosomes and was predicted to be probably damaging. Patient respiratory cilia were immotile and lacked outer and inner dynein arms.

**.0002 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, ARG136TER [c.406C>T; p.Arg136X]

In affected members of an Arabic-origin family (UCL66/67) with primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2), Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous c.406C-T transition in exon 4 of the DNAAF3 gene, resulting in an arg136-to-ter (R136X) substitution. Unaffected family members were heterozygous. The mutation was absent from 148 Arabic control chromosomes.

**.0003 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, 1-BP INS, 762T [c.762_763insT; p.Val255CysfsX12]

In affected members of a UK-Pakistani first-cousin family (UCL71) with primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2), Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous 1-bp insertion (c.762_763insT) in exon 6 of the DNAAF3 gene. The insertion was predicted to result in a frameshift and premature termination (Val255CysfsX12). Unaffected family members were heterozygous. The mutation was absent from 172 UK-Pakistani control chromosomes.

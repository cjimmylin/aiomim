### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the DNAAF3 gene on chromosome 19q13.

### Description

Primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD) is an autosomal recessive disorder with chronic sinusitis, bronchiectasis, and subfertility. Situs inversus is present in about half of cases (Kartagener syndrome). The clinical features result from defective ciliary function, which is associated with ultrastructural abnormalities such as absent dynein arms, absent radial spokes, and disturbed ciliary orientation (Meeks et al., 2000). CILD2 is caused by mutation in DNAAF3, a cytoplasmic factor needed to preassemble axonemal dyneins. Patients have immotile respiratory cilia that lack both outer and inner dynein arms (Mitchison et al., 2012).

### Clinical Features

Mitchison et al. (2012) studied 10 patients with PCD and DNAAF3 mutations from 3 families. Two of the families were consanguineous and of Arabic origin; the third (UCL71) was a UK-Pakistani family from a first-cousin union. The parents of all affected individuals except 2 were first cousins. All of the families had classic clinical symptoms of PCD: neonatal respiratory distress, recurrent cough and wheezing, nasal discharge and chest infections, sinusitis, otitis media, nasal polyps, and bronchiectasis. Four of the 10 affected individuals had situs inversus. One family (UCL89) included affected monozygotic twins. In nasal brushing biopsies, cilia were immotile in all affected family members. Where electron microscopy of respiratory cilia was available, it showed a shared defect of reduced or absent inner and outer dynein arms. Mitchison et al. (2012) concluded that loss of DNAAF3 gives rise to classical PCD with defective left-right organ asymmetry and male infertility.

### Biochemical Features

Mitchison et al. (2012) used immunofluorescence on nasal brushing samples from patients with DNAAF3 mutations. The outer dynein arm proteins DNAH5, DNAH9, and DNAI2 and the inner dynein arm light chain DNALI1 were all absent from the patients' cilia. This indicated that assembly of outer dynein arm types 1 and 2 and of DNALI1-containing inner dynein arms was disrupted along the full length of the axoneme. In some patient cells, residual DNAH5 and DNALI1 were seen in the apical cytoplasm, which was not seen in control cells.

### Inheritance

Meeks et al. (2000) stated that primary ciliary dyskinesia is an autosomal recessive condition. In the families reported by Mitchison et al. (2012), all affected individuals were homozygous for DNAAF3 mutations and unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance of CILD2.

### Mapping

Meeks et al. (2000) performed a genome scan in 5 Arabic families with PCD. Using GENEHUNTER, they obtained a maximum multipoint lod score (HLOD) of 4.4 on chromosome 19q13.3-qter at an alpha (proportion of linked families) of 0.7. Recombinations at D19S572 and D19S218 defined a 15-cM critical region.

### Heterogeneity

In the genome scan by Meeks et al. (2000), the proportion of linked families was estimated at 0.7, which confirmed locus heterogeneity in primary ciliary dyskinesia.

### Molecular Genetics

Mitchison et al. (2012) sequenced DNAAF3, which lies within the CILD2 locus on chromosome 19q13, in consanguineous Arabic-origin PCD families previously linked to this locus. Two of the 3 originally linked families had homozygous mutations; in both, affected individuals had large regions of marker homozygosity spanning DNAAF3. Family UCL89 carried a missense mutation (L108P) and family UCL66/67 carried a nonsense mutation (R136X). Neither variant was found on 148 population-matched control Arabic chromosomes.

Mitchison et al. (2012) then sequenced DNAAF3 in 112 more PCD families. Of these, 107 had outer dynein arm defects and 9 had evidence for linkage to the chromosome 19 locus. One linked family (UCL71), a UK-Pakistani first-cousin union, was homozygous for a 1-bp insertion that was predicted to cause a frameshift and premature termination (V255CfsTer12). This variant was absent from 172 control UK-Pakistani chromosomes. A Belgian patient was homozygous for an ala325-to-thr (A325T) substitution. However, this variant was found on 1 of 192 Caucasian control chromosomes, was predicted to be benign, and affected a nonconserved residue, so it was considered likely to be a Caucasian polymorphism. For the 3 pathogenic mutations, all affected individuals were homozygous and unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous. None of the 3 mutations was found in the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, or NHLBI-ESP databases.

### Animal Model

Mitchison et al. (2012) found that the Chlamydomonas reinhardtii pf22 mutant, which is nonmotile and has paralyzed half-length flagella, carries a nonsense mutation (trp79-to-ter) in DAB1, the ortholog of DNAAF3. The resulting flagellar defects were rescued by transformation with the wildtype gene. The pf22 mutant failed to assemble outer dynein arms and inner dynein arms 'b' and 'c', but retained inner arm 'f'. Tagged PF22 protein was found only in the cytoplasm. In pf22 cytoplasm, outer arm heavy chain levels were near normal, but intermediate chain IC2 was hyperabundant. Heavy chain trypsin sensitivity was less strongly altered in pf22 than in the pf13 and oda7 mutants. Immunoprecipitation showed that dynein complexes formed in pf22 cytoplasm but had abnormal properties, including a buried HC-beta epitope and a reduced amount of HC-gamma. Mitchison et al. (2012) suggested that PF22 acts at a later step of cytoplasmic dynein preassembly than PF13 and ODA7.

Mitchison et al. (2012) knocked down dnaaf3 in zebrafish with morpholinos. Morphant embryos had immotile olfactory placode cilia with reduced or missing outer and inner dynein arms. Other phenotypes included body axis curvature (64% and 78% of embryos with the 2 splice-blocking morpholinos), hydrocephalus (18% and 34%), pronephric cysts, abnormal otolith number, and reduced and disorganized beating of pronephric and spinal cord cilia. About half of dnaaf3MOex8 embryos had reversed heart looping or no heart looping.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, LEU108PRO [c.323T>C; p.Leu108Pro]

In affected members of a consanguineous Arabic-origin family (UCL89) with primary ciliary dyskinesia-2, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous c.323T-C transition in exon 3 of the DNAAF3 gene, resulting in a leu108-to-pro (L108P) substitution at a residue that is highly conserved across ciliated species. The affected family members included monozygotic twins. Unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous for the mutation. The mutation was absent from 148 population-matched control Arabic chromosomes and from the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, and NHLBI-ESP databases, and PolyPhen-2 and SIFT predicted it to be damaging. Respiratory cilia from affected individuals lacked outer and inner dynein arms on electron microscopy, lacked DNAH5 and DNALI1 on immunofluorescence, and were immotile.

**.0002 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, ARG136TER [c.406C>T; p.Arg136X]

In affected members of an Arabic-origin family (UCL66/67) with primary ciliary dyskinesia-2, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous c.406C-T transition in exon 4 of the DNAAF3 gene, resulting in an arg136-to-ter (R136X) substitution. Unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous for the mutation. The mutation was absent from 148 population-matched control Arabic chromosomes and from the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, and NHLBI-ESP databases.

**.0003 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, 1-BP INS, 762T [c.762_763insT; p.Val255CysfsX12]

In affected members of a UK-Pakistani family (UCL71) from a first-cousin union with primary ciliary dyskinesia-2, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous 1-bp insertion (c.762_763insT) in exon 6 of the DNAAF3 gene. The insertion was predicted to cause a val255-to-cys substitution followed by a frameshift that adds 11 novel amino acids before a premature stop codon (V255CfsTer12). Unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous for the mutation. The mutation was absent from 172 control UK-Pakistani chromosomes and from the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, and NHLBI-ESP databases.

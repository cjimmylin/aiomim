### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive limb-girdle muscular dystrophy-21 (LGMDR21) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the POGLUT1 gene, which lies within a region of chromosome 3q13.13-q21.2.

### Description

Autosomal recessive limb-girdle muscular dystrophy-21 (LGMDR21) causes proximal muscle weakness that mainly affects the lower limbs and progresses slowly. Scapular winging is common, and some patients eventually need a wheelchair. Onset ranges from congenital and infantile to adult. Serum creatine kinase is usually normal or only mildly raised. Muscle imaging shows a distinctive 'inside-to-outside' pattern of fatty degeneration in the thigh muscles. Muscle biopsies show reduced Notch signaling, fewer satellite cells, and hypoglycosylation of alpha-dystroglycan (Servian-Morilla et al., 2016, 2020).

### Nomenclature

Servian-Morilla et al. (2020) referred to the POGLUT1-related disorder as LGMD2Z or LGMD R21. A revised nomenclature and classification of the limb-girdle muscular dystrophies was the subject of the 229th ENMC international workshop (Straub et al., 2018).

### Clinical Features

Servian-Morilla et al. (2016) studied a consanguineous family from southern Spain made up of 17 individuals across 3 generations. Four of 5 sibs in generation II had a phenotype consistent with limb-girdle muscular dystrophy. Weakness mainly affected the proximal lower limbs and began in the third decade. The disease was progressive and led to scapular winging and wheelchair confinement. Serum creatine kinase was normal in 3 patients and mildly elevated in 1. Muscle biopsies from all 4 affected sibs showed changes ranging from very mild myopathic changes to classic dystrophic pathology. Proteins usually affected in myopathies were expressed normally, except that alpha-dystroglycan was reduced. Immunofluorescence showed a variable reduction of glycosylated alpha-dystroglycan at the sarcolemma, while staining for the alpha-dystroglycan core protein, beta-dystroglycan, and laminin alpha-2 was normal. Laminin binding was reduced in muscle, but agrin binding was normal. Transmission electron microscopy showed normal muscle ultrastructure with normal basement membrane compaction. Muscle MRI of the legs showed early fatty replacement of the internal regions of the thigh muscles, with the external areas spared. This 'from inside-to-outside' pattern of fatty degeneration progressed over the years and did not match the patterns typical of other muscular dystrophies. Patient skin fibroblasts showed normal alpha-dystroglycan glycosylation and laminin binding. The patients had no skin abnormalities.

Servian-Morilla et al. (2020) reported 15 patients from 9 unrelated families from Spain, Bulgaria, Italy, Germany, the USA, Iran, and the United Arab Emirates. This total included the 4 sibs of the original family. Consanguinity was recognized in 4 of the 9 families. One patient had congenital presentation, 2 had infantile onset before 3 years of age, and 12 had adult onset (58.3% in the second decade, 25% in the third decade, and 16.6% in the fourth to fifth decades). The tabulated decades of onset for the original family were the second decade for 2 sibs and the third decade for the other 2. Weakness was proximal, clearly predominant in the lower limbs, and slowly progressive. Seven patients (46.6%) with onset in their teens were confined to a wheelchair between the third and fifth decades. Mild to moderate scapular winging was present in 11 of the 15 patients. The congenital case first presented with hypotonia and proximal lower limb weakness after gait acquisition. The disease then progressed, with mild weakness, wasting, and contractures of the upper limbs, along with mild facial weakness, ptosis, and nasal voice. Six patients had mild to severe respiratory involvement from their mid-forties onward, whatever their age at onset; 2 of them needed ventilatory support at night. No significant bulbar, cardiac, or other systemic manifestations were found. Serum CK was normal except in 4 patients, who had mild elevations of 2 to 6 times normal. Other findings in individual patients included elbow contractures, myopathic facies, arched palate, ankle contracture, calf hypertrophy, scoliosis, and hyperlordosis. One patient with infantile onset also had nystagmus and retinitis pigmentosa. Muscle imaging of the lower limbs was available for 11 patients, and all of them showed the 'inside-to-outside' pattern of fatty degeneration. This pattern involved the vastus lateralis, vastus medialis, and vastus intermedius, the posterior thigh compartment, and the gastrocnemius. It was most evident early in the disease but could still be seen in the vastus lateralis at end stage. Lower leg muscles were better preserved than thigh muscles. ⚠︎

Servian-Morilla et al. (2020) studied muscle biopsies from 9 patients in 6 families. Vastus lateralis biopsies showed a dystrophic pattern. Biceps brachii biopsies showed mild myopathic features, with lobulated fibers in some cases. All biopsies showed reduced glycosylated alpha-dystroglycan at the sarcolemma, while staining for the alpha-dystroglycan core was normal. Laminin-binding activity was also reduced. The level of the NOTCH1 intracellular domain was decreased, and PAX7+ satellite cells were fewer than in controls. Patient muscles had no or only a few scattered regenerating fibers positive for embryonic myosin heavy chain, whereas disease controls had many.

### Inheritance

Servian-Morilla et al. (2016) found autosomal recessive inheritance in a consanguineous family in which 4 affected sibs were homozygous for a POGLUT1 mutation. Servian-Morilla et al. (2020) confirmed this pattern in further families: affected individuals carried homozygous or compound heterozygous mutations, and the mutations segregated with the disease. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Servian-Morilla et al. (2016) carried out genomewide SNP genotyping in a consanguineous Spanish family with LGMD. They identified a single 14.6-Mb region of homozygosity on chromosome 3q13.13-q21.2, flanked by rs13081374 and rs2333038. This region was identical by descent in the 4 affected sibs and absent in their healthy sister, and it contained 112 protein-coding genes, including POGLUT1.

### Molecular Genetics

In 4 sibs with autosomal recessive LGMD from a consanguineous Spanish family, Servian-Morilla et al. (2016) combined homozygosity mapping with whole-exome sequencing and identified a homozygous c.699T-G transversion in the POGLUT1 gene, resulting in an asp233-to-glu (D233E) substitution. The mutation segregated with the disease. It was absent from the ExAC database and from 919 DNA samples representing 57 populations worldwide. The affected residue lies in the CAP10 domain and is highly conserved across vertebrates. The mutation did not change POGLUT1 mRNA or protein levels or the protein's localization to the endoplasmic reticulum. The purified mutant protein had markedly reduced O-glucosyltransferase activity toward an EGF repeat of factor IX and toward EGF repeats of mouse Notch1, and reduced O-xylosyltransferase activity, but some residual activity remained. Patient muscle showed decreased NOTCH1 intracellular domain and HES1 mRNA, decreased PAX7 mRNA, and fewer PAX7+ cells. Patient primary myoblasts proliferated slowly, had fewer self-renewing cells, and differentiated more readily. In immortalized patient myoblasts, overexpression of the NOTCH1 intracellular domain rescued the proliferation and differentiation defects but did not restore the number of PAX7+ cells. In Drosophila lacking Rumi (Poglut1) activity, wild-type human POGLUT1 rescued the temperature-sensitive defects in indirect flight muscle development, whereas the D233E mutant gave only a weak and variable rescue.

Using next-generation sequencing, Servian-Morilla et al. (2020) identified 9 missense mutations and 1 nonsense mutation in the POGLUT1 gene, in the homozygous or compound heterozygous state, in 15 patients from 9 families with LGMD. These included the 4 original patients homozygous for D233E. Homozygous missense mutations were found in families 3 to 7 and family 9: I129T, R98W, Y57C, W308L, C102F, and R183W. Two affected sibs in family 8 were compound heterozygous for Y156N and a c.552G-A transition causing a trp184-to-ter (W184X) substitution. The patient with congenital onset (family 2) carried R279Q. The text described this patient as homozygous for R279Q, but the clinical table and figures listed him as compound heterozygous for D233E and R279Q. All variants were confirmed by Sanger sequencing, and segregation with disease was confirmed in affected and unaffected relatives. Each novel variant was either absent from ExAC or present in the heterozygous state in 1 or 2 individuals. Functional studies supported pathogenicity. The R183W and Y57C mutants were secreted normally from HEK293T cells but had much lower enzyme activity than wild type. The I129T, R98W, C102F, and W308L mutants were poorly secreted and showed little or no rescue of Notch1 EGF1-18 secretion in POGLUT1-knockout HEK293T cells. In the Drosophila myogenesis rescue assay, R183W, I129T, and Y57C gave only partial rescue, similar to D233E, and C102F did not rescue the muscle phenotype. Primary myoblasts from the patient carrying D233E and R279Q proliferated less and differentiated more readily than control myoblasts.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Servian-Morilla et al. (2020) found no relevant intra- or interfamilial variability in clinical features among patients with adult onset. The main variability across the cohort was age at onset. Compared with the adult-onset cases, the patient with congenital onset, who carried D233E and R279Q, had faster and more severe progression and additional features: facial involvement, ptosis, and mild contractures.

### Pathogenesis

Servian-Morilla et al. (2016) proposed that the main disease mechanism is loss of satellite cells caused by reduced Notch signaling, which results from decreased O-glucosylation of Notch by mutant POGLUT1. They argued that alpha-dystroglycan hypoglycosylation is probably secondary for several reasons. Alpha-dystroglycan has no POGLUT1 target sites. Its glycosylation and laminin binding were normal in patient fibroblasts. Patient muscle bound agrin normally, and the basement membrane was normal. In addition, inhibiting Notch signaling in differentiating C2C12 cells reduced glycosylated alpha-dystroglycan. Muscles from patients with secondary dystroglycanopathies did not show the reduced Notch activation or the decrease in PAX7+ cells seen in the POGLUT1 patients. Servian-Morilla et al. (2020) concluded that altered satellite cell biology caused by reduced Notch1 signaling leads to the muscular dystrophy, probably with an additional contribution from alpha-dystroglycan hypoglycosylation.

### Diagnosis

Servian-Morilla et al. (2020) proposed that LGMDR21 should be considered, and POGLUT1 sequenced, in a patient with recessive LGMD who has the 'inside-to-outside' MRI pattern of fatty degeneration in the thigh muscles together with a muscle biopsy showing alpha-dystroglycan hypoglycosylation and reduced NOTCH1 intracellular domain and PAX7+ cells. They noted that normal or mildly elevated CK may help distinguish LGMDR21 from other recessive LGMDs, which usually have very high CK. They also cautioned that in collagen VI-related myopathies, early changes in the vastus lateralis are most evident in the outer areas of the muscle, which is the opposite of the POGLUT1 pattern. ⚠︎

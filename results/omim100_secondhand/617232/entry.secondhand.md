### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive limb-girdle muscular dystrophy-21 (LGMDR21) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the POGLUT1 gene on chromosome 3q.

### Description

Autosomal recessive limb-girdle muscular dystrophy-21 (LGMDR21) is a progressive muscle disorder. Weakness is mainly proximal and predominantly affects the lower limbs. Age at onset ranges from congenital and infantile to adult, and many patients eventually use a wheelchair. Scapular winging is common. Serum creatine kinase is usually normal or only mildly elevated. Muscle imaging shows a distinctive 'inside-to-outside' pattern of fatty degeneration of the thigh muscles. Muscle biopsy shows reduced alpha-dystroglycan glycosylation, reduced activated NOTCH1, and a reduced number of satellite cells (Servian-Morilla et al., 2016; Servian-Morilla et al., 2020).

### Nomenclature

Straub et al. (2018) reported a revised nomenclature and classification of the limb-girdle muscular dystrophies, the outcome of an international workshop. Servian-Morilla et al. (2020) referred to the POGLUT1-related disorder as LGMD2Z or LGMD R21 POGLUT1-related.

### Clinical Features

Servian-Morilla et al. (2016) studied a consanguineous family from southern Spain in which 4 of 5 sibs had limb-girdle muscular dystrophy. Muscle weakness was mainly in the proximal lower limbs and began in the third decade. The disease progressed to scapular winging and wheelchair confinement. Serum creatine kinase was normal in 3 patients and mildly elevated in 1. Muscle biopsies from all 4 affected sibs ranged from very mild myopathic changes to classic dystrophic pathology. Proteins typically affected in myopathies were expressed normally, except for a reduction in alpha-dystroglycan. Muscle MRI of the legs showed early fatty replacement of the internal regions of the thigh muscles, sparing the external areas. This 'from inside-to-outside' pattern of fatty degeneration progressed over the years and did not match the patterns typical of other muscular dystrophies. Electron microscopy showed normal muscle ultrastructure with normal basement membrane compaction. The patients had no skin abnormalities.

Servian-Morilla et al. (2020) reported 15 patients with LGMDR21 from 9 unrelated families, including the original Spanish family. The families came from Spain, Bulgaria, Italy, Germany, the USA, Iran, and the United Arab Emirates, and 4 were known to be consanguineous. One patient had a congenital presentation, 2 had onset before 3 years of age, and 12 had adult onset (58.3% in the second decade, 25% in the third decade, and 16.6% in the fourth to fifth decade). In their table, 2 of the 4 sibs of the original family were listed as having onset in the second decade, whereas Servian-Morilla et al. (2016) had described onset in the third decade. Weakness was proximal, clearly predominant in the lower limbs, and slowly progressive. Seven patients (46.6%) with onset in their teens were confined to a wheelchair between the third and fifth decades. Mild to moderate scapular winging was present in 11 of the 15 patients. The congenital case presented with hypotonia and proximal lower limb weakness after gait acquisition, followed by mild weakness, wasting, and contractures of the upper limbs. This patient also had mild facial weakness, ptosis, and nasal voice. Six patients had mild to severe respiratory involvement from the mid-forties onward, regardless of age at onset, and 2 of them needed ventilatory support at night. No significant bulbar, cardiac, or other systemic manifestations were found. Serum CK was normal except in 4 patients, who had mild elevations of 2 to 6 times normal. Other findings in individual patients included elbow or ankle contractures, myopathic facies, arched palate, calf hypertrophy, scoliosis, and hyperlordosis. One patient with infantile onset had nystagmus and retinitis pigmentosa. Intra- and interfamilial variability was not relevant within the adult-onset group, and age at onset was the main source of variability. ⚠︎

Servian-Morilla et al. (2020) had muscle imaging of the lower limbs for 11 of the 15 patients, and MRI or CT showed the 'from inside-to-outside' pattern of fatty degeneration in all of them. The inner segments of the muscles were affected first, specifically in the vastus lateralis, medialis, and intermedius, the posterior thigh compartment, and the gastrocnemius. The pattern was most evident early in the disease but could still be seen in the vastus lateralis at end stage. The lower leg muscles were better preserved than the thigh muscles. Muscle biopsies were studied from 9 patients in 6 families. Vastus lateralis biopsies showed a dystrophic pattern, and biceps brachii biopsies showed mild myopathic features, with lobulated fibers in some cases. All biopsies showed reduced glycosylated alpha-dystroglycan at the sarcolemma with normal staining for the alpha-dystroglycan core. Laminin binding was reduced, NOTCH1 intracellular domain (NICD) levels were decreased, and PAX7-positive satellite cells were fewer. Patient muscles showed no or only a few scattered regenerating fibers positive for embryonic myosin heavy chain, whereas disease controls had many.

### Inheritance

Servian-Morilla et al. (2016) established autosomal recessive inheritance of LGMDR21 in a consanguineous family in which affected sibs were homozygous for a POGLUT1 mutation. Servian-Morilla et al. (2020) found that the disease segregated with homozygous or compound heterozygous POGLUT1 variants in 8 more families and identified no asymptomatic carriers.

### Mapping

Servian-Morilla et al. (2016) performed genomewide SNP genotyping in the 4 affected sibs and their unaffected sister. They found a single region of homozygosity identical by descent in the affected sibs that was absent in the healthy sister. This 14.6-Mb region on chromosome 3q13.13-q21.2 was flanked by SNPs rs13081374 and rs2333038 and contained 112 protein-coding genes.

### Molecular Genetics

Servian-Morilla et al. (2016) performed whole-exome sequencing in 2 patients and their healthy sister from a consanguineous Spanish family with LGMDR21. The 4 affected sibs were homozygous for a missense mutation in the POGLUT1 gene (D233E), which lay within the region of homozygosity and segregated with the disease. The mutation was not found in the ExAC database or in 919 DNA samples from 57 populations worldwide. Mutant protein levels and subcellular localization were normal. However, the mutant protein had markedly reduced O-glucosyltransferase activity toward EGF repeats from factor IX and mouse Notch1 and reduced O-xylosyltransferase activity, though some residual activity remained. Wildtype human POGLUT1 rescued the temperature-sensitive flight muscle defects of Drosophila rumi mutants, but the mutant protein gave only weak and variable rescue.

Servian-Morilla et al. (2020) found 10 recessive POGLUT1 variants by next-generation sequencing (gene panels or whole-exome sequencing) in 15 patients from 9 families with LGMDR21, and confirmed them by Sanger sequencing. Nine variants were missense and 1 was nonsense, and each was present in the homozygous or compound heterozygous state. The 4 sibs of the original family were homozygous for D233E. One patient with congenital onset was compound heterozygous for D233E and R279Q, a variant previously reported in the heterozygous state in a patient with Dowling-Degos disease. Patients from 6 families were homozygous for I129T, R98W, Y57C, W308L, C102F, or R183W. R183W had also previously been reported in Dowling-Degos disease. Two affected brothers were compound heterozygous for Y156N and W184X. The novel variants were absent from ExAC or found in the heterozygous state in only 1 or 2 of more than 60,000 non-Finnish European controls. In transfected HEK293T cells, the R183W and Y57C mutants were secreted normally but had much lower enzymatic activity than wildtype. The I129T, R98W, C102F, and W308L mutants were poorly secreted, suggesting destabilization, and gave little or no rescue of Notch1 EGF1-18 secretion from POGLUT1-knockout cells. In Drosophila rumi mutants, R183W, I129T, and Y57C gave only partial rescue of indirect flight muscle development, similar to D233E, and C102F gave no rescue. The W184X mutation was predicted to remove the substrate-binding site.

### Pathogenesis

Servian-Morilla et al. (2016) found reduced NOTCH1 intracellular domain, reduced HES1 and PAX7 mRNA, and fewer PAX7-positive satellite cells in muscle from patients homozygous for the D233E mutation. These changes were not seen in muscle from patients with other muscular dystrophies or with secondary dystroglycanopathies. In patient muscle, alpha-dystroglycan glycosylation and laminin binding were reduced, but agrin binding was normal. In patient skin fibroblasts, alpha-dystroglycan glycosylation and laminin binding were normal. Primary myoblasts from patients proliferated slowly, had fewer self-renewing PAX7-positive cells, and differentiated more readily, with increased fusion and myogenin expression. In immortalized patient myoblasts, overexpression of the NOTCH1 intracellular domain reversed the proliferation defect and the facilitated differentiation, but it did not restore the number of PAX7-positive cells. In differentiating C2C12 cells, inhibiting Notch reduced alpha-dystroglycan glycosylation. Servian-Morilla et al. (2016) concluded that Notch-dependent loss of satellite cells is a key disease mechanism and that alpha-dystroglycan hypoglycosylation is secondary.

Servian-Morilla et al. (2020) cultured primary myoblasts from a patient compound heterozygous for D233E and R279Q. These cells showed reduced proliferation, fewer proliferating and self-renewing cells, and more differentiating cells, with increased myogenin expression and fusion index. The authors concluded that altered satellite cell biology caused by reduced NOTCH1 signaling causes the muscular dystrophy, probably with an added contribution from alpha-dystroglycan hypoglycosylation.

### Diagnosis

Servian-Morilla et al. (2020) proposed muscle MRI as a useful screening tool for LGMDR21 because the 'inside-to-outside' pattern was seen in every patient and did not match the patterns of other muscular dystrophies. They noted that in collagen VI-related myopathies, degeneration of the vastus lateralis begins in the outer areas of the muscle. In POGLUT1-related disease, by contrast, it begins inside and also involves the posterior thigh muscles. They recommended sequencing POGLUT1 when a patient with recessive LGMD has alpha-dystroglycan hypoglycosylation, reduced PAX7-positive cells and NICD levels on muscle biopsy, and this MRI pattern. Usually normal CK levels could also help distinguish LGMDR21 from other recessive LGMDs.

**.0001 MUSCULAR DYSTROPHY, LIMB-GIRDLE, AUTOSOMAL RECESSIVE 21**  
POGLUT1, ASP233GLU [c.699T>G; p.D233E]

Servian-Morilla et al. (2016) studied 4 sibs from a consanguineous Spanish family with LGMDR21. They were homozygous for a c.699T-G transversion in the POGLUT1 gene, resulting in an asp233-to-glu (D233E) substitution in the CAP10 domain. The mutation was found by homozygosity mapping and exome sequencing and segregated with the disease. It was absent from ExAC and from 919 samples from 57 populations. The mutant protein was expressed normally but had markedly reduced O-glucosyltransferase and O-xylosyltransferase activity, with some residual activity. It only weakly rescued muscle development in Drosophila rumi mutants. Patient muscle showed decreased Notch signaling and fewer satellite cells.

Servian-Morilla et al. (2020) found D233E in compound heterozygosity with R279Q in a 24-year-old man with congenital-onset LGMDR21. Primary myoblasts from this patient showed decreased proliferation and facilitated differentiation.

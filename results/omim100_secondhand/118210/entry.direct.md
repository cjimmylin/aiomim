### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that axonal Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2A1 (CMT2A1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the KIF1B gene on chromosome 1p35-p36. ⚠︎

### Description

Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2 (CMT2) is an inherited peripheral neuropathy. It has a variable age of onset and normal or slightly reduced nerve conduction velocity, and it differs from CMT type 1 both pathologically and genetically (Ben Othmane et al., 1993). CMT2A1 has been associated with mutations in the kinesin motor gene KIF1B (Zhao et al., 2001). Xu et al. (2018) noted that KIF1B mutations had been reported in only a few pedigrees and that the gene responsible for CMT2A remained controversial.

### Clinical Features

Ben Othmane et al. (1993) described CMT2 as an inherited peripheral neuropathy with variable age of onset and normal or slightly diminished nerve conduction velocity. They studied 6 large families with autosomal dominant CMT2.

Saito et al. (1997) described 2 Japanese families with CMT2A. Affected members had weakness of similar degree in the posterior and anterior tibial muscles. Tendon reflexes were preserved in the upper extremities but reduced or absent in the lower extremities, and the peripheral nerves were not enlarged. Only 20% of affected individuals had sensory disturbance, which was mild. The authors used a median nerve motor conduction velocity greater than 38 m/sec to define CMT2.

Xu et al. (2018) reported 2 independent pedigrees with hereditary neuropathy diagnosed as CMT2. Patient 1 was a left-handed Caucasian man diagnosed at age 55 years. He had a progressive, symmetric, length-dependent, predominantly axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy with intermittent cranial nerve involvement, including Bell palsy, as well as hearing loss. Brain MRI was normal. Electrophysiologic studies showed a severe symmetric length-dependent axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy with acute and chronic features and a mixed axonal and demyelinating neuropathy of the right radial nerve. Median nerve conduction velocity was 38.9 m/s. His 3 brothers, 3 sisters, and mother had no symptoms, and his father had died in an accident at a young age.

Patient 2 in the report by Xu et al. (2018) was a left-handed Caucasian man from an American pedigree, diagnosed at age 40 years. He had mental retardation, bipolar disorder, hypertension, cystic kidney disease, and a right basal ganglia lacune. Brain MRI showed thinning of the posterior half of the corpus callosum. Electromyography and nerve conduction studies indicated a predominantly length-dependent axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy. His brother had the same combination of mental retardation, psychiatric disease, kidney disease, and axonal CMT. Their mother had CMT and mild cognitive dysfunction and died in her sixties. Their father was asymptomatic at age 72 years.

### Inheritance

Ben Othmane et al. (1993) performed linkage analysis in families with autosomal dominant CMT2, and Hentati et al. (1992) studied autosomal dominant forms of the neuronal type of CMT. Xu et al. (2018) reported a heterozygous KIF1B mutation in a pedigree consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Mapping

Hentati et al. (1992) studied 2 CMT2 pedigrees. They excluded the CMT2 locus from the chromosome 17 region containing the CMT1A gene (lod = -2.80 at theta = 0.05 for D17S58). They also obtained negative lod scores for the chromosome 1 region containing the CMT1B gene (lod = -3.09 at theta = 0.05 for D1S61). They concluded that the CMT2 gene was not allelic to either of the CMT1 genes mapped at that time.

Ben Othmane et al. (1993) performed linkage analysis in 6 large families with autosomal dominant CMT2. They found linkage, with evidence of heterogeneity, to microsatellite markers (D1S160, D1S170, D1S244, D1S228, and D1S199) on distal chromosome 1p. On admixture analysis, 3 families had posterior probabilities of 1.0, 0.98, and 0.88 of being of the linked type. Multipoint analysis in these families placed the CMT2A gene most likely in the interval flanked by D1S244 and D1S228, with odds of about 70:1 in favor of that interval.

Saito et al. (1997) performed linkage analysis in 2 Japanese CMT2 families and obtained a maximum cumulative multipoint lod score of 3.69 at D1S244, within the CMT2A region on 1p35-p36. Linkage to the CMT2B and CMT2D loci was excluded. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Hentati et al. (1992) found evidence of genetic heterogeneity between the hypertrophic (CMT1) and neuronal (CMT2) forms of Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease. Ben Othmane et al. (1993) found that only some autosomal dominant CMT2 families were linked to chromosome 1p, which indicated that at least 2 different genes cause the CMT2 phenotype.

Xu et al. (2018) noted that mutations in the MFN2 gene, which lies in the same genomic interval as KIF1B, cause the most common known axonal form of CMT, designated CMT2A2.

### Molecular Genetics

Zhao et al. (2001) found a loss-of-function mutation in the motor domain of the KIF1B gene in patients with CMT2A. The disease had previously been mapped to an interval containing KIF1B. According to a later summary by Xu et al. (2018), the mutation was found in 1 pedigree and was a gln98-to-leu (Q98L) substitution in the middle of the P loop, the conserved ATP-binding site of the kinesin motor.

Xu et al. (2018) studied 2 independent pedigrees with hereditary neuropathy and CMT2A symptoms using a sequencing panel of 33 genes related to CMT2 and neuropathy, including MFN2. In affected members of both pedigrees they found the same heterozygous c.3260A-G transition in the KIF1B gene, resulting in a tyr1087-to-cys (Y1087C) substitution in the KIF1B-beta isoform. In the American pedigree, the mutation was present in the proband and his affected brother. The 1000 Genomes minor allele frequency of the variant was 0.0395. Of the variants in the 33 genes, it was the only one that the PolyPhen-2 and SIFT programs predicted to be pathogenic. Tyr1087 lies within the stalk-domain region (residues 885-1,410) that Xu et al. (2018) showed binds the IGF1R beta subunit directly. In GST-pulldown assays, the mutation reduced binding of KIF1B-beta to IGF1R but not to synaptophysin or DENN/MADD. In Kif1b-null mouse hippocampal neurons, the Y1087C mutant only partially restored axonal outgrowth, surface expression of IGF1R in the axon, IGF-I-induced Akt phosphorylation, and transport of IGF1R-containing vesicles. The mutation did not change motor velocity. Xu et al. (2018) noted that their findings contrasted with earlier negative predictions for this variant. They concluded that the mutation is at least responsible for increased susceptibility to neuropathy through reduced IGF-I signaling.

### Pathogenesis

Zhao et al. (2001) concluded that a mutated motor protein causing defective axonal transport can underlie human peripheral neuropathy. Xu et al. (2018) showed that KIF1B-beta transports IGF1R-containing vesicles along the axon, separately from synaptic vesicle precursors. Kif1b-null neurons had reduced surface IGF1R, impaired IGF-I-induced Akt and ERK signaling, and impaired axonal outgrowth. Constitutively active Ras V12 rescued the outgrowth defect. Xu et al. (2018) proposed that impaired IGF1R transport and signaling contributes to neuropathy.

### Animal Model

Zhao et al. (2001) found that Kif1b knockout mice died at birth from apnea due to nervous system defects. In culture, death of knockout neurons was rescued by expression of the beta isoform. Kif1b heterozygous mice had a defect in transport of synaptic vesicle precursors and developed progressive muscle weakness resembling human neuropathy. Xu et al. (2018) stated that, in the Zhao et al. (2001) study, 1-year-old heterozygous mice had significantly shorter retention times on the rotarod test than wildtype mice. Xu et al. (2018) also confirmed malformation of the corpus callosum in Kif1b-null mice.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CHARCOT-MARIE-TOOTH DISEASE, AXONAL, TYPE 2A1**  
KIF1B, GLN98LEU [Q98L]

Zhao et al. (2001) found a loss-of-function mutation in the motor domain of the KIF1B gene in patients with CMT2A. As summarized by Xu et al. (2018), this mutation was a gln98-to-leu (Q98L) substitution in KIF1B-beta, found in 1 CMT2A1 pedigree. It lies in the middle of the P loop, the conserved ATP-binding site of the kinesin motor. Xu et al. (2018) also found that the Q98L mutant failed to restore axonal outgrowth in Kif1b-null mouse hippocampal neurons.

**.0002 CHARCOT-MARIE-TOOTH DISEASE, AXONAL, TYPE 2A1**  
KIF1B, TYR1087CYS [c.3260A>G; p.Tyr1087Cys; Y1087C]

Xu et al. (2018) studied 2 independent pedigrees with hereditary neuropathy and CMT2A symptoms. Using a 33-gene neuropathy panel, they found a heterozygous c.3260A-G transition in the KIF1B gene, resulting in a tyr1087-to-cys (Y1087C) substitution in KIF1B-beta. In an American pedigree, the mutation was present in the proband, who had axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy, mental retardation, bipolar disorder, cystic kidney disease, and a thin corpus callosum, and in his similarly affected brother. Their mother had CMT. The variant had a 1000 Genomes minor allele frequency of 0.0395. The mutation reduced binding of KIF1B-beta to IGF1R but not to synaptophysin or DENN/MADD. In Kif1b-null neurons it only partially restored axonal outgrowth, surface IGF1R, IGF-I-induced Akt phosphorylation, and transport of IGF1R vesicles. Xu et al. (2018) concluded that the mutation is at least responsible for increased susceptibility to neuropathy.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2A1 (CMT2A1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the KIF1B gene on chromosome 1p35-p36. ⚠︎

### Description

Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2 (CMT2) is an inherited peripheral neuropathy with variable age of onset and normal or slightly diminished nerve conduction velocity (Ben Othmane et al., 1993). It is defined by a median nerve motor conduction velocity greater than 38 m/sec (Saito et al., 1997), distinguishing it from demyelinating CMT1, which has nerve conduction velocities below 38 m/s (Xu et al., 2018). CMT2A1 is the autosomal dominant axonal form mapped to chromosome 1p and associated with mutation in KIF1B, a kinesin motor protein; the role of KIF1B in CMT2A has been regarded as controversial because mutations have been reported in only a limited number of pedigrees (Xu et al., 2018). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Ben Othmane et al. (1993) studied 6 large families with autosomal dominant CMT2, which they characterized as an inherited peripheral neuropathy with variable age of onset and normal or slightly diminished nerve conduction velocity, pathologically distinct from CMT1.

Saito et al. (1997) described 2 Japanese families with CMT2A. Clinical features included similar levels of weakness of the posterior and anterior tibial muscles, tendon reflexes preserved in the upper extremities but reduced or absent in the lower extremities, no enlargement of the peripheral nerves, and mild sensory disturbance in only 20% of affected individuals.

Xu et al. (2018) reported 2 unrelated patients with hereditary neuropathy diagnosed as CMT2. Patient 1, a left-handed Caucasian man diagnosed at age 55 years, had a progressive, symmetric, length-dependent, predominantly axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy with intermittent cranial nerve involvement, including Bell palsy, as well as hearing loss. Brain MRI was normal, median nerve conduction velocity was 38.9 m/s, and the right radial nerve showed mixed axonal and demyelinating neuropathy. His 3 brothers, 3 sisters, and mother were reported to be asymptomatic; his father had died in an accident at a young age. Patient 2, a left-handed Caucasian man diagnosed at age 40 years, had a predominantly length-dependent axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy on electromyography and nerve conduction studies, as well as mental retardation, bipolar disorder, hypertension, cystic kidney disease, and a right basal ganglia lacune; brain MRI showed thinning of the posterior half of the corpus callosum. His brother had mental retardation, psychiatric disease, kidney disease, and axonal CMT, and their mother, who died in her 60s, had been clinically diagnosed with CMT2 and had mild cognitive dysfunction.

### Inheritance

The families with CMT2 linked to chromosome 1p studied by Ben Othmane et al. (1993) showed autosomal dominant inheritance. In 1 of the families reported by Xu et al. (2018), the KIF1B mutation was found in heterozygous state and the disorder was transmitted in an autosomal dominant pattern, with an affected mother and 2 affected sons.

### Mapping

Hentati et al. (1992) studied 1 CMT1 and 2 CMT2 pedigrees and excluded the CMT2 locus from the region of chromosome 17 containing the CMT1A gene (lod score = -2.80 at theta = 0.05 for D17S58) and from the region of chromosome 1 containing the CMT1B gene (lod score = -3.09 at theta = 0.05 for D1S61), indicating that CMT2 is not allelic to either of the CMT1 genes mapped at that time.

By linkage analysis in 6 large families with autosomal dominant CMT2, Ben Othmane et al. (1993) demonstrated linkage and heterogeneity to microsatellite markers (D1S160, D1S170, D1S244, D1S228, and D1S199) in the distal region of the short arm of chromosome 1. Three families (DUK662, DUK1241, and 1523) had posterior probabilities of 1.0, 0.98, and 0.88, respectively, of being of the linked type. Multipoint analysis of the linked families placed the CMT2A gene most likely in the interval flanked by D1S244 and D1S228 (odds of approximately 70:1 of lying within versus outside that interval).

Saito et al. (1997) performed linkage analysis in 2 Japanese families with CMT2 and obtained a highest cumulative multipoint lod score of 3.69 at D1S244, consistent with the CMT2A locus on 1p35-p36. The CMT2B and CMT2D loci were excluded using markers D3S1551, D3S1290, and D7S484.

### Heterogeneity

Hentati et al. (1992) provided evidence for genetic heterogeneity between the hypertrophic (CMT1) and neuronal (CMT2) forms of Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease. Ben Othmane et al. (1993) found significant evidence for heterogeneity among CMT2 families by admixture analysis, with only some families linked to chromosome 1p, suggesting that the CMT2 phenotype results from at least 2 different genes.

Xu et al. (2018) noted that mutations in MFN2, located in the same genomic interval, are responsible for the most common known axonal form of CMT, designated CMT2A2. MFN2 was not mutated in the 2 pedigrees with the KIF1B mutation that they studied.

### Molecular Genetics

Zhao et al. (2001) noted that CMT2A had been mapped to an interval containing the KIF1B gene and identified a loss-of-function mutation in the motor domain of KIF1B (Q98L) in patients with CMT2A from a Japanese family. The mutation lies in the conserved ATP-binding site of the motor domain and caused a significant reduction in ATPase and in vitro motor activities. Xu et al. (2018) stated that this mutation, located in the middle of the P loop, resulted in perinuclear accumulation of the mutant protein. †

In affected members of 2 unrelated families with hereditary neuropathy and CMT2 symptoms, Xu et al. (2018) identified a heterozygous mutation in the KIF1B gene (Y1087C) by sequencing of a panel of 33 CMT2/neuropathy-related genes. In 1 family, the mutation was confirmed in the proband and his affected brother; their mother had been clinically diagnosed with CMT2. In silico programs (PolyPhen-2 and SIFT) predicted this variant to be the only disease-causing variant among the 33 genes, which included MFN2. The variant had a minor allele frequency of 0.0395 in the 1000 Genomes Project database. Tyr1087 is located in the KIF1B-beta stalk region that binds IGF1R, and the mutation reduced binding to IGF1R but not to synaptophysin or DENN/MADD. In Kif1b-null mouse hippocampal neurons, the mutant protein was impaired in rescuing axonal outgrowth, IGF-I-induced Akt phosphorylation, surface axonal IGF1R expression, and anterograde transport of IGF1R-containing vesicles, although motor velocity was unaffected. Xu et al. (2018) concluded that the mutation is at least responsible for increasing susceptibility to neuropathy by reducing IGF-I signal transduction. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Zhao et al. (2001) concluded that the identification of a loss-of-function mutation in the KIF1B motor domain in CMT2A patients indicated that defects in axonal transport due to a mutated motor protein can underlie human peripheral neuropathy.

Xu et al. (2018) identified IGF1R as a direct binding partner and cargo of KIF1B-beta, with the IGF1R-beta intracellular domain binding residues 885 to 1,410 in the KIF1B-beta stalk domain; IGF1R did not bind the corresponding region of KIF1A. IGF1R-containing vesicles were transported independently of synaptic vesicle precursors. Kif1b-null mouse neurons showed impaired axonal outgrowth, decreased axonal surface IGF1R, insensitivity to IGF-I, and reduced IGF-I-induced Akt and ERK phosphorylation; axonal outgrowth was rescued by constitutively active Ras V12. Xu et al. (2018) proposed that impaired KIF1B-beta-mediated IGF1R transport and IGF-I signaling contribute to the neuropathic pathogenesis.

### Animal Model

Zhao et al. (2001) found that Kif1b knockout mice died at birth from apnea due to nervous system defects. Death of knockout neurons in culture could be rescued by expression of the KIF1B-beta isoform. Kif1b heterozygous mice had a defect in transporting synaptic vesicle precursors and developed progressive muscle weakness similar to human neuropathies.

Xu et al. (2018) noted that 1-year-old Kif1b heterozygous mice had significantly reduced retention times on the rotarod test compared with wildtype mice, and confirmed by histologic analysis that the corpus callosum of Kif1b-null mice was malformed. Hippocampal neurons from Kif1b-null mice showed impaired axonal outgrowth and reduced axonal transport and surface expression of IGF1R.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CHARCOT-MARIE-TOOTH DISEASE, AXONAL, TYPE 2A1**  
KIF1B, GLN98LEU [p.Gln98Leu]

In affected members of a Japanese family with CMT2A1, Zhao et al. (2001) identified a gln98-to-leu (Q98L) mutation in the motor domain of the KIF1B gene. The mutation affects the conserved ATP-binding site of the kinesin motor and caused a significant reduction in ATPase and in vitro motor activities, indicating loss of function. Xu et al. (2018) stated that the mutation, in the middle of the P loop, resulted in perinuclear accumulation of the mutant protein, and found that the Q98L mutant failed to rescue impaired axonal outgrowth in Kif1b-null mouse hippocampal neurons. †

**.0002 CHARCOT-MARIE-TOOTH DISEASE, AXONAL, TYPE 2A1**  
KIF1B, TYR1087CYS [c.3260A>G; p.Tyr1087Cys]

In affected individuals from 2 unrelated families with hereditary neuropathy diagnosed as CMT2, Xu et al. (2018) identified a heterozygous c.3260A-G transition in the KIF1B gene, resulting in a tyr1087-to-cys (Y1087C) substitution in the stalk region of KIF1B-beta. The mutation was identified by sequencing of a panel of 33 neuropathy-related genes and was the only variant predicted to be disease-causing; it had a minor allele frequency of 0.0395 in the 1000 Genomes Project database. In 1 family, the mutation was present in the proband and his affected brother, whose mother had CMT2. The mutation reduced binding of KIF1B-beta to IGF1R without affecting binding to synaptophysin or DENN/MADD, and the mutant was impaired in rescuing axonal outgrowth, IGF-I-induced Akt phosphorylation, axonal surface IGF1R expression, and IGF1R transport in Kif1b-null mouse neurons. Xu et al. (2018) concluded that the mutation is at least responsible for increasing susceptibility to neuropathy. ⚠︎

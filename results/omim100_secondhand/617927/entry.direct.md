### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII (OFD18) is caused by homozygous mutation in the IFT57 gene on chromosome 3.

### Description

Orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII (OFD18) is a form of oral-facial-digital syndrome that also involves skeletal dysplasia. Oral anomalies include supernumerary frenulae and missing incisors. Facial features include medial clefting of the upper lip. Digital anomalies include brachydactyly with mesophalangeal shortening and pre- and postaxial polydactyly. Patients also have short stature and other skeletal findings, such as short femoral necks and genu valgum, while intelligence is normal (Phadke et al., 1999; Thevenon et al., 2016).

### Clinical Features

Phadke et al. (1999) reported a consanguineous family with a new type of oral-facial-digital syndrome that included acromelic short stature and genu valgum, features that overlapped with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome. One of the sibs had urinary incontinence and growth hormone deficiency. The authors noted that these features had not been reported before in any type of OFDS.

Thevenon et al. (2016) reassessed the family reported by Phadke et al. (1999), which had 3 affected sibs born to healthy consanguineous parents and had been followed up for 15 years. One sib (II-3) died at 17 years of age from a complicated pulmonary infection. Subject II-1, a man, was examined at 25 years of age. His intelligence was normal. He had short stature with a final height of 155 cm (-3 SD), a weight of 45 kg (less than -3 SD), and an occipitofrontal circumference of 50 cm (mean). He had a square face with a short forehead, upslanting palpebral fissures, a high nasal bridge, a pyriform nose, a short philtrum, and a medial cleft of the upper lip. Oral findings were supernumerary frenulae and missing incisors. He had pronounced brachydactyly and postaxial polydactyly of the upper limbs. In the feet there was spacing between the first and second rays. X-rays showed severe mesophalangeal shortening and duplication of the first metatarsal, which was interpreted as preaxial polydactyly. He also had bilateral short femoral necks with varus femurs and symmetric genu valgum, with no vertebral anomalies and no thoracic deformity. Heart, renal, and abdominal ultrasound examinations were normal.

Subject II-2 in the family reported by Thevenon et al. (2016), a woman, was examined at 22 years of age. Her intelligence was normal and puberty was not delayed. She had short stature with a final height of 122 cm (-5 SD), a weight of 37 kg (-2 SD), and an occipitofrontal circumference of 51 cm (mean). She had a square face with a narrow forehead, enophthalmia, upslanting palpebral fissures, and a broad nasal bridge. She also had bilateral hypopigmented pseudoclefts with medial labial clefting, missing incisors, and supernumerary frenulae. She had marked brachydactyly and postaxial polydactyly of all 4 limbs, which was surgically removed in infancy. Hand X-rays showed mesophalangeal shortening and residual right-hand postaxial polydactyly with fused proximal phalanges and bifid distal phalanges. Foot X-rays showed duplication of the first metatarsal and absent intermediate phalanges, suggesting preaxial polydactyly of the lower limbs. Additional skeletal findings were vertebral bodies with medial notches but no platyspondyly, bilateral cervical ribs without a narrow thorax, gracile long bones, and shortened femoral necks. Brain MRI and abdominal and heart ultrasounds were normal. Thevenon et al. (2016) noted that the patients did not have short ribs or a narrow or deformed thorax. They concluded that the phenotype was an unclassified OFDS subtype with skeletal dysplasia rather than Ellis-van Creveld syndrome.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OFD18 in the family reported by Phadke et al. (1999) and Thevenon et al. (2016) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Three sibs born to healthy consanguineous parents were affected, and all 3 carried a homozygous mutation in the IFT57 gene.

### Mapping

Thevenon et al. (2016) performed homozygosity mapping in 2 affected sibs from the consanguineous family originally reported by Phadke et al. (1999). They found shared runs of homozygosity totaling 11 Mb, at chr3:104822019-111671862 and chr10:1372501-6029726 (GRCh37/hg19). Neither interval overlapped genes previously associated with a recessive ciliopathy. The chromosome 10 interval was later excluded by segregation analysis in the third affected sib.

### Molecular Genetics

Thevenon et al. (2016) studied 3 affected sibs from the consanguineous Indian family originally reported by Phadke et al. (1999). Using homozygosity mapping and exome sequencing, they identified a homozygous synonymous c.777G-A mutation (lys259-to-lys; K259K) in the IFT57 gene, which affects the last base of exon 6. Sanger sequencing confirmed that all 3 affected sibs were homozygous for the mutation. The variant was absent from the ExAC database and from 50 healthy individuals from northern India. In patient fibroblasts, the mutation caused a complex splicing pattern. Normal transcript was present, along with 3 abnormal transcripts that were detected only after nonsense-mediated mRNA decay was inhibited: in-frame skipping of exon 6, in-frame skipping of exons 5 and 6, and out-of-frame skipping of exons 4, 5, and 6. Overall, IFT57 mRNA levels in patient fibroblasts were significantly lower than in controls. The number and length of primary cilia were similar to controls. However, IFT57 staining at the ciliary tip was significantly decreased (90% vs 35%) and staining at the basal body was increased, indicating reduced anterograde ciliary transport. After stimulation with a Smoothened agonist, patient fibroblasts showed poor induction of GLI1 expression, indicating impaired sonic hedgehog signaling. The same impairment was seen in control fibroblasts after siRNA knockdown of IFT57. Thevenon et al. (2016) concluded that the mutation was hypomorphic.

Thevenon et al. (2016) found no causative IFT57 mutations by Sanger sequencing in 13 patients with OFDS or in 12 patients with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome who were negative for EVC1 and EVC2 mutations. Exome or ciliome data from more than 172 patients with various ciliopathy phenotypes also showed no biallelic causative IFT57 mutations.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 OROFACIODIGITAL SYNDROME XVIII**  
IFT57, 777G-A [NM_018010.3:c.777G>A; p.Lys259Lys; chr3:g.107910368C>T]

Thevenon et al. (2016) studied 3 affected sibs from the consanguineous Indian family with orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII originally reported by Phadke et al. (1999). All 3 sibs were homozygous for a synonymous c.777G-A transition in the IFT57 gene, which affects the last base of exon 6 (lys259-to-lys; K259K). The mutation was found by homozygosity mapping and exome sequencing, and segregation was confirmed by Sanger sequencing. The variant was absent from the ExAC database and from 50 healthy individuals from northern India. In patient fibroblasts, the mutation caused aberrant splicing, with skipping of exon 6, exons 5 and 6, or exons 4 to 6. It also reduced IFT57 mRNA levels, decreased anterograde ciliary transport, and impaired sonic hedgehog signaling, while ciliary assembly was unaffected.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII (OFD18) is caused by homozygous mutation in the IFT57 gene on chromosome 3.

### Description

Orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII is an autosomal recessive ciliopathy. Its features include oral anomalies such as supernumerary frenulae and missing incisors, median clefting of the upper lip, and pre- and postaxial polydactyly. Unlike other orofaciodigital syndromes, it also includes skeletal dysplasia with short stature, brachymesophalangia, and short femoral necks. Several features overlap with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome (Phadke et al., 1999; Thevenon et al., 2016). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Phadke et al. (1999) reported a consanguineous family with a new variety of oral-facial-digital syndrome associated with acromelic short stature and genu valgum. Some features overlapped with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome. One of the sibs had urinary incontinence and growth hormone deficiency, which the authors noted had not previously been reported in any type of OFDS. The family included 3 affected sibs. †

Thevenon et al. (2016) restudied the family reported by Phadke et al. (1999), which had been followed for 15 years. The 3 affected sibs were born to healthy consanguineous parents. One sib (II-3) died at 17 years of age from a complicated pulmonary infection. Subject II-1, a man examined at 25 years of age, had normal intelligence. His final height was 155 cm (-3 SD), his weight was 45 kg (less than -3 SD), and his occipitofrontal circumference was 50 cm (mean). He had a square face with a short forehead, upslanting palpebral fissures, a high nasal bridge, and a pyriform nose above a short philtrum, together with median clefting of the upper lip. Oral anomalies included supernumerary frenulae and missing incisors. He had pronounced brachydactyly and upper-limb postaxial polydactyly. In the feet, the first and second rays were widely spaced. X-rays showed severe mesophalangeal shortening and duplication of the first metatarsal, which was interpreted as preaxial polydactyly. He also had bilateral short femoral necks with varus femurs and symmetric genu valgum, but no vertebral anomalies. Heart, renal, and abdominal ultrasound examinations were normal.

Subject II-2, a woman examined at 22 years of age, had normal intelligence and normal timing of puberty. Her final height was 122 cm (-5 SD), her weight was 37 kg (-2 SD), and her occipitofrontal circumference was 51 cm (mean). She had a square face with a narrow forehead, enophthalmos with upslanting palpebral fissures, and a broad nasal bridge. She also had bilateral hypopigmented pseudoclefts with median labial clefting, missing incisors, and supernumerary frenulae. She had marked brachydactyly and postaxial polydactyly of all 4 limbs, which was surgically removed in infancy. Hand X-rays showed mesophalangeal shortening and residual right-hand postaxial polydactyly with fused proximal phalanges and bifid terminal phalanges. Foot X-rays showed duplication of the first metatarsal and absent intermediate phalanges, suggesting preaxial polydactyly. Other skeletal findings were vertebral bodies with medial notches without platyspondyly, bilateral cervical ribs without a narrow thorax, gracile long bones, and shortened femoral necks. Neither sib had a thoracic deformity. Brain MRI and abdominal and heart ultrasounds were normal. On review, Thevenon et al. (2016) considered the phenotype to be an unclassified OFDS with skeletal dysplasia rather than Ellis-van Creveld syndrome, because the affected sibs had no rib shortening or narrowing or deformity of the thorax.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OFD18 in the family reported by Phadke et al. (1999) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. The family was consanguineous, and Thevenon et al. (2016) found a mutation in homozygous state in all 3 affected sibs.

### Mapping

Thevenon et al. (2016) performed homozygosity mapping in affected members of the consanguineous family originally reported by Phadke et al. (1999). They identified 11 Mb of shared runs of homozygosity, located at chr3:104822019-111671862 and chr10:1372501-6029726 (GRCh37). Segregation analysis in the third affected sib excluded the chromosome 10 interval.

### Molecular Genetics

Thevenon et al. (2016) studied 3 affected sibs from a consanguineous Indian family with OFD18, originally reported by Phadke et al. (1999). Using homozygosity mapping and exome sequencing, they identified a homozygous synonymous c.777G-A transition in the IFT57 gene, resulting in lys259-to-lys (K259K). The variant affects the last base of exon 6. Sanger sequencing confirmed that all 3 affected sibs were homozygous. The variant was not found in the ExAC database or in 50 healthy individuals from northern India. In patient fibroblasts, the mutation produced a complex splicing pattern. Normal transcript was present, together with 3 abnormal transcripts that were detected only after inhibition of nonsense-mediated mRNA decay. The abnormal transcripts resulted from in-frame skipping of exon 6, in-frame skipping of exons 5 and 6, and out-of-frame skipping of exons 4 to 6. IFT57 mRNA levels in patient fibroblasts were significantly lower than in controls. Primary cilia were normal in number and length. However, IFT57 staining at the ciliary tip was significantly decreased (90% vs 35%; p less than 0.001) and staining at the basal body was increased, indicating defective anterograde ciliary transport. After stimulation with a Smoothened agonist, patient fibroblasts showed poor induction of GLI1 expression, indicating impaired sonic hedgehog signaling. siRNA knockdown of IFT57 in control fibroblasts also abolished this induction. Thevenon et al. (2016) concluded that the mutation was hypomorphic. Sanger sequencing of IFT57 in 13 patients with OFDS and 12 patients with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome found no causative mutations. Exome or ciliome data from more than 172 patients with various ciliopathy phenotypes also identified no biallelic causative mutations.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 OROFACIODIGITAL SYNDROME XVIII**  
IFT57, 777G-A [NM_018010.3:c.777G>A; p.Lys259Lys; chr3:g.107910368C>T]

In 3 sibs with orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII from a consanguineous Indian family, originally reported by Phadke et al. (1999), Thevenon et al. (2016) identified a homozygous c.777G-A transition in the IFT57 gene. The change is synonymous, lys259-to-lys (K259K), and affects the last base of exon 6. It was found by homozygosity mapping and exome sequencing and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. The variant was absent from the ExAC database and from 50 healthy individuals from northern India. In patient fibroblasts, normal transcript was present along with transcripts lacking exon 6, exons 5 and 6, or exons 4 to 6. Overall IFT57 mRNA was reduced. Anterograde ciliary transport was decreased, and sonic hedgehog signaling was impaired.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that atrial standstill-1 (ATRST1) is caused by mutation in the SCN5A gene. Coinheritance of rare polymorphisms in the gene encoding the atrial-specific gap junction protein connexin-40 (Cx40) may modify whether the disorder appears.

### Description

Atrial standstill is a rare arrhythmia that occasionally appears to be genetically determined (Groenewegen et al., 2003). The surface ECG shows no P waves, and the atrium cannot be captured by pacing (Makita et al., 2005). Familial atrial standstill has been reported together with atrioventricular block (Williams et al., 1972) and with syncope, dilated cardiomyopathy, and sudden cardiac death (Fazelifar et al., 2005). † ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Allensworth et al. (1969) described persistent atrial standstill in a family with myocardial disease.

Harrison and Derrick (1969) reviewed atrial standstill and reported 2 new cases that were familial and unusual in nature. One of these patients received epicardial pacing.

Williams et al. (1972) described a familial disease characterized by atrial arrhythmias and atrioventricular block. They called it 'familial atrial cardiomyopathy with heart block', which was the first use of the term 'atrial cardiomyopathy'. †

Amat-y-Leon et al. (1974) reported familial atrial dysrhythmia with A-V block. They studied it with intracellular microelectrode, clinical electrophysiologic, and morphologic methods.

Shah et al. (1992) reported familial total atrial standstill.

Groenewegen et al. (2003) clinically evaluated 44 members of a large family. One deceased relative and 3 living relatives were unambiguously affected by atrial standstill, and all other relatives appeared unaffected.

Fazelifar et al. (2005) reported a family with atrial standstill associated with syncope, dilated cardiomyopathy, and sudden cardiac death.

Makita et al. (2005) studied the family of an apparently sporadic case of atrial standstill. The proband, an 11-year-old boy, had no P waves on the surface ECG, and his right atrium could not be captured by pacing. His father, who carried the same SCN5A mutation, was in normal sinus rhythm. His mother was asymptomatic.

### Inheritance

Familial occurrence of atrial standstill was reported by Allensworth et al. (1969), Harrison and Derrick (1969), Shah et al. (1992), and Fazelifar et al. (2005). Penetrance of SCN5A mutations was incomplete. In the family studied by Groenewegen et al. (2003), 5 carriers of the mutation were unaffected. In the family studied by Makita et al. (2005), the proband's father carried the mutation and was in normal sinus rhythm. Both groups proposed that coinheritance of rare polymorphisms in the connexin-40 gene contributed to the disease, which would make the inheritance digenic. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Groenewegen et al. (2003) screened candidate genes in a large family with atrial standstill. They found a novel mutation in the SCN5A gene, asp1275-to-asn (D1275N), in all 3 affected living relatives and in 5 unaffected relatives. The affected deceased relative was an obligate carrier. They also found 2 closely linked polymorphisms in regulatory regions of the gene for the atrial-specific gap junction protein connexin-40 (Cx40), at nucleotides -44 (G-A) and +71 (A-G). Eight relatives were homozygous for both polymorphisms, a genotype found in only about 7% of control subjects, and 3 of these 8 were affected. The 3 living patients were the only relatives who had inherited both the rare Cx40 genotype and the D1275N mutation. Compared with wildtype channels, D1275N channels showed a small depolarizing shift in activation. In reporter gene analysis, the rare Cx40 genotype reduced reporter gene expression compared with the more common genotype. Groenewegen et al. (2003) proposed that each genetic change has a relatively benign functional effect, but that together they eventually lead to total atrial standstill.

Makita et al. (2005) screened SCN5A and atrial-specific genes, including Cx40, in the family of an 11-year-old boy with atrial standstill. They found a novel SCN5A mutation, leu212-to-pro (L212P), in the proband and in his father, who was in normal sinus rhythm. In whole-cell patch clamp studies in a heterologous expression system, the L212P channel showed a large hyperpolarizing shift in the voltage dependence of both activation (wildtype, -48.1 +/- 0.9 mV; L212P, -63.5 +/- 1.5 mV; P less than 0.001) and inactivation (wildtype, -86.6 +/- 0.9 mV; L212P, -95.6 +/- 0.8 mV; P less than 0.001). Recovery from inactivation was also delayed. The authors then looked for genetic variants that might lessen the effect of L212P. The proband, but not his father, carried Cx40 polymorphisms inherited from his asymptomatic mother. Makita et al. (2005) concluded that SCN5A defects most likely underlie atrial standstill and that coinheritance of Cx40 polymorphisms may modify how the disorder is expressed.

### Clinical Management

Harrison and Derrick (1969) used epicardial pacing in 1 of their 2 patients with familial atrial standstill.

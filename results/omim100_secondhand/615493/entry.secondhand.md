### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive intellectual developmental disorder-37 (MRT37) is caused by homozygous mutation in the ANK3 gene.

### Description

Autosomal recessive intellectual developmental disorder-37 (MRT37) is a neurodevelopmental disorder marked by moderate intellectual disability, an attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)-like phenotype, and behavioral problems. It was described in a consanguineous family carrying a homozygous truncating mutation that affects the longest isoform of ANK3 (Iqbal et al., 2013).

### Clinical Features

Iqbal et al. (2013) reported a consanguineous family in which several members had moderate intellectual disability, an ADHD-like phenotype, and behavioral problems. Affected family members also had severe behavioral problems. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of MRT37 in the consanguineous family reported by Iqbal et al. (2013) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of a consanguineous family with moderate intellectual disability, an ADHD-like phenotype, and behavioral problems, Iqbal et al. (2013) identified a homozygous single-basepair truncating frameshift mutation in the ANK3 gene. The mutation affected only the longest ANK3 isoform. Iqbal et al. (2013) described it as the first familial ANK3 mutation reported. A Drosophila knockdown model showed memory defects, which supported a causal role for the mutation. ⚠︎

In the same report, Iqbal et al. (2013) described an unrelated patient with borderline intelligence, severe ADHD, autism, and sleeping problems. In this patient, a balanced translocation disrupted all isoforms of the ANK3 gene.

### Animal Model

Iqbal et al. (2013) found that knockdown of the ANK3 ortholog in Drosophila caused memory defects. They took this as support for a role of ANK3 in cognitive function.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that adult-onset autosomal dominant leukodystrophy without amyloid angiopathy (ADLDWA) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the CST3 gene, which encodes cystatin C.

### Description

Adult-onset autosomal dominant leukodystrophy without amyloid angiopathy (ADLDWA) is a white matter disorder that begins in mid to older adulthood. Patients have recurrent episodes of hemiplegic migraine with transient unilateral focal deficits, together with slowly progressive motor symptoms and cognitive decline. Some patients have acute clinical deterioration with a prolonged episode of reduced consciousness, which can lead to early death. Brain imaging shows characteristic white matter changes at typical predilection sites, including the globus pallidus. Neuropathology shows cystic degeneration of the white matter without angiopathy (Bergner et al., 2024).

### Clinical Features

Bergner et al. (2024) reported 16 patients from 8 families with an adult-onset leukodystrophy. The patients had recurrent episodes of hemiplegic migraine with transient unilateral focal deficits, along with slowly progressive motor symptoms and cognitive decline in mid to older adulthood. In some patients, an acute clinical deterioration led to a prolonged episode of reduced consciousness and even early death. Bergner et al. (2024) described the radiologic changes as pathognomonic. They involved the deep cerebral white matter but spared a periventricular rim and a rim directly beneath the cortex. Changes were also seen in the middle blade of the corpus callosum, the posterior limb of the internal capsule, the middle cerebellar peduncles, the cerebral peduncles, and specifically the globus pallidus. The clinical and radiologic features differed markedly from those of Icelandic cerebral amyloid angiopathy, which is associated with a different CST3 substitution.

Zhang and Wu (2024) reported Chinese patients with adult-onset leukodystrophy caused by CST3 variants.

### Inheritance

Bergner et al. (2024) showed that the leukodystrophy in their 8 families was transmitted in an autosomal dominant pattern and was caused by heterozygous dominant variants in the CST3 gene. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Bergner et al. (2024) studied 16 patients with adult-onset leukodystrophy from 8 families. Each patient carried 1 of 4 different dominant stop-gain or frameshift variants in the CST3 gene. A structural comparison of the variants suggested that specific truncations of cystatin C cause abnormal protein function, possibly by making the protein more prone to aggregation. Bergner et al. (2024) stated that further studies were needed to confirm this effect on the protein.

Zhang and Wu (2024) reported CST3 variants in Chinese patients with adult-onset leukodystrophy.

### Pathogenesis

Bergner et al. (2024) studied 2 autopsy cases. Histopathology showed no angiopathy. Instead, there was micro- to macrocystic degeneration of the white matter. Astrocytes were activated at early stages, and later showed severe degeneration and loss. Despite the loss of myelin, the number of oligodendrocytes was increased, and some of them were apoptotic.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Hoxha-Aliu syndrome (HXAL) is caused by homozygous null mutation in the ERI1 gene on chromosome 8p23.1. ⚠︎

### Description

Hoxha-Aliu syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder. Its main features are developmental delay or mild intellectual disability and abnormalities of the distal limbs, including brachydactyly, clinodactyly, brachytelephalangy, short metacarpals, nail dysplasia, and syndactyly (Hoxha and Aliu, 2023; Guo et al., 2023). Some patients also had cardiac malformations (Choucair et al., 2017). The disorder results from biallelic null variants in ERI1. Biallelic ERI1 missense variants cause a different and more severe phenotype, spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia (Guo et al., 2023). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Choucair et al. (2017) reported a boy born to consanguineous parents. He had intellectual disability, short stature, dysmorphic facial features, synpolydactyly, and cardiac malformations.

Szot et al. (2021) studied patients with congenital NAD deficiency disorder. One of them (F5.II.1) was a girl homozygous for a missense variant in KYNU who also carried a homozygous loss-of-function variant in ERI1 (see MOLECULAR GENETICS). Her cardiac findings were a perimembranous ventricular septal defect and pulmonary vein stenosis. She had reduction defects of the hands and feet. The right hand showed a transverse terminal limb reduction with a rudimentary fifth digit. The left hand had hypoplastic, deep-set nails on several digits. On the right foot, digits 1 and 5 had no nails and digits 2 to 4 were absent. On the left foot, digits 3 to 5 were absent, digits 1 and 2 had no nails, and there was syndactyly. Other features were microcephaly, global developmental delay, and autism. Craniofacial features included brachycephaly, a flat occiput, bitemporal narrowing, a small nose, and cupped, prominent ears. She also had bilateral inguinal hernias. Szot et al. (2021) noted that her limb and cardiac abnormalities resembled those of the patient reported by Choucair et al. (2017). ⚠︎

Hoxha and Aliu (2023) reported a girl who had mild intellectual disability and eyelid ptosis. Her hand and foot anomalies were brachydactyly, clinodactyly, dysplastic or short nails of the halluces, brachytelephalangy, short metacarpals, and toe syndactyly.

Guo et al. (2023) reported 8 affected individuals from 7 unrelated families with biallelic ERI1 variants. Those with biallelic null variants had mild intellectual disability and digital anomalies, but not the severe spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia seen in the 5 individuals with missense variants.

### Inheritance

Hoxha and Aliu (2023) described the disorder as autosomal recessive. Their patient was homozygous for a nonsense variant in ERI1. The patient reported by Choucair et al. (2017) was born to consanguineous parents and carried a homozygous deletion that included ERI1. Guo et al. (2023) found biallelic ERI1 variants in their affected individuals. ⚠︎

### Cytogenetics

In a boy with intellectual disability, synpolydactyly, and cardiac malformations, Choucair et al. (2017) used chromosomal microarray analysis and found a homozygous deletion at 8p23.1. The deletion contained the microRNA miR-4660 and the ERI1 and MFHAS1 genes. Choucair et al. (2017) discussed how loss of each deleted gene might contribute to the patient's features. They noted that ERI1 is needed for correct patterning of the skeleton by defining HOXC8 expression.

### Molecular Genetics

Szot et al. (2021) used trio exome or genome sequencing in a girl (F5.II.1) with limb reduction defects, cardiac anomalies, microcephaly, developmental delay, and autism. She was homozygous for a missense variant in KYNU, glu206-to-lys (E206K). She was also homozygous for a loss-of-function variant in ERI1, c.514C-T (gln172-to-ter; Q172X). In yeast complementation assays, the KYNU variant was among those with the mildest effect on NAD levels. Szot et al. (2021) suggested that the ERI1 variant might partly explain her distinct phenotype and could confound the role of mild NAD deficiency. They noted that the functional link between ERI1 mutation and these defects had not been assessed.

In a girl with mild intellectual disability, ptosis, and hand and foot anomalies, Hoxha and Aliu (2023) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in the ERI1 gene (K188X). Hoxha and Aliu (2023) stated that this was the first reported case of a disorder associated with an ERI1 variant. ⚠︎

Guo et al. (2023) identified biallelic ERI1 variants in 8 affected individuals from 7 unrelated families. Individuals with biallelic null variants had mild intellectual disability and digital anomalies. The 5 individuals with missense variants had severe spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia. The missense variants caused loss of exoribonuclease activity, which led to defective trimming of the 3-prime end of 5.8S rRNA and decreased degradation of replication-dependent histone mRNAs. Induced pluripotent stem cells derived from affected individuals showed impaired chondrogenesis in vitro, with downregulation of genes that regulate skeletal patterning.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Guo et al. (2023) described a phenotypic dichotomy among individuals with biallelic ERI1 variants. Missense variants caused severe spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia. Biallelic null variants did not cause it, and those individuals had mild intellectual disability and digital anomalies instead. Guo et al. (2023) concluded that within recessive genotypes the missense alleles had a more severe effect than the null alleles.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 HOXHA-ALIU SYNDROME**  
ERI1, LYS188TER [K188Stop; p.Lys188Ter]

Hoxha and Aliu (2023) reported a girl with Hoxha-Aliu syndrome who was homozygous for a lys188-to-ter (K188X) nonsense mutation in the ERI1 gene. Her features were mild intellectual disability, eyelid ptosis, brachydactyly, clinodactyly, dysplastic or short nails of the halluces, brachytelephalangy, short metacarpals, and toe syndactyly. ⚠︎

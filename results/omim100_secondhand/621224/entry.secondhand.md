### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that ectodermal dysplasia-17 with or without limb malformations (ECTD17) is caused by heterozygous or homozygous mutation in the LEF1 gene on chromosome 4q25.

### Description

Ectodermal dysplasia-17 with or without limb malformations (ECTD17) is a disorder caused by alterations of LEF1, a transcription factor acting downstream of the WNT-beta-catenin signaling pathway. Ectodermal features include severe oligodontia of primary and permanent dentition, hypotrichosis, and hypohidrosis, and limb malformations can include radial ray defects, polydactyly, or split hand/foot. Inheritance can be autosomal dominant or autosomal recessive, depending on the type and location of the LEF1 variant (Levy et al., 2020; Dufour et al., 2022).

### Clinical Features

Levy et al. (2020) reported 2 unrelated patients, a 23-year-old female and a 6-year-old male, with severe oligodontia of primary and permanent dentition, hypotrichosis, and hypohidrosis, compatible with hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia. Taurodontism and a particular alveolar bone defect were observed in both patients. ✗

Dufour et al. (2022) reported 12 affected individuals from 5 unrelated families, including 1 consanguineous family. The phenotypic spectrum included various limb malformations, such as radial ray defects, polydactyly, or split hand/foot, and ectodermal dysplasia.

Hassan et al. (2024) reported a family with hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia in the context of LEF1-related disorders.

### Inheritance

Dufour et al. (2022) found that, depending on the type and location of the LEF1 variant, the inheritance of ECTD17 can be either autosomal dominant or autosomal recessive. The 4q25 deletions encompassing LEF1 in the 2 patients reported by Levy et al. (2020) occurred de novo.

### Cytogenetics

In 2 unrelated patients with hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia, Levy et al. (2020) identified de novo deletions at chromosome 4q25 encompassing the LEF1 gene. The authors noted that no pathogenic variants or variations involving the LEF1 gene had previously been reported in humans, and concluded that LEF1 haploinsufficiency causes ectodermal dysplasia.

### Molecular Genetics

In 11 affected individuals from 4 unrelated families with ectodermal dysplasia and/or limb malformations, Dufour et al. (2022) identified monoallelic variants in the LEF1 gene using high-throughput sequencing. In an affected individual from a consanguineous family, they identified a homozygous in-frame duplication in the LEF1 gene (c.69_71dupGAT; p.Met23dup). Functional studies, including immunofluorescence, immunoblotting, pulldown assays, and/or RNA sequencing, indicated that haploinsufficiency or loss of DNA binding was responsible for a mild to moderate phenotype, whereas loss of beta-catenin binding caused by biallelic variants was associated with a severe phenotype. Transcriptomic studies revealed an alteration of WNT signaling. ⚠︎

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Dufour et al. (2022) found that haploinsufficiency or loss of DNA binding caused by monoallelic LEF1 variants was associated with a mild to moderate phenotype, whereas loss of beta-catenin binding caused by biallelic variants was associated with a severe phenotype.

### Animal Model

Levy et al. (2020) noted that, in mice, targeted inactivation of the Lef1 gene results in a complete block of development of multiple ectodermal appendages.

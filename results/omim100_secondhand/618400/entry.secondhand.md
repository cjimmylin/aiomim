### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that axonal Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2EE (CMT2EE) is caused by homozygous mutation in the MPV17 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2EE (CMT2EE) is an autosomal recessive axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy with juvenile or adult onset. Patients have distal muscle weakness and atrophy, mainly in the lower limbs, along with distal sensory loss. The liver and brain are not affected (Choi et al., 2015; Baumann et al., 2019).

### Clinical Features

Blakely et al. (2012) reported a 21-year-old Pakistani man, born to nonconsanguineous parents, who developed walking difficulties at age 14 years. Over several years this progressed to bilateral foot drop requiring orthoses. He also had symmetric distal numbness in the feet that ascended to the knees, with milder involvement of the hands. On examination he had wasting of the hands and distal lower limbs, clawed toes, and severe distal weakness. Tendon reflexes were absent. Pin-prick and light-touch perception were decreased distal to the knees, vibration sense was impaired to the ankles, and joint position sense was absent at the toes. Nerve conduction studies confirmed an axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy. Sural nerve biopsy showed a severe chronic axonal neuropathy with only occasional surviving myelinated axons. This patient also had mild hepatomegaly, and liver biopsy showed patchy steatosis with early bridging fibrosis, although he had no significant chronic liver disease clinically. Cranial MRI showed diffuse hyperintense T2-weighted signal in the cerebral white matter and cerebellum. In the CSF, protein was increased to 1963 mg/L and lactate was raised to 4.3 mmol/L. About 20% of fibers in a skeletal muscle biopsy were COX-deficient, and ragged-red fibers were present. The patient's sister had died of a progressive hepatitis at age 9 years. Blakely et al. (2012) considered the phenotype similar to the classic form of Navajo neurohepatopathy. ⚠︎

Choi et al. (2015) reported 2 patients from 2 unrelated Korean families with axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy. Both were born to nonconsanguineous parents. The first patient, a 34-year-old woman, noticed distal lower limb weakness at age 9 years and began walking with short leg braces at age 12. At age 34 she had bilateral distal weakness and atrophy, predominantly in the lower limbs. She had severe atrophy of the intrinsic hand, foot, and calf muscles, flexion deformities of the interphalangeal joints, ankle joint deformity, and scoliosis. Vibration and position senses were more severely disturbed than pain and touch. Knee and ankle jerks were absent, and there were no pyramidal or cerebellar signs. The second patient, a 22-year-old man, first had gait and balance problems at age 13 years and began walking with short leg braces at age 20. He had distal weakness and atrophy, flat feet, atrophy of the intrinsic hand, foot, and calf muscles, and absent deep tendon reflexes. Vibration and position senses were more severely affected than pain and touch. Neither patient had other features of Navajo neurohepatopathy, such as growth retardation, gastrointestinal dysmotility, hepatomegaly, cognitive impairment, ophthalmoplegia, corneal scarring, or hypoglycemic attacks. Serum lactate was mildly elevated (2.1 and 1.9 mmol/L).

In both patients, Choi et al. (2015) found that sural nerve sensory nerve action potentials were lost early in the disease. Motor conduction velocities and CMAPs were decreased in the median and ulnar nerves. Peroneal and tibial CMAPs could not be elicited, and no sensory potentials were obtained. Visual evoked potentials, brainstem auditory evoked potentials, and brain MRI were normal. Lower leg MRI showed severe atrophy and fatty replacement of the soleus muscles, with relative sparing of the tibialis posterior and lateral gastrocnemius muscles. The authors considered this pattern consistent with length-dependent axonal degeneration. A sural nerve biopsy from the first patient at age 34 showed complete loss of large and medium-sized myelinated fibers (10/mm2, compared with 9,200/mm2 in a control) and no evidence of demyelination or onion bulb formation. A vastus lateralis biopsy from the second patient at age 22 showed a few ragged-red fibers and subsarcolemmal accumulation of enlarged mitochondria.

Baumann et al. (2019) described 5 patients from 2 unrelated families with sensorimotor axonal neuropathy without hepatocerebral involvement. Onset was juvenile or adult.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CMT2EE in the families reported by Choi et al. (2015) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Affected individuals carried homozygous mutations in the MPV17 gene (Choi et al., 2015; Baumann et al., 2019).

### Molecular Genetics

Blakely et al. (2012) sequenced the POLG, DGUOK, and MPV17 genes in a Pakistani man with neuropathy, leukoencephalopathy, and liver involvement. They identified a homozygous c.298C-T transition in MPV17, predicted to cause a pro98-to-leu (P98L) substitution at a highly conserved residue (P98L). This variant had previously been reported as a recessive mutation associated with mtDNA depletion in liver. Parental samples were not available. Long-range PCR of the patient's skeletal muscle showed multiple mtDNA deletions with a normal mtDNA copy number. Many COX-deficient fibers contained high levels of a clonally expanded mtDNA deletion.

Choi et al. (2015) performed whole-exome sequencing on 3 members of each of 2 unrelated Korean families with axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy. In both affected individuals they identified a homozygous c.122G-A transition in the MPV17 gene, resulting in an arg41-to-gln (R41Q) substitution (R41Q), which was confirmed by Sanger sequencing. A mutant allele was putatively inherited from each parent. The mutation was absent from 300 controls and from 302 in-house exomes, and it was present at very low frequency in the Exome Sequencing Project and ExAC databases. In a mouse motor neuronal cell line (NSC34), knockdown of MPV17 reduced cell proliferation and viability under H2O2 treatment, and expression of the R41Q mutant protein significantly inhibited cell proliferation.

Baumann et al. (2019) identified homozygous MPV17 variants in 5 patients from 2 unrelated families with axonal sensorimotor neuropathy. Patients in the first family carried the R41Q (c.122G-A) mutation. Affected members of the second family carried a novel near-splice variant, c.376-9T-G, which was shown to result in an in-frame deletion of 11 amino acids.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal dominant distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-14 (HMND14) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the DCTN1 gene on chromosome 2p13. †

### Description

Autosomal dominant distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-14 (HMND14), also called HMN VIIB, is a slowly progressive lower motor neuron disease. Puls et al. (2003) described onset in young adults with vocal cord paralysis causing respiratory distress, followed by progressive weakness and atrophy of the hand and facial muscles and later involvement of the lower limbs. †

### Clinical Features

Puls et al. (2003) studied a slowly progressive, autosomal dominant lower motor neuron disease. Patients presented as young adults with vocal cord paralysis causing respiratory distress. They developed progressive muscle weakness and atrophy in the hands and facial muscles, and the lower limbs became involved later. †

### Inheritance

Puls et al. (2003) reported that the lower motor neuron disease linked to chromosome 2p13 shows autosomal dominant inheritance. †

### Mapping

Puls et al. (2003) linked a lower motor neuron disease to a 4-Mb region on chromosome 2p13.

### Molecular Genetics

Puls et al. (2003) analyzed the DCTN1 gene, which lies in the linked 2p13 interval and encodes p150(Glued), the largest subunit of the axonal transport protein dynactin. In patients with lower motor neuron disease, they found a heterozygous single-basepair change that resulted in a gly59-to-ser (G59S) substitution. The mutation lies in the cytoskeleton-associated protein glycine-rich (CAP-Gly) domain, which binds microtubules, and was predicted to distort the folding of this domain. In binding assays, the mutant protein bound microtubules less well than normal. Puls et al. (2003) concluded that dysfunction of dynactin-mediated transport can lead to human motor neuron disease. †

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that annular epidermolytic ichthyosis-1 (AEI1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding keratin 10 (KRT10).

### Description

Annular epidermolytic ichthyosis is a rare variant of bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma, also called epidermolytic ichthyosis. Patients have bullous ichthyosis in early life. Later they develop hyperkeratotic plaques in the flexures and on extensor surfaces, along with intermittent episodes of annular and polycyclic, erythematous, scaly plaques on the trunk and proximal limbs. Skin biopsy shows epidermolytic hyperkeratosis (Joh et al., 1997; Suga et al., 1998; Mikilita et al., 2020).

### Nomenclature

Sahn et al. (1992) used the term 'annular epidermolytic ichthyosis' for the phenotype in their family and suggested that the kindred might represent a new phenotype of epidermolytic (acanthokeratolytic) ichthyosis.

### Clinical Features

Sahn et al. (1992) described a 30-year-old white woman who developed bullous and ichthyosiform skin lesions at age 8 months. The blistering stopped at puberty, but hyperkeratotic plaques persisted. At age 27 years she developed many annular and polycyclic, erythematous, hyperkeratotic plaques on the trunk and limbs. These plaques were itchy, slowly enlarged, and then resolved. Biopsy of the annular plaques showed epidermolytic hyperkeratosis. On electron microscopy there were interrupted perinuclear tonofilament rings, consistent with either bullous ichthyotic erythroderma of Brocq or ichthyosis bullosa of Siemens. Two of her 5 children had a bullous type of ichthyosis. In a literature review, Mikilita et al. (2020) noted that this woman also had mild palmoplantar keratoderma with fissures. Her 2-year-old son had developed papules at age 6 months that progressed to blisters and crusts, and he later had keratotic plaques on the neck and in the armpits.

Joh et al. (1997) reported a family with affected members in 2 generations. The proband had bullous ichthyosis and episodes of disease activity marked by many annular and polycyclic, erythematous, hyperkeratotic plaques on the trunk and proximal limbs. Histology showed the typical changes of epidermolytic hyperkeratosis. Ultrastructural analysis showed abnormal keratin filament networks and perinuclear clumping of tonofilaments. As summarized by Mikilita et al. (2020), the affected members were a father and his daughter. The father had widespread blisters from birth. From his first year until age 16 years he had intermittent, itchy, hyperkeratotic, erythematous plaques with blisters. At age 31 years he again had intermittent blisters and hyperkeratotic plaques, together with annular, migratory, polycyclic, scaly, erythematous plaques on the trunk and proximal limbs. His daughter had generalized bullous lesions and moderate erythema from her second day of life.

Suga et al. (1998) described a third kindred with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis. The proband was an 11-year-old boy, and his mother and sister were also affected. They noted that people with this variant have bullous ichthyosis in early childhood and later develop hyperkeratotic, lichenified plaques in the flexures and on extensor surfaces, as well as intermittent bouts of annular and polycyclic, erythematous, scaly plaques on the trunk and proximal limbs. In their review, Mikilita et al. (2020) noted that the proband had no history of blisters. He had xerotic plaques in the flexures and on extensor surfaces from age 7 months, and at age 11 years he had intermittent annular, erythematous, scaly, serpiginous plaques. †

Mikilita et al. (2020) reported an 8-year-old Brazilian girl with skin disease that began in the first months of life. Her parents were not consanguineous, and no other family members were affected. On examination she had erythematous, scaly, hyperkeratotic plaques with prominent borders on the mesogastrium, cubital and popliteal fossae, groin, and neck. She also had palmoplantar hyperkeratosis and yellowish hyperkeratotic plaques on the scalp and nasal introitus. Hair, nails, and mucosa were normal. One month later the affected area had grown, but the polycyclic pattern had disappeared. Biopsy of the forearm showed acanthosis, papillomatosis, and hyperkeratosis with marked epidermolysis of the granular layer. The initial working diagnosis had been erythrokeratodermia variabilis et progressiva. Mikilita et al. (2020) also reviewed 19 previously reported cases from 10 publications.

### Inheritance

In the family reported by Sahn et al. (1992), an affected mother had 2 affected children. Joh et al. (1997) stated that the inheritance pattern of annular epidermolytic ichthyosis is consistent with autosomal dominant transmission, and they reported a family with affected members in 2 generations.

### Heterogeneity

Mikilita et al. (2020) stated that annular epidermolytic ichthyosis results from dominant mutations in either the keratin 1 or the keratin 10 gene.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of a 2-generation family with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis, Joh et al. (1997) found a heterozygous tandem 2-bp CG-to-GA mutation in the KRT10 gene. Both changes were on the same allele. The mutation caused an arg-to-glu substitution at residue 83 of the 2B helical segment, also reported as c.1264_1265delinsGA, p.Arg422Glu. ⚠︎

In a third kindred with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis, Suga et al. (1998) found a heterozygous mutation in the KRT10 gene that caused an ile-to-thr substitution at residue 107 of the 2B segment (codon 446; c.1337T-C, I446T). This residue lies in the highly conserved helix termination motif at the end of the keratin 10 rod domain. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

On electron microscopy, Sahn et al. (1992) and Joh et al. (1997) found abnormal tonofilaments in lesional skin, including interrupted perinuclear tonofilament rings, abnormal keratin filament networks, and perinuclear clumping of tonofilaments. Joh et al. (1997) concluded that annular epidermolytic ichthyosis should be regarded as a variant of bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma.

### Diagnosis

Mikilita et al. (2020) identified erythrokeratodermia variabilis et progressiva as the main differential diagnosis. They noted that this disorder begins in childhood, does not involve epidermal fragility, and does not show epidermolysis on histology. In their patient, the finding of epidermolytic hyperkeratosis on biopsy led to the diagnosis of annular epidermolytic ichthyosis.

### Clinical Management

In their review of reported cases, Mikilita et al. (2020) found that topical treatments gave little benefit. These included retinoids, corticosteroids, propylene glycol, calcipotriene, and keratolytic agents. Systemic retinoids gave a good response in some patients: oral acitretin (20 mg per day in the father reported by Joh et al. (1997)) and low-dose oral isotretinoin. One mother and son did not improve with etretinate.

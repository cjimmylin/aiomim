### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia (DYTSPG) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the ATP5MC3 gene on chromosome 2q.

### Description

Early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia is an autosomal dominant neurologic disorder. Affected individuals have childhood-onset dystonia, which may generalize and respond to pallidal deep brain stimulation, adult-onset slowly progressive spastic paraplegia, or both (Gilbert et al., 2009; Neilson et al., 2022). Some patients with de novo mutations have had developmental delay, pyramidal signs, or lower-extremity spasticity (Zech et al., 2022).

### Clinical Features

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported a large, multigenerational Ohio kindred with an autosomal dominant, highly penetrant neurologic disorder causing spasticity and dystonia. Spasticity and weakness began between the first year and the sixth decade of life. Dystonia began between the first and third decades. Affected members had no ataxia and no apparent cognitive involvement. Neuroimaging and peripheral neurophysiology were normal. In 1 child, generalized dystonia improved markedly with deep brain stimulation.

Neilson et al. (2022) provided further details on the family reported by Gilbert et al. (2009) (Family-1). The autosomal dominant pedigree included 40 individuals. Of these, 18 were affected with either childhood-onset, progressive generalized dystonia or adult-onset, gradually progressive spastic paraplegia. In the proband, dystonia began in one upper limb at age 2 years and generalized to the trunk and all limbs, and he lost the ability to walk. Bilateral pallidal deep brain stimulation, implanted at age 9 years, restored walking and limb use, and the response remained excellent at age 25 years. The proband's father and grandfather had the spastic paraplegia phenotype, with progressive leg weakness and spasticity beginning after age 20 years. Some family members had less severe, later-onset focal limb and neck dystonia and spasmodic dysphonia, which Neilson et al. (2022) suggested might be related to trauma or overuse. Brain MRI and peripheral nerve physiology were normal in those tested. In the proband and his father, metabolic studies were normal; these included whole blood lactate and pyruvate, serum amino acids, urine organic acids, and blood and urine acylcarnitine profiles. Neilson et al. (2022) noted that the dystonia in this pedigree showed incomplete penetrance. The spastic paraplegia began in adulthood, progressed gradually, and was not accompanied by other neurologic problems. ⚠︎

Neilson et al. (2022) also reported a second, unrelated family (Family-2). The proband was a right-handed boy of European descent born to healthy unrelated parents, with no family history of dystonia or related movement disorders. He presented at age 11 years with a 4-year history of gradually worsening upper limb dystonia. Laboratory studies of blood, urine, and cerebrospinal fluid were unrevealing. Brain MRI showed nonspecific mild bilateral frontoparietal white matter changes. In the introduction to their report, Neilson et al. (2022) described this proband as having generalized dystonia; in the discussion they described him as having upper limb dystonia.

Zech et al. (2022) reported 3 unrelated patients with de novo heterozygous ATP5MC3 mutations. Patient 8 was the Family-2 proband of Neilson et al. (2022), whom they described as a 12-year-old boy with isolated upper-limb dystonia. Patient 9 was a 15-year-old girl with delayed milestones, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia with prominent upper-body involvement. Patient 10 was a 6-year-old boy with delayed psychomotor development, lower-extremity spasticity, and elevated blood lactate. Zech et al. (2022) combined their patients with the 15 individuals from the multigenerational family of Neilson et al. (2022) who had clinical information. In this combined group, features included dystonia (12 of 18), spasticity or pyramidal signs (17 of 18), and developmental delay or intellectual disability (2 of 18). Ten individuals had dystonia-predominant syndromes. In patients 8 and 9, brain MRI was nondiagnostic.

### Inheritance

Gilbert et al. (2009) and Neilson et al. (2022) established autosomal dominant inheritance of this disorder in a large multigenerational family. Neilson et al. (2022) and Zech et al. (2022) also reported affected individuals with de novo heterozygous mutations.

### Mapping

Gilbert et al. (2009) performed single and multipoint linkage analysis in a large Ohio kindred using microsatellite and SNP array genotyping. They mapped the disease locus to chromosome 2q24-q31, in a region flanked by markers rs1424937 and rs1559510 and proximal to SPG13. No known spastic paraplegia or dystonia genes lay in this region. In a secondary analysis looking for candidate regions segregating with the dystonia phenotype, they identified 2 regions with parametric lod scores above 2.0.

### Molecular Genetics

Neilson et al. (2022) performed whole-exome sequencing, restricted to the 2q24-q31 linkage region, in the proband and a distant cousin with childhood-onset dystonia from the family reported by Gilbert et al. (2009). They identified a heterozygous c.318C-G transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in an asn106-to-lys (N106K) substitution. On Sanger sequencing, only affected family members carried the mutation. The mutation was absent from gnomAD (v2.1.1 and v3.1.1). Asn106 lies in a highly conserved loop of subunit c that interacts directly with the F1 subunit. Fibroblasts from the proband (dystonia) and his affected father (spastic paraplegia) had normal activities of complexes I through IV. However, complex V activity was reduced by 20%, cellular ATP by 20 to 32%, and oxygen consumption by 21 to 25%. Because oxygen consumption and ATP were reduced in proportion, the authors concluded that uncoupling of oxidative phosphorylation was not supported as the mechanism. Through GeneMatcher, they identified the same mutation in heterozygous state in an unrelated boy with dystonia (Family-2), in whom trio exome sequencing showed that it had arisen de novo. No ATP5MC1, ATP5MC2, or ATP5MC3 variants were found in an additional 384 patients with dystonia (362) or hereditary spastic paraplegia (22). ⚠︎

Zech et al. (2022) analyzed exome data from 2,962 patients with mitochondrial disease and/or dystonia and used international collaboration to find more patients. They identified de novo heterozygous missense mutations in the ATP5MC3 gene in 3 unrelated patients: N106K in a previously reported patient, and pro107-to-ala (P107A) and gly79-to-val (G79V) in 2 new patients. The variants were not present in gnomAD or local control exome databases. They affected strictly conserved residues in the C-terminal helical motifs of the protein. Fibroblasts from the patient with the G79V mutation had deficient ATPase activity and a moderate decrease in assembled ATPase on BN-PAGE. Fibroblasts from the patient with N106K had a slight but significant decrease in ATPase activity and reduced oxygen consumption. The authors suggested that dominant-negative effects were possible.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Zech et al. (2022) compared fibroblasts from 2 patients. The G79V mutation, found in a patient with a more severe presentation of neurodevelopmental disease and spasticity, produced a significantly greater ATPase enzyme deficit than the N106K mutation, found in a patient with isolated dystonia. The authors stated that further studies were needed before genotype-phenotype correlations could be clearly established.

### Animal Model

Neilson et al. (2022) generated transgenic Drosophila expressing the fly ATP5MC3 ortholog (CG1746). The fly constructs carried either the mutation equivalent to human N106K (N102K) or the mutation equivalent to an E. coli atpE mutation (N102E), and were expressed under the armadillo-GAL4 driver. Flies with either mutation had reduced mobility on geotaxis assays compared with wildtype flies and flies overexpressing the wildtype gene, and the N102K mutant was slowest. In fly mitochondria, complex V activity was reduced by 53% with N102K and by 37% with N102E.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, ASN106LYS [NM_001689.4:c.318C>G; NP_001680.1:p.Asn106Lys]

Neilson et al. (2022) identified a heterozygous c.318C-G transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in an asn106-to-lys (N106K) substitution, in a large multigenerational family with autosomal dominant dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia, previously reported by Gilbert et al. (2009). The mutation was found by exome sequencing and segregated with the disorder in the family. It was absent from gnomAD. Fibroblasts from the proband and his affected father had 20% lower complex V activity than controls, as well as reduced cellular ATP and oxygen consumption. Drosophila carrying the equivalent mutation had reduced mobility and reduced complex V activity. Neilson et al. (2022) also found the same mutation, arising de novo, in an unrelated boy with upper limb dystonia. ⚠︎

Zech et al. (2022) studied fibroblasts from the de novo N106K patient reported by Neilson et al. (2022). They found a slight but significant decrease in ATPase activity and reduced oxygen consumption.

**.0002 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, PRO107ALA [NM_001689.5:c.319C>G; NP_001680.1:p.Pro107Ala]

Zech et al. (2022) identified a de novo heterozygous c.319C-G transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in a pro107-to-ala (P107A) substitution, in a 15-year-old girl (patient 9). She had delayed milestones, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia with prominent upper-body involvement. The mutation was found by exome sequencing and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. It was not present in gnomAD or local control exome databases. Structural modeling placed Pro107 at the interaction site between the F0 and F1 subcomplexes.

**.0003 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, GLY79VAL [NM_001689.5:c.236G>T; NP_001680.1:p.Gly79Val]

Zech et al. (2022) identified a de novo heterozygous c.236G-T transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in a gly79-to-val (G79V) substitution, in a 6-year-old boy (patient 10). He had delayed psychomotor development, lower-extremity spasticity, and elevated blood lactate. The mutation was not present in gnomAD or local control exome databases. Patient fibroblasts had deficient ATPase activity and a moderate decrease in assembled ATPase on BN-PAGE. The ATPase deficit was greater than that seen in fibroblasts from a patient with the N106K mutation.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that nanophthalmos-4 (NNO4) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TMEM98 gene on chromosome 17.

### Description

Nanophthalmos is a congenital disorder in which the eyes are small but structurally normal. Its main complications are severe hyperopia and angle-closure glaucoma (Awadalla et al., 2014). Patients also have a higher risk of complications after eye surgery (Khorram et al., 2015). The form described in this entry is inherited as an autosomal dominant trait. ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

Yu et al. (2009) and Hu et al. (2011) called the disorder in the Chinese family they studied 'simple microphthalmia,' meaning a structurally normal but small globe. Khorram et al. (2015) treated nanophthalmos and simple microphthalmia as the same condition: small eyes that are otherwise normal. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Yu et al. (2009) studied a 5-generation Chinese family in which isolated microphthalmia was inherited in an autosomal dominant manner. Nine affected members, aged 6 to 66 years (mean, 42.8 years), had bilateral microphthalmia present from birth and no other physical anomalies. Hyperopia ranged from +4.50 to +11.50 diopters (mean, +8.00 diopters). Axial length ranged from 18.22 mm to 20.26 mm (mean, 19.29 mm). Best corrected visual acuity was less than 0.5 in most eyes. Three patients, all older than 50 years, had angle-closure glaucoma or occludable anterior chamber angles. They were treated with trabeculectomy, but the disease continued to progress, and 2 of them developed atrophia bulbi despite repeated surgeries. Histologic examination of the sclera showed diffusely swollen collagen fibers that were twisted or frayed and mixed with normal fibers. In the atrophied globes, the alignment of collagen fibers was disrupted, the sclera contained many coil-like amorphous materials and was 3 times the normal thickness, and the corneas were also thickened. The authors also noted learning disabilities in one-third of the cases. Karyotypes were normal in all 9 affected members.

Awadalla et al. (2014) studied a large white family (NNO-SA1) with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos, recruited in South Australia. They defined nanophthalmos as an axial length of less than 20.0 mm and/or a refractive error greater than +7.00. Of 35 available family members, 16 (46%) had nanophthalmos, with a mean refraction of +11.8 D and a mean axial length of 17.6 mm.

Khorram et al. (2015) reported 2 large pedigrees with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos. Inheritance was highly penetrant and spanned at least 4 generations in each. In a Caucasian family of German heritage living in the United States (pedigree 991-F), 6 of 10 examined members had nanophthalmos, and 3 more were affected according to family history. Affected members had a mean axial eye length of 17.1 +/- 0.31 mm and spherical equivalent refractions of +13.0 +/- 0.89 diopters, compared with +0.71 +/- 0.81 diopters in unaffected members. In a Pacific Islander family of Micronesian heritage living in the Mariana Islands (pedigree 951-N), 8 of 10 examined members had nanophthalmos, and 3 more were affected according to family history. Affected members had a mean axial eye length of 17.4 +/- 0.94 mm, compared with 22.7 +/- 0.38 mm in unaffected members, and spherical equivalent refractions of +3.93 +/- 1.57 diopters. Across the patients with nanophthalmos, axial eye lengths ranged from 15.9 to 18.4 mm.

### Inheritance

Yu et al. (2009) found that nanophthalmos was transmitted in an autosomal dominant manner in a Chinese family. Awadalla et al. (2014) and Khorram et al. (2015) reported the same pattern in a white family and in 2 further pedigrees. ⚠︎

### Mapping

In the Chinese family they studied, Yu et al. (2009) used 23 microsatellite markers to test linkage to known microphthalmia loci and candidate genes, including CHX10, PAX6, MFRP, and loci on chromosomes 14q32, 11p, and 2q11-q14. All 2-point lod scores were below 1, and the authors predicted that a novel locus was responsible.

Hu et al. (2011) carried out a genomewide scan with 382 microsatellite markers in a Chinese family with autosomal dominant congenital simple microphthalmia, after excluding the candidate loci previously reported. The highest lod score was 4.97 at D17S1824 (theta = 0.00). Haplotype analysis placed the disease gene in a 21.57-cM interval between D17S900 and D17S1872 on chromosome 17p12-q12. Sequencing of 14 candidate genes, including CRYBA1 and NCOR1, found no mutation, and genomewide SNP genotyping found no pathogenic copy number variation.

In a large white family with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos, Awadalla et al. (2014) found significant linkage on chromosome 17p12-q12, between SNP markers rs2323659 and rs967293, with a maximum location score of 4.1.

Khorram et al. (2015) found that a 20.2-Mbp segment of chromosome 17p12-q12, bounded by SNPs rs530664 and rs16523, was coinherited with nanophthalmos in a US family (pedigree 991-F). In a Mariana Islands family (pedigree 951-N), an overlapping 19.7-Mb segment of chromosome 17, bounded by SNPs rs917541 and rs16523, was coinherited with the disease.

### Molecular Genetics

In a large white family with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos linked to 17p12-q12, Awadalla et al. (2014) used exome sequencing to identify a missense mutation in exon 8 of the TMEM98 gene, c.577G-C (A193P). It was the only novel variant within the linked region that segregated with the disease. The variant was absent from the Exome Variant Server database and from 285 local white controls. TMEM98 was expressed in all ocular tissues tested, including the sclera and optic nerve head.

In a Caucasian family from the United States with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos (pedigree 991-F), Khorram et al. (2015) performed exome sequencing within the regions linked to the disease. This identified a his196-to-pro (H196P) mutation in the TMEM98 gene. Sanger sequencing confirmed that all other affected family members carried the mutation, and it was absent from healthy family members. Of the 4 nonsynonymous variants found in the chromosome 17 critical region, H196P was the only one that both segregated with the disease and was predicted to be pathogenic by SIFT, PolyPhen, and BLOSUM62. The affected histidine is highly conserved across species. In a Pacific Islander family from the Mariana Islands with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos (pedigree 951-N), the authors found a heterozygous 34-bp deletion in TMEM98 in all affected family members; it was absent from healthy family members. The deletion spans the last 28 bp of exon 4 and the first 6 bp of intron 4, removing a splice site, and is predicted to cause premature termination of translation. Neither mutation was present in the Exome Variant Server or 1000 Genomes Project databases.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 NANOPHTHALMOS 4**  
TMEM98, ALA193PRO [c.577G>C; p.Ala193Pro]

In affected members of a large white family with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos (NNO4) linked to chromosome 17p12-q12, Awadalla et al. (2014) identified a c.577G-C transversion in exon 8 of the TMEM98 gene, resulting in an ala193-to-pro (A193P) substitution. The mutation was found by exome sequencing and was the only novel segregating variant in the linked region. It was absent from the Exome Variant Server database and from 285 local white controls.

**.0002 NANOPHTHALMOS 4**  
TMEM98, HIS196PRO [p.His196Pro]

In affected members of a Caucasian family from the United States with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos (NNO4) (pedigree 991-F), Khorram et al. (2015) identified a his196-to-pro (H196P) mutation in the TMEM98 gene. The mutation was found by exome sequencing and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. It segregated with the disease, was absent from healthy family members and from the Exome Variant Server and 1000 Genomes Project databases, and affected a highly conserved histidine. SIFT, PolyPhen, and BLOSUM62 all predicted it to be pathogenic.

In all affected members of a Pacific Islander family from the Mariana Islands with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos (NNO4) (pedigree 951-N), Khorram et al. It removes a splice site and is predicted to cause premature termination of translation.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that nanophthalmos-4 (NNO4) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TMEM98 gene on chromosome 17p12-q12.

### Description

Nanophthalmos is a congenital disorder in which the eye is small but structurally normal. Its main complications are severe hyperopia and angle-closure glaucoma (Awadalla et al., 2014). Affected individuals also have a higher risk of complications after eye surgery (Khorram et al., 2015). NNO4 is inherited as an autosomal dominant trait. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Yu et al. (2009) studied a 5-generation Chinese family with simple microphthalmia inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern. Nine affected members were aged 6 to 66 years (mean, 42.8 years) and had bilateral isolated microphthalmia present from birth. No family members had other physical anomalies. Hyperopia ranged from +4.50 to +11.50 diopters (mean, +8.00 diopters). Axial length ranged from 18.22 mm to 20.26 mm (mean, 19.29 mm). In most eyes, best corrected visual acuity was less than 0.5. Three patients, all older than 50 years, had angle-closure glaucoma or occludable anterior chamber angles. They were treated by trabeculectomy, but the disease continued to progress, and 2 patients developed atrophia bulbi despite repeated surgeries. On histology, collagen fibers in the scleral stroma were diffusely swollen, twisted, or frayed and mixed with normal fibers. In the 2 atrophied globes, the collagen fibers were misaligned and the sclera contained abundant coil-like amorphous material and was 3 times the normal thickness. The corneas in these globes were also thickened, pigment cells had proliferated, and bone-like tissue had formed. Karyotypes were normal in all 9 affected members. In their discussion, Yu et al. (2009) noted learning disabilities in one-third of the cases.

Awadalla et al. (2014) studied a large white family (NNO-SA1) from South Australia with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos. Nanophthalmos was defined as an axial length of less than 20.0 mm and/or a refractive error greater than +7.00. Of 35 available family members, 16 (46%) were diagnosed with nanophthalmos. Their mean refraction was +11.8 D and their mean axial length was 17.6 mm.

Khorram et al. (2015) reported 2 large families with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos showing high penetrance over at least 4 generations. The first family (991-F) was white, of German heritage, and lived in the United States. Six of 10 examined members had nanophthalmos, and 3 more were affected by family history. Mean axial eye length in the available patients was 17.1 +/- 0.31 mm. Mean spherical equivalent refraction was +13.0 +/- 0.89 diopters, compared with +0.71 +/- 0.81 diopters in unaffected members. The second family (951-N) was a Pacific Islander family of Micronesian heritage from the Mariana Islands. Eight of 10 examined members had nanophthalmos, with a mean axial eye length of 17.4 +/- 0.94 mm, compared with 22.7 +/- 0.38 mm in unaffected members. Their mean spherical equivalent refraction was +3.93 +/- 1.57 diopters. Three more members of this family were affected by family history. Across both families, axial eye length in affected individuals ranged from 15.9 to 18.4 mm.

### Inheritance

Autosomal dominant inheritance of nanophthalmos was observed in a Chinese family by Yu et al. (2009), in a white Australian family by Awadalla et al. (2014), and in 2 families, one from the United States and one from the Mariana Islands, by Khorram et al. (2015). ⚠︎

### Mapping

In the Chinese family with autosomal dominant simple microphthalmia, Yu et al. (2009) excluded linkage to the candidate genes CHX10, PAX6, and MFRP and to the loci previously reported on chromosomes 14q32, 11p, and 2q11-q14. Two-point LOD scores were below 1 for all 23 markers tested.

Hu et al. (2011) carried out a genomewide scan in the same Chinese family using 382 microsatellite markers. The highest 2-point LOD score was 4.97 at D17S1824 (recombination fraction 0.00). Haplotype analysis placed the disease gene in a 21.57-cM interval between D17S900 and D17S1872 on chromosome 17p12-q12. ⚠︎

Awadalla et al. (2014) performed a genomewide linkage scan in the Australian family NNO-SA1 using a 10K SNP array. They found significant linkage on chromosome 17 between SNPs rs2323659 and rs967293, with a maximum location score of 4.1.

Khorram et al. (2015) carried out linkage analysis with 6,020 SNPs. In family 991-F, a 20.2-Mbp segment of chromosome 17p12-q12 between SNPs rs530664 and rs16523 was inherited with nanophthalmos in all affected members. In family 951-N, an overlapping 19.7-Mb segment between SNPs rs917541 and rs16523 was likewise inherited with the disease.

### Molecular Genetics

In the Chinese family with autosomal dominant microphthalmia linked to 17p12-q12, Hu et al. (2011) sequenced 14 candidate genes, including CRYBA1 and NCOR1, and performed genomewide SNP genotyping. They found no causative mutation or copy number variation.

Awadalla et al. (2014) performed exome sequencing in the large white Australian family with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos linked to chromosome 17p12-q12. Within the linked region they identified a single novel segregating missense variant, a heterozygous c.577G-C transversion in exon 8 of the TMEM98 gene that results in an ala193-to-pro (A193P) substitution. The variant was absent from the Exome Variant Server database and from 285 local white controls. RT-PCR showed TMEM98 expression in all ocular tissues tested, including sclera and optic nerve head. ⚠︎

Khorram et al. (2015) performed exome sequencing in an affected member of family 991-F. Within the chromosome 17 linked region, they found a heterozygous his196-to-pro (H196P) mutation in TMEM98. Sanger sequencing showed that all other affected family members carried the mutation and that it was absent from unaffected members. Sanger sequencing of TMEM98 in family 951-N identified a heterozygous 34-bp deletion (c.694_721delAGAATGAAGACTGGATCGAAGATGCCTCgtaagg) in all affected family members, and it was absent from healthy members. The deletion spans the last 28 bp of exon 4 and the first 6 bp of intron 4. It removes a splice site and results in premature termination of translation. Neither mutation was found in the Exome Variant Server or the 1000 Genomes Project. ⚠︎

Awadalla et al. (2014) studied a large white Australian family with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos. In affected members they identified a heterozygous c.577G-C transversion in exon 8 of the TMEM98 gene, resulting in an ala193-to-pro (A193P) substitution. **.0002 NANOPHTHALMOS 4**  
TMEM98, HIS196PRO [p.His196Pro]

Khorram et al. (2015) studied a white family from the United States (991-F) with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos. In affected members they identified a heterozygous his196-to-pro (H196P) mutation in the TMEM98 gene. The mutation was found by exome sequencing within a linked region on chromosome 17p12-q12 and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. It was present in all affected family members and absent from healthy members. The mutation was predicted to be damaging by SIFT, PolyPhen, and BLOSUM62, alters a histidine conserved across species, and was absent from the Exome Variant Server and 1000 Genomes Project databases. ⚠︎

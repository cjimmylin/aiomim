A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy-2 (CHN2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the MPZ gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy is a hereditary demyelinating peripheral neuropathy that begins in early infancy. Its features include distal muscle weakness, hypotonia, areflexia, and severely slowed nerve conduction velocities (Mandich et al., 1999). Onset may be prenatal, neonatal, or early infantile. Histopathologically, myelin sheaths are absent or thin, and most of the few patients described in the literature die within the first years of life (Smit et al., 2008).

### Clinical Features

Mandich et al. (1999) described the clinical, morphologic, and immunohistochemical features of a patient with congenital hypomyelination. The patient's clinical features were quite distinct from those of the more frequent forms of MPZ-related neuropathy, Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1B (CMT1B) and Dejerine-Sottas syndrome (DSS).

Szigeti et al. (2003) reported a patient with congenital hypomyelination who also had cranial nerve dysfunction, respiratory failure, and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, which confounded the clinical picture. The muscle biopsy findings suggested that abnormal innervation had disturbed early postnatal muscle fiber differentiation. ⚠︎

Kochanski et al. (2004) described the clinical and morphologic features of a patient with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy. A sural nerve biopsy showed nonmyelinated and poorly myelinated axons with basal lamina onion bulbs and no myelin breakdown products. ⚠︎

Smit et al. (2008) reported long-term follow-up of a family with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy in which the disorder was present in 2 generations. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The MPZ mutations in the patients reported by Mandich et al. (1999) and Szigeti et al. (2003) occurred de novo. Smit et al. (2008) confirmed a heterozygous MPZ mutation in 2 generations of a family, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Kochanski et al. (2004) and Smit et al. (2008) noted that congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy has been associated with mutations in several genes: MPZ, EGR2, PMP22, and MTMR2. Kochanski et al. (2004) stated that the disorder can be inherited as either an autosomal dominant or an autosomal recessive trait.

### Molecular Genetics

Warner et al. (1996) identified 5 novel mutations in the MPZ gene, which encodes P0, the major structural protein of peripheral nerve myelin. These mutations were found in patients with CMT1B, DSS, or congenital hypomyelination. The authors suggested that these disorders may not be distinct pathophysiologic entities but rather a spectrum of related 'myelinopathies' caused by an underlying defect in myelination.

In a patient with congenital hypomyelination, Mandich et al. (1999) identified a de novo gln215-to-ter (Q215X) mutation in the MPZ gene. They concluded that this finding confirmed that congenital hypomyelination is allelic with disorders that have a less severe phenotype and a different clinical and neuropathologic profile.

In a patient with congenital hypomyelination, Szigeti et al. (2003) identified a complex de novo mutation in the MPZ gene. It consisted of a 3-bp deletion of CTA from nucleotides 550 to 552 together with insertion of a G at nucleotide 550. By conceptual translation, the change was predicted to cause a frameshift.

In a patient with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy, Kochanski et al. (2004) identified a novel thr124-to-lys (T124K) mutation in the MPZ gene. ⚠︎

In a family with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy, Smit et al. (2008) identified a heterozygous mutation in the MPZ gene that was confirmed in 2 generations.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Warner et al. (1996) hypothesized that the differences in clinical severity among patients with MPZ mutations depend on the type of mutation and its effect on protein function, that is, whether it causes loss of function or acts in a dominant-negative manner.

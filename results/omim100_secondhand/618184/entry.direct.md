### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy-2 (CHN2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding myelin protein zero (MPZ). ⚠︎

### Description

Congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy is a hereditary demyelinating peripheral neuropathy. Onset is in early infancy, and features include distal muscle weakness, hypotonia, areflexia, and severely slowed nerve conduction velocities (Mandich et al., 1999). Sural nerve biopsy shows nonmyelinated and poorly myelinated axons with basal lamina onion bulbs and no myelin breakdown products (Kochanski et al., 2004). Smit et al. (2008) noted that most of the few patients described in the literature died within the first years of life.

### Clinical Features

Mandich et al. (1999) described the clinical, morphologic, and immunohistochemical features of a patient with congenital hypomyelination. The patient's clinical features were quite distinct from those of the more common Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1B (CMT1B) and Dejerine-Sottas syndrome (DSS).

Szigeti et al. (2003) reported a patient with congenital hypomyelination who also had cranial nerve dysfunction, respiratory failure, and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, which confounded the clinical picture. The muscle biopsy findings suggested that abnormal innervation affected early postnatal muscle fiber differentiation.

Kochanski et al. (2004) described the clinical and morphologic features of a patient with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy.

Smit et al. (2008) reported a long-term follow-up study of a family in which congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy affected 2 generations. The disorder is characterized by onset of hypotonia, paresis, and areflexia before birth, in the neonatal period, or in early infancy. On histopathology, myelin sheaths are absent or thin.

### Inheritance

Mandich et al. (1999) and Szigeti et al. (2003) found de novo MPZ mutations in patients with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy. Smit et al. (2008) found a heterozygous MPZ mutation in affected members of 2 generations of a family, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. Kochanski et al. (2004) stated that congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy can be inherited as either an autosomal dominant or an autosomal recessive trait. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Kochanski et al. (2004) and Smit et al. (2008) noted that congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy has been associated with mutations in the MTMR2, PMP22, and EGR2 genes as well as in MPZ.

### Molecular Genetics

Warner et al. (1996) identified 5 novel mutations in the MPZ gene in patients with CMT1B, DSS, or congenital hypomyelination. They suggested that these disorders may not be distinct pathophysiologic entities but may instead form a spectrum of related 'myelinopathies' caused by an underlying defect in myelination. They hypothesized that the differences in clinical severity among MPZ mutations depend on the type of mutation and its effect on protein function, that is, loss of function versus dominant-negative effect.

Mandich et al. (1999) identified a de novo gln215-to-ter (Q215X) mutation in the MPZ gene in a patient with congenital hypomyelination. The authors concluded that congenital hypomyelination is allelic with disorders that have a milder phenotype and a different clinical and neuropathologic profile.

In a patient with congenital hypomyelination, Szigeti et al. (2003) identified a complex de novo mutation in the MPZ gene. The mutation was a 3-bp deletion of CTA at nucleotides 550 to 552 combined with an insertion of G at nucleotide 550. By conceptual translation, it was predicted to cause a frameshift.

Kochanski et al. (2004) identified a novel thr124-to-lys (T124K) mutation in the MPZ gene in a patient with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy.

Smit et al. (2008) identified a heterozygous MPZ mutation in a family with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy and confirmed it in 2 generations.

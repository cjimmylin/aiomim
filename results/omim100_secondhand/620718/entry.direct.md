### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that orofaciodigital syndrome XX (OFD20) is caused by biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) mutation in the RAB34 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Orofaciodigital syndrome XX (OFD20) is a ciliopathy. It causes defects in the development of the face and oral cavity together with digit anomalies. It can also involve the heart, brain, skeleton, and anorectal region (Bruel et al., 2023). One reported fetus had cleft lip and palate, micrognathia, short extremities, and both preaxial and postaxial polydactyly (Batkovskyte et al., 2024).

### Clinical Features

Bruel et al. (2023) reported 4 individuals from 3 unrelated families with a new form of oral-facial-digital syndrome, which they called OFDS-RAB34. Besides the oral, facial, and digital anomalies, the patients had cardiac, cerebral, skeletal, and anorectal defects.

Batkovskyte et al. (2024) reported a fetus with an atypical skeletal ciliopathy phenotype and multiple malformations. These included posterior neck edema, micrognathia, low-set and small ears, auricular hypoplasia, cleft lip and palate, short extremities, and a rare combination of preaxial and postaxial polydactyly.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OFD20 in the families reported by Bruel et al. (2023) and Batkovskyte et al. (2024) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Affected individuals carried biallelic RAB34 mutations, and in the family studied by Batkovskyte et al. (2024), each parent contributed 1 of the variants. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Bruel et al. (2023) used exome sequencing to study 4 individuals with OFD20 from 3 unrelated families. They found biallelic missense variants in the RAB34 gene, which encodes a member of the Rab GTPase superfamily. The variants clustered near the C terminus of RAB34. Functional studies showed that the mutant proteins had a strong loss of function. Some mutant proteins could still be recruited to the mother centriole, but cells expressing mutant RAB34 had a significant defect in cilium assembly. Bruel et al. (2023) noted that RAB34 acts selectively in cell types that use the intracellular ciliogenesis pathway.

Batkovskyte et al. (2024) used genome sequencing in a fetus with a skeletal ciliopathy phenotype. They found compound heterozygous variants in the RAB34 gene: c.254T-C, causing an ile85-to-thr (I85T) substitution, inherited from the mother; and c.691C-T, causing an arg231-to-ter (R231X) substitution, inherited from the father. An unaffected sib carried only the paternal variant. The authors noted that Rab34-null mice have a ciliopathy phenotype that includes cleft palate and polydactyly, which they took as support for pathogenicity.

### Animal Model

Batkovskyte et al. (2024) cited earlier work showing that Rab34-null mice have a ciliopathy phenotype that includes cleft palate and polydactyly.

Batkovskyte et al. (2024) used genome sequencing in a fetus with OFD20 and found compound heterozygous mutations in the RAB34 gene. One was a maternally inherited c.254T-C transition, causing an ile85-to-thr (I85T) substitution.

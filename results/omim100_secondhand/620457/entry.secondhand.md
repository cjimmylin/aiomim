### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that auriculocondylar syndrome-4 (ARCND4) is caused by a 430-kb tandem duplication within the HDAC9 gene on chromosome 7p.

### Description

Auriculocondylar syndrome (ARCND) is a rare disorder that affects structures derived from the first and second pharyngeal arches. Its main features are micrognathia, question mark ears and mandibular condyle hypoplasia. Less common features include microstomia, glossoptosis, postauricular tags and prominent cheeks. Clinical variability is wide, and some cases have isolated ear anomalies (Romanelli Tavares et al., 2022). ARCND4 is caused by a tandem duplication at the HDAC9/TWIST1 locus on chromosome 7 (Romanelli Tavares et al., 2022).

### Clinical Features

Masotti et al. (2008) described a multigeneration family (F1) with 11 individuals affected with auriculocondylar syndrome and found considerable variability in features within the family. Facial asymmetry, a feature not highlighted in earlier reports of the syndrome, was highly prevalent among the affected members.

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) restudied the family (F1) reported by Masotti et al. (2008). This nonconsanguineous 4-generation Brazilian family had 11 members with typical features of ARCND. Among the affected members with documentation, most had question mark ears (8 of 10), microstomia (8 of 10) and micrognathia (6 of 10). Intrafamilial variability was considerable. None of the patients had the large, open fontanelles that are the hallmark of trisomy 7p. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

In the family studied by Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022), ARCND4 was transmitted in an autosomal dominant pattern. The causative duplication segregated with the disease across 4 generations, and all unaffected relatives were negative for it, suggesting full penetrance in this family.

### Mapping

In the ARCND family F1, Masotti et al. (2008) used segregation analysis to exclude the loci for Treacher Collins syndrome, oculoauriculovertebral spectrum and Townes-Brocks syndrome. A genomewide search mapped a locus to chromosome 1p21.1-q23.3 in a second family (F2), but this locus was not linked to the phenotype in F1. ⚠︎

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) genotyped 9 affected and 3 unaffected members of family F1 using a 50K SNP array and found 3 regions with positive LOD scores. The highest LOD score was 2.88, close to the maximum theoretical LOD score of 2.93 for this family. It was obtained for a region of about 17.6 Mb on chromosome 7 (chr7:14395902-32017194, hg38). Lower positive LOD scores were obtained on chromosome 14 (2.41) and chromosome 18 (1.67). Genotyping of microsatellite markers narrowed the chromosome 7 region to chr7:14395902-28158440 (hg38).

### Heterogeneity

The phenotype in family F1 was not linked to the chromosome 1p21.1-q23.3 locus mapped in another ARCND family. Masotti et al. (2008) concluded that auriculocondylar syndrome is genetically heterogeneous, with at least 2 loci. Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) found no pathogenic variants in PLCB4, GNAI3 or EDN1 in family F1 and identified a fourth ARCND locus on chromosome 7.

### Molecular Genetics

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) studied the Brazilian ARCND family F1, which had originally been reported by Masotti et al. (2008). Sanger sequencing of the coding regions, 5-prime UTRs and splice sites of PLCB4, GNAI3 and EDN1 found no pathogenic variants. Whole-exome sequencing was performed in 4 affected individuals. The only variant left in the linked regions was a synonymous change in TRIL, and it did not segregate with the disease. No obvious pathogenic variants were found in endothelin pathway genes. Gene prioritization ranked TWIST1 as the top candidate within the chromosome 7 region. Targeted resequencing of a 2.4-Mb region around TWIST1 found no likely pathogenic variant in TWIST1. It did find a tandem duplication within HDAC9, present only in the 3 affected family members tested. The duplication spanned 430,302 bp (NC_000007.14:g.18437239_18867540dup), lay telomeric to TWIST1 and covered most of the HDAC9 gene. Several short HDAC9 transcript isoforms were duplicated in their entirety. The full-length, catalytically active isoforms extended beyond the breakpoint and were predicted to be disrupted. Breakpoint PCR and Sanger sequencing showed that the duplication segregated with the phenotype in the family (LOD score of 2.88). All unaffected individuals were negative. ⚠︎

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) also searched DECIPHER and found 19 patients with copy-number gains in the region. Most of these gains were much larger than the ARCND duplication. Seven of the 19 patients had ear malformation and 7 had micrognathia. One duplication of 179 kb lay entirely within the ARCND duplicated region but did not include the TWIST1 regulatory elements, and that patient had no ARCND features.

### Pathogenesis

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) used Capture-C in human embryonic calvarial mesenchymal cells with the TWIST1 promoter as the viewpoint. They found multiple cis interactions, most frequently with sequences within HDAC9 and the duplicated region, suggesting that this region contains elements regulating TWIST1. Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) were derived from patients and differentiated into neural crest cells (NCCs). Patient-derived NCCs had increased HDAC9 mRNA (3.15-fold) and TWIST1 mRNA (2.03-fold) compared with controls, whereas iPSCs showed no difference. Expression of BARX1, NKX3.2 and GSC did not differ significantly. Patient-derived NCCs had normal cell cycle distribution but significantly reduced migratory capacity (4.3-fold decrease). NCC-derived mesenchymal stem cells from patients had significantly lower alkaline phosphatase activity after 9 days of osteoinduction (20.3-fold decrease) and showed a subtle decrease in matrix mineralization. The authors concluded that the duplication causes deregulation of TWIST1 expression, which leads to ARCND features through impaired neural crest migration and osteogenic differentiation.

In affected members of a 4-generation Brazilian family with auriculocondylar syndrome-4 (ARCND4), previously reported by Masotti et al. (2008), Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) identified a 430,302-bp tandem duplication (NC_000007.14:g.18437239_18867540dup) covering most of the HDAC9 gene, telomeric to TWIST1. The duplication was found by targeted resequencing of the TWIST1 region after linkage analysis, and breakpoint PCR confirmed that it segregated with the disease (LOD score of 2.88).

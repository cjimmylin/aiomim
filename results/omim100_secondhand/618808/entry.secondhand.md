### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that intellectual developmental disorder with poor growth and with or without seizures or ataxia (IDPOGSA) is caused by homozygous mutation in the ABCA2 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Intellectual developmental disorder with poor growth and with or without seizures or ataxia (IDPOGSA) is an autosomal recessive neurologic disorder caused by biallelic truncating variants in ABCA2. Reported patients had global developmental delay and intellectual disability (Maddirevula et al., 2019). Patients in a second family had cerebellar ataxia with dysarthria (Aslam and Naz, 2019). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Maddirevula et al. (2019) reported 2 families with global developmental delay and intellectual disability. Facial photographs of the index patient in 1 family (16DG0071) showed no obvious gross dysmorphism. Maddirevula et al. (2019) noted that this phenotype differed from the amyotrophic lateral sclerosis previously reported in association with ABCA2 by Steinberg et al. They suggested that it might represent a distinct allelic disorder.

Aslam and Naz (2019) studied 2 sibs from a consanguineous Pakistani family with a movement disorder. Their symptoms fit cerebellar ataxia with dysarthria. Magnetic resonance imaging of the older sib showed no brain abnormalities. Blood low density lipoprotein levels were raised in both affected individuals. The authors noted that the patients' phenotype closely matched that of Abca2 knockout mice.

### Biochemical Features

Aslam and Naz (2019) found raised blood low density lipoprotein levels in 2 sibs with ABCA2-related ataxia and dysarthria.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of IDPOGSA in the families reported by Maddirevula et al. (2019) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. In these families, homozygous ABCA2 variants fully segregated with the disorder. Aslam and Naz (2019) also considered the disorder in their consanguineous family to be recessively inherited. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Maddirevula et al. (2019) searched the autozygome and exome data of patients with suspected Mendelian phenotypes. In 2 families with global developmental delay and intellectual disability, they identified 2 different homozygous truncating mutations in the ABCA2 gene: a 1-bp duplication (c.740dupT), predicted to cause a frameshift (Gly248ArgfsTer38), and a c.1027C-T transition causing a gln343-to-ter (Q343X) substitution. Both mutations fully segregated with the disorder in their respective families.

Aslam and Naz (2019) studied 2 affected sibs from a consanguineous Pakistani family with cerebellar ataxia and dysarthria. Using whole-exome sequencing, they identified a 1-bp deletion in the ABCA2 gene (c.4993delG), predicted to cause a frameshift (Val1665TyrfsTer36). Sanger sequencing showed that the variant segregated with the disorder. The variant was absent from all publicly available databases and from ethnically matched controls.

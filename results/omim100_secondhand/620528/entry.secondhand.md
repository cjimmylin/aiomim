### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal dominant distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-11 (HMND11) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the SPTAN1 gene, which encodes alpha-II spectrin.

### Description

Autosomal dominant distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-11 (HMND11) is a juvenile-onset form of distal hereditary motor neuropathy. Distal hereditary motor neuropathies are a rare subgroup of inherited peripheral neuropathies characterized by length-dependent axonal degeneration of lower motor neurons without significant involvement of sensory neurons. In HMND11, penetrance can be variable and severity differs greatly between patients (Beijer et al., 2019).

### Clinical Features

Beijer et al. (2019) reported 3 unrelated families with autosomal dominant juvenile-onset hereditary motor neuropathy. Affected individuals had a length-dependent axonal degeneration of lower motor neurons without significant involvement of sensory neurons. Penetrance was variable in 2 of the 3 families, and phenotype severity differed greatly between patients. ⚠︎

Dong et al. (2021) reported a Chinese family with hereditary motor neuropathy.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous SPTAN1 mutations found in families with HMND11 by Beijer et al. (2019) segregated in an autosomal dominant pattern, with variable penetrance in 2 of 3 families. Dong et al. (2021) reported a de novo SPTAN1 nonsense mutation in a Chinese family with hereditary motor neuropathy.

### Molecular Genetics

Beijer et al. (2019) used next-generation sequencing in 3 unrelated families with autosomal dominant juvenile-onset hereditary motor neuropathy and found heterozygous nonsense mutations in the SPTAN1 gene. Mutant mRNA with the nonsense mutations was degraded by nonsense-mediated decay, and protein levels were reduced in patient cells. They noted that dominant-negative SPTAN1 mutations had already been described as causing a range of epilepsy phenotypes.

Ylikallio et al. (2020) identified a de novo SPTAN1 mutation in a patient with axonal sensorimotor neuropathy and a developmental disorder. The patient was a 16-year-old male with a frameshift variant in SPTAN1. In addition to peripheral neuropathy, he had severe dyslexia and difficulties with executive function. †

Dong et al. (2021) identified a novel de novo heterozygous nonsense mutation in the SPTAN1 gene in a Chinese family with hereditary motor neuropathy. ⚠︎

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that otofaciocervical syndrome-1 (OTFCS) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the EYA1 gene on chromosome 8q13. ⚠︎

### Description

Otofaciocervical syndrome is a developmental disorder clinically similar to branchiootorenal (BOR) syndrome. It has additional features, notably changes of the face and shoulder girdle (Rickard et al., 2001; Estefanía et al., 2006). Distinguishing features include a long face with a narrow nose, a high arched palate, prominent and dysmorphic ears, a long neck, sloping shoulders and clavicles, low winged scapulae, and deafness (Dallapiccola and Mingarelli, 1995).

### Clinical Features

Fára et al. (1967) gave the first description of otofaciocervical syndrome, in a family. †

Dallapiccola and Mingarelli (1995) described a sporadic patient with OFC syndrome. Distinguishing features were a long face with a narrow nose, a high arched palate, prominent and dysmorphic ears, a long neck, sloping shoulders and clavicles, and winged, low, laterally set scapulae. The patient also had tetralogy of Fallot and deafness due to a cochlear malformation. Some features of the original family, including lateral cervical fistulae, had suggested that OFC syndrome might be a variant of BOR syndrome. However, this patient had no preauricular tags, lacrimal duct stenosis, or renal malformations. The patient also had distinct facial and radiographic findings and markedly downward-sloping shoulders. Dallapiccola and Mingarelli (1995) concluded that these findings supported OFC syndrome being a unique, clinically recognizable entity.

Rickard et al. (2001) reported a patient with OFC syndrome and a complex phenotype that included features of BOR syndrome.

Estefanía et al. (2006) reported a patient with OFC syndrome. The authors noted that OFC is hard to distinguish from BOR syndrome for two reasons: BOR is often associated with other diverse malformations, and its expressivity varies even within a family.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous EYA1 mutation reported in OTFCS by Rickard et al. (2001) occurred de novo. Fára et al. (1967) originally described the disorder in a family. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Pohl et al. (2013) studied a large consanguineous Turkish family with autosomal recessive otofaciocervical syndrome. Whole-exome sequencing of a single pooled DNA sample from 4 affected individuals identified a homozygous missense mutation in the PAX1 gene (c.497G-T; G166V) that cosegregated with the disease. In a luciferase reporter assay, the corresponding mouse Pax1 mutant showed significantly reduced transactivation of an Nkx3-2 promoter sequence.

### Molecular Genetics

Rickard et al. (2001) developed a fluorescent quantitative multiplex PCR dosage assay for 3 exons at the 3-prime end of the EYA1 gene (exons 7, 9, and 13). Combining this assay with microsatellite marker analysis, they found de novo deletions of EYA1 and the surrounding region in 2 patients with complex phenotypes that included features of BOR syndrome. One of these patients had OFC syndrome and carried a large deletion of the EYA1 gene region. Rickard et al. (2001) concluded that OFC syndrome is allelic with BOR syndrome and is a contiguous gene deletion syndrome involving EYA1. They compared the boundaries of this deletion with other reported rearrangements of the region. This comparison narrowed the critical region for Duane syndrome to the interval between markers D8S553 and D8S1797, a genetic distance of about 1 cM.

Estefanía et al. (2006) found a heterozygous single-nucleotide substitution at a splice site of the EYA1 gene (c.639+1G-A) in a patient with OFC syndrome. Earlier findings had led to the hypothesis that OFC is caused by contiguous deletions of EYA1 and adjacent genes. Estefanía et al. (2006) concluded instead that patients with OFC syndrome can carry not only major rearrangements but also point mutations that alter the EYA1 reading frame. ⚠︎

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 OTOFACIOCERVICAL SYNDROME 1**  
EYA1, IVS8DS, G-A, +1 [c.639+1G>A]

Estefanía et al. (2006) reported a patient with otofaciocervical syndrome. The patient carried a heterozygous G-to-A substitution at the invariant donor splice site of intron 8 of the EYA1 gene (c.639+1G-A), which was predicted to alter the EYA1 reading frame. ⚠︎

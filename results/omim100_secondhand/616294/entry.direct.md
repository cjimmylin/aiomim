### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Cole-Carpenter syndrome-2 (CLCRP2) is caused by compound heterozygous mutation in the SEC24D gene.

### Description

Cole-Carpenter syndrome-2 is a rare skeletal dysplasia associated with low bone mass or an osteogenesis imperfecta-like phenotype (Takeyari et al., 2018). Its features include severely disturbed ossification of the skull and multiple fractures beginning before birth. Patients also have short stature and craniofacial malformations, including macrocephaly, frontal bossing, midface hypoplasia, and micrognathia (Garbes et al., 2015). ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

Garbes et al. (2015) described the disorder caused by SEC24D mutations as a syndromic form of osteogenesis imperfecta. In their report, one patient most closely resembled Cole-Carpenter syndrome and 2 fetuses were initially suspected to have a severe type of osteogenesis imperfecta. Moosa et al. (2016) and Zhang et al. (2017) reported SEC24D mutations in patients with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta. Takeyari et al. (2018) noted that SEC24D variants had been reported both in Cole-Carpenter syndrome type 2 and in autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta.

### Clinical Features

Garbes et al. (2015) reported 3 affected individuals from 2 families with a similar skeletal phenotype. They were a 7-year-old boy whose phenotype most closely resembled Cole-Carpenter syndrome and 2 fetuses initially suspected to have a severe type of osteogenesis imperfecta. All 3 had severely disturbed ossification of the skull and multiple fractures with prenatal onset. The boy had short stature and craniofacial malformations, including macrocephaly, midface hypoplasia, micrognathia, frontal bossing, and downslanting palpebral fissures. The authors compared the disorder with cranio-lenticulo-sutural dysplasia (CLSD), which is caused by SEC23A mutations and has a largely overlapping craniofacial phenotype. However, CLSD is not characterized by generalized bone fragility, and the original family with CLSD had cataracts.

Zhang et al. (2017) studied 2 Chinese families with osteogenesis imperfecta. The proband in family 1 was a 23-year-old man with recurrent fractures, dentinogenesis imperfecta, an unclosed anterior fontanel, and facial dysmorphism. Three-dimensional CT imaging of his cranium showed skull deformities with a broad ossification defect in the frontoapical area, a widened sagittal suture, and wormian bones. The proband in family 2 was a 7-year-old boy who also had recurrent fractures, dentinogenesis imperfecta, and an unclosed anterior fontanel, but no obvious facial dysmorphism.

Takeyari et al. (2018) described a 15-year-old Japanese boy with short-trunk short stature and craniofacial abnormalities, including ocular proptosis, marked frontal bossing, midface hypoplasia, and micrognathia. These features were consistent with a diagnosis of Cole-Carpenter syndrome. He also had low bone mineral density and basilar impression.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of Cole-Carpenter syndrome-2 is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Affected individuals have been compound heterozygous for SEC24D mutations (Garbes et al., 2015; Zhang et al., 2017; Takeyari et al., 2018). ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Using whole-exome sequencing, Garbes et al. (2015) identified 3 mutant SEC24D alleles in 3 affected individuals from 2 families. The alleles were a truncating mutation, c.613C-T (Q205X), and 2 missense mutations: c.3044C-T (S1015F), located in a cargo-binding pocket, and c.2933A-C (Q978P), located in the gelsolin-like domain. Each affected individual was compound heterozygous for 2 of these alleles. The c.3044C-T mutation lay within a 14-Mb founder haplotype shared by all 3. In skin fibroblasts from the affected boy, electron and immunofluorescence microscopy showed inefficient export of procollagen from the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and dilated ER tubules. This reproduced the cellular phenotype of individuals with CLSD. The authors noted that the cellular and morphologic phenotypes were in concordance with those of the Sec24d-deficient fish mutants vbi (medaka) and bulldog (zebrafish).

Moosa et al. (2016) reported mutations in the SEC24D gene in patients with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta.

Using whole-exome sequencing in 2 Chinese probands with osteogenesis imperfecta, Zhang et al. (2017) identified compound heterozygous missense mutations in the SEC24D gene in each proband. The proband in family 1 carried c.2723G-A (C908Y) and c.2842T-C (S948P). The proband in family 2 carried c.938G-A (R313H) and c.875C-T (P292L).

Using whole-exome sequencing, Takeyari et al. (2018) identified compound heterozygous variants in the SEC24D gene, R484X and R313H, in a Japanese boy with Cole-Carpenter syndrome.

### Animal Model

Garbes et al. (2015) noted that the cellular and morphologic phenotypes of their patients were in concordance with those described for the Sec24d-deficient fish mutants vbi (medaka) and bulldog (zebrafish).

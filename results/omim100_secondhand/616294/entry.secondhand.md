### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Cole-Carpenter syndrome-2 (CLCRP2) is caused by compound heterozygous mutation in the SEC24D gene.

### Description

Cole-Carpenter syndrome-2 is an autosomal recessive skeletal disorder resembling osteogenesis imperfecta. Its main features are bone fragility with multiple fractures, which can begin before birth, and severely disturbed ossification of the skull. Patients also have craniofacial malformations such as macrocephaly, frontal bossing, midface hypoplasia, and micrognathia (Garbes et al., 2015; Takeyari et al., 2018). In some patients reported with SEC24D mutations, the facial dysmorphism is mild or absent, and these patients have been diagnosed with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta (Zhang et al., 2017). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Garbes et al. (2015) reported 3 affected individuals from 2 families with a similar skeletal phenotype. One was a 7-year-old boy whose phenotype most closely resembled Cole-Carpenter syndrome. The other 2 were fetuses initially suspected to have a severe type of osteogenesis imperfecta. All 3 had severely disturbed ossification of the skull and multiple fractures beginning before birth. The 7-year-old boy had short stature and craniofacial malformations including macrocephaly, midface hypoplasia, micrognathia, frontal bossing, and downslanting palpebral fissures. Garbes et al. (2015) noted that cranio-lenticulo-sutural dysplasia (CLSD), which is caused by SEC23A mutations, has a largely overlapping craniofacial phenotype. However, CLSD does not feature generalized bone fragility, and the original family with CLSD had cataracts.

Zhang et al. (2017) studied 2 Chinese families with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta. The proband in family 1 was a 23-year-old man with recurrent fractures, dentinogenesis imperfecta, an unclosed anterior fontanel, and facial dysmorphism. Three-dimensional CT imaging of his skull showed skull deformities with a broad ossification defect in the frontoapical area, a widened sagittal suture, and wormian bones. The proband in family 2 was a 7-year-old boy who also had recurrent fractures, dentinogenesis imperfecta, and an unclosed anterior fontanel, but no obvious facial dysmorphism.

Takeyari et al. (2018) reported a 15-year-old Japanese boy with short-trunk short stature and craniofacial abnormalities including ocular proptosis, marked frontal bossing, midface hypoplasia, and micrognathia. These features were consistent with a diagnosis of Cole-Carpenter syndrome. He also had low bone mineral density and basilar impression.

### Inheritance

Patients with mutations in the SEC24D gene have been compound heterozygous (Garbes et al., 2015; Zhang et al., 2017; Takeyari et al., 2018), which is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Moosa et al. (2016) and Zhang et al. (2017) both described SEC24D-related osteogenesis imperfecta as autosomal recessive.

### Molecular Genetics

Garbes et al. (2015) carried out whole-exome sequencing in 3 affected individuals from 2 families with a syndromic form of osteogenesis imperfecta. Each was compound heterozygous for 2 of 3 mutant alleles in the SEC24D gene. The alleles were a truncating mutation (Q205X), a missense mutation in a cargo-binding pocket (S1015F), and a missense mutation in the gelsolin-like domain (Q978P). The c.3044C-T (S1015F) mutation lay on a 14-Mb founder haplotype shared by all 3 individuals. Electron and immunofluorescence microscopy of skin fibroblasts from the affected boy showed that procollagen was exported inefficiently from the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and that the ER tubules were dilated. This closely reproduced the cellular phenotype of individuals with CLSD. ⚠︎

Moosa et al. (2016) reported mutations in the SEC24D gene in patients with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta.

Zhang et al. (2017) carried out whole-exome sequencing in the probands of 2 Chinese families with osteogenesis imperfecta and found novel compound heterozygous missense mutations in the SEC24D gene. The proband in family 1 carried C908Y and S948P. The proband in family 2 carried R313H and P292L.

Takeyari et al. (2018) used whole-exome sequencing in a Japanese boy with Cole-Carpenter syndrome and found compound heterozygous variants in the SEC24D gene: R484X and R313H.

### Animal Model

Garbes et al. (2015) noted that the cellular and morphologic phenotypes of patients with SEC24D mutations matched those described in Sec24d-deficient fish mutants: vbi in medaka and bulldog in zebrafish.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 COLE-CARPENTER SYNDROME 2**  
SEC24D, GLN205TER [c.613C>T; p.Gln205Ter]

In affected individuals from 2 families with a syndromic form of osteogenesis imperfecta, Garbes et al. (2015) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the SEC24D gene, one of which was a truncating c.613C-T transition, resulting in a gln205-to-ter (Q205X) substitution. Each affected individual was compound heterozygous for 2 of 3 mutant SEC24D alleles. All 3 affected individuals shared the S1015F mutation on a founder haplotype.

**.0002 COLE-CARPENTER SYNDROME 2**  
SEC24D, SER1015PHE [c.3044C>T; p.Ser1015Phe]

Garbes et al. (2015) found a c.3044C-T transition in the SEC24D gene, resulting in a ser1015-to-phe (S1015F) substitution in a cargo-binding pocket. They found it in compound heterozygous state in 3 affected individuals from 2 families: a 7-year-old boy with features resembling Cole-Carpenter syndrome and 2 fetuses suspected to have severe osteogenesis imperfecta. In each, the second allele was either Q205X or Q978P. The mutation lay on a 14-Mb founder haplotype shared by all 3 individuals. Skin fibroblasts from the boy showed inefficient export of procollagen from the ER and dilated ER tubules.

**.0003 COLE-CARPENTER SYNDROME 2**  
SEC24D, GLN978PRO [c.2933A>C; p.Gln978Pro]

Garbes et al. (2015) found a c.2933A-C transversion in the SEC24D gene, resulting in a gln978-to-pro (Q978P) substitution in the gelsolin-like domain. It occurred in compound heterozygous state with the S1015F mutation in individuals with a syndromic form of osteogenesis imperfecta.

**.0004 COLE-CARPENTER SYNDROME 2**

Whole-exome sequencing found compound heterozygous mutations in the SEC24D gene: a c.2723G-A transition resulting in a cys908-to-tyr (C908Y) substitution, and a c.2842T-C transition resulting in a ser948-to-pro (S948P) substitution.

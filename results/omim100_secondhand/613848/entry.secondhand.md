### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that osteogenesis imperfecta type X (OI10) is caused by homozygous mutation in the SERPINH1 gene, which encodes the collagen chaperone protein HSP47.

### Description

Osteogenesis imperfecta type X is an autosomal recessive form of osteogenesis imperfecta (OI), a disorder of bone fragility and fractures. The fractures may come with bone deformity, dentinogenesis imperfecta, short stature, and shortened life span (Christiansen et al., 2010). It is caused by defects in HSP47, the type I procollagen chaperone encoded by SERPINH1. Christiansen et al. (2010) described a severe OI phenotype. Duran et al. (2015) noted that mutations in SERPINH1, like those in FKBP10, mostly cause a moderately severe form of OI.

### Clinical Features

Christiansen et al. (2010) reported severe recessive OI in a young child who was homozygous for a missense mutation in SERPINH1. ⚠︎

Duran et al. (2015) described a patient with OI caused by a new mutation in SERPINH1. ⚠︎

### Biochemical Features

In fibroblasts from a patient homozygous for a SERPINH1 leu78-to-pro mutation, Christiansen et al. (2010) found that the endoplasmic reticulum (ER)-resident protein HSP47 was degraded by the proteasome. Type I procollagen built up in the Golgi of the patient's fibroblasts, and part of the secreted type I procollagen was sensitive to protease. The authors suggested that HSP47 checks the integrity of the type I procollagen triple helix at the ER/cis-Golgi boundary. When HSP47 is absent, procollagen moves from the ER to the Golgi faster and its helical structure is compromised. Because the prolyl residue at position 986 of the proalpha1(I) triple helical domain was normally 3-hydroxylated, they placed HSP47 downstream of the CRTAP/P3H1/CyPB complex that carries out prolyl 3-hydroxylation.

Duran et al. (2015) found that a new SERPINH1 mutation destabilized and mislocalized HSP47 and had similar secondary effects on FKBP65. The evidence suggested that HSP47 and FKBP65 normally work together during posttranslational maturation of type I procollagen but fail to interact properly in cells with mutant HSP47. The authors concluded that OI due to HSP47 deficiency and OI due to FKBP65 deficiency share a common cellular pathway.

### Inheritance

Christiansen et al. (2010) found a homozygous SERPINH1 mutation in a patient with severe OI, which established autosomal recessive inheritance for OI type X.

### Molecular Genetics

In a young child with severe OI, Christiansen et al. (2010) identified a homozygous c.233T-C transition in the SERPINH1 gene, resulting in a leu78-to-pro (L78P) substitution in HSP47. The mutant protein was degraded by the proteasome, and the patient's fibroblasts handled type I procollagen abnormally. ⚠︎

Duran et al. (2015) identified a new mutation in SERPINH1 in a patient with OI. The mutation destabilized and mislocalized HSP47 and disrupted its interaction with FKBP65. ⚠︎

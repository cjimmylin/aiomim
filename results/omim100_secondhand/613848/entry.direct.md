### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that osteogenesis imperfecta type X (OI10) is caused by homozygous mutation in the SERPINH1 gene, which encodes the collagen chaperone protein HSP47.

### Description

Osteogenesis imperfecta type X (OI10) is an autosomal recessive form of osteogenesis imperfecta caused by mutation in SERPINH1, which encodes the type I procollagen chaperone HSP47. Christiansen et al. (2010) described it as a severe form of OI. Osteogenesis imperfecta is characterized by bone fragility and fractures. These may be accompanied by bone deformity, dentinogenesis imperfecta, short stature, and shortened life span. Duran et al. (2015) noted that mutations in SERPINH1 and in FKBP10, which encodes the chaperone FKBP65, mainly produce a moderately severe form of OI.

### Inheritance

Christiansen et al. (2010) found that the severe OI phenotype caused by a missense mutation in SERPINH1 was inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern, with the affected individual being homozygous for the mutation.

### Molecular Genetics

In an individual with severe osteogenesis imperfecta, Christiansen et al. (2010) identified a homozygous c.233T-C transition in the SERPINH1 gene, resulting in a leu78-to-pro (L78P) substitution. The mutation caused the endoplasmic reticulum (ER)-resident HSP47 protein to be degraded by the proteasome. In fibroblasts from the affected individual, type I procollagen accumulated in the Golgi, and part of the secreted type I procollagen was sensitive to protease.

Duran et al. (2015) identified a new OI mutation in the SERPINH1 gene. The mutation destabilized HSP47 and caused it to be mislocalized. It had similar secondary effects on FKBP65.

### Pathogenesis

Christiansen et al. (2010) proposed that HSP47 checks the integrity of the type I procollagen triple helix at the boundary between the ER and the cis-Golgi. In their model, when HSP47 is absent, procollagen moves from the ER to the Golgi faster and its helical structure is compromised. In the patient's cells, the prolyl residue at position 986 of the proalpha1(I) triple helical domain was normally 3-hydroxylated. This placed the role of HSP47 downstream of the CRTAP/P3H1/CyPB complex, which is involved in prolyl 3-hydroxylation.

Duran et al. (2015) found evidence that HSP47 and FKBP65 work together during posttranslational maturation of type I procollagen. In cells with mutant HSP47, FKBP65 and HSP47 did not interact properly. The authors concluded that OI caused by HSP47 deficiency and OI caused by FKBP65 deficiency share a common cellular pathway.

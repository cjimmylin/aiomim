### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GABRB1 gene, which encodes the beta-1 subunit of the GABA(A) receptor. In all reported patients the mutation occurred de novo. ⚠︎

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is a severe neurologic disorder. Seizures begin in infancy and are refractory, and patients have developmental delay or impairment. Reported seizure phenotypes include infantile spasms (Epi4K Consortium et al., 2013; Janve et al., 2016) and epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (Burgess et al., 2019). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

The Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) studied 264 probands with 1 of 2 classic epileptic encephalopathies, infantile spasms (149 patients) or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome (115 patients). Patients with infantile spasms were required to have hypsarrhythmia on EEG. All patients had no evidence of moderate-to-severe developmental impairment before seizure onset. They also had normal brain imaging or only mild diffuse atrophy or focal cortical dysplasia. Janve et al. (2016) noted that the proband in this cohort with a GABRB1 mutation had infantile spasms.

Lien et al. (2016) described a second patient with epileptic encephalopathy and a de novo GABRB1 mutation. The child was diagnosed at 3 months of age with refractory epilepsy and severe developmental delay. †

Burgess et al. (2019) reported a patient (patient 63) with epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (EIMFS) and a de novo GABRB1 mutation. EIMFS was defined as focal seizures with clinical and/or electrical migration between the hemispheres and seizure onset before 6 months of age. The patient was studied at 2 years of age. Seizures began at 4 months and were focal at onset. The patient had profound to severe developmental impairment and hypotonia, but did not have microcephaly. Unlike most of the cohort, this patient was listed as seizure-free. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

All GABRB1 mutations reported in DEE45 patients occurred de novo and in the heterozygous state (Epi4K Consortium et al., 2013; Janve et al., 2016; Lien et al., 2016; Burgess et al., 2019). This is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

The Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) performed exome sequencing of 264 probands with infantile spasms or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome and their parents, and confirmed 329 de novo mutations by Sanger sequencing. Six of the de novo mutations affected subunits of the ionotropic GABA receptor: 4 in GABRB3, 1 in GABRA1, and 1 in GABRB1. The authors suggested that individual rare mutations in different genes may converge on shared pathways. Janve et al. (2016) identified the GABRB1 mutation in this cohort as a heterozygous de novo phe246-to-ser (F246S) substitution in a patient with infantile spasms.

Janve et al. (2016) studied the F246S mutation functionally by coexpressing it with GABA(A) receptor alpha-1 and gamma-2L subunits in HEK293T cells. F246 is an invariant residue across GABA(A) receptor subunits and lies in transmembrane domain 1. In the homozygous expression condition, the mutation caused a minor (25%) reduction in peak GABA-evoked current density. In the heterozygous condition, it had no effect on current density. The mutation slowed current deactivation. At the single-channel level, it increased mean open time about 3.75-fold and burst duration about 3.2-fold, decreased opening frequency, and reduced single-channel conductance to about 21 pS, while channel open probability was unchanged. The mutation also increased holding currents and caused spontaneous channel openings, with a 4-fold increase in spontaneous open probability. Surface levels of the mutant subunit were slightly but significantly reduced, without a reduction in total levels. Janve et al. (2016) concluded that the mutation impaired GABA(A) receptor function and suggested GABRB1 as a candidate human epilepsy gene.

In a patient with epileptic encephalopathy, Lien et al. (2016) identified a de novo c.860C-T transition in the GABRB1 gene, resulting in a thr287-to-ile (T287I) substitution in the second transmembrane helix of the beta-1 subunit. Functional data on this variant were not reported. †

In a patient with EIMFS (patient 63), Burgess et al. (2019) identified a heterozygous de novo ile247-to-thr (I247T) mutation in the GABRB1 gene by whole-exome sequencing. They found the mutation in a study of 135 patients from 128 unrelated families with EIMFS. The variant was absent from the ExAC and gnomAD databases and was classified as likely pathogenic.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DEVELOPMENTAL AND EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY 45**  
GABRB1, PHE246SER [F246S; p.Phe246Ser]

The Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) found a de novo GABRB1 mutation in a proband with epileptic encephalopathy by exome sequencing of patient-parent trios. Janve et al. (2016) identified it as a heterozygous de novo phe246-to-ser (F246S) substitution in a patient with infantile spasms. F246 is an invariant residue in transmembrane domain 1. Janve et al. (2016) expressed the mutation in HEK293T cells. It caused a minor reduction in peak GABA-evoked current in the homozygous condition only, and it slowed current deactivation. At the single-channel level, it increased mean open time and burst duration, decreased opening frequency, and reduced single-channel conductance. It also increased spontaneous channel openings and slightly reduced surface expression of the mutant subunit.

**.0003 DEVELOPMENTAL AND EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY 45**  
GABRB1, ILE247THR [p.I247T; Chr4:47405633T>C]

In a patient with epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (patient 63), Burgess et al. (2019) identified a heterozygous de novo ile247-to-thr (I247T) mutation in the GABRB1 gene (Chr4:47405633T-C, GRCh37) by whole-exome sequencing. Seizures began at 4 months of age. The variant was absent from the ExAC and gnomAD databases and was classified as likely pathogenic.

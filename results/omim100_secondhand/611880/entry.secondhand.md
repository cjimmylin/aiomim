### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive dilated cardiomyopathy-2A (CMD2A) is caused by homozygous mutation in the TNNI3 gene, which encodes cardiac troponin I. ⚠︎

### Description

Familial isolated idiopathic dilated cardiomyopathy is usually inherited as an autosomal dominant trait with variable penetrance and expressivity. CMD2A is an autosomal recessive form of the disorder (Goldblatt et al., 1987). Murphy et al. (2004) identified the cardiac troponin I gene TNNI3 as the first gene for recessive idiopathic dilated cardiomyopathy.

### Clinical Features

Goldblatt et al. (1987) described autosomal recessive idiopathic dilated cardiomyopathy in 3 members of a consanguineous Madeira Portuguese family. After reviewing the literature, they concluded that the autosomal recessive form of dilated cardiomyopathy appeared to be more common than previously reported.

Seliem et al. (2000) studied 55 consecutive patients younger than 10 years with dilated cardiomyopathy, seen over a 5-year period in the Eastern Province of Saudi Arabia. Echocardiography was the main diagnostic method. The 55 patients made up 20% of the offspring of 41 families of Arab descent. In 19 families (46%) the parents were first cousins, and in 22 families (54%) there was no obvious consanguinity. In 95% of patients, age at presentation was under 30 months (range, 1 to 100 months). Of the patients, 38% were male and 62% were female. Twenty-five patients (46%) died, 15 (27%) improved, and 15 (27%) recovered. At diagnosis, the left ventricular shortening fraction ranged from 5 to 28%, and it did not differ among those who died, improved, or recovered.

### Inheritance

Goldblatt et al. (1987) reported autosomal recessive inheritance of idiopathic dilated cardiomyopathy in a consanguineous Madeira Portuguese family. In infants of Arab descent from Saudi Arabia, Seliem et al. (2000) performed complex segregation analysis using the mixed model of inheritance and found that a recessive model fit the family data best. In the family studied by Murphy et al. (2004), the disease was recessive.

### Heterogeneity

Carballo et al. (2009) found 2 heterozygous missense TNNI3 mutations, lys36-to-gln (K36Q) and asn185-to-lys (N185K), in a panel of 96 probands with dilated cardiomyopathy. Each mutation was associated with severe, early-onset familial dilated cardiomyopathy. These were the first reported autosomal dominant mutations in TNNI3 causing dilated cardiomyopathy. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Murphy et al. (2004) analyzed the TNNI3 gene in 235 patients with dilated cardiomyopathy, using a method able to detect both dominant and recessive mutations. In a family with recessive disease, they found a novel TNNI3 mutation. In functional studies, the mutation impaired troponin interactions, which could reduce myocardial contractility.

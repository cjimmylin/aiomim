### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal dominant pseudohypoaldosteronism type I (PHA1A) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding the mineralocorticoid receptor (NR3C2).

### Description

Pseudohypoaldosteronism type I is a salt-wasting disorder caused by target organ unresponsiveness to mineralocorticoids. It presents in the newborn with renal salt wasting, dehydration, hypotension, hyperkalemia, and metabolic acidosis, even though aldosterone levels are high. The autosomal dominant or sporadic form is milder than the autosomal recessive form, involves only the kidney, and remits with age (Hanukoglu, 1991; Geller et al., 1998). ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

Hanukoglu (1991) concluded that type I PHA includes 2 clinically and genetically distinct entities. One is an autosomal dominant form with a renal defect. The other is an autosomal recessive form with defects in multiple target organs.

### Clinical Features

Armanini et al. (1985) studied 3 patients with pseudohypoaldosteronism: a 28-year-old index case in Melbourne and 2 sibs in Munich, aged 8 and 2 years, whose parents were first cousins. The younger sib, a boy, was less severely affected clinically than his sister. The authors described the syndrome as salt wasting and failure to thrive, usually presenting in infancy with high urinary sodium despite hyponatremia, together with hyperkalemia, hyperreninemia, and elevated aldosterone levels.

Hanukoglu (1991) studied 2 kindreds with a total of 9 patients with PHA. In kindred I, the proband presented in infancy with renal salt wasting: vomiting, failure to thrive, short stature, hyponatremia, and hyperkalemia. The proband responded dramatically to a high-salt diet (2.5 g/day), and sodium supplementation was stopped at age 2 years. In 7 more members of the family across 3 generations, clinical expression of PHA ranged from none to moderate. In the affected members (the proband, the mother, and 2 brothers), hyperaldosteronism persisted for 13 years, whereas plasma renin activity gradually fell to near-normal values. Hanukoglu (1991) took this pattern to indicate tertiary hyperaldosteronism caused by an autonomously functioning zona glomerulosa. In kindred II, the proband was born to consanguineous parents and developed severe renal salt loss at age 9 days. She also had raised salivary and sweat electrolytes, consistent with unresponsiveness to mineralocorticoids in multiple organs. Life-threatening salt-wasting episodes recurred after age 2 years, and at age 5 years she still needed 14 g/day of salt supplements. A sister had died at 9 days of age with symptoms of PHA.

Geller et al. (1998) described PHA1 as neonatal renal salt wasting with dehydration, hypotension, hyperkalemia, and metabolic acidosis despite elevated aldosterone levels. They noted that the autosomal dominant or sporadic form is milder and remits with age, whereas in the autosomal recessive form the disease is severe and persists into adulthood.

Tajima et al. (2000) studied a Japanese family with the renal form of PHA1. They described the disorder as neonatal salt loss with dehydration, hypotension, hyperkalemia, and metabolic acidosis despite elevated plasma aldosterone levels and plasma renin activity. In the autosomal dominant form, salt wasting is milder and gradually improves with age.

### Biochemical Features

Armanini et al. (1985) looked for abnormal type I ('mineralocorticoid-like') receptors in mononuclear leukocytes from 3 patients with pseudohypoaldosteronism. In control monocytes, [3H]aldosterone was bound with high affinity (Kd approximately 3 nM) at 150 to 600 sites per cell. On repeated testing, monocytes from 2 of the patients showed no [3H]aldosterone binding. The third patient, who was less severely affected, had 62 sites per cell, more than 2 SD below the control value. Binding was normal in the parents of the 2 sibs. The authors concluded that pseudohypoaldosteronism is caused by a defect in the type I receptor, which may be complete or partial.

### Inheritance

In kindred I reported by Hanukoglu (1991), the pedigree was consistent with autosomal dominant transmission with variable expression. Geller et al. (1998) found heterozygous mineralocorticoid receptor gene mutations in 4 dominant kindreds and 1 sporadic kindred with PHA1, including 1 de novo mutation.

Armanini et al. (1985) found reduced or absent aldosterone binding in sibs born to first-cousin parents and suggested that transmission of pseudohypoaldosteronism might be autosomal recessive.

### Heterogeneity

Geller et al. (1998) found no ENaC gene mutations in 6 dominant and 7 sporadic PHA1 kindreds, and found mineralocorticoid receptor mutations in only 5 of these kindreds.

Viemann et al. (2001) found no NR3C2 mutation in 2 kindreds with autosomal dominant PHA1. They concluded that autosomal dominant or sporadic PHA1 is genetically heterogeneous and involves other genes that have not yet been identified.

Sartorato et al. (2003) found NR3C2 mutations in 70% of their familial cases of autosomal dominant PHA1. They considered that the absence of mutations in some families calls for extensive study of the NR3C2 gene and precise diagnostic procedures, so that other genes possibly involved in the disease can be identified.

Pujo et al. (2007) found mineralocorticoid receptor mutations to be very frequent in PHA1 patients classified by aldosterone and potassium levels. In 3 PHA1 kindreds, a genocopy remained possible. They suggested that the other patients without NR3C2 mutations might have different diseases that resemble PHA1 in the neonatal period.

### Molecular Genetics

Geller et al. (1998) screened the mineralocorticoid receptor gene (MLR; NR3C2) in 6 dominant and 7 sporadic PHA1 kindreds. They found heterozygous mutations in 1 sporadic and 4 dominant kindreds: 2 frameshift mutations (one of them de novo), 2 premature termination codons, and 1 splice donor mutation. The mutations segregated with PHA1 and were not found in unaffected subjects.

In all affected members of a Japanese family with renal PHA1, Tajima et al. (2000) used PCR and direct sequencing to identify a heterozygous missense mutation in the NR3C2 gene (L924P). Aldosterone did not increase reporter activity in transfected COS-1 cells expressing the mutant receptor. The authors stated that this was the first missense mutation in the NR3C2 gene reported in renal PHA1.

In a sporadic case of PHA1, Viemann et al. (2001) identified a heterozygous frameshift mutation in exon 9 of the NR3C2 gene (Ins2871C). The mutation affects the last 27 amino acids of the hormone-binding domain. Asymptomatic family members did not carry it. ⚠︎

Sartorato et al. (2003) analyzed the NR3C2 gene in 14 families with autosomal dominant or sporadic PHA1 and detected 6 heterozygous mutations. Two frameshift mutations in exon 2 (insT1354 and del8bp537) and a nonsense mutation in exon 4 (C645X) produce truncated proteins. Three missense mutations (G633R, Q776R, and L979P) affected receptor function in different ways. L979P had a transdominant negative effect on wildtype receptor activity, whereas G633R and Q776R probably cause PHA1 through haploinsufficiency.

In 6 patients with PHA1, Riepe et al. (2006) detected 6 heterozygous NR3C2 mutations. One frameshift mutation (c.1131dupT) had been reported before. A second frameshift mutation was c.2871dupC. There were 2 novel nonsense mutations that produce a truncated receptor protein, and 2 missense mutations, S818L and E972G. The authors studied aldosterone binding, transactivation, nuclear translocation, proteolysis, and 3-dimensional modeling to assess the functional effects of the mutations.

Pujo et al. (2007) searched the coding regions, intron-exon junctions, and untranslated exons of the NR3C2 gene for nucleotide substitutions, and also looked for large deletions. They found 22 abnormalities of the gene in 33 patients with PHA1.

### History

Cheek and Perry (1958) first described PHA type I as a salt-wasting syndrome in infancy caused by a defective renal tubular response to mineralocorticoids. Donnell et al. (1959) reported renal sodium loss, hyponatremia, and hyperkalemia that they attributed to renal tubular insensitivity to mineralocorticoids, and they called the condition 'pseudohypo-adrenalocorticism.' Raine and Roy (1962) also described a salt-losing syndrome in infancy, which they called pseudohypoadrenocorticalism. Hanukoglu et al. (1978) wrote about the inheritance of pseudohypoaldosteronism. ⚠︎

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 PSEUDOHYPOALDOSTERONISM, TYPE I, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
NR3C2, LEU924PRO [L924P; CTG-to-CCG at codon 924]

In all affected members of a Japanese family with renal PHA1, Tajima et al. (2000) identified a heterozygous mutation that changes codon 924 from CTG (leu) to CCG (pro), a leu924-to-pro (L924P) substitution. In COS-1 cells cotransfected with a glucocorticoid response element-tk-CAT reporter, aldosterone increased CAT activity when the wildtype receptor was expressed but had no effect when the mutant receptor was expressed.

**.0002 PSEUDOHYPOALDOSTERONISM, TYPE I, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
NR3C2, 1-BP INS, 2871C [Ins2871C; c.2871dupC]

In a sporadic case of PHA1, Viemann et al. (2001) identified a heterozygous frameshift mutation in exon 9 of the NR3C2 gene, a 1-bp insertion (Ins2871C). The mutation affects the last 27 amino acids of the hormone-binding domain. Asymptomatic family members did not carry it. ⚠︎

Riepe et al. (2006) found that this frameshift mutation (c.2871dupC) abolished aldosterone binding and transactivation because of a major change in receptor conformation.

**.0003 PSEUDOHYPOALDOSTERONISM, TYPE I, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
NR3C2, 1-BP INS, 1354T [insT1354]

In a family with autosomal dominant or sporadic PHA1, Sartorato et al. (2003) identified a heterozygous frameshift mutation in exon 2 of the NR3C2 gene (insT1354). The mutation creates a premature stop codon and a truncated protein.

**.0004 PSEUDOHYPOALDOSTERONISM, TYPE I, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
NR3C2, 8-BP DEL, NT537 [del8bp537]

In a family with autosomal dominant or sporadic PHA1, Sartorato et al. (2003) identified a heterozygous 8-bp deletion in exon 2 of the NR3C2 gene (del8bp537). The resulting frameshift creates a premature stop codon and a truncated protein.

**.0005 PSEUDOHYPOALDOSTERONISM, TYPE I, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
NR3C2, CYS645TER [C2157A; Cys645stop]

In a family with autosomal dominant or sporadic PHA1, Sartorato et al. (2003) identified a heterozygous 2157C-A transversion in exon 4 of the NR3C2 gene, which causes a cys645-to-ter (C645X) substitution and a truncated protein.

**.0006 PSEUDOHYPOALDOSTERONISM, TYPE I, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
NR3C2, GLY633ARG [G633R]

In a family with autosomal dominant or sporadic PHA1, Sartorato et al. (2003) identified a heterozygous gly633-to-arg (G633R) mutation in the DNA-binding domain of the NR3C2 gene. Maximal transactivation by the mutant receptor was reduced, but its binding characteristics and the ED50 of transactivation were similar to those of the wildtype receptor. The authors suggested that the mutation probably causes PHA1 through haploinsufficiency.

**.0007 PSEUDOHYPOALDOSTERONISM, TYPE I, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
NR3C2, GLN776ARG [Q776R]

In a family with autosomal dominant or sporadic PHA1, Sartorato et al. (2003) identified a heterozygous gln776-to-arg (Q776R) mutation in the ligand-binding domain of the NR3C2 gene. The mutant receptor showed reduced aldosterone binding and reduced ligand-dependent transactivation. The authors suggested that the mutation probably causes PHA1 through haploinsufficiency.

**.0008 PSEUDOHYPOALDOSTERONISM, TYPE I, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
NR3C2, LEU979PRO [L979P]

In a family with autosomal dominant or sporadic PHA1, Sartorato et al. (2003) identified a heterozygous leu979-to-pro (L979P) mutation in the ligand-binding domain of the NR3C2 gene. The mutant receptor did not bind aldosterone, had no ligand-dependent transactivation capacity, and had a transdominant negative effect on wildtype receptor activity.

**.0009 PSEUDOHYPOALDOSTERONISM, TYPE I, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
NR3C2, SER818LEU [S818L]

In a patient with PHA1, Riepe et al. (2006) identified a heterozygous ser818-to-leu (S818L) mutation in the NR3C2 gene. The mutant receptor did not bind aldosterone, did not activate transcription, and did not translocate into the nucleus. Three-dimensional modeling showed a major displacement of several residues involved in aldosterone binding. ⚠︎

**.0010 PSEUDOHYPOALDOSTERONISM, TYPE I, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
NR3C2, GLU972GLY [E972G]

In a patient with PHA1, Riepe et al. (2006) identified a novel heterozygous glu972-to-gly (E972G) mutation in the NR3C2 gene. The mutant receptor had markedly lower ligand-binding affinity and only 9% of wildtype transcriptional activity, which the authors attributed to major changes in receptor conformation.

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that multiple epiphyseal dysplasia-2 (EDM2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding the alpha-2 chain of type IX collagen (COL9A2) on chromosome 1p32.3-p33. ⚠︎

### Description

Multiple epiphyseal dysplasia (MED) is a dominantly inherited chondrodysplasia characterized by mild short stature and early-onset osteoarthrosis (Briggs et al., 1994). The EDM2 form is a subtype of MED in which the hips are not markedly affected at an early age. From childhood, patients have pain and stiffness of the knees, ankles, elbows, and finger joints, and they are prone to early degenerative joint disease (Fiedler et al., 2002).

### Clinical Features

Barrie et al. (1958) published a report titled 'Multiple epiphysial dysplasia.'

Versteylen et al. (1988) described 2 families that appeared to have multiple epiphyseal dysplasia. Severe osteochondritis of the knees was strikingly common in both families. The authors suggested that the osteochondritis was caused by the fragmented and maldeveloped epiphyses typical of epiphyseal dysplasia.

Briggs et al. (1994) studied a large family with MED, a dominantly inherited chondrodysplasia characterized by mild short stature and early-onset osteoarthrosis.

Fiedler et al. (2002) reported a family of 19 individuals over 4 generations, of whom 12 had a variant of multiple epiphyseal dysplasia with relative sparing of the hips during childhood. Beginning in childhood, the disease caused pain and stiffness of the knees, ankles, elbows, and finger joints. Some adult patients had repeated episodes of free articular bodies that caused locking of the joint. Affected individuals were prone to early degenerative joint disease. On electron microscopy, the diameter of collagen fibrils from free articular bodies removed arthroscopically from 2 patients did not differ obviously from that of normal articular cartilage.

In a report titled 'Double-layered patella in multiple epiphyseal dysplasia is not exclusive to DTDST mutation,' Nakashima et al. (2005) addressed the presence of a double-layered patella in MED that was not due to DTDST mutation.

### Inheritance

Briggs et al. (1994) described MED as a dominantly inherited chondrodysplasia and mapped the EDM2 locus in a large family with MED. Fiedler et al. (2002) reported a 4-generation family in which a COL9A2 mutation was present in affected members. Both reports are consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance of EDM2.

### Mapping

Briggs et al. (1994) performed linkage studies in a large family with MED. Linkage to the chromosome 19 interval previously linked to pseudoachondroplasia and a form of MED was excluded. Using this family, the authors identified an MED locus, designated EDM2, on the short arm of chromosome 1. This region contains the COL9A2 gene, which encodes the alpha-2 chain of type IX collagen, a structural component of the cartilage extracellular matrix.

Using fluorescence in situ hybridization, Warman et al. (1994) mapped the COL9A2 gene to chromosome 1p32.3-p33.

### Heterogeneity

Briggs et al. (1994) noted that both mild and severe pseudoachondroplasia, as well as a form of MED, had been linked to the same interval on chromosome 19. However, linkage to this interval was excluded in their family with MED, which instead mapped to chromosome 1p. This finding showed that MED is genetically heterogeneous.

### Molecular Genetics

Muragaki et al. (1996) reported that a mutation in the COL9A2 gene causes multiple epiphyseal dysplasia (EDM2).

In a 4-generation family with EDM2, Fiedler et al. (2002) screened candidate regions and identified a G-to-C substitution at position -1 in the exon 3/intron 3 splicing region of the COL9A2 gene. The authors considered it most likely that this change alters splicing. They confirmed the substitution in 8 clinically affected family members and in 3 presymptomatic young children, using both automated sequencing and BsaWI digestion of amplicons. Fiedler et al. (2002) concluded that, together with previous reports, their results indicated that mutations causing skipping of exon 3 within the COL3 domain of the alpha-2 chain of collagen IX may be relatively common in patients with the MED subtype in which the hips are not markedly affected at an early age.

### Diagnosis

Fiedler et al. (2002) suggested that in patients with EDM2 and their families, mutation screening of the candidate regions may help confirm the diagnosis, guide appropriate lifestyle advice, and support well-based genetic counseling.

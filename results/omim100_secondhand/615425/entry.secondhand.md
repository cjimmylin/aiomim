### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that epidermolysis bullosa simplex-3, localized or generalized intermediate, with BP230 deficiency (EBS3) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the dystonin gene (DST). ⚠︎

### Description

Epidermolysis bullosa simplex-3 (EBS3) is an autosomal recessive skin fragility disorder. Patients have trauma-induced blisters and erosions, mainly around the ankles and feet. The disorder is caused by loss of the epithelial isoform of bullous pemphigoid antigen-1 (BPAG1-e, also known as BP230), which is encoded by the DST gene. Skin from affected individuals shows loss of hemidesmosomal inner plaques and no immunostaining for BPAG1-e (Groves et al., 2010; Liu et al., 2012; Ganani et al., 2021). †

### Clinical Features

Groves et al. (2010) reported a 38-year-old man from a Kuwaiti family. He had lifelong generalized trauma-induced spontaneous blisters and erosions, particularly around the ankles. From age 37 years he had episodic numbness in his limbs. Groves et al. (2010) noted that these neurologic symptoms might also be due to the DST mutation. However, the patient also had a diagnosis of CADASIL, a cerebral small-vessel arteriopathy, which complicated the genotype-phenotype interpretation. ⚠︎

Liu et al. (2012) reported a 34-year-old Iranian woman born to consanguineous parents. She had blisters on the feet and blisters induced by friction. Liu et al. (2012) noted that some heterozygous carriers, such as parents of affected individuals, recalled blistering in childhood that usually improved and then stopped completely by the teenage years. †

Ganani et al. (2021) reported 5 patients with EBS from 3 families. Ganani et al. (2021) also reviewed 6 previously reported cases, bringing the total number of individuals with EBS due to DST variants to 11. They observed that patients had distinctive phenotypes regardless of the causative variant.

### Other Features

In the patient reported by Groves et al. (2010), transmission electron microscopy of skin showed loss of the hemidesmosomal inner plaques. Immunostaining of skin showed complete absence of BPAG1-e and reduced labeling for plectin, the beta-4 integrin subunit, and type XVII collagen. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Groves et al. (2010) and Liu et al. (2012) established autosomal recessive inheritance of EBS3, having found homozygous DST mutations in affected individuals from consanguineous families. Ganani et al. (2021) found biallelic DST mutations in further patients. Liu et al. (2012) observed that some heterozygous carriers recalled blistering in childhood that resolved by the teenage years. † ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In a 38-year-old Kuwaiti man with EBS3, Groves et al. (2010) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in the DST gene, gln1124-to-ter (Q1124X). The mutation lies in the region encoding the coiled-coil domain of BPAG1-e. Transmission electron microscopy had shown complete loss of hemidesmosomal inner plaques. Immunofluorescence microscopy with antibodies to hemidesmosomal inner plaque components showed complete absence of BP230 staining. ⚠︎

In an Iranian woman with EBS3 born to consanguineous parents, Liu et al. (2012) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in the DST gene, arg1249-to-ter (R1249X). The mutation lies within the coiled-coil domain of the epidermis-specific isoform of dystonin. †

Ganani et al. (2021) used whole-exome and direct sequencing to identify biallelic DST mutations in 5 patients with EBS from 3 families. The mutations included a novel 1-bp duplication, c.7097dupA, which results in a tyr2366-to-ter (Y2366X) substitution, and a second novel mutation, a 1-bp deletion (c.7429delC) that results in a frameshift (Leu2477SerfsTer13).

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Ganani et al. (2021) reviewed 11 individuals with EBS due to DST variants and found that patients had distinctive phenotypes regardless of the causative variant. ⚠︎

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that epidermolysis bullosa simplex-3 (EBS3), localized or generalized intermediate, with BP230 deficiency, is caused by homozygous or biallelic mutation in the DST gene, which encodes the epithelial isoform of bullous pemphigoid antigen-1 (BPAG1-e, also known as BP230). ⚠︎

### Description

Epidermolysis bullosa simplex (EBS) is a heterogeneous group of inherited disorders characterized by skin fragility due to intraepidermal separation (Ganani et al., 2021). Most cases result from heterozygous mutations in KRT5 or KRT14, but a minority of affected individuals carry mutations in non-keratin genes, including DST, which encodes an epithelial isoform of dystonin (Ganani et al., 2021). DST-associated EBS is transmitted as an autosomal recessive trait and was described by Groves et al. (2010) as a new subtype of EBS associated with loss of BPAG1-e (BP230).

### Clinical Features

Groves et al. (2010) reported a 38-year-old man with an inherited skin fragility disorder who had lifelong generalized trauma-induced spontaneous blisters and erosions, particularly around the ankles. He also had episodic numbness in his limbs, which started at the age of 37 years. Groves et al. (2010) noted that these neurologic symptoms might also be due to the DST mutation, but that he had a concomitant diagnosis of CADASIL (cerebral arteriopathy, autosomal dominant, with subcortical infarcts and leukoencephalopathy), a cerebral small-vessel arteriopathy, which complicated genotype-phenotype interpretation. Skin from the patient showed loss of hemidesmosomal inner plaques and complete absence of immunostaining for BPAG1-e, as well as reduced labeling for plectin, the beta-4 integrin subunit, and type XVII collagen.

Liu et al. (2012) reported autosomal recessive epidermolysis bullosa simplex due to loss of BPAG1-e expression.

Ganani et al. (2021) reported 5 patients with EBS from 3 families and reviewed previously reported cases, bringing the total number of individuals with EBS due to DST variants to 11. Patients displayed distinctive phenotypes regardless of the causative variant.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of EBS3 is autosomal recessive. Groves et al. (2010) identified a homozygous DST mutation in an affected individual, and Ganani et al. (2021) reported patients carrying biallelic DST mutations, noting that DST-associated EBS is transmitted as an autosomal recessive trait.

### Molecular Genetics

In a 38-year-old man with generalized trauma-induced skin blistering, Groves et al. (2010) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in the DST gene (Q1124X) within the region encoding the coiled-coil domain of the epithelial isoform BPAG1-e. The mutation was associated with loss of hemidesmosomal inner plaques and complete absence of skin immunostaining for BPAG1-e.

Liu et al. (2012) reported autosomal recessive EBS due to loss of BPAG1-e expression.

By whole-exome and direct sequencing in 5 patients with EBS from 3 families, Ganani et al. (2021) identified biallelic mutations in the DST gene, including 2 previously unreported variants: c.7097dupA (Y2366X) and c.7429delC (L2477SfsTer13).

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Ganani et al. (2021) reviewed 11 individuals with EBS due to DST variants, including 5 newly reported and 6 previously reported cases, to delineate phenotype-genotype correlations, and found that patients displayed distinctive phenotypes regardless of the causative variant.

The mutation was found by whole-exome and direct sequencing in a series of 5 patients from 3 families carrying biallelic DST mutations.

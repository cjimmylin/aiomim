### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that pyridoxine-refractory autosomal recessive sideroblastic anemia-2 (SIDBA2) is caused by mutation in the SLC25A38 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Sideroblastic anemia-2 is a congenital, nonsyndromic, autosomal recessive form of sideroblastic anemia that does not respond to pyridoxine. The sideroblastic anemias are a heterogeneous group of congenital and acquired blood disorders. Their defining feature is ringed sideroblasts: erythroid precursors in the bone marrow with abnormal iron deposits inside their mitochondria (Guernsey et al., 2009). Affected individuals may have severe anemia from birth that requires transfusions (Jardine et al., 1994).

### Clinical Features

Manabe et al. (1982) described a female infant with congenital refractory sideroblastic anemia that did not respond to pyridoxine therapy.

Jardine et al. (1994) reported a brother and sister, born to unaffected parents, who had transfusion-dependent, pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia from birth. Their hemoglobin levels were 4.3 and 6.4 g/dl, respectively. The mother did not have a dimorphic erythrocyte population.

Guernsey et al. (2009) found that patients with congenital sideroblastic anemia caused by SLC25A38 mutations had early-onset, severe, microcytic, hypochromic anemia without syndromic features. Both sexes were affected. †

### Biochemical Features

In a female infant with pyridoxine-refractory congenital sideroblastic anemia, Manabe et al. (1982) found markedly reduced delta-aminolevulinic acid (ALA) synthetase activity in erythroblasts, with and without pyridoxal phosphate. Mitochondrial neutral protease in erythroblasts specifically inactivates the apo form of ALA synthetase. Both the activity of this protease and the sensitivity of apo-ALA synthetase to it were normal. Manabe et al. (1982) therefore suggested that the low ALA synthetase activity resulted from impaired production of the enzyme rather than faster destruction, which could explain why the patient did not respond to pyridoxine.

Jardine et al. (1994) measured delta-aminolevulinate synthase activity in erythroblasts from fractionated marrow of an affected girl. The value was 135 pmol delta-aminolevulinate formed/10(6) erythroblasts/hour (normal range, 110-650 pmol).

### Inheritance

Jardine et al. (1994) studied affected sibs born to unaffected parents. They excluded the ALAS2 gene and showed that the mother was not a carrier of an X-linked sideroblastic anemia mutation. They concluded that the disorder in this family was very likely an autosomal recessive trait. Guernsey et al. (2009) confirmed autosomal recessive inheritance of nonsyndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia caused by SLC25A38 mutations.

### Mapping

Guernsey et al. (2009) used positional cloning to identify the locus for autosomal recessive nonsyndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia.

### Heterogeneity

Mutations in the erythroid-specific delta-aminolevulinate synthase gene ALAS2 on Xp11.21 cause X-linked sideroblastic anemia. In a boy with pyridoxine-refractory congenital sideroblastic anemia, Jardine et al. (1994) found no ALAS2 mutations in the coding region, the intron/exon boundaries, or the 1-kb 5-prime promoter region. They identified a G-to-A polymorphism 220 nucleotides 3-prime of the AATAAA polyadenylation signal, with a gene frequency of 25% among 148 alleles. Segregation of this polymorphism showed that the affected sibs had inherited different maternal ALAS2 alleles, which excluded ALAS2 as the cause of disease in this family. The mother also showed random X inactivation in her peripheral leukocytes.

### Molecular Genetics

Guernsey et al. (2009) used positional cloning to identify mutations in the SLC25A38 gene in patients with autosomal recessive, nonsyndromic, pyridoxine-refractory congenital sideroblastic anemia who had a normal ALAS2 gene. The mutations included stop codon, frameshift, and splice site mutations. SLC25A38 encodes an erythroid-specific member of the mitochondrial carrier family. Guernsey et al. (2009) showed that the protein is important for heme biosynthesis in eukaryotes. Based on its requirement for erythropoiesis, they predicted that it is an amino acid carrier that can transport glycine. † ⚠︎

### Animal Model

Guernsey et al. (2009) carried out knockdown experiments in zebrafish that supported a role for Slc25a38 in congenital sideroblastic anemia. †

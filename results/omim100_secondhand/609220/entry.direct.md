### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Bruck syndrome-2 (BRKS2) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the PLOD2 gene on chromosome 3q23-q24.

### Description

Bruck syndrome is a recessively inherited disorder in which skeletal changes resembling osteogenesis imperfecta, including bone fragility and multiple fractures, occur together with congenital contractures of the large joints (Ha-Vinh et al., 2004). Bruck syndrome-2 is the form caused by mutation in the PLOD2 gene, which encodes lysyl hydroxylase-2, the putative telopeptide lysyl hydroxylase (van der Slot et al., 2003; Ha-Vinh et al., 2004). Mutations in PLOD2 also cause autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta phenotypes of variable severity, without the contractures (Puig-Hervas et al., 2012). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) reported a boy with Bruck syndrome who had congenital contractures with pterygia at birth, together with severe osteopenia resembling that of osteogenesis imperfecta and multiple fractures. His urine contained high amounts of hydroxyproline but low amounts of collagen crosslink degradation products. Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) noted that the combination of pterygia with bone fragility was difficult to explain and suggested that telopeptide lysyl hydroxylation must be involved in prenatal joint formation and morphogenesis.

Puig-Hervas et al. (2012) studied 6 consanguineous Bruck syndrome families. They also described a patient with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta, and 2 brothers, one affected with mild autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta and the other with mild Bruck syndrome, all with PLOD2 mutations (see MOLECULAR GENETICS), indicating that PLOD2 mutations cause connective tissue disorders within a Bruck syndrome-osteogenesis imperfecta phenotypic spectrum.

### Biochemical Features

Van der Slot et al. (2003) noted that Bruck syndrome is characterized by a pyridinoline deficiency in bone collagen; pyridinoline crosslinks are derived from hydroxylated lysine residues within the collagen telopeptides.

In the patient with Bruck syndrome-2 reported by Ha-Vinh et al. (2004), the urine contained high amounts of hydroxyproline but low amounts of collagen crosslink degradation products. Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) suggested that measurement of collagen degradation products in urine, together with PLOD2 mutation analysis, may be used to diagnose Bruck syndrome and to differentiate it from osteogenesis imperfecta.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of Bruck syndrome-2 is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) found a homozygous PLOD2 mutation in an affected boy, and Puig-Hervas et al. (2012) found homozygous or compound heterozygous PLOD2 mutations in patients from consanguineous families. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Bank et al. (1999) studied a family with Bruck syndrome in which lysine residues within the telopeptides of type I collagen in bone were underhydroxylated, leading to aberrant crosslinking, whereas cartilage and ligament showed normal crosslinking. A genome screen identified only a single region, on chromosome 17p12, where all affected sibs shared a cluster of haplotypes identical by descent, suggesting that this region might contain the Bruck syndrome locus (see BRKS1).

Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) noted that the identification of PLOD2 mutations in Bruck syndrome by van der Slot et al. (2003), in addition to the locus mapped to chromosome 17p12, showed genetic heterogeneity, and they referred to the 17p-linked form as Bruck syndrome type 1 and the PLOD2-associated form as Bruck syndrome type 2. The proportion of cases of each type remained uncertain.

Among 6 consanguineous Bruck syndrome families, Puig-Hervas et al. (2012) detected mutations in either PLOD2 or FKBP10 in all cases.

### Molecular Genetics

By analyzing the genetic defect in Bruck syndrome, van der Slot et al. (2003) found 2 missense mutations in exon 17 of the PLOD2 gene, thereby identifying PLOD2 as a putative telopeptide lysyl hydroxylase gene. Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) referred to these mutations as gly601-to-val (G601V) and thr608-to-ile (T608I). ⚠︎

In a boy with Bruck syndrome, Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) identified a homozygous arg598-to-his (R598H) mutation in the PLOD2 gene. The mutation was adjacent to the 2 mutations previously reported by van der Slot et al. (2003), suggesting a functionally important hotspot in PLOD2. ⚠︎

In 6 consanguineous families with Bruck syndrome, Puig-Hervas et al. (2012) detected mutations in either PLOD2 or FKBP10 in all cases. Two probands had a homozygous frameshift mutation in the alternative exon 13a of PLOD2, indicating that specific inactivation of the longer protein isoform encoded by the gene is sufficient to cause Bruck syndrome. By homozygosity mapping followed by a candidate gene approach, they also identified a homozygous donor splice site mutation in PLOD2 in a patient with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta. Screening of additional samples revealed compound heterozygous PLOD2 mutations in 2 brothers, one affected with mild autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta and the other with mild Bruck syndrome.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Puig-Hervas et al. (2012) found that, in addition to causing Bruck syndrome, PLOD2 mutations were associated with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta phenotypes of variable severity; 2 brothers carrying the same compound heterozygous PLOD2 mutations had mild osteogenesis imperfecta and mild Bruck syndrome, respectively. ⚠︎

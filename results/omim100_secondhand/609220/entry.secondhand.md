## BRUCK SYNDROME 2; BRKS2

OSTEOGENESIS IMPERFECTA WITH CONGENITAL JOINT CONTRACTURES

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Bruck syndrome-2 (BRKS2) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the PLOD2 gene on chromosome 3q23-q24.

### Description

Bruck syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder in which skeletal changes resembling osteogenesis imperfecta, including osteopenia, fragile bones and multiple fractures, occur together with congenital contractures of the large joints. Pterygia may be present at birth (Ha-Vinh et al., 2004). Bank et al. (1999) described Bruck syndrome as osteoporosis, joint contractures, fragile bones, and short stature. The biochemical defect is underhydroxylation of lysine residues in the telopeptides of type I collagen in bone, which leads to reduced pyridinoline crosslinks (Bank et al., 1999; van der Slot et al., 2003). ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) referred to Bruck syndrome linked to chromosome 17p as BS type 1, and to Bruck syndrome caused by mutation in PLOD2 as BS type 2.

### Clinical Features

Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) reported a boy who had congenital contractures with pterygia at birth, along with severe osteopenia resembling osteogenesis imperfecta and multiple fractures. His urine contained high amounts of hydroxyproline but low amounts of collagen crosslink degradation products.

Puig-Hervás et al. (2012) studied consanguineous families with Bruck syndrome. In 2 brothers carrying the same PLOD2 mutations, one had mild Bruck syndrome and the other had mild autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta.

### Biochemical Features

Bank et al. (1999) found that, in bone from patients with Bruck syndrome, lysine residues in the telopeptides of type I collagen were underhydroxylated, which led to aberrant crosslinking. Lysine residues in the triple helix were modified normally. Unlike bone, cartilage and ligament had normal telopeptide hydroxylation, as shown by normal crosslinking patterns. Bank et al. (1999) concluded that collagen crosslinking is regulated mainly by tissue-specific enzymes that hydroxylate only telopeptide lysine residues, and they proposed that a bone-specific telopeptide lysyl hydroxylase was defective in Bruck syndrome.

Van der Slot et al. (2003) noted that Bruck syndrome is characterized by a deficiency of pyridinoline crosslinks in bone collagen.

### Inheritance

Bruck syndrome-2 is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) reported a patient homozygous for a PLOD2 mutation. Puig-Hervás et al. (2012) found homozygous and compound heterozygous PLOD2 mutations in patients, including patients from consanguineous families.

### Mapping

In a genome screen of a family with Bruck syndrome, Bank et al. (1999) found only one region, on chromosome 17p12, where all affected sibs shared a cluster of haplotypes identical by descent. They suggested that this region might contain the Bruck syndrome locus and the gene for the bone telopeptidyl lysyl hydroxylase.

### Heterogeneity

Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) noted that one Bruck syndrome locus had been mapped to 17p12, whereas mutations in the PLOD2 gene on 3q23-q24 had also been found in Bruck syndrome, which showed genetic heterogeneity. The proportion of cases due to each type was not known. Puig-Hervás et al. (2012) studied 6 consanguineous families with Bruck syndrome and found mutations in either PLOD2 or FKBP10 in every family.

### Molecular Genetics

By analyzing the genetic defect in Bruck syndrome, van der Slot et al. (2003) identified 2 missense mutations in exon 17 of the PLOD2 gene. This identified PLOD2 as a putative telopeptide lysyl hydroxylase gene.

In a boy with Bruck syndrome, Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) identified a homozygous mutation in the PLOD2 gene that resulted in an arg598-to-his (R598H) substitution. The mutation lay next to 2 previously reported mutations, gly601-to-val (G601V) and thr608-to-ile (T608I). Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) suggested that this region is a functionally important hotspot in PLOD2.

Puig-Hervás et al. (2012) studied 6 consanguineous families with Bruck syndrome. In 2 probands, they found a homozygous frameshift mutation (c.1559dupC) in the alternative exon 13a of the PLOD2 gene. This indicated that specific inactivation of the longer protein isoform encoded by PLOD2 is enough to cause Bruck syndrome. A homozygous c.1856G-A mutation in PLOD2, resulting in an arg619-to-his (R619H) substitution, was found in an Egyptian patient. In a patient with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta, homozygosity mapping followed by a candidate gene approach identified a homozygous donor splice site mutation in PLOD2. Screening of further samples found compound heterozygous PLOD2 mutations, including a c.1864G-T change resulting in a gly622-to-cys (G622C) substitution, in 2 Spanish brothers. One brother had mild autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta and the other had mild Bruck syndrome. Puig-Hervás et al. (2012) concluded that PLOD2 mutations cause Bruck syndrome and are also associated with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta of variable severity. † ⚠︎

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Puig-Hervás et al. (2012) found that 2 brothers with the same compound heterozygous PLOD2 mutations had different phenotypes: one had mild autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta without the features of Bruck syndrome, and the other had mild Bruck syndrome. They concluded that PLOD2 mutations cause a phenotypic spectrum ranging from Bruck syndrome to osteogenesis imperfecta.

### Pathogenesis

Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) found it hard to explain why pterygia occur together with bone fragility. They suggested that telopeptide lysyl hydroxylation must be involved in prenatal joint formation and morphogenesis.

### Diagnosis

Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) suggested that collagen degradation products in urine, together with mutation analysis of PLOD2, could be used to diagnose Bruck syndrome and to distinguish it from osteogenesis imperfecta. They reported that the urinary ratio of the pyridinoline crosslinks hydroxylysylpyridinoline and lysylpyridinoline to hydroxyproline was a useful diagnostic marker. ⚠︎

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that severe achondroplasia with developmental delay and acanthosis nigricans (SADDAN) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the fibroblast growth factor receptor-3 gene (FGFR3). ⚠︎

### Description

Severe achondroplasia with developmental delay and acanthosis nigricans (SADDAN) is a skeletal dysplasia. Its disturbances of endochondral bone growth approach and overlap those of thanatophoric dysplasia type I, but it is most often compatible with survival into adulthood. Surviving patients develop progressive acanthosis nigricans, central nervous system structural anomalies, seizures, and severe developmental delay. Unlike thanatophoric dysplasia type II, which is caused by a mutation at the same FGFR3 codon, SADDAN is not associated with cloverleaf skull or craniosynostosis (Tavormina et al., 1999; Bellus et al., 1999).

### Nomenclature

Tavormina et al. (1999) called the phenotype caused by the FGFR3 lys650-to-met mutation 'severe achondroplasia with developmental delay and acanthosis nigricans' (SADDAN) because it differed significantly from the phenotypes caused by other known FGFR3 mutations.

### Clinical Features

Tavormina et al. (1999) described 4 unrelated individuals whose skeletal dysplasia approached the severity seen in thanatophoric dysplasia type I. However, 3 of the 4 developed extensive areas of acanthosis nigricans beginning in early childhood, had severe neurologic impairments, and survived past infancy without prolonged life-support measures.

Bellus et al. (1999) gave a more detailed clinical account of the SADDAN phenotype in the patients reported by Tavormina et al. (1999). The disturbances in endochondral bone growth approached and overlapped those seen in thanatophoric dysplasia type I, but the disorder was most often compatible with survival into adulthood. Some patients had other unusual bone deformities, such as femoral bowing with reverse (posterior apex) bowing of the tibia and fibula, and 'ram's horn' bowing of the clavicle. Besides the skeletal dysplasia, surviving patients had progressive acanthosis nigricans, central nervous system structural anomalies, seizures, and severe developmental delay. Cloverleaf skull and craniosynostosis were not present. ⚠︎

Zankl et al. (2008) described the prenatal and postnatal features of a patient with SADDAN whose clinical diagnosis was confirmed by finding the FGFR3 lys650-to-met mutation. They noted that this was one of only 6 molecularly analyzed cases reported to date. The case confirmed the severe morbidity of SADDAN and added to the reports of early mortality. Zankl et al. (2008) also reviewed the clinical and radiologic characteristics of all reported cases.

### Molecular Genetics

In 4 unrelated individuals with SADDAN, Tavormina et al. (1999) identified a missense mutation in the FGFR3 gene, an A1949T change resulting in a lys650-to-met (K650M) substitution. The mutation lies at the nucleotide next to the mutation that causes thanatophoric dysplasia type II (A1948G; lys650-to-glu, K650E). It produces a different amino acid substitution at a highly conserved codon in the activation loop of the kinase domain. In transient transfection studies, the K650M mutation caused a dramatic increase in constitutive receptor kinase activity, about 3 times greater than that seen with the K650E mutation.

In a patient with SADDAN, Zankl et al. (2008) identified the FGFR3 K650M mutation by mutation analysis.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Bellus et al. (1999) noted that the K650M mutation occurs in the same FGFR3 codon as the thanatophoric dysplasia type II mutation (K650E) and has a similar effect on constitutive activation of the FGFR3 tyrosine kinase. Even so, K650M was not associated with cloverleaf skull or craniosynostosis. Tavormina et al. (1999) found that K650M caused about 3 times more constitutive kinase activity than K650E, yet most patients with SADDAN survived past infancy.

### Diagnosis

Zankl et al. (2008) reported the prenatal presentation of SADDAN in a patient whose diagnosis was confirmed by FGFR3 mutation analysis.

### Animal Model

Iwata et al. (2001) introduced the point mutation K644M, which corresponds to human K650M, into the mouse Fgfr3 gene. Heterozygous mutant mice had a phenotype similar to human SADDAN; for example, most of them survived the perinatal period. This suggested that the survival of SADDAN patients is due to the K650M mutation itself. The long bone abnormalities in the SADDAN mice were milder than in the mouse model of thanatophoric dysplasia type II. The SADDAN mice also showed overgrowth of cartilaginous tissue in the rib cartilage, trachea, and nasal septum. At low concentration, FGF ligand activated MAP kinase differently in primary chondrocyte cultures from wildtype and SADDAN mice.

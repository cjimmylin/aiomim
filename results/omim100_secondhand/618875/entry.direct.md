### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that early-onset seizures with neurodegeneration and brain calcifications (SENEBAC) is caused by homozygous or other biallelic mutation in the NRROS gene.

### Description

Early-onset seizures with neurodegeneration and brain calcifications (SENEBAC) is a severe neurodegenerative disorder. Development is normal or mildly delayed at first. Refractory seizures begin in infancy or the second year of life and are followed by developmental regression. Brain imaging shows intracranial calcification, white matter abnormalities, and progressive cerebral atrophy, and death in early childhood has been reported (Smith et al., 2020; Dong et al., 2020). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Smith et al. (2020) reported 3 patients from 2 families. Two were female sibs born to nonconsanguineous parents of Maori descent; the third was a boy born to first-cousin parents of south Asian descent. All 3 children were born after a normal pregnancy and delivery, and early development was unremarkable. In the second year of life, they developed refractory seizures and neurodegeneration and died between 27 and 36 months of age. Metabolic testing, including testing for mitochondrial dysfunction, did not identify a cause. Initial brain imaging showed fine calcification at the depths of the cerebral gyri and normal white matter. CT scans showed widespread spot and linear calcification in the deep and subcortical white matter, and early MRI showed mild cerebral volume loss with relatively good myelination. As the disease progressed, repeat imaging showed more calcification, severe generalized atrophy with ventricular dilatation, and diffuse signal changes in the cerebral and cerebellar white matter. Two patients had rapidly progressive cortical and subcortical atrophy on follow-up. Postmortem examination of 1 patient found abnormalities only in the CNS. These included marked cerebral atrophy, gray and white matter pathology throughout the cerebrum, cerebellum, and brainstem, focal calcification in the neuropil, and widespread neuronal loss with reactive gliosis. The most striking finding was an accumulation of foamy macrophages, mostly around blood vessels, throughout the white matter and along the descending corticospinal pathways. These cells stained for CD68, MHC class II, and p22phox but not for CD163, IBA1, NRROS, CD3, P2Y12, or TMEM119. Myelin basic protein expression was reduced in the white matter, with preservation of U fibers, and occasional axonal spheroids were seen. The authors noted marked abnormalities of parenchymal microglia and no evidence of immune-mediated inflammation.

Dong et al. (2020) reported 6 patients from 4 families with neurodegenerative disease, refractory epilepsy, developmental regression, and reduced white matter volume with delayed myelination. Development was normal or mildly delayed at first. Seizures began within the first year of life and were followed by developmental regression. Three of the patients had intracranial calcification. The authors noted that the phenotype overlapped with the condition caused by TGF-beta-1 deficiency.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of SENEBAC in the families reported by Smith et al. (2020) and Dong et al. (2020) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Smith et al. (2020) found homozygous mutations, including in a child of first-cousin parents, and Dong et al. (2020) found biallelic variants. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Smith et al. (2020) used exome sequencing in 3 patients from 2 families with SENEBAC and found a homozygous mutation in the NRROS gene in each family. The affected sibs of Maori descent carried a c.1777C-T transition that results in a gln593-to-ter (Q593X) substitution. The boy of south Asian descent carried a 1-bp deletion (c.1257del) that causes a frameshift and premature termination (Gly420AlafsTer14). Both variants were predicted to produce a truncated protein. The Q593X variant had not been reported before, and the 1-bp deletion was found on only 1 of 251,438 alleles in the gnomAD database. No patient cells were available for study. By analyzing transcriptomic datasets, Smith et al. (2020) found that NRROS expression in human brain was more than 50-fold higher in microglia than in whole brain.

Dong et al. (2020) found biallelic variants in the NRROS gene in 6 patients from 4 families with SENEBAC. The variants affected 2 significant functional domains of NRROS. In HEK293T cells, confocal microscopy showed that both wildtype and mutant NRROS proteins colocalized with latent TGF-beta-1 inside the cell. However, flow cytometry showed that the mutant proteins failed to anchor latent TGF-beta-1 at the cell surface, unlike wildtype NRROS. Wildtype NRROS rescued the ability of the mutants to present latent TGF-beta-1 at the cell surface. The authors concluded that loss of NRROS function causes the disorder.

### Animal Model

Wong et al. (2017) found that mice lacking Nrros, a myeloid-expressed transmembrane protein of the endoplasmic reticulum, developed spontaneous neurologic disorders with motor defects and died before 6 months of age. Nrros -/- mice showed astrogliosis and lacked normal CD11b-high/CD45-low microglia, but they had no detectable demyelination or neuronal loss. Instead, the CNS was populated by myeloid cells resembling perivascular macrophages. Cx3cr1-driven deletion of Nrros showed that the gene is crucial for microglial establishment during early embryonic stages. NRROS was also required for normal expression of Sall1 and other genes important for microglial development and function.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 SEIZURES, EARLY-ONSET, WITH NEURODEGENERATION AND BRAIN CALCIFICATIONS**  
NRROS, GLN593TER [c.1777C>T; p.Gln593Ter]

Smith et al. (2020) found a homozygous c.1777C-T transition in the NRROS gene in 2 female sibs of Maori descent with SENEBAC (Smith et al., 2020). Their parents were nonconsanguineous. The mutation, which results in a gln593-to-ter (Q593X) substitution, was found by exome sequencing and was predicted to produce a truncated protein. It had not been reported before.

**.0002 SEIZURES, EARLY-ONSET, WITH NEURODEGENERATION AND BRAIN CALCIFICATIONS**  
NRROS, 1-BP DEL, 1257 [c.1257del; p.Gly420AlafsTer14]

Smith et al. (2020) found a homozygous 1-bp deletion (c.1257del) in the NRROS gene in a boy with SENEBAC whose parents were first cousins of south Asian descent (Smith et al., 2020). The deletion was found by exome sequencing and causes a frameshift and premature termination (Gly420AlafsTer14), which was predicted to produce a truncated protein. It was found on only 1 of 251,438 alleles in the gnomAD database. The boy died at an age between 27 and 36 months, and postmortem examination showed accumulation of perivascular foamy macrophages in the white matter and widespread neuronal loss.

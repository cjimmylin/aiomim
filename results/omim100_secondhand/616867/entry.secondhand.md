### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spinal muscular atrophy with congenital bone fractures-2 (SMABF2) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the ASCC1 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Spinal muscular atrophy with congenital bone fractures-2 (SMABF2) is a rare, severe autosomal recessive neuromuscular disorder. It begins before birth and causes muscle weakness, multiple congenital contractures (arthrogryposis multiplex congenita), congenital bone fractures, neonatal hypotonia and respiratory insufficiency. Its outlook is poor and affected infants often die in the neonatal period (Knierim et al., 2016; Oliveira et al., 2017; Bohm et al., 2019; Giuffrida et al., 2020). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Knierim et al. (2016) studied 4 families with a disorder caused by mutations in subunits of the activating signal cointegrator-1 (ASC-1) complex. Affected individuals had prenatal-onset spinal muscular atrophy, multiple congenital contractures (arthrogryposis multiplex congenita), respiratory distress and congenital bone fractures.

Oliveira et al. (2017) reported a female newborn with a severe neuromuscular disorder. She had generalized hypotonia, congenital bone fractures, no spontaneous movements and poor respiratory effort, and she died within the first days of life. Her karyotype and screening of several genes related to neuromuscular disease were normal. A male sib was later born with the same clinical presentation. Oliveira et al. (2017) concluded that the disorder lay within the spinal muscular atrophy or primary muscle disease spectrum.

Bohm et al. (2019) described the clinical, histologic and genetic findings in 3 families with ASCC1 mutations. All patients had severe prenatal-onset muscle weakness, neonatal hypotonia, arthrogryposis and congenital bone fractures. Muscle biopsies from the affected infants showed intense oxidative rims beneath the sarcolemma and scattered remnants of sarcomeres with enlarged Z-bands. The authors suggested that these changes may be a histopathologic hallmark of the disorder. Biopsies also showed variable fiber size and myofibrillar disorganization. EMG findings pointed to a severe congenital myopathy rather than motor neuron involvement. †

Giuffrida et al. (2020) reported a stillbirth with SMABF2 in whom the course of the disease had been documented during the prenatal period. Arthrogryposis and hydrops led to a request for microarray analysis.

### Inheritance

SMABF2 is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. This was shown by the homozygous and compound heterozygous ASCC1 mutations found in affected individuals by Knierim et al. (2016), Oliveira et al. (2017), Bohm et al. (2019) and Giuffrida et al. (2020). ⚠︎

### Mapping

Knierim et al. (2016) used autozygosity mapping and whole-exome sequencing to search for pathogenic mutations in 4 families with prenatal-onset spinal muscular atrophy and congenital bone fractures.

### Heterogeneity

Knierim et al. (2016) found that recessive loss-of-function mutations in either of 2 genes encoding subunits of the ASC-1 complex, TRIP4 or ASCC1, cause a similar phenotype of prenatal-onset spinal muscular atrophy and congenital bone fractures.

### Molecular Genetics

In 4 families with prenatal-onset spinal muscular atrophy, arthrogryposis multiplex congenita, respiratory distress and congenital bone fractures, Knierim et al. (2016) used autozygosity mapping and whole-exome sequencing. They identified homozygous and compound heterozygous nonsense and frameshift mutations in the TRIP4 and ASCC1 genes. The mutations segregated with the disease and led to truncation or complete loss of the encoded proteins. Fibroblasts with ASCC1 mutations showed downregulation of genes associated with neurogenesis, neuronal migration and pathfinding (SERPINF1, DAB1, SEMA3D, SEMA3A) and with bone development (TNFRSF11B, RASSF2, STC1). Immunoprecipitation of the ASC-1 complex consistently copurified CSRP1, a transcriptional cofactor.

In a female newborn with a severe neuromuscular disorder and congenital bone fractures, Oliveira et al. (2017) performed whole-exome sequencing and filtered variants under a recessive model. They identified a homozygous frameshift mutation (c.157dupG, p.Glu53GlyfsTer19) in the ASCC1 gene. The authors noted that this was the second reported case with an ASCC1 mutation, which confirmed the role of ASCC1 in the disorder.

In affected members of 3 families with prenatal-onset muscle weakness, arthrogryposis and congenital bone fractures, Bohm et al. (2019) used sequencing to identify recessive nonsense or frameshift mutations in the ASCC1 gene. Two of the mutations had not been reported before.

In a stillbirth with SMABF2, Giuffrida et al. (2020) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the ASCC1 gene. Microarray analysis found a microdeletion of about 64 kb spanning 4 exons of ASCC1 (exons 7-10). Exome analysis then found a c.1027C-T transition on the other allele, which introduces a premature termination codon (arg343-to-ter; R343X). The authors stated that this was the first reported case of ASCC1 compound heterozygosity involving an exonic deletion and a nucleotide variant. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

In 17.5-day-old mouse embryos, Knierim et al. (2016) found that Trip4 and Ascc1 had identical expression patterns, with high levels in the spinal cord, brain, paraspinal ganglia, thyroid and submandibular glands. They concluded that dysfunction of a transcriptional coactivator complex can cause a clinical syndrome affecting the neuromuscular system.

Knierim et al. (2016) described the disorder as prenatal-onset spinal muscular atrophy. Bohm et al. (2019) found myopathic histopathology and EMG findings consistent with a severe congenital myopathy in their 3 families, without signs of motor neuron involvement. They stressed the importance of the ASC-1 complex in fetal muscle and bone development. ✗

### Animal Model

Knierim et al. (2016) found that antisense morpholino knockdown of ascc1 or trip4 in zebrafish disrupted the normal patterned outgrowth of alpha-motoneurons and the formation of myotomes and neuromuscular junctions. The knockdown larvae had a swimming defect.

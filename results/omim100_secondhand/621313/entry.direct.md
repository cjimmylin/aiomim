### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that brain small vessel disease-4 (BSVD4) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the NIT1 gene.

### Description

Brain small vessel disease-4 (BSVD4) is an autosomal recessive cerebral small vessel disease caused by loss of function of the NIT1 enzyme. Rutten et al. (2024) described it as a triad of movement disorders, massively dilated perivascular spaces in the basal ganglia, and intracerebral hemorrhage, with onset in mid-adulthood. Rendu et al. (2019) earlier described a family with neuropsychiatric disorders, bilateral cystic lesions in the basal ganglia, and a positive urine sulfite test. Patients had a deaminated glutathione (dGSH) derivative in the urine.

### Clinical Features

Rendu et al. (2019) reported 3 affected brothers from 1 family. The index patient (II.4) was a 34-year-old man admitted with sudden left hemiparesis. He had a history of nonsevere mental retardation and had been admitted to a psychiatry unit 2 years earlier for an acute psychotic episode followed by generalized seizures. On examination he had a pure motor left hemiparesis. CT and MRI showed bilateral small cystic lesions in the basal ganglia. Arteriography, EEG, EMG, Doppler ultrasound, and echocardiography were normal. A dipstick urine test for sulfites was positive. Over a 20-year period his condition progressively worsened, with severe neuropsychological deterioration and spastic tetraplegia, and he died in 2011. A brother (II.5), a 58-year-old man with diabetes mellitus who had led an active professional life, was admitted to intensive care in 2014 for sudden coma with cardiovascular collapse. His CT and MRI showed the same abnormalities, described as a deep cavitating state, and his urine sulfite test was positive. He gradually recovered but was left with neuropsychological deterioration (BREF 14/18, MMSE 22/30). A third brother (II.7) had been in a psychiatric institution for schizophrenia diagnosed 40 years earlier. His clinical examination was normal, but CT and MRI showed the same brain lesions and his urine sulfite test was positive. The mother and the unaffected sibs who were tested had no symptoms and negative urine sulfite tests. The authors noted that the cavities differed from the cavitary lesions usually reported in adults because they were located in the basal ganglia. Sulfite oxidase deficiency and molybdenum cofactor deficiency were excluded.

Rutten et al. (2024) studied 7 patients from 5 unrelated pedigrees: 3 sibs and 4 single cases. Patients presented in mid-adulthood with movement disorders. Brain MRI in all patients showed striking abnormalities, with numerous and massively dilated perivascular spaces in the basal ganglia. Three patients had non-lobar intracerebral hemorrhage between ages 45 and 60, which was fatal in 2. On brain autopsy, the walls of small and medium-sized cerebral arteries contained large electron-dense deposits.

### Biochemical Features

Rendu et al. (2019) performed qualitative organic acid analysis on urine. A compound with a mass spectrum identical to a dGSH derivative was present in the urine of the index patient and of his 2 affected brothers. The compound was undetectable in the unaffected sibs and the mother who were tested. Its mass spectrum resembled that of a compound found in the urine of Nit1-knockout mice. The authors proposed that dGSH accumulation was probably responsible for the positive urine sulfite test. In patient fibroblasts, the activity of cytoplasmic Cu/Zn superoxide dismutase (SOD) was unchanged, but mitochondrial MnSOD activity was increased twofold compared with controls.

Rutten et al. (2024) confirmed loss of NIT1 enzymatic function by metabolic analysis of patient urine.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of BSVD4 in the families reported by Rendu et al. (2019) and Rutten et al. (2024) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Rendu et al. (2019) performed exome sequencing in 2 affected brothers (II.5 and II.7) from the family with 3 affected brothers. Both were compound heterozygous for 2 mutations in the NIT1 gene: a c.457G-A transition predicted to cause a gly153-to-arg (G153R) substitution, and a 1-bp duplication (c.670dupA) predicted to cause a frameshift (Thr224AsnfsTer41). The c.670dupA mutation was inherited from the unaffected mother. The father's DNA was not available. Molecular cloning of a PCR fragment from patient II.5 showed that the 2 variants were on different alleles. The unaffected sibs who were tested carried neither variant. The c.457G-A change affects the last nucleotide of exon 4, and sequencing of NIT1 transcripts from a skin biopsy of patient II.5 showed that it altered splicing. NIT1 protein was undetectable on Western blot of fibroblasts from patient II.5. ⚠︎

Rutten et al. (2024) used exome sequencing to study 7 patients from 5 unrelated pedigrees. Three affected sibs were compound heterozygous for 2 mutations in the NIT1 gene: a c.727C-T transition resulting in an arg243-to-trp (R243W) substitution, and a 2-bp deletion (c.198_199del) resulting in a premature stop codon (Ala68Ter). The other 4 patients were single cases from 4 unrelated pedigrees, and all were homozygous for the R243W mutation. Western blot showed absence of NIT1 protein in patient fibroblasts.

### Pathogenesis

Rendu et al. (2019) noted that NIT1 encodes an amidase that recycles deaminated glutathione (dGSH) into 2-ketoglutarate and cysteinylglycine. They proposed that loss of NIT1 function causes dGSH to accumulate and may lead to oxidative stress, as indicated by increased mitochondrial SOD activity in patient fibroblasts. They stated that the underlying pathophysiologic mechanisms required further study.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 BRAIN SMALL VESSEL DISEASE 4**  
NIT1, GLY153ARG [NM_005600:c.457G>A; p.Gly153Arg]

Rendu et al. (2019) found a c.457G-A transition in the NIT1 gene, predicted to cause a gly153-to-arg (G153R) substitution at a highly conserved residue, in 2 affected brothers with BSVD4. They identified it by exome sequencing. The brothers were compound heterozygous for this mutation and a 1-bp duplication (c.670dupA). The mutation lies on the last nucleotide of exon 4, and patient transcript sequencing showed that it affected splicing. NIT1 protein was undetectable in patient fibroblasts.

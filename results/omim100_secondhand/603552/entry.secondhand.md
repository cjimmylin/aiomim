### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis-4 (FHL4) is caused by homozygous mutation in the gene encoding syntaxin-11 (STX11) on chromosome 6q24.

### Description

Familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (FHL) is a rare autosomal recessive disorder characterized by hyperactive phagocytes and defects in natural killer (NK) cell function (zur Stadt et al., 2005). It presents as a severe hyperinflammatory syndrome with activated macrophages and T lymphocytes and affects young children (Zur Stadt et al., 2006). Compared with other forms of FHL, FHL4 has been associated with a milder course, including long disease-free remissions without specific treatment, although some affected children develop severe psychomotor retardation, myelodysplastic syndrome, or acute myelogenous leukemia (Rudd et al., 2006).

### Clinical Features

zur Stadt et al. (2005) studied a large consanguineous Kurdish kindred in which 5 children were affected with FHL. They also identified 5 further consanguineous Turkish/Kurdish FHL kindreds with STX11 mutations.

Rudd et al. (2006) investigated the clinical features of 6 children from 4 unrelated families with STX11 mutations. Three patients had long periods (1 year or more) in remission without specific treatment, which is very uncommon in FHL. Despite the milder phenotype, some children developed severe psychomotor retardation. Two of the 6 patients developed myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) or acute myelogenous leukemia (AML). Rudd et al. (2006) suggested that STX11 mutations may be associated with secondary malignancies (MDS/AML).

Muller et al. (2014) reported 3 patients from 3 unrelated consanguineous Pakistani families who presented with HLH: 2 boys diagnosed at 2 months and 5 years of age and a girl diagnosed at 48 months. Only patient B had a family history of the disease. Patients A and B had fever, splenomegaly, hepatomegaly, anemia (hemoglobin 55 and 71 g/L), neutropenia (0.3 and 0.4 x 10(9)/L), thrombocytopenia (13 and 8 x 10(9)/L), hypertriglyceridemia (5.1 and 5.5 mmol/L), hypofibrinogenemia (0.28 and 0.16 g/L), and hyperferritinemia (8,636 and 1,929 microg/L). Hemophagocytosis was not observed in either patient, and neither had neurologic manifestations. Clinical data at presentation could not be retrieved for patient C. All 3 patients had deficient NK cell cytotoxicity and degranulation. Patients A and B were treated with dexamethasone, cyclosporine A, and etoposide and were in remission at 2 months. Patient A underwent hematopoietic stem cell transplantation at 15 months; both patients A and B died. Patient C was lost to follow-up.

### Inheritance

zur Stadt et al. (2005) found homozygous STX11 mutations in affected members of consanguineous families, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Muller et al. (2014) found that the parents of patients with homozygous STX11 mutations were heterozygous carriers with no recorded history of inflammatory disease.

### Mapping

zur Stadt et al. (2005) carried out genome-wide homozygosity mapping in a large consanguineous Kurdish kindred with 5 children affected with FHL. They found linkage to a 10-cM region on chromosome 6q24 between markers D6S1569 and D6S960, which defined a new FHL locus.

### Heterogeneity

FHL is genetically heterogeneous. Mutations in the perforin-1 gene (PRF1) and in UNC13D cause FHL2 and FHL3, respectively (zur Stadt et al., 2005). Muller et al. (2014) noted that FHL is caused by autosomal recessive mutations in PRF1, UNC13D, STX11, and STXBP2.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of a large consanguineous Kurdish kindred with FHL4 mapped to chromosome 6q24, zur Stadt et al. (2005) screened positional candidate genes and identified a homozygous 5-bp deletion in the STX11 gene. Syntaxin-11 protein was absent from the mononuclear cell fraction of patients homozygous for the deletion. zur Stadt et al. (2005) also found homozygous STX11 mutations in 5 other consanguineous Turkish/Kurdish FHL kindreds: the 5-bp deletion in 2 families, a large 19.2-kb genomic deletion spanning the entire coding region of STX11 (exon 2) in 1 family, and a nonsense mutation leading to a premature stop codon in the C-terminal end of the protein in 2 families. Because both STX11 and UNC13D are involved in vesicle trafficking and membrane fusion, the authors concluded that defects in the endocytotic or exocytotic pathway may be a common mechanism in FHL.

Zur Stadt et al. (2006) analyzed STX11, PRF1, and UNC13D in 63 unrelated patients with FHL from Turkey (32), Germany (23), and other origins (8). STX11 mutations were found in 6 patients, all from Turkey; none of the German patients or patients of other origins had STX11 mutations.

Rudd et al. (2006) used direct sequencing to analyze STX11 in 28 FHL families without PRF1 mutations. They found 2 previously reported STX11 mutations, a nonsense mutation and a deletion, in 6 of 34 children from 4 of the 28 families (14%).

In 3 patients with FHL4 from 3 unrelated consanguineous Pakistani families, Muller et al. (2014) sequenced the coding regions and splice sites of UNC13D, STX11, and STXBP2. All 3 patients were homozygous for a missense mutation in STX11 (L58P). The parents were heterozygous for the mutation. Patient A also carried a heterozygous UNC13D variant (P271S) and a homozygous UNC13D variant (R928C). This patient presented at a younger age than the other 2 patients and showed less restoration of NK cell degranulation after IL-2 stimulation, and the authors suggested that the UNC13D variants may have contributed to the severity of his disease. Syntaxin-11 expression was greatly reduced in PBMCs and LAK cells from patient C, whereas Munc18-2 expression was normal. In transfected HEK293T cells, the L58P mutant was expressed at levels similar to wildtype but did not bind Munc18-2.

### Pathogenesis

Muller et al. (2014) found that NK cells from patients homozygous for the STX11 L58P mutation had defective lysis of K562 target cells and defective degranulation in response to K562 cells or CD16 engagement. Both functions were partially restored by IL-2 stimulation. Expression of perforin, granzyme B, and CD107a was normal, indicating that granule integrity was not impaired. In pull-down experiments, both the patient-derived L58P mutation in the Habc domain and an engineered mutation in the N-terminal peptide (R4A) abolished binding of syntaxin-11 to Munc18-2, whereas the C-terminal Q268X mutant still bound Munc18-2. The authors concluded that both the N terminus and the Habc domain of syntaxin-11 are needed to bind Munc18-2, and suggested that loss of this interaction may destabilize syntaxin-11 and explain the low levels of the mutant protein in patient cells.

### Clinical Management

Locatelli et al. (2020) conducted an open-label, single-group, phase 2-3 study of emapalumab, a human anti-interferon-gamma antibody, given with dexamethasone to 34 patients aged 18 years or younger with primary HLH (27 previously treated and 7 previously untreated). Of the previously treated patients, 63% responded, compared with the prespecified null hypothesis of 40% (P = 0.02); 65% of all patients who received emapalumab responded (P = 0.005). Seventy percent of the previously treated patients and 65% of all treated patients were able to proceed to transplantation. Emapalumab was not associated with organ toxicity. Severe infections developed in 10 patients during treatment, and emapalumab was discontinued in 1 patient because of disseminated histoplasmosis.

### Population Genetics

Zur Stadt et al. (2006) found STX11 mutations in 6 of 32 Turkish patients with FHL but in none of 23 German patients or 8 patients of other origins. Rudd et al. (2006) found STX11 mutations in 14% of PRF1-negative FHL families in their cohort.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type Ib (CDAN1B) is caused by homozygous mutation in the C15ORF41 gene.

### Description

The congenital dyserythropoietic anemias are a heterogeneous group of rare disorders that mainly affect erythropoiesis. They show characteristic morphologic abnormalities and a block in erythroid maturation (Babbs et al., 2013). Mutations in the CDAN1 gene cause most cases of congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I. Type Ib is caused by mutations in C15ORF41, a gene of previously unknown function, in some CDAN1 mutation-negative cases (Babbs et al., 2013). Some patients with CDA type I have been reported with nonhematologic features, including distal limb anomalies (Sabry et al., 1997).

### Clinical Features

Sabry et al. (1997) described dysmorphic features in 3 Bedouin sibs with congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I. The features were growth retardation/short stature, congenital ptosis, abnormal tarsal bones, metatarsal duplication/hypoplasia, nail/phalangeal hypoplasia of the fingers and toes, Madelung deformity, syndactyly of the toes, and hallux valgus. Peripheral blood lymphocyte cultures from the patients had a very low mitotic index. The phenotype varied among the 3 sibs. Sabry et al. (1997) concluded that their report confirmed an association between a subset of CDA type I and a specific form of distal limb anomalies. They suggested that congenital ptosis and a low mitotic index might also be part of the syndrome profile. The abstract did not say whether this family's disease was due to mutation in C15ORF41. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CDAN1B is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Babbs et al. (2013) found homozygous C15ORF41 mutations in affected members of a consanguineous pedigree and of 2 further pedigrees. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Ahmed et al. (2006) performed linkage and mutational analysis of the CDAN1 gene and found evidence of genetic heterogeneity in congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I. About 80% of known cases of CDA type I were associated with CDAN1 mutations. †

Babbs et al. (2013) noted that no likely pathogenic CDAN1 mutation had been found in about 20% of CDA type I cases, which suggested that at least one other locus existed.

### Molecular Genetics

Babbs et al. (2013) studied a consanguineous pedigree of Middle Eastern origin with CDA type I. Using whole-genome sequencing and segregation analysis, they found a homozygous c.533T-A transversion in the C15ORF41 gene, predicted to cause a leu178-to-gln (L178Q) substitution. They then sequenced C15ORF41 in other CDAN1 mutation-negative CDA type I pedigrees. In 2 further pedigrees of Southeast Asian origin, they found a homozygous c.281A-G transition, predicted to cause a tyr94-to-cys (Y94C) substitution. The haplotype surrounding the c.281A-G change suggested a founder effect for this mutation in Pakistan. Detailed sequence similarity searches indicated that C15ORF41 encodes a novel restriction endonuclease belonging to the Holliday junction resolvase family of proteins.

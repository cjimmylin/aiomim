### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type Ib (CDAN1B) is caused by homozygous mutation in the C15ORF41 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

The congenital dyserythropoietic anemias are a heterogeneous group of rare disorders that mainly affect erythropoiesis. They show characteristic morphologic abnormalities and a block in erythroid maturation (Babbs et al., 2013). Most cases of congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I are caused by mutation in the CDAN1 gene. Congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type Ib is the form caused by mutation in the C15ORF41 gene, which encodes a predicted endonuclease (Babbs et al., 2013). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Sabry et al. (1997) described 3 Bedouin sibs with congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I who had dysmorphic features. These features included growth retardation/short stature, congenital ptosis, abnormal tarsal bones, metatarsal duplication/hypoplasia, nail/phalangeal hypoplasia of the fingers and toes, Madelung deformity, syndactyly of the toes, and hallux valgus. Peripheral blood lymphocyte cultures from the patients had a very low mitotic index. The phenotype varied among the 3 sibs. Sabry et al. (1997) concluded that their report confirmed an association between a subset of congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I and a specific form of distal limb anomalies. They suggested that congenital ptosis and a low mitotic index might also be part of the syndrome. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Babbs et al. (2013) found homozygous C15ORF41 mutations in affected members of a consanguineous pedigree and of 2 other pedigrees, which is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance of congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type Ib. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Two reports by Ahmed et al. (2006) presented evidence of genetic heterogeneity in congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I, including linkage and mutation analysis of the CDAN1 gene. Babbs et al. (2013) noted that no likely pathogenic CDAN1 mutation had been found in about 20% of cases of congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I, which suggested that at least 1 other locus was involved. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Babbs et al. (2013) studied a consanguineous pedigree of Middle Eastern origin with congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I. Using whole-genome sequencing and segregation analysis, they identified a homozygous c.533T-A transversion in the C15ORF41 gene, predicted to result in a leu178-to-gln (L178Q) substitution. They then sequenced C15ORF41 in other CDAN1 mutation-negative pedigrees with congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I. In 2 pedigrees of Southeast Asian origin, they found a homozygous c.281A-G transition predicted to result in a tyr94-to-cys (Y94C) substitution. The haplotype surrounding the c.281A-G change suggested a founder effect for this mutation in Pakistan. Detailed sequence similarity searches indicated that C15ORF41 encodes a novel restriction endonuclease belonging to the Holliday junction resolvase family of proteins.

### Population Genetics

Babbs et al. (2013) found the C15ORF41 Y94C mutation in 2 pedigrees of Southeast Asian origin. The haplotype surrounding the mutation suggested a founder effect in Pakistan.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal dominant autoinflammation, panniculitis, and dermatosis syndrome (AIPDSA) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the OTULIN gene.

### Description

Autosomal dominant autoinflammation, panniculitis, and dermatosis syndrome (AIPDSA) is a severe autoinflammatory disorder with onset in infancy. Features include fever, raised inflammatory markers, neutrophilia, and sterile neutrophilic skin lesions such as panniculitis, pustules at sites of trauma, erythema nodosum, and necrotizing wounds. Some patients have also had acute respiratory deterioration and growth failure. The disorder resembles OTULIN-related autoinflammatory syndrome (ORAS), which is caused by biallelic loss-of-function mutations in OTULIN. In AIPDSA, however, a single de novo heterozygous OTULIN mutation acts in a dominant-negative way. Treatment with corticosteroids and TNF blockade has been effective (Davidson et al., 2024; Takeda et al., 2024). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Davidson et al. (2024) reported a 22-month-old boy (P1) born to nonconsanguineous Caucasian parents at 31 weeks' gestation. At 4 days of age he developed periumbilical erythema, presumed to be an abscess, that did not respond to broad-spectrum antibiotics. At surgery there was marked inflammation around a urachal remnant, with many neutrophils on histopathology but no bacteria cultured. The surgical closure dehisced and left an abdominal wound that would not close. At 8 weeks of age he had an acute respiratory deterioration with extensive body edema, hypotension, and presumed pulmonary edema; no organisms other than rhinovirus were isolated. His lungs improved immediately with corticosteroids, and the abdominal wound began to heal. He later developed a spontaneous cutaneous stoma from the duodenum. He also developed a systolic murmur with an echogenic focus on the tricuspid valve and was treated for infective endocarditis, although no organism was identified. He had repeated areas of swelling and erythema consistent with panniculitis, as well as lesions thought to represent pathergy: sterile pustular abscesses at sites of prior trauma, such as injection and cannula sites. Investigations showed raised C-reactive protein (100-200 mg/L), elevated neutrophils (30-50 x 10(9)/L) and monocytes (2-10 x 10(9)/L), eosinophilia (1-3 x 10(9)/L), and persistent thrombocytopenia (below 100 x 10(9)/L) until the inflammation was controlled. He initially had hypogammaglobulinemia, which may have been related to prematurity, but lymphocyte subsets were largely normal. A biopsy of perilesional skin showed heavy acute inflammatory exudate with diffuse suppuration and areas of necrosis.

Davidson et al. (2024) also reported a Saudi girl (P2) born at term to nonconsanguineous parents. She was well until 7 months of age, when she presented with multiple tender skin lesions on both upper and lower limbs, together with fever (38-39 degrees C) and generalized edema. This followed her routine 6-month vaccinations by 2 weeks. She had leukocytosis (45.5 x 10(9)/L) with neutrophilia (40.9 x 10(9)/L) and a C-reactive protein of 264 mg/L, and cultures were negative. Despite intravenous antibiotics, some lesions progressed to tender cellulitis-like plaques and then to necrotizing fasciitis with ulceration, which required debridement and fasciotomy. Biopsies showed mixed infiltration of neutrophils and plasma cells with necrosis, and all cultures were negative. At 12 months she presented again with fever and new tender erythematous plaques and patches on the trunk and both lower limbs. She had leukocytosis (19.9 x 10(9)/L), neutrophilia, and a C-reactive protein of 393 mg/L, and a skin biopsy showed panniculitis. She improved promptly with corticosteroids. She later had recurrent simple chest infections.

Takeda et al. (2024) reported a Japanese girl born at 33 weeks and 1 day of gestation to nonconsanguineous healthy parents. Shortly after birth she developed meningitis with neutrophil-dominant leukocytosis and elevated C-reactive protein; blood and cerebrospinal fluid cultures were negative. At 1 month she developed omphalitis that did not respond to antibiotics. Omphalitis cultures grew methicillin-susceptible Staphylococcus aureus, and she required surgical debridement because the inflammation extended over most of her abdominal wall. Histopathology showed massive infiltration by leukocytes, mainly neutrophils, with inflammatory granulomas and significant cell death on TUNEL and cleaved caspase-3 staining. Pustulosis developed at skin puncture and surgical sites. From 3 months of age she had 3 episodes of acute respiratory distress syndrome associated with pneumonia and thrombocytopenia. These responded to high-dose dexamethasone, and serum IL-1-beta and IL-6 were markedly raised during the first episode. Febrile episodes recurred whenever steroids were tapered. These episodes were accompanied by leukocytosis with neutrophil predominance, thrombocytosis, C-reactive protein levels of 100-500 mg/liter, and new skin nodules. Biopsy of erythema nodosum at 1 year of age showed diffuse infiltration of the dermis by neutrophils and mononuclear cells. She had severely impaired physical and psychological development, and no autoantibodies were detected.

### Inheritance

In all 3 reported patients, the heterozygous OTULIN mutation occurred de novo: 2 unrelated patients reported by Davidson et al. (2024) and 1 patient reported by Takeda et al. (2024). The mutations acted in a dominant-negative manner, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

In 2 unrelated patients with an ORAS-like autoinflammatory disease, Davidson et al. (2024) identified de novo heterozygous missense mutations in the OTULIN gene that both caused a cys129-to-ser (C129S) substitution. In P1 the mutation was a c.386G-C change, found by trio exome sequencing. In P2 it was a c.385T-A change, found by whole-genome sequencing and shown to be de novo by testing the parents. The variant was absent from gnomAD. Cys129 lies in the OTULIN catalytic domain. The C129S protein had wildtype-like stability, and patient fibroblasts contained normal amounts of OTULIN and LUBAC components, with wildtype (active) and C129S (inactive) OTULIN present in roughly equal amounts. However, recombinant C129S protein could not cleave M1-linked diubiquitin. Patient fibroblasts accumulated linear ubiquitin chains. Transfecting C129S, but not wildtype or the loss-of-function variants Y244C and N341D, into HEK293T cells that express endogenous OTULIN caused M1-ubiquitin to accumulate and activated NF-kappa-B, indicating a dominant-negative effect. C129S OTULIN bound linear ubiquitin with high affinity and promoted autoubiquitination of LUBAC. In patient fibroblasts, more OTULIN was bound to LUBAC, and most of the bound OTULIN was catalytically inactive. Recruitment of LUBAC to the TNF receptor signaling complex was reduced. Patient fibroblasts were as sensitive to TNF-induced cell death as fibroblasts from patients with biallelic ORAS, and much more sensitive than fibroblasts from healthy donors or from OTULIN-haploinsufficient individuals; the death was mainly apoptotic. Patient cells also showed increased expression of IFNB1 and IL6 and a raised interferon-stimulated gene score. ⚠︎

In a Japanese girl with an ORAS-like disorder, Takeda et al. (2024) used trio whole-exome sequencing and found 2 heterozygous OTULIN variants on different alleles. One was a de novo c.917G-A transition causing an arg306-to-gln (R306Q) substitution. The other was a maternally inherited c.455C-T transition causing a pro152-to-leu (P152L) substitution, which her healthy sister also carried. The R306Q variant was extremely rare in gnomAD (1 of 1,611,754 alleles). The P152L variant was relatively rare (51 of 1,611,754 alleles) and was predicted to be benign. Recombinant R306Q protein had markedly reduced deubiquitinase activity and failed to bind linear diubiquitin, whereas P152L protein was as active as wildtype. Patient cells contained normal amounts of OTULIN, with the P152L and R306Q proteins present in similar amounts, but showed increased linear ubiquitination. Mesenchymal stem cells derived from patient induced pluripotent stem cells showed increased linear ubiquitination of LUBAC subunits after TNF stimulation and greater TNF-induced cell death, while TNF-mediated NF-kappa-B activation was unaffected. Base-editing the P152L allele back to wildtype did not rescue these abnormalities, whereas correcting R306Q did. In OTULIN-null cells, R306Q reversed, in a dose-dependent way, the reduction in linear ubiquitin chains and the protection from cell death provided by wildtype or P152L OTULIN, consistent with a dominant-negative effect. ⚠︎

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

In OTULIN-null HeLa cells, the recessive ORAS variant L272P caused much more TNF-dependent linear autoubiquitination of LUBAC subunits than Y244C or R306Q did. From this, Takeda et al. (2024) suggested that the severity of ORAS symptoms reflects residual OTULIN function rather than the mode of inheritance. They also noted that their patient's symptoms were milder than those of reported neonatal-onset recessive cases, which they attributed partly to high-dose prednisolone treatment.

### Clinical Management

Both patients reported by Davidson et al. (2024) responded to corticosteroids. P1 was also treated with adalimumab, which lowered C-reactive protein and circulating myeloid cell counts and supported abdominal wound healing; he also received ruxolitinib, although its benefit was unclear. Wound healing in P1 was better under broad-spectrum antibiotic prophylaxis. P2 was treated with infliximab and methotrexate and was weaned off corticosteroids. Prophylactic antibiotics reduced her recurrent chest infections, and she had been in remission for 2 years. The patient reported by Takeda et al. (2024) started etanercept at 2 years of age, which completely resolved her symptoms; she reached steroid-free remission and her development began to catch up.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 AUTOINFLAMMATION, PANNICULITIS, AND DERMATOSIS SYNDROME, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
OTULIN, CYS129SER, 386G-C [ENST00000284274.5:c.386G>C; p.Cys129Ser]

In a 22-month-old Caucasian boy (P1) with neonatal-onset autoinflammatory disease, Davidson et al. (2024) identified a de novo heterozygous c.386G-C transversion in the OTULIN gene by trio exome sequencing. The change causes a cys129-to-ser (C129S) substitution at a catalytic-domain residue. His features included a nonhealing abdominal wound, panniculitis, pathergy, and acute respiratory deterioration. The variant was absent from gnomAD. The C129S protein was stable but catalytically inactive, and it acted in a dominant-negative way: it caused linear ubiquitin chains to accumulate and promoted LUBAC autoubiquitination even when wildtype OTULIN was present. The patient's fibroblasts were more sensitive to TNF-induced cell death.

**.0002 AUTOINFLAMMATION, PANNICULITIS, AND DERMATOSIS SYNDROME, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
OTULIN, CYS129SER, 385T-A [ENST00000284274.5:c.385T>A; p.Cys129Ser]

In a Saudi girl (P2) with autoinflammatory disease, Davidson et al. (2024) identified a de novo heterozygous c.385T-A transversion in the OTULIN gene by whole-genome sequencing. The change causes the same cys129-to-ser (C129S) substitution as in another patient. She had onset at 7 months with fever, tender skin lesions that progressed to necrotizing fasciitis, and panniculitis. Testing of her parents confirmed that the variant was de novo. Functional studies of C129S OTULIN showed loss of deubiquitinase activity and a dominant-negative effect.

OTULIN, ARG306GLN [NM_138348.6:c.917G>A; p.Arg306Gln]

In a Japanese girl with neonatal-onset autoinflammation, sterile pustulosis, erythema nodosum, acute respiratory distress, and growth failure, Takeda et al. (2024) identified a de novo heterozygous c.917G-A transition in the OTULIN gene by trio whole-exome sequencing. The change causes an arg306-to-gln (R306Q) substitution. She also carried a maternally inherited P152L variant on the other allele; the P152L protein had wildtype activity and her healthy sister also carried it. The R306Q protein had markedly reduced deubiquitinase activity and did not bind linear diubiquitin, but its protein level was normal. Mesenchymal stem cells in which P152L was base-edited back to wildtype, leaving only R306Q, still showed increased linear ubiquitination and TNF-induced cell death. In OTULIN-null cells, R306Q reversed, in a dose-dependent way, the effects of wildtype OTULIN on linear ubiquitin chains and cell death, indicating a dominant-negative effect. ⚠︎

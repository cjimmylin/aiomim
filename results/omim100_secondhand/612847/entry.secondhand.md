### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that brachyolmia type 4 with mild epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes (BCYM4) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the PAPSS2 gene on chromosome 10q23-q24.

### Description

Brachyolmia type 4 with mild epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes is an autosomal recessive skeletal dysplasia that mainly affects the spine. Features include short stature, platyspondyly or rectangular vertebral bodies with irregular end plates and narrow intervertebral spaces, mild brachydactyly or mildly shortened metacarpals, and epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes of the tubular bones that are mild and vary between patients (Ahmad et al., 1998; Miyake et al., 2012). Miyake et al. (2012) concluded that PAPSS2 mutations cause a family of skeletal dysplasias whose phenotypes range from brachyolmia to spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia. Because PAPSS2 deficiency also impairs sulfation of DHEA, a girl with compound heterozygous PAPSS2 mutations had premature pubarche and androgen excess (Noordam et al., 2009). ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) called the human gene ATPSK2, which encodes an ATP sulfurylase/APS kinase. Later reports used the name PAPSS2 (phosphoadenosine-phosphosulfate synthetase 2) (Noordam et al., 2009; Miyake et al., 2012).

### Clinical Features

Ahmad et al. (1998) described a large inbred kindred from a remote area of Pakistan, spanning 8 generations, with a distinct form of spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia. They evaluated 16 affected individuals (11 males and 5 females). Clinical findings included short stature evident at birth, short bowed lower limbs, mild brachydactyly, kyphoscoliosis, an abnormal gait, enlarged knee joints, precocious osteoarthropathy, and normal intelligence. Radiographs showed delayed epiphyseal ossification at the hips and knees, platyspondyly with irregular end plates and narrowed joint spaces, diffuse early osteoarthritic changes mainly in the spine and hands, and mild brachydactyly. Mild metaphyseal abnormalities were seen mainly at the hips and knees. The authors considered this phenotype distinct from other autosomal recessive forms of SEMD because of the mild metaphyseal involvement, the type of brachydactyly, and the absence of loose joints or other clinical findings.

Noordam et al. (2009) reported a girl with premature pubarche, hyperandrogenic anovulation, very low DHEAS levels, and increased androgen levels.

Miyake et al. (2012) studied 3 affected members of a Turkish family with autosomal recessive brachyolmia and 3 patients with similar phenotypes (2 Japanese and 1 Korean). All 6 patients had short-trunk short stature that became obvious during childhood, with normal intelligence and facies. Radiographic features included rectangular vertebral bodies with irregular end plates and narrow intervertebral discs, precocious calcification of the rib cartilages, short femoral neck, and mildly shortened metacarpals. Spinal changes were very similar in all 6 patients, but epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes of the tubular bones varied.

### Biochemical Features

In a girl with compound heterozygous PAPSS2 mutations, Noordam et al. (2009) found very low DHEAS levels and increased androgen levels. The sulfotransferase SULT2A1 converts the androgen precursor DHEA to its inactive sulfate ester, DHEAS, and needs PAPS for catalytic activity. The authors concluded that PAPSS2 deficiency is a monogenic adrenocortical cause of androgen excess.

### Inheritance

Ahmad et al. (1998) found that the pedigree of a large inbred Pakistani kindred strongly suggested autosomal recessive inheritance, and that consanguineous loops could account for all affected individuals being homozygous for the abnormal allele. Miyake et al. (2012) also observed autosomal recessive inheritance in a Turkish family with brachyolmia.

### Mapping

In a large inbred Pakistani family with recessively inherited SEMD, Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) mapped the disease gene to chromosome 10q23-q24. This region is syntenic with the locus of the brachymorphic mutation on mouse chromosome 19.

### Molecular Genetics

In an inbred Pakistani SEMD family, Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) found a nonsense mutation in the ATPSK2 (PAPSS2) gene. ATPSK2 encodes an ATP sulfurylase/APS kinase that converts inorganic sulfate into PAPS, the universal sulfate donor for posttranslational protein sulfation.

In a girl with premature pubarche, hyperandrogenic anovulation, very low DHEAS levels, and increased androgen levels, Noordam et al. (2009) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the PAPSS2 gene. In vitro, human SULT2A1 was incubated with either wild-type or mutant PAPSS2 protein, and the results confirmed that the mutations were inactivating.

In 3 affected members of a Turkish family with autosomal recessive brachyolmia, Miyake et al. (2012) used exon capture followed by next-generation sequencing and identified a homozygous loss-of-function mutation in the PAPSS2 gene (c.337_338insG; p.A113GfsX18). In 3 further patients with similar brachyolmia phenotypes (2 Japanese and 1 Korean), they also found loss-of-function mutations in PAPSS2. One patient was homozygous for an IVS3+2delT mutation. The second was compound heterozygous for c.616_634del19 (p.V206SfsX9) and c.1309_1310delAG (p.R437GfsX19). The third was compound heterozygous for c.480_481insCGTA (p.K161RfsX6) and c.661delA (p.I221SfsX40).

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Miyake et al. (2012) found very similar spinal changes in 6 patients with PAPSS2 mutations, but variable epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes of the tubular bones. They concluded that PAPSS2 mutations produce a family of skeletal dysplasias with phenotypes ranging from brachyolmia to spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia.

### Animal Model

In the brachymorphic mouse, Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) found a missense mutation in the part of the Atpsk2 gene that encodes the APS kinase activity. Atpsk2 is the mouse ortholog of human ATPSK2. The authors noted that the human and mouse phenotypes are both cartilage-specific. They took this as further evidence that sulfate activation plays a critical role in the maturation of cartilage extracellular matrix molecules and in skeletogenesis.

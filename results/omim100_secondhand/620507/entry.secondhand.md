### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that ichthyosis with erythrokeratoderma (IEKD) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding kallikrein-related peptidase 11 (KLK11).

### Description

Ichthyosis with erythrokeratoderma (IEKD) is an autosomal dominant Mendelian disorder of cornification. It starts early in life, with ichthyosiform erythroderma or erythrokeratoderma, and is associated with abnormal skin desquamation and a thickened, dense stratum corneum (Gong et al., 2023). Takeichi et al. (2023) described large hyperkeratotic pigmented plaques on the trunk as a feature of KLK11 ichthyosis.

### Clinical Features

Gong et al. (2023) studied 3 families with a previously undescribed cornification disorder: 1 familial case and 2 sporadic cases. The disorder was characterized by early-onset ichthyosiform erythroderma or erythrokeratoderma. The patients' stratum corneum was thickened and dense, which indicated abnormal skin desquamation. Ultrastructural study of the patients' stratum corneum showed delayed degeneration of corneodesmosomes.

Takeichi et al. (2023) reported patients with KLK11 ichthyosis. They highlighted large hyperkeratotic pigmented plaques on the trunk as a feature of a distinct autosomal dominant disorder of cornification. The phenotype in these patients resembled erythrokeratodermia. † ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Gong et al. (2023) established autosomal dominant inheritance of IEKD. The finding was based on heterozygous KLK11 mutations in 1 familial case and 2 sporadic cases. Takeichi et al. (2023) also described KLK11 ichthyosis as an autosomal dominant disorder of cornification.

### Molecular Genetics

Gong et al. (2023) performed whole-exome sequencing in 3 families with IEKD: 1 familial case and 2 sporadic cases. They identified 2 heterozygous mutations in the KLK11 gene, both affecting gly50, one of which was a gly50-to-glu (G50E) substitution. The mutations lay near the signal peptide cleavage site of KLK11. When overexpressed in eukaryotic cells, the mutations disrupted signal peptide cleavage. For the G50E variant, this led to mislocalization within the cell and reduced secretion from the cell. Trypsin-like and chymotrypsin-like proteolytic activity was significantly decreased in samples of the patients' stratum corneum. Proteolysis of desmoglein-1 was reduced, and corneodesmosomes degenerated more slowly, which indicated delayed skin desquamation.

Takeichi et al. (2023) reported patients with KLK11 ichthyosis and large hyperkeratotic pigmented plaques on the trunk. Only the title of this report was available, so the specific KLK11 mutations are not described here. ⚠︎

### Animal Model

Gong et al. (2023) created mice homozygous for a c.131G-A (G44E) Klk11 mutation, which is equivalent to the human KLK11 c.149G-A (G50E) mutation. The mice had hyperkeratosis and abnormal desquamation, which partly reproduced the human phenotype.

The study included a familial case and 2 sporadic cases.

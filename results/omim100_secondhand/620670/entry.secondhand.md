### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that common variable immunodeficiency-15 (CVID15) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the SEC61A1 gene on chromosome 3.

### Description

Common variable immunodeficiency-15 (CVID15) is an autosomal dominant primary antibody deficiency. It is characterized by hypogammaglobulinemia involving IgM, IgG, and IgA, and by severe recurrent bacterial infections of the respiratory tract that usually begin in the first year of life. Peripheral B- and T-cell subpopulations are normal, but B cells have an intrinsic defect in differentiating into plasma cells. Expressivity is variable and, in at least 1 family, penetrance is incomplete (Schubert et al., 2018). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Schubert et al. (2018) studied 10 individuals in a family (Family I) who had antibody isotype deficiencies involving IgM, IgG, and IgA. They had severe recurrent bacterial infections, including tonsillitis, otitis, sinusitis, pneumonias, and gastrointestinal infections. Onset was mostly in the first year of life. Affected individuals did not respond to polysaccharide vaccination and had variable responses to tetanus toxin vaccination. After starting immunoglobulin replacement therapy with intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG), they had far fewer and less severe infections. Flow cytometry showed normal ratios of B, T, and NK cells and normal T-cell subsets, including regulatory T cells and recent thymic emigrants. Peripheral B-cell subpopulations were also normal, including naive, unswitched memory, switched memory, IgA+, IgG+, transitional, and CD21-low B cells. Only the very small population of peripheral CD27+CD38-high plasmablasts appeared reduced in Family I, and the authors noted that this measurement is error-prone. The patients had no clinical symptoms other than infections. Serum levels of proteins secreted by the liver and pancreas were normal, as were the laboratory values reported to be altered in patients with SEC61A1-associated autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease.

Schubert et al. (2018) also described an unrelated family (Family II). The index patient was an 8-year-old boy who had hypogammaglobulinemia from birth. Because he was treated with IVIG, he had not had severe or recurrent infections. Repeated attempts to stop IVIG failed because his serum immunoglobulin levels stayed low. His grandmother, who also carried the mutation, reported more frequent upper respiratory infections in childhood, with recurrent bilateral bacterial otitis media. At age 66 years she had normal serum immunoglobulin levels. The boy's mother also carried the mutation but was not included in the study because she had a secondary immunodeficiency.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CVID15 in the families reported by Schubert et al. (2018) was consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. The heterozygous missense mutation segregated with the disease in Family I, which the authors considered fully penetrant. They suggested that the heterozygous nonsense mutation in Family II caused hypogammaglobulinemia, possibly transient, with variable expressivity and incomplete penetrance. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Schubert et al. (2018) performed a genome-wide linkage analysis with microsatellite markers in Family I. Only one region, on chromosome 3, had LOD scores greater than 2. Fine mapping confirmed linkage to chromosome 3, with a peak single-marker and multiple-marker LOD score of 3.31, and placed the gene between markers D3S3697 and D3S3684.

### Molecular Genetics

Schubert et al. (2018) performed whole-exome sequencing on 6 members of Family I (4 affected and 2 unaffected). Among the variants shared by the affected individuals, they found a heterozygous c.254T-A transversion in the SEC61A1 gene, resulting in a val85-to-asp (V85D) substitution. SEC61A1 was the only candidate gene in the chromosome 3 linkage region. Sanger sequencing showed that the mutation segregated with the disease in the family. V85 is a highly conserved residue and one of 6 bulky hydrophobic amino acids that form the pore ring of the Sec61 channel. In HeLa cells in which endogenous SEC61A1 was silenced and replaced, the V85D mutant was significantly less able than wildtype to carry out cotranslational transport of preprolactin. HeLa cells overproducing the mutant had a significantly lower ER calcium load, which the authors interpreted as a dominant effect consistent with increased calcium leakage. In multiple myeloma cell lines, stable expression of V85D led to the loss of transduced cells and induced the terminal unfolded protein response, with upregulation of IRE1A, PERK, XBP1s, ATF4, and CHOP. Other lymphoid and nonlymphoid cell lines were not affected. In thapsigargin-sensitized HEK293T cells, V85D induced XBP1s to the same degree whether it was expressed alone or together with wildtype SEC61A1, indicating a dominant effect.

Schubert et al. (2018) performed targeted next-generation sequencing in more than 200 additional patients with primary antibody deficiency. In the index patient of Family II, they identified a heterozygous c.1325G-T transversion in the SEC61A1 gene, resulting in a glu381-to-ter (E381X) substitution. The mutation was confirmed by Sanger sequencing and was also present in his mother and grandmother. The gnomAD database contained no SEC61A1 variants causing a premature stop codon. Naive B cells from the mutation carriers had reduced SEC61A1 protein levels, whereas levels in T cells were comparable to control. A truncated protein was not detected, which supported nonsense-mediated mRNA decay and SEC61A1 haploinsufficiency.

Schubert et al. (2018) tested B cells from mutation carriers in both families. When purified B cells were stimulated in vitro under T cell-dependent (CD40L and IL21) or T cell-independent (anti-IgM, BAFF, CpG) conditions, significantly fewer differentiated into plasmablasts and plasma cells than control cells. The patient cells also produced less IgG and IgA. EBV-transformed B-cell lines from patients had reduced SEC61A1 protein and mRNA levels. Unlike control lines, the patient lines lacked a CD38-high CD138+ plasma cell subpopulation, and the IgG, IgA, and IgM secretion capacity per cell was reduced.

### Pathogenesis

Schubert et al. (2018) noted that SEC61A1 is a target gene of spliced XBP1 (XBP1s) and is strongly induced during plasma cell differentiation. On the basis of proteomic data, they found that SEC61A1 protein was 2-fold more abundant in human peripheral blood plasmablasts than in naive and memory B cells. They proposed 2 mechanisms. In Family I, the dominant-negative V85D mutation would cause functional haploinsufficiency together with a dominant disturbance of calcium homeostasis. In Family II, haploinsufficiency would result from the nonsense mutation. Plasma cells might need 2 functional SEC61A1 alleles to make enough SEC61A1 protein for very high immunoglobulin production. The authors described CVID15 as the first monogenic defect known to specifically impair plasma cell homeostasis without affecting B-cell development, activation, or memory formation.

Reimold et al. (2001) studied mouse lymphoid chimeras deficient in Xbp1. These mice had normal numbers of activated B lymphocytes and formed normal germinal centers. However, they secreted very little immunoglobulin of any isotype and lacked plasma cells, and they failed to control infection with the B cell-dependent polyoma virus. In contrast, Taubenheim et al. (2012) found that antibody-secreting cells formed efficiently without Xbp1, both in vitro and in vivo, and were present at normal frequencies in resting and immunized mice. Xbp1 deficiency reduced, but did not abolish, antibody secretion, and the antibody-secreting cells failed to develop their characteristic morphology.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-46 (DEE46) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GRIN2D gene.

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-46 (DEE46) is a neurodevelopmental disorder marked by severe developmental delay and intractable epilepsy. The seizures have been refractory to conventional antiepileptic medications (Li et al., 2016; Tsuchida et al., 2018).

### Clinical Features

Li et al. (2016) reported 2 unrelated children with epileptic encephalopathy. In both children, the seizures did not respond to conventional antiepileptic medications. The older proband later developed refractory status epilepticus.

Tsuchida et al. (2018) reported 3 unrelated patients with severe developmental delay and intractable epilepsy. Their clinical features were similar to those of the 2 patients reported by Li et al. (2016).

### Inheritance

The heterozygous GRIN2D mutation found by Li et al. (2016) in 2 unrelated children with DEE46 arose de novo, which is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Li et al. (2016) studied 2 unrelated children with epileptic encephalopathy using exome and candidate panel sequencing. Both carried the same de novo heterozygous missense mutation in the GRIN2D gene: a c.1999G-A transition causing a val667-to-ile (V667I) substitution in the M3 transmembrane domain, which is involved in channel gating. Functional studies showed a gain-of-function effect. The mutation doubled glutamate and glycine potency, increased channel open probability 6-fold, and reduced receptor sensitivity to endogenous negative modulators such as extracellular protons. It also prolonged the deactivation time course after glutamate removal. When cultured neurons were transfected with mutant human GRIN2D cDNA, they showed dendritic swelling and cell death, suggesting excitotoxicity from NMDA receptor overactivation.

Tsuchida et al. (2018) used whole-exome sequencing to find novel heterozygous missense variants in the GRIN2D gene in 3 unrelated patients with severe developmental delay and intractable epilepsy. All of the altered residues were highly conserved across vertebrates and among the 4 GluN2 subunits. Structural analysis suggested that all 3 variants probably impair GluN2D function, either by affecting interactions between subunits or by altering channel gating.

### Clinical Management

Li et al. (2016) tested mutant NMDA receptors for sensitivity to FDA-approved NMDA receptor antagonists. Based on the results, they gave oral memantine to 2 children with the GRIN2D V667I mutation, and seizure burden and development improved mildly to moderately. The older proband later developed refractory status epilepticus, which improved dramatically on electroclinical measures after treatment with ketamine and magnesium. Li et al. (2016) suggested that NMDA receptor antagonists may be useful as adjuvant epilepsy therapy in patients with GRIN2D gain-of-function mutations.

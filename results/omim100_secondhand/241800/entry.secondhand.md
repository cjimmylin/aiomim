### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Pallister-Hall-like syndrome (PHLS) is caused by biallelic mutation in the SMO gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Pallister-Hall-like syndrome is a developmental disorder caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in the SMO gene. Its features vary widely. They can affect the brain (hypothalamic hamartoma and microcephaly), the heart (atrioventricular septal defect), the skeleton (postaxial polydactyly, narrow chest, and shortening of long bones), and the enteric nervous system (aganglionosis) (Le et al., 2020). ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

Encha-Razavi et al. (1992) proposed that skeletal dysplasia and oro-facial abnormalities, found in association with congenital hypothalamic hamartoma, be taken as the minimum criteria for a nosologically distinct, possibly familial entity. They suggested calling it 'congenital hypothalamic hamartoma syndrome' (CHHS).

### Clinical Features

Marcuse et al. (1953) reported 2 cases of hamartoma of the hypothalamus with associated developmental defects.

Encha-Razavi et al. (1992) reported congenital hypothalamic hamartomas found at autopsy in 3 unrelated fetuses. In the first 2 fetuses, the tumor was associated only with skeletal dysplasia. In the third, it was part of a nonrandom association of congenital malformations suggestive of Meckel syndrome. In one family, an earlier boy had died soon after birth with similar craniofacial and skeletal abnormalities. The authors noted that cases reported as Pallister-Hall syndrome also showed skeletal and oro-facial abnormalities, which were similar to those in their first 2 cases.

Rubino et al. (2019) described 2 brothers with a familial syndrome of hypothalamic hamartomas and polydactyly. One brother had a large hypothalamic hamartoma that underwent endoscopic endonasal biopsy and subtotal resection, and its histopathologic findings were described.

Le et al. (2020) reported 7 individuals from 5 unrelated families. Their developmental anomalies spanned a wide range and affected the brain (hypothalamic hamartoma and microcephaly), the heart (atrioventricular septal defect), the skeleton (postaxial polydactyly, narrow chest, and shortening of long bones), and the enteric nervous system (aganglionosis).

### Inheritance

Encha-Razavi et al. (1992) suggested that congenital hypothalamic hamartoma with skeletal dysplasia might be a familial entity. In one of their families, an earlier boy had similar abnormalities. Rubino et al. (2019) described affected brothers. Le et al. (2020) found biallelic SMO variants in affected individuals, which is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Rubino et al. (2019) reported 2 brothers with hypothalamic hamartomas and polydactyly who carried germline mutations in the SMO gene. The authors described this as the first familial syndrome caused by germline SMO mutations, and the first familial syndrome other than Pallister-Hall syndrome to be associated with hypothalamic hamartomas.

Le et al. (2020) identified biallelic loss-of-function variants in the SMO gene in 7 individuals from 5 unrelated families with a wide range of developmental anomalies. In cells from affected individuals, ciliogenesis was normal, but hedgehog signal transduction was severely altered. This resulted either from altered trafficking through the primary cilium or from abnormal activation of the pathway downstream of SMO. These cells also showed hedgehog-independent accumulation of GLI2 at the tip of the primary cilium. The authors suggested that SMO may help regulate basal ciliary trafficking of GLI2 when the pathway is off. They concluded that loss of SMO function disrupts the dynamics of key hedgehog pathway components in the primary cilium and leads to a broad continuum of malformations in humans.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that mitochondrial complex I deficiency nuclear type 7 (MC1DN7) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the NDUFV2 gene.

### Description

Mitochondrial complex I deficiency nuclear type 7 (MC1DN7) is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by isolated deficiency of respiratory chain complex I. Affected individuals have had early-onset hypertrophic cardiomyopathy with truncal hypotonia and encephalopathy (Bénit et al., 2003). Other affected individuals have had Leigh syndrome without cardiac involvement (Cameron et al., 2015). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Bénit et al. (2003) reported 3 affected sibs from a consanguineous family who had complex I deficiency and early-onset hypertrophic cardiomyopathy with trunk hypotonia and encephalopathy. Mutations in several other complex I subunit genes mainly cause neurologic symptoms. Bénit et al. (2003) noted that, in contrast, this disorder was strikingly associated with cardiomyopathy, as had previously been seen only in patients with NDUFS2 mutations (Loeffen et al., 2001).

Pagniez-Mammeri et al. (2009) studied 8 patients with biochemically proven isolated complex I deficiency.

Cameron et al. (2015) reported 2 sibs with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and brain atrophy. They were diagnosed with complex I deficiency based on low enzyme activity in muscle and a high lactate/pyruvate ratio in fibroblasts. Cameron et al. (2015) also reported a second, consanguineous family in which 3 of 5 sibs were affected. These sibs had Leigh syndrome but no cardiac involvement.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of MC1DN7 in the families reported by Bénit et al. (2003) and Cameron et al. (2015) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Affected sibs carried homozygous or compound heterozygous NDUFV2 mutations, and 2 of the families were consanguineous. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Bénit et al. (2003) used denaturing high performance liquid chromatography and sequence analysis to study 3 affected sibs from a consanguineous family with MC1DN7. They identified a homozygous 4-bp deletion in intron 2 of the NDUFV2 gene (IVS2+5_+8delGTAA). The mutation altered the consensus splice-donor site of exon 2 and caused a 70% decrease in NDUFV2 protein, as well as complex I deficiency. Patient fibroblasts showed a significant reduction of NDUFV2 protein in mitochondria. †

Pagniez-Mammeri et al. (2009) screened 8 nuclear complex I genes in 8 patients with isolated complex I deficiency. The genes were NDUFS1, NDUFS2, NDUFS3, NDUFS4, NDUFS7, NDUFS8, NDUFV1, and NDUFV2. The screen used Surveyor nuclease digestion of cDNA fragments covering the coding sequences, and it found molecular defects in 3 patients.

Cameron et al. (2015) performed whole-exome sequencing on fibroblast genomic DNA from 1 of 2 sibs with MC1DN7. Both sibs were compound heterozygous for 2 mutations in the NDUFV2 gene. The first was the previously reported intronic 4-bp deletion, which they designated c.IVS2+1delGTAA. The second was a novel 1-bp insertion (c.669_670insG), predicted to cause a frameshift (Ser224ValfsTer3). Cameron et al. (2015) then screened 10 probands with complex I deficiency from different families. This identified homozygosity for the intronic deletion in a second, consanguineous family, in which 3 of 5 sibs were affected with Leigh syndrome.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Cameron et al. (2015) noted that the homozygous intronic NDUFV2 deletion had previously been reported in 2 families with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, trunk hypotonia, and encephalopathy. In contrast, the 3 affected sibs in their consanguineous family had the same genotype but presented with Leigh syndrome and no cardiac involvement. This finding widened the range of clinical phenotypes associated with this mutation.

Bénit et al. (2003) studied 3 sibs from a consanguineous family who had complex I deficiency (MC1DN7) with early-onset hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, trunk hypotonia, and encephalopathy. All 3 were homozygous for a 4-bp deletion in intron 2 of the NDUFV2 gene (IVS2+5_+8delGTAA). The deletion altered the consensus splice-donor site of exon 2 and caused a 70% decrease in NDUFV2 protein, as well as complex I deficiency.

Cameron et al. (2015) identified this mutation, which they designated c.IVS2+1delGTAA, in 2 families. In the first, 2 sibs with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and brain atrophy were compound heterozygous for the deletion and a 1-bp insertion (c.669_670insG). In the second, a consanguineous family, 3 of 5 sibs were homozygous for the deletion and had Leigh syndrome without cardiac involvement.

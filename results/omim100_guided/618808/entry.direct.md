### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that intellectual developmental disorder with poor growth and with or without seizures or ataxia (IDPOGSA) is caused by homozygous mutation in the ABCA2 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

IDPOGSA is an autosomal recessive neurodevelopmental disorder. Maddirevula et al. (2019) described global developmental delay and intellectual disability in affected individuals. Aslam and Naz (2019) described cerebellar ataxia with dysarthria in affected sibs, with no brain abnormalities on MRI and with elevated low density lipoprotein levels in blood. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Maddirevula et al. (2019) reported 2 families in which global developmental delay and intellectual disability segregated with homozygous truncating ABCA2 variants. The index patient in 1 family (16DG0071) showed no apparent gross dysmorphism. The authors considered this phenotype different enough from the amyotrophic lateral sclerosis reported by Steinberg et al. that it might be a distinct allelic disorder.

Aslam and Naz (2019) studied a consanguineous Pakistani family with 2 affected sibs. Both had symptoms consistent with cerebellar ataxia with dysarthria. Magnetic resonance imaging of the older sib showed no brain abnormalities. Both affected individuals had elevated blood levels of low density lipoproteins. The authors noted that the phenotype closely matched that of Abca2 knockout mice. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Maddirevula et al. (2019) found that homozygous ABCA2 variants fully segregated with the disorder in 2 families, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Maddirevula et al. (2019) used autozygome and exome analysis to study 2 families with global developmental delay and intellectual disability. They identified a different homozygous truncating mutation in the ABCA2 gene in each family: c.740dupT (Gly248ArgfsTer38) and c.1027C-T (Gln343Ter). In both families, the mutation fully segregated with the disorder.

Aslam and Naz (2019) used whole-exome sequencing to study a consanguineous Pakistani family with 2 sibs affected by cerebellar ataxia with dysarthria. They identified a frameshift mutation in the ABCA2 gene (c.4993delG; Val1665TyrfsTer36). Sanger sequencing showed that the mutation segregated with the disorder. It was absent from all publicly available databases and from ethnically matched controls.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that intellectual developmental disorder with poor growth and with or without seizures or ataxia (IDPOGSA) is caused by homozygous mutation in the ABCA2 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

IDPOGSA is an autosomal recessive neurodevelopmental disorder. Reported patients had global developmental delay and intellectual disability (Maddirevula et al., 2019). In one family, the affected sibs had cerebellar ataxia with dysarthria and elevated low density lipoprotein levels (Aslam and Naz, 2019). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Maddirevula et al. (2019) reported 2 families in which affected individuals had global developmental delay and intellectual disability. The index patient (16DG0071) of 1 family had no apparent gross dysmorphism.

Aslam and Naz (2019) studied 2 sibs from a consanguineous Pakistani family who had cerebellar ataxia with dysarthria. Brain MRI of the older sib showed no abnormalities. Both affected sibs had elevated blood levels of low density lipoproteins.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of IDPOGSA in the families reported by Maddirevula et al. (2019) and in the consanguineous Pakistani family reported by Aslam and Naz (2019) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of 2 families with global developmental delay and intellectual disability, Maddirevula et al. (2019) used autozygome and exome analysis to identify homozygous truncating mutations in the ABCA2 gene (c.740dupT, p.Gly248Argfs*38 and c.1027C-T, p.Gln343Ter; NM_212533.2). The variants fully segregated with the disorder in the families. The authors suggested that the phenotype was distinct from the amyotrophic lateral sclerosis reported by Steinberg et al., and might represent a distinct allelic disorder.

In 2 sibs from a consanguineous Pakistani family with cerebellar ataxia and dysarthria, Aslam and Naz (2019) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a homozygous 1-bp deletion in the ABCA2 gene (c.4993delG, NM_212533.2), predicted to cause a frameshift and premature termination (Val1665TyrfsTer36). Sanger sequencing confirmed segregation of the mutation with the disorder. The variant was absent from all publicly available databases and from ethnically matched controls. The authors noted that the phenotype was highly concordant with that seen in Abca2 knockout mice. ⚠︎

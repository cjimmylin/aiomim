### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that annular epidermolytic ichthyosis-1 (AEI1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding keratin-10 (KRT10).

### Description

Annular epidermolytic ichthyosis is a rare phenotypic variant of epidermolytic ichthyosis (bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma). Affected individuals have bullous ichthyosis in early childhood and hyperkeratotic lichenified plaques in flexural areas and on extensor surfaces at later ages. They also have intermittent bouts of annular and polycyclic, erythematous, scaly plaques on the trunk and proximal extremities (Suga et al., 1998). Palmoplantar keratoderma can be present (Mikilita et al., 2020). ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

Sahn et al. (1992) proposed that the kindred they reported might represent a new phenotype of epidermolytic (acanthokeratolytic) ichthyosis. Joh et al. (1997) concluded that annular epidermolytic ichthyosis should be considered a variant of bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma.

### Clinical Features

Sahn et al. (1992) reported a 30-year-old white woman who developed bullous and ichthyosiform skin lesions at the age of 8 months. Blistering stopped at puberty, but the hyperkeratotic plaques persisted. At age 27 years, she developed numerous pruritic annular and polycyclic, erythematous, hyperkeratotic plaques on the trunk and extremities that enlarged slowly and then resolved. Biopsy of the annular plaques showed epidermolytic hyperkeratosis, and electron microscopy showed interrupted perinuclear tonofilament rings. Two of her 5 children had a bullous type of ichthyosis.

Joh et al. (1997) reported a second family with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis, with 2 affected generations. The proband had bullous ichthyosis, with bouts of disease activity in which numerous annular and polycyclic erythematous, hyperkeratotic plaques developed on the trunk and proximal extremities. Histology showed epidermolytic hyperkeratosis, and ultrastructural study showed abnormal keratin filament networks and perinuclear clumping of tonofilaments.

Suga et al. (1998) described a third kindred with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis.

Mikilita et al. (2020) reported an 8-year-old girl, with no family history of the disorder and no parental consanguinity, who had diffuse skin disease beginning in the first months of life. She had erythematous, scaly, hyperkeratotic plaques with prominent geographic borders on the mesogastrium, cubital and popliteal fossae, and inguinal and cervical regions, together with palmoplantar hyperkeratosis and yellowish hyperkeratotic plaques on the scalp and nasal introitus. Hair, nails, and mucosa were normal. When reexamined 1 month later, the plaques had changed in appearance, the affected area had expanded, and the polycyclic pattern had disappeared. A biopsy from the forearm showed acanthosis, papillomatosis, and hyperkeratosis with marked epidermolysis in the granular layer.

### Inheritance

In the kindred reported by Sahn et al. (1992), an affected woman had 2 affected children. Joh et al. (1997) described a family with 2 affected generations and stated that the pattern of inheritance fits autosomal dominant transmission.

### Heterogeneity

In a literature review, Mikilita et al. (2020) noted that annular epidermolytic ichthyosis results from dominant mutations in either the keratin-1 or the keratin-10 gene.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of a family with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis, Joh et al. (1997) identified a tandem 2-bp CG-to-GA mutation on a single allele of the KRT10 gene. The mutation caused an arg-to-glu substitution at residue 83 (R83E) of the 2B helical segment.

In a third kindred with AEI, Suga et al. (1998) identified a mutation in the KRT10 gene that caused an ile-to-thr substitution at residue 107 (codon 446). The substituted residue lies in the highly conserved helix termination motif at the end of the keratin-10 rod domain.

### Diagnosis

In their patient, Mikilita et al. (2020) first suspected erythrokeratodermia variabilis et progressiva. They made the diagnosis of annular epidermolytic ichthyosis after histopathology showed epidermolytic hyperkeratosis. They noted that erythrokeratodermia variabilis et progressiva differs in its onset during childhood, its lack of epidermal fragility, and its histology without epidermolysis.

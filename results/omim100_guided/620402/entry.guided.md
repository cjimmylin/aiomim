### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-9 (HMNR9) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the COQ7 gene on chromosome 16. ⚠︎

### Description

Autosomal recessive distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-9 (HMNR9) is a length-dependent, pure motor axonal neuropathy that begins in childhood or adolescence. It causes slowly progressive distal weakness and atrophy, affecting the lower limbs first and more severely, with later involvement of the hands. Sensation is clinically preserved. Some patients have pyramidal signs (Jacquier et al., 2023; Smith et al., 2023). Patient fibroblasts show reduced coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10) levels and accumulation of the COQ7 substrate demethoxy-CoQ10.

### Clinical Features

Jacquier et al. (2023) reported 2 brothers and a sister, aged 42, 36, and 25 years, born to nonconsanguineous Portuguese parents. All had distal hereditary motor neuropathy, and early development was normal. Walking difficulties began at ages 10, 12, and 9 years, respectively. The brothers developed hand weakness at ages 22 and 32 years, and the sister had difficulty with precise hand movements from age 13 years. On examination, all 3 had distal weakness that was worse in the lower limbs and mainly affected foot dorsiflexion. They also had atrophy of the lower limbs and hands, pes cavus, inability to walk on heels or tiptoes, and a normal sensory examination. The 36-year-old brother had brisk reflexes in all 4 limbs, absent Achilles reflexes, and a Hoffmann sign. The sister had a Babinski sign on the left foot and reported difficulty feeling cold and warm temperatures on her feet. The oldest brother had absent Achilles reflexes, complete loss of plantar and dorsal flexion of the feet, and needed ankle-foot orthoses from about age 20 years. Two patients had a positive Gowers maneuver. No patient had cognitive impairment, and all remained ambulant. Electroneuromyography in 2 patients showed a pure motor axonal neuropathy that mainly affected the distal lower limbs. Motor amplitudes were reduced or not obtained, sensory responses were normal, and needle examination showed fibrillations and neurogenic recruitment. In 1 patient, brain and spinal cord MRI, echocardiography, ECG, and audiometry were normal, and the creatine kinase level was mildly elevated at 215 U/l (normal less than 200). ⚠︎

Smith et al. (2023) reported 3 sibs, a 24-year-old woman and 2 brothers aged 23 and 12 years, born to Syrian parents who were first cousins. All had a severe length-dependent motor neuropathy. Distal weakness began between 10 and 14 years of age, and muscle atrophy was greater in the distal lower limbs than the upper limbs. Sensation and deep tendon reflexes were preserved, and the sibs had no developmental delay, cognitive impairment, or upper motor neuron features. Electrodiagnostic testing showed a pattern of reduced distal motor amplitudes with preserved sensory responses. Needle EMG showed distal chronic reinnervation without active denervation. Muscle MRI in 2 sibs showed normal proximal muscles with distal wasting and moderate to severe fat infiltration, most pronounced in the calves. A quadriceps biopsy showed neurogenic atrophy with group atrophy and fiber type grouping, without ragged red or cytochrome oxidase-negative fibers. Two of the affected sibs and 1 unaffected sib were deaf. The deafness did not segregate with the neuropathy and was explained by a homozygous GJB2 mutation.

Liu et al. (2023) reported 2 Chinese families with distal hereditary motor neuropathy caused by biallelic variants in COQ7.

### Biochemical Features

Jacquier et al. (2023) found 6-demethoxycoenzyme Q10 in the plasma of an affected patient but not in controls. They suggested that measuring it in blood could help confirm whether a COQ7 variant is pathogenic.

Smith et al. (2023) found that CoQ10 in the muscle of an affected sib was 67% lower than in an unrelated control (63 vs 192.7 ng/mg protein). No DMQ10 was detected in the muscle, whereas DMQ10 accumulated in cultured fibroblasts from the affected sibs.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of HMNR9 in the families reported by Jacquier et al. (2023) and Smith et al. (2023) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. In both families, affected sibs were homozygous for a COQ7 mutation and their unaffected parents were heterozygous carriers.

### Molecular Genetics

In 3 sibs from a Portuguese family with HMNR9, Jacquier et al. (2023) used whole-exome sequencing of the sibs and their parents to identify a homozygous c.3G-T transversion (c.3G-T, NM_016138.5) in the COQ7 gene. The variant disrupts the start codon of isoform 1 (p.Met1?). Sanger sequencing confirmed it, and it segregated with the disorder: the unaffected parents were heterozygous. The variant was absent from the ExAC database and was found once in the heterozygous state in gnomAD (allele frequency 0.000004065). Isoform 1 is the main isoform in spinal cord. Alternative start codons used by isoforms 2 and 3 would not be affected. When mutant COQ7 was expressed in SH-EP neuroblastoma cells, COQ7 protein levels were 85% lower than with wildtype, while mRNA levels were unchanged. COQ7 protein was barely detectable in patient fibroblasts. CoQ10 levels in patient fibroblasts were 12.5 pmol/mg protein compared with 71.5 pmol/mg protein in controls, and 6-demethoxy-CoQ10 accumulated. The CoQ10 precursor 2,4-dihydroxybenzoic acid normalized 6-demethoxy-CoQ10 levels and slightly increased CoQ10 levels in patient fibroblasts. In patient fibroblasts, mitochondrial ATP production was reduced to 42% of total ATP production, compared with 63.3% and 56.4% in control fibroblasts, and glycolytic ATP production was increased. In galactose medium, patient cells proliferated less, with 50% fewer cells than controls, and CoQ10 supplementation increased their number by 100%.

In 3 sibs from a consanguineous Syrian family with HMNR9, Smith et al. (2023) used duo-exome sequencing to identify a homozygous c.1A-G transition (c.1A-G, NM_016138.5) in the COQ7 gene. The variant causes loss of the initiating methionine and is predicted to start translation at methionine-39, which would remove the cleavable mitochondrial targeting sequence. The parents and an unaffected sib were heterozygous carriers, and the variant was absent from gnomAD. Fibroblasts from the affected sibs contained on average 87% less CoQ10 than fibroblasts from their father and unaffected sib. The affected sibs' fibroblasts also accumulated demethoxy-CoQ10 (DMQ10), and the COQ7-to-porin ratio was 85% +/- 7% lower. No COQ7 protein was detected in muscle from an affected sib. Maximal mitochondrial respiration was impaired in both patient fibroblasts and muscle.

Liu et al. (2023) identified biallelic COQ7 variants in 2 Chinese families with distal hereditary motor neuropathy.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Previously reported COQ7 mutations lie in the catalytic domain and cause severe disease beginning at birth or in early childhood. Jacquier et al. (2023) proposed that their patients' disease was milder because the start-codon mutation affects only isoform 1. Isoform 2, translated from a downstream start codon, was expressed in transfected cells and colocalized with mitochondria, so it might partly compensate.

Wongkittichote et al. (2023) described 2 distinct presentations of COQ7-related primary CoQ10 deficiency. The first began in the neonatal period with severe neuromuscular, cardiorespiratory, and renal involvement. The second began later with progressive neuropathy, lower extremity weakness, abnormal gait, and variable developmental delay. In a yeast model, several variants partially rescued the oxidative growth defect, indicating that they were hypomorphic alleles.

### Clinical Management

Jacquier et al. (2023) reported that the affected sister's symptoms stabilized while she took CoQ10 (30 mg per day) between 15 and 18 years of age. Her symptoms began to worsen again at age 18 years, when supplementation was stopped. In patient fibroblasts, CoQ10 supplementation rescued basal and maximal respiration.

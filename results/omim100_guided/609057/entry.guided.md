A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that epidermolysis bullosa simplex with nephropathy and deafness (EBS7) is caused by homozygous mutation in the CD151 gene on chromosome 11p15.5. ⚠︎

### Description

Epidermolysis bullosa simplex with nephropathy and deafness (EBS7) combines hereditary nephritis that progresses to end-stage renal disease, pretibial epidermolysis bullosa, and sensorineural deafness. Affected individuals lack the MER2 blood group antigen, which is carried by CD151 on erythrocytes (Kagan et al., 1988; Karamatic Crew et al., 2004).

### Clinical Features

Kagan et al. (1988) described 2 sibs who had hereditary nephritis with end-stage renal disease, along with pretibial epidermolysis bullosa and beta-thalassemia minor.

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) studied 3 MER2-negative patients of Indian Jewish origin who had end-stage kidney disease. Two of the patients were sibs. Besides hereditary nephritis, the sibs had sensorineural deafness, pretibial epidermolysis bullosa, and beta-thalassemia minor.

### Inheritance

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) found a homozygous mutation in affected patients, including a pair of sibs.

### Molecular Genetics

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) showed that CD151 expresses the MER2 blood group antigen and is present on erythrocytes. They studied 3 MER2-negative patients of Indian Jewish origin with end-stage kidney disease, 2 of whom were sibs. All 3 were homozygous for a 1-bp insertion (G383) in exon 5 of the CD151 gene. The insertion causes a frameshift and a premature stop signal at codon 140. The resulting truncated protein would lack its integrin-binding domain. The authors concluded that CD151 is essential for the correct assembly of the glomerular and tubular basement membranes in the kidney, that it has functional significance in the skin, and that it is probably a component of the inner ear.

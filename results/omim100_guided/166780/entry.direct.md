### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that otofaciocervical syndrome-1 (OTFCS1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the EYA1 gene on chromosome 8q13.3. Rickard et al. (2001) found a large de novo deletion including the EYA1 gene region in a patient with OFC syndrome, and Estefanía et al. (2006) found a single-nucleotide splice site substitution in EYA1 in another patient. ⚠︎

### Description

Otofaciocervical (OFC) syndrome is clinically similar to branchiootorenal (BOR) syndrome, but has features in addition to those of BOR syndrome (Rickard et al., 2001). Estefanía et al. (2006) described OFC syndrome as having changes of the face and shoulder girdle in addition to the malformations seen in BOR syndrome. BOR syndrome includes developmental defects of branchial arch structures, the middle and/or inner ear, and the kidney.

### Clinical Features

Fára et al. (1967) reported familial otofaciocervical dysmorphia. According to Dallapiccola and Mingarelli (1995), features in this original family included lateral cervical fistulae.

Dallapiccola and Mingarelli (1995) described a sporadic patient with OFC syndrome. Distinguishing features were a long face with a narrow nose, high-arched palate, prominent and dysmorphic ears, long neck, sloping shoulders and clavicles, winged, low, and laterally set scapulae, tetralogy of Fallot, and deafness caused by cochlear malformation. The patient did not have preauricular tags, lacrimal duct stenosis, or renal malformations. The authors noted that some features of the original family, including lateral cervical fistulae, had suggested that OFC syndrome might be a variant of BOR syndrome. However, they concluded that this patient's distinct facial and radiographic findings and markedly downward-sloping shoulders supported OFC syndrome as a unique, clinically recognizable entity.

### Inheritance

Fára et al. (1967) described a familial occurrence of OFC syndrome. Dallapiccola and Mingarelli (1995) reported a sporadic case. Rickard et al. (2001) found a de novo heterozygous deletion including EYA1 in a patient with OFC syndrome. They concluded that OFC syndrome is allelic with BOR syndrome, which is an autosomal dominant disorder. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Pohl et al. (2013) studied a large consanguineous Turkish family with autosomal recessive OFC syndrome. Features included facial dysmorphism, external ear anomalies with preauricular pits, hearing impairment, branchial cysts or fistulas, anomalies of the vertebrae and shoulder girdle, and mild intellectual disability. Whole-exome sequencing of a pooled DNA sample from 4 affected individuals identified a homozygous c.497G-T variant in the PAX1 gene, which resulted in a gly166-to-val (G166V) substitution within the paired-box domain. The variant cosegregated with the disease in the family. In a luciferase reporter assay in HEK293T cells, the corresponding mouse mutant Pax1 (G157V) showed significantly reduced transactivation of a regulatory sequence in the Nkx3-2 promoter compared with wildtype. This result suggested that the mutant protein had reduced DNA-binding affinity. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Rickard et al. (2001) developed a fluorescent quantitative multiplex PCR dosage assay for 3 of the 3-prime exons of the EYA1 gene (exons 7, 9, and 13) and combined it with microsatellite marker analysis. Using this approach, they found de novo deletions of the EYA1 gene and the surrounding region in 2 patients with complex phenotypes that included features of BOR syndrome. One of these patients had OFC syndrome and carried a large deletion of the EYA1 gene region. The authors concluded that OFC syndrome is allelic with BOR syndrome and is a contiguous gene deletion syndrome. They compared the boundaries of this deletion with other reported rearrangements of the region. This comparison narrowed the critical region for Duane syndrome to an interval of about 1 cM between markers D8S553 and D8S1797.

In a patient with OFC syndrome, Estefanía et al. (2006) identified a single-nucleotide substitution in a splice site of the EYA1 gene. They concluded that patients with OFC syndrome can carry point mutations that alter the EYA1 reading frame, as well as large rearrangements. This finding argued against the hypothesis that OFC syndrome is caused only by contiguous deletions of EYA1 and adjacent genes.

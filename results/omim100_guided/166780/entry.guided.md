### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that otofaciocervical syndrome-1 (OTFCS1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the EYA1 gene on chromosome 8q13.3. ⚠︎

### Description

Otofaciocervical syndrome is clinically similar to branchiootorenal (BOR) syndrome but has extra features. Dallapiccola and Mingarelli (1995) listed these as a long face with a narrow nose, high-arched palate, prominent and dysmorphic ears, a long neck, sloping shoulders, and low, laterally set winged scapulae, along with deafness. Estefanía et al. (2006) described OFC as BOR-type malformations plus trophic changes of the face and shoulder girdle. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Fára et al. (1967) reported familial otofaciocervical dysmorphia.

Dallapiccola and Mingarelli (1995) described a sporadic patient with OFC syndrome. The patient had a long face with a narrow nose, a high-arched palate, prominent and dysmorphic ears, and a long neck. Other findings were sloping shoulders and clavicles, low, laterally set winged scapulae, tetralogy of Fallot, and deafness caused by a cochlear malformation. The patient had no preauricular tags, lacrimal duct stenosis, or renal malformations. The authors noted that some features of the original family, including lateral cervical fistulae, had suggested that OFC syndrome might be a variant of BOR syndrome. However, they concluded that the distinct facial and radiographic findings and the markedly downward-sloping shoulders in their patient supported OFC syndrome as a unique, clinically recognizable entity.

### Heterogeneity

Pohl et al. (2013) studied a large consanguineous Turkish family with autosomal recessive otofaciocervical syndrome. Features included facial dysmorphism, external ear anomalies with preauricular pits, hearing impairment, branchial cysts or fistulas, anomalies of the vertebrae and shoulder girdle, and mild intellectual disability. Whole-exome sequencing of pooled DNA from 4 affected individuals identified a homozygous c.497G-T mutation in the PAX1 gene, predicted to cause a gly166-to-val (G166V) substitution in the paired-box domain. The mutation cosegregated with the disease in the family. In a luciferase reporter assay in HEK293T cells, the mutant protein showed significantly reduced transactivation of an Nkx3-2 promoter sequence. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Rickard et al. (2001) used a fluorescent quantitative multiplex PCR dosage assay for EYA1 exons 7, 9, and 13, together with microsatellite marker analysis. In a patient with OFC syndrome, they found a large de novo deletion that included the EYA1 gene and the surrounding region. The authors concluded that OFC syndrome is allelic with BOR syndrome and is a contiguous gene deletion syndrome involving EYA1. By comparing the boundaries of this deletion with other reported rearrangements of the region, they narrowed the critical region for Duane syndrome to the interval between markers D8S553 and D8S1797, about 1 cM.

Estefanía et al. (2006) found a single-nucleotide substitution at a splice site of the EYA1 gene in a patient with OFC syndrome. They concluded that OFC syndrome can result from point mutations that alter the EYA1 reading frame, not only from large rearrangements.

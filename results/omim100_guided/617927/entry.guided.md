### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII (OFD18) is caused by homozygous mutation in the IFT57 gene on chromosome 3.

### Description

Orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII (OFD18) is a ciliopathy with oral, facial, and digital anomalies together with skeletal dysplasia. Features include median clefting of the upper lip, supernumerary oral frenulae, missing incisors, short stature, brachymesophalangia, and preaxial and postaxial polydactyly. The features overlap with those of Ellis-van Creveld syndrome (Phadke et al., 1999; Thevenon et al., 2016).

### Clinical Features

Phadke et al. (1999) reported a consanguineous family with a new form of orofaciodigital syndrome (OFDS) associated with acromelic short stature and genu valgum. Some of the features overlapped with those of Ellis-van Creveld syndrome. One of the sibs had urinary incontinence and growth hormone deficiency.

Thevenon et al. (2016) restudied the family reported by Phadke et al. (1999), which included 3 affected sibs born to healthy consanguineous parents, after 15 years of follow-up. One sib died at 17 years of age from a complicated pulmonary infection. At 25 years of age, a male sib had normal intelligence and short stature, with a final height of 155 cm (-3 SD). He had a square face with a short forehead, upslanting palpebral fissures, a high nasal bridge, a pyriform nose, a short philtrum, and a median cleft of the upper lip. Oral anomalies included supernumerary frenulae and missing incisors. He had pronounced brachydactyly, postaxial polydactyly of the upper limbs, spacing between the first and second toes, and preaxial polydactyly of the feet due to duplication of the first metatarsal. X-rays showed severe shortening of the middle phalanges, bilateral short femoral necks with varus femurs, and symmetric genu valgum. At 22 years of age, a female sib had normal intelligence and a final height of 122 cm (-5 SD). She had a square face with a narrow forehead, enophthalmos, upslanting palpebral fissures, a broad nasal bridge, bilateral hypopigmented pseudoclefts with median labial clefting, missing incisors, and supernumerary frenulae. She had marked brachydactyly and postaxial polydactyly of all 4 limbs. Hand x-rays showed shortening of the middle phalanges and residual postaxial polydactyly with fused proximal phalanges and bifid distal phalanges. Foot x-rays showed duplication of the first metatarsal and absent intermediate phalanges. She also had vertebral bodies with medial notches, bilateral cervical ribs without a narrow thorax, and gracile long bones with short femoral necks. Neither sib had a thoracic deformity, and brain MRI, heart ultrasound, and abdominal ultrasound were normal. On review, Thevenon et al. (2016) favored an unclassified type of OFDS with skeletal dysplasia over Ellis-van Creveld syndrome, because the sibs had no rib shortening or narrow thorax. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OFD18 in the family reported by Phadke et al. (1999) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. The affected sibs were born to consanguineous parents and carried a homozygous IFT57 mutation (Thevenon et al., 2016).

### Mapping

In the consanguineous family with OFD18 originally reported by Phadke et al. (1999), Thevenon et al. (2016) carried out homozygosity mapping in affected sibs. They found shared runs of homozygosity totaling 11 Mb, on chromosome 3 (chr3:104,822,019-111,671,862) and chromosome 10 (chr10:1,372,501-6,029,726; hg19). The chromosome 10 interval was excluded by segregation in the third affected sib.

### Molecular Genetics

In 3 sibs from a consanguineous family with OFD18, originally reported by Phadke et al. (1999), Thevenon et al. (2016) identified a homozygous synonymous c.777G-A transition (c.777G-A, NM_018010.3) in the IFT57 gene, resulting in lys259-to-lys (K259K). The mutation affected the last base of exon 6. It was found by homozygosity mapping and exome sequencing and was confirmed by Sanger sequencing in all 3 affected sibs. The variant was absent from the ExAC database and from 50 healthy individuals from northern India. In patient fibroblasts, the mutation caused a complex splicing pattern. Normal transcript was still present, and 3 abnormal transcripts were detected only after inhibition of nonsense-mediated mRNA decay: in-frame skipping of exon 6, in-frame skipping of exons 5 and 6, and out-of-frame skipping of exons 4, 5, and 6. IFT57 mRNA levels were significantly reduced in patient fibroblasts. The number and length of primary cilia were normal. However, IFT57 staining at the ciliary tip was significantly decreased (90% vs 35%; p less than 0.001) and staining at the basal body was increased, indicating defective anterograde ciliary transport. After stimulation with a Smoothened agonist, GLI1 induction was poor in patient fibroblasts and was absent in control fibroblasts after siRNA knockdown of IFT57. Thevenon et al. (2016) concluded that the mutation was hypomorphic and impaired sonic hedgehog signaling. Sanger sequencing of IFT57 in 13 patients with OFDS and 12 patients with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome found no causative mutations. Exome or ciliome data from more than 172 patients with various ciliopathies found no biallelic causative mutations.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 OROFACIODIGITAL SYNDROME XVIII**  
IFT57, 777G-A [NM_018010.3:c.777G>A; p.Lys259Lys]

In 3 sibs from a consanguineous family with orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII (OFD18), originally reported by Phadke et al. (1999), Thevenon et al. (2016) identified a homozygous synonymous c.777G-A transition in the last base of exon 6 of the IFT57 gene (lys259-to-lys; K259K). The mutation was found by homozygosity mapping and exome sequencing and was confirmed by Sanger sequencing. It was absent from the ExAC database and from 50 healthy individuals from northern India. In patient fibroblasts, the mutation led to abnormal transcripts skipping exon 6, exons 5 and 6, or exons 4 through 6, along with residual normal transcript. Patient fibroblasts also showed reduced IFT57 mRNA, decreased IFT57 staining at the ciliary tip, and impaired GLI1 induction after Smoothened agonist stimulation. Thevenon et al. (2016) concluded that the mutation was hypomorphic.

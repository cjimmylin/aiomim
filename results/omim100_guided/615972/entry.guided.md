### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that nanophthalmos-4 (NNO4) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TMEM98 gene on chromosome 17.

### Description

Nanophthalmos is a congenital disorder in which the eye is small but otherwise structurally normal. The main complications are severe hyperopia and angle-closure glaucoma (Awadalla et al., 2014). Khorram et al. (2015) noted that patients with nanophthalmos also have a higher risk of complications after surgery. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Yu et al. (2009) studied a 5-generation Chinese family with simple microphthalmia. The 9 affected members were aged 6 to 66 years (mean, 42.8 years) and had bilateral isolated microphthalmia from birth, with no other physical anomalies. Hyperopia ranged from +4.50 to +11.50 diopters (mean, +8.00 diopters) and axial length from 18.22 to 20.26 mm (mean, 19.29 mm). Best corrected visual acuity was less than 0.5 in most eyes. Three patients, all older than 50 years, had angle-closure glaucoma or occludable anterior chamber angles. Their disease continued to progress after trabeculectomy, and 2 developed atrophia bulbi despite repeated surgeries. The authors also observed learning disabilities in one-third of cases. On histology, the sclera contained diffusely swollen, twisted, or frayed collagen fibers mixed with normal fibers. In the 2 atrophied globes, collagen alignment was disrupted, the sclera was 3 times normal thickness, the cornea was thickened, pigment cells had proliferated, and bone-like tissue had formed. Karyotypes were normal.

Awadalla et al. (2014) studied a large white family (NNO-SA1) from South Australia with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos, defined as an axial length less than 20.0 mm and/or a refractive error greater than +7.00. Of the 35 family members available for study, 16 had nanophthalmos. Their mean refraction was +11.8 D and their mean axial length was 17.6 mm.

Khorram et al. (2015) studied 2 families with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos spanning at least 4 generations. The first was a white family of German heritage living in the United States (pedigree 991-F). Six of the 10 members who were examined had nanophthalmos, and 3 more were affected by family history report. Affected members had a mean axial length of 17.1 +/- 0.31 mm and spherical equivalent refractions of +13.0 +/- 0.89 diopters. The second was a Pacific Islander family of Micronesian heritage from the Mariana Islands (pedigree 951-N). Eight of the 10 members who were examined had nanophthalmos, with a mean axial length of 17.4 +/- 0.94 mm compared with 22.7 +/- 0.38 mm in unaffected members, and spherical equivalent refractions of +3.93 +/- 1.57 diopters. Across the 2 families, axial lengths in affected individuals ranged from 15.9 to 18.4 mm.

### Inheritance

Yu et al. (2009) found autosomal dominant inheritance of simple microphthalmia in a 5-generation Chinese family. Awadalla et al. (2014) and Khorram et al. (2015) also reported families with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos.

### Mapping

In the Chinese family with autosomal dominant microphthalmia, Yu et al. (2009) used two-point linkage analysis with 23 microsatellite markers. All LOD scores were less than 1, which excluded linkage to the candidate genes CHX10, PAX6, and MFRP and to loci on chromosomes 14q32, 11p, and 2q11-q14.

Hu et al. (2011) studied a Chinese family with autosomal dominant congenital simple microphthalmia. After excluding reported candidate loci, they performed a whole-genome scan with 382 microsatellite markers. The maximum LOD score was 4.97 at D17S1824 (theta = 0.00). Haplotype analysis placed the disease gene in a 21.57-cM interval between D17S900 and D17S1872 on chromosome 17p12-q12. Screening of 14 candidate genes in the interval, including CRYBA1 and NCOR1, found no mutation, and genome-wide SNP genotyping found no pathogenic copy number variation. ⚠︎

In a large white family with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos, Awadalla et al. (2014) found significant linkage to chromosome 17 between SNPs rs2323659 and rs967293, with a maximum location score of 4.1.

In 2 families with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos, Khorram et al. (2015) found overlapping segments of chromosome 17p12-q12 that cosegregated with the disease. In the US family the segment was 20.2 Mb, bounded by rs530664 and rs16523. In the Mariana Islands family it was 19.7 Mb, bounded by rs917541 and rs16523.

### Molecular Genetics

In a large white family with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos linked to chromosome 17p12-q12, Awadalla et al. (2014) performed whole-exome sequencing. They found a single novel missense variant in the linked region that segregated with the disease: a c.577G-C change in exon 8 of the TMEM98 gene, predicting an ala193-to-pro (A193P) substitution. The variant was absent from the Exome Variant Server database and from 285 local white controls. TMEM98 was expressed in all ocular tissues tested, including the sclera and optic nerve head.

In affected members of a US family with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos, Khorram et al. (2015) identified a his196-to-pro (H196P) mutation in the TMEM98 gene. They found it by exome sequencing within the regions linked to the disease and confirmed it by Sanger sequencing. In affected members of a family from the Mariana Islands, they identified a heterozygous 34-bp deletion in TMEM98 that spans the 3-prime end of exon 4 and the start of intron 4. In each family the mutation segregated with the disease and was absent from healthy family members. Neither mutation was found in the Exome Variant Server or 1000 Genomes Project databases.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 NANOPHTHALMOS 4**  
TMEM98, ALA193PRO [c.577G>C; p.Ala193Pro]

Awadalla et al. (2014) studied a large white family with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos (NNO4). In affected members they identified a c.577G-C change in exon 8 of the TMEM98 gene, resulting in an ala193-to-pro (A193P) substitution. The variant was found by whole-exome sequencing within the region linked to the disease on chromosome 17. It segregated with the disease and was absent from the Exome Variant Server database and from 285 local white controls.

**.0002 NANOPHTHALMOS 4**  
TMEM98, HIS196PRO [p.His196Pro]

Khorram et al. (2015) studied a US family of German heritage with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos (NNO4). In affected members they identified a his196-to-pro (H196P) mutation in the TMEM98 gene. The mutation was found by exome sequencing within the linked chromosome 17 region and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. It segregated with the disease, was absent from healthy family members, and was not present in the Exome Variant Server or 1000 Genomes Project databases. The altered histidine is highly conserved across species, and SIFT, PolyPhen-2, and BLOSUM62 all predicted the change to be pathogenic.

**.0003 NANOPHTHALMOS 4**  
TMEM98, 34-BP DEL, NT694 [c.694_721delAGAATGAAGACTGGATCGAAGATGCCTCgtaagg]

Khorram et al. (2015) studied a Pacific Islander family from the Mariana Islands with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos (NNO4). Sanger sequencing identified a heterozygous 34-bp deletion in the TMEM98 gene (c.694_721delAGAATGAAGACTGGATCGAAGATGCCTCgtaagg) in all affected family members. The deletion spans the last 28 bp of exon 4 and the first 6 bp of intron 4. It removes a splice site and results in premature termination of translation. The mutation segregated with the disease, was absent from healthy family members, and was not present in public sequence databases.

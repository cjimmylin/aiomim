### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that early-onset seizures with neurodegeneration and brain calcifications (SENEBAC) is caused by homozygous or biallelic mutation in the NRROS gene.

### Description

Early-onset seizures with neurodegeneration and brain calcifications (SENEBAC) is a severe neurodegenerative disorder. Development is normal or mildly delayed at first. Refractory seizures begin in infancy or early childhood and are followed by developmental regression. Neuroimaging shows intracranial calcification, reduced white matter volume with delayed myelination, and progressive cerebral atrophy, and affected children have died in early childhood (Smith et al., 2020; Dong et al., 2020).

### Clinical Features

Smith et al. (2020) reported 3 patients from 2 families. Two sisters (P1 and P2) were born to nonconsanguineous parents of Maori descent. The third patient (P3), a boy, was born to first-cousin South Asian parents. All 3 children were born after a normal pregnancy and delivery, and their early development was unremarkable. In the second year of life, they developed refractory seizures and neurodegeneration and died between the ages of 27 and 36 months. Metabolic testing, including for mitochondrial dysfunction, was noncontributory. Early neuroimaging showed fine calcification at the depths of the cerebral gyri with normal white matter. CT showed widespread spot and linear calcification in the deep and subcortical white matter. As the disease progressed, repeat imaging showed increased calcification, severe generalized atrophy with ventricular dilatation, and diffuse signal changes in the cerebral and cerebellar white matter. Postmortem examination of P3 found abnormalities confined to the CNS, with significant cerebral atrophy. There was widespread neuronal loss with reactive gliosis throughout the grey matter, and focal calcification was present in the neuropil. The most striking finding was an accumulation of foamy macrophages, mainly in a perivascular distribution, throughout the cerebral and cerebellar white matter and the descending corticospinal pathways. These cells were immunoreactive for CD68, MHC class II, and p22phox but not for CD163, IBA1, NRROS, P2Y12, or TMEM119. Myelin basic protein expression in the white matter was reduced, although U fibers were preserved, and occasional axonal spheroids were seen. Parenchymal microglia were markedly abnormal, with loss of P2Y12 staining and weak TMEM119 immunoreactivity.

Dong et al. (2020) reported 6 individuals from 4 families who had neurodegenerative disease with refractory epilepsy, developmental regression, and reduced white matter volume with delayed myelination. Development was normal or mildly delayed at first. Seizures began within the first year of life and were followed by developmental regression. Intracranial calcification was detected in 3 individuals.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of SENEBAC in the families reported by Smith et al. (2020) and Dong et al. (2020) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Smith et al. (2020) found homozygous mutations, including in a child of first-cousin parents, and Dong et al. (2020) found biallelic variants. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In 3 patients from 2 unrelated families with SENEBAC, Smith et al. (2020) used exome sequencing to identify homozygous mutations in the NRROS gene. The 2 affected Maori sisters carried a c.1777C-T nonsense mutation (Q593X). The South Asian boy carried a 1-bp deletion, c.1257del, which was predicted to cause a frameshift (Gly420AlafsTer14). Both mutations were predicted to produce a truncated protein. The Q593X mutation had not been previously recorded, and the c.1257del variant had been reported on only 1 of 251,438 alleles in gnomAD. No cellular material from the patients was available for functional studies. In transcriptomic datasets, NRROS expression was enriched more than 50-fold in human microglia compared with whole brain.

In 6 individuals from 4 families with SENEBAC, Dong et al. (2020) identified biallelic NRROS variants affecting 2 significant functional domains of the protein. The mutant NRROS proteins co-localized with latent TGF-beta-1 (TGFB1) inside HEK293T cells. However, flow cytometry showed that the mutant proteins failed to anchor latent TGF-beta-1 at the cell surface, unlike wildtype NRROS. Wildtype NRROS rescued this defect. The authors concluded that loss of NRROS function causes the disorder.

### Animal Model

Wong et al. (2017) found that Nrros-deficient mice developed spontaneous neurological disorders, with motor defects and death before 6 months of age. The mice showed astrogliosis and lacked normal CD11b-hi/CD45-lo microglia, but they had no detectable demyelination or neuronal loss. Instead, perivascular macrophage-like myeloid cells populated the CNS. Cx3cr1-driven deletion of Nrros showed that the gene is crucial for microglial establishment during early embryonic stages. NRROS was required for normal expression of Sall1 and other microglial genes.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 SEIZURES, EARLY-ONSET, WITH NEURODEGENERATION AND BRAIN CALCIFICATIONS**  
NRROS, GLN593TER [c.1777C>T; p.Gln593Ter]

In 2 sisters of Maori descent with SENEBAC, born to nonconsanguineous parents, Smith et al. (2020) used exome sequencing to identify a homozygous c.1777C-T transition in the NRROS gene, resulting in a gln593-to-ter (Q593X) substitution. The mutation was predicted to produce a truncated protein and had not been previously recorded.

**.0002 SEIZURES, EARLY-ONSET, WITH NEURODEGENERATION AND BRAIN CALCIFICATIONS**  
NRROS, 1-BP DEL, 1257 [c.1257del; p.Gly420AlafsTer14]

In a boy with SENEBAC, born to first-cousin South Asian parents, Smith et al. (2020) used exome sequencing to identify a homozygous 1-bp deletion (c.1257del) in the NRROS gene. The deletion was predicted to cause a frameshift and premature termination (Gly420AlafsTer14), producing a truncated protein. The variant had been reported on 1 of 251,438 alleles in gnomAD. Postmortem examination showed foamy perivascular macrophages throughout the white matter and marked abnormalities of the parenchymal microglia.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that osteogenesis imperfecta type X (OI10) is caused by homozygous mutation in the SERPINH1 gene, which encodes the collagen chaperone protein HSP47.

### Description

Osteogenesis imperfecta type X (OI10) is an autosomal recessive form of OI caused by mutation in SERPINH1, which encodes the type I procollagen chaperone HSP47 (Christiansen et al., 2010). Duran et al. (2015) stated that mutations in SERPINH1 mostly cause a moderately severe form of OI. The severe phenotype reported by Christiansen et al. (2010) was described as severe recessive OI.

### Inheritance

Christiansen et al. (2010) found that OI10 caused by a missense mutation in SERPINH1 followed autosomal recessive inheritance, with the affected individual homozygous for the mutation.

### Molecular Genetics

In a patient with severe OI, Christiansen et al. (2010) identified a homozygous missense mutation in the SERPINH1 gene (c.233T-C; leu78-to-pro, L78P). The mutation caused degradation of HSP47, an endoplasmic reticulum resident protein, through the proteasome. In fibroblasts from the affected individual, type I procollagen built up in the Golgi, and part of the secreted type I procollagen was protease sensitive. Prolyl residue 986 in the triple helical domain of proalpha1(I) chains showed normal 3-hydroxylation. The authors concluded that HSP47 functions downstream of the CRTAP/P3H1/CyPB complex that carries out prolyl 3-hydroxylation.

Duran et al. (2015) identified a new OI mutation in SERPINH1 that destabilized and mislocalized HSP47. The mutation had similar secondary effects on FKBP65, the type I procollagen chaperone encoded by FKBP10. In cells with mutant HSP47, HSP47 and FKBP65 did not interact properly.

### Pathogenesis

Christiansen et al. (2010) proposed that HSP47 checks the integrity of the type I procollagen triple helix at the ER/cis-Golgi boundary. In their model, when HSP47 is absent, procollagen moves from the ER to the Golgi faster than normal and its helical structure is compromised.

Duran et al. (2015) found evidence that HSP47 and FKBP65 work together during posttranslational maturation of type I procollagen. They concluded that OI caused by HSP47 deficiency and OI caused by FKBP65 deficiency share a common cellular pathway.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 OSTEOGENESIS IMPERFECTA, TYPE X**  
SERPINH1, LEU78PRO [c.233T>C; p.Leu78Pro]

In a patient with severe autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta type X (OI10), Christiansen et al. (2010) identified a homozygous c.233T-C transition in the SERPINH1 gene, resulting in a leu78-to-pro (L78P) substitution. The mutant HSP47 protein was degraded by the proteasome. In the patient's fibroblasts, type I procollagen accumulated in the Golgi, and a fraction of the secreted type I procollagen was protease sensitive.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal dominant distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-11 (HMND11) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the SPTAN1 gene, which encodes alpha-II spectrin.

### Description

Distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-11 (HMND11) is an autosomal dominant inherited peripheral neuropathy with juvenile onset. It is marked by length-dependent axonal degeneration of lower motor neurons without significant involvement of sensory neurons. Penetrance is variable, and severity differs greatly between patients (Beijer et al., 2019).

### Clinical Features

Beijer et al. (2019) reported 3 unrelated families with autosomal dominant juvenile-onset hereditary motor neuropathy. Phenotype severity differed greatly between patients.

Dong et al. (2021) reported a Chinese family with hereditary motor neuropathy.

### Inheritance

Beijer et al. (2019) found autosomal dominant inheritance of HMND11 in 3 families. Penetrance was variable in 2 of the 3 families. Dong et al. (2021) reported a de novo SPTAN1 nonsense variant in a Chinese family with hereditary motor neuropathy.

### Heterogeneity

Ylikallio et al. (2020) reported a de novo SPTAN1 mutation in a patient with axonal sensorimotor neuropathy and a developmental disorder. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Beijer et al. (2019) used next-generation sequencing to identify heterozygous nonsense mutations in the SPTAN1 gene in affected members of 3 unrelated families with dominant hereditary motor neuropathy. Mutant mRNA carrying the nonsense mutations was broken down by nonsense-mediated decay, and protein levels in patient cells were reduced. The authors noted that dominant-negative SPTAN1 mutations had been described earlier as causing a range of epilepsy phenotypes.

Dong et al. (2021) identified a novel de novo nonsense variant in the SPTAN1 gene in a Chinese family with hereditary motor neuropathy.

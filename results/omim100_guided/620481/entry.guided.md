### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia-2 (CAMT2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the gene encoding thrombopoietin (THPO) on chromosome 3.

### Description

Congenital amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia-2 (CAMT2) is an autosomal recessive disorder with severe thrombocytopenia and reduced bone marrow megakaryocytes. In some patients it progresses to trilineage bone marrow failure in childhood. Serum thrombopoietin is absent or lower than expected. Bone marrow failure caused by THPO mutation did not respond to hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, but hematopoiesis improved with thrombopoietin receptor agonists (Seo et al., 2017; Pecci et al., 2018; Capaci et al., 2023). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Dasouki et al. (2013) reported 2 sibs from a Micronesian family with idiopathic, autosomal recessive aplastic anemia. Pecci et al. (2018) noted that in these 2 patients, bone marrow aplasia had been recognized at ages 16 and 28 years. Two other sibs had died of aplastic anemia in adolescence. Dasouki et al. (2013) found that the parents and a heterozygous sib had reduced platelet counts.

Seo et al. (2017) reported 5 individuals from 3 unrelated families with severe thrombocytopenia that progressed to trilineage bone marrow failure. Four of the children received hematopoietic stem cell transplants. All 4 had poor graft function, with severe cytopenias that persisted even after repeated transplants from different donors. Pecci et al. (2018) noted that the 3 families were consanguineous, and that 3 of the children died early after transplantation.

Pecci et al. (2018) reported 3 children with CAMT born to Egyptian parents who were first cousins. The proband, a boy, presented at age 3.5 years with ecchymotic patches. He had very severe thrombocytopenia (platelets 3 x 10(9)/l) with normal platelet size, hyporegenerative anemia, and severe neutropenia. Bone marrow was hypocellular, with very rare immature megakaryocytes. He went on to have recurrent major hemorrhages (epistaxis and gum and rectal bleeding) and recurrent severe infections (febrile neutropenia or otitis media). His brother had severe isolated thrombocytopenia at age 7 months, with a markedly decreased number of bone marrow megakaryocytes. Recurrent spontaneous bleeding began in the brother at age 1.6 years. A younger sister developed a hematoma at a vaccination site at age 2 months and had anemia and severe thrombocytopenia. The parents had normal blood counts. Before treatment, serum THPO levels in the 2 older sibs were within the normal range (10.9 and 14.1 pg/ml). The authors considered these levels inappropriately low, because serum THPO is markedly increased in bone marrow aplasia. They noted that the oldest sib had trilineage bone marrow hypoplasia, which suggested that the hematologic picture worsens with age.

Capaci et al. (2023) reported a 3.5-year-old boy with CAMT born to Muslim Palestinian parents who were first cousins. He presented with easy bruising and thrombocytopenia (platelet count 21 x 10(9)/L) with a normal mean platelet volume. His platelet count had been 70 x 10(9)/L shortly after birth and 20 x 10(9)/L at age 2 years. Apart from mild occasional neutropenia, he had no other cytopenias. Bone marrow from 2 biopsies was normocellular, with severe hypoplasia of megakaryocytes, a few small immature forms, and mild dyserythropoiesis. His serum THPO concentration was 126 pg/mL (reference range 15-80 pg/mL for children). The authors considered this much lower than expected given the reduced megakaryocyte and platelet mass. His mother had mild thrombocytopenia, with platelet counts stable around 80 x 10(9)/L. A younger sib had a platelet count of 130 x 10(9)/L around birth.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CAMT2 in the family reported by Pecci et al. (2018) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance: the affected sibs were homozygous for a THPO mutation and the unaffected parents were heterozygous. Heterozygous carriers can have mild thrombocytopenia. Dasouki et al. (2013) found reduced platelet counts in heterozygous family members, and Capaci et al. (2023) found asymptomatic mild thrombocytopenia in a heterozygous mother and sib.

### Molecular Genetics

In 2 sibs from a Micronesian family with autosomal recessive aplastic anemia, Dasouki et al. (2013) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a homozygous c.112C-T mutation in the THPO gene. The mutation results in an arg38-to-cys (R38C) substitution, or R17C when the 21-amino acid signal peptide is excluded, in the receptor-binding domain. In silico modeling predicted that the extra cysteine would disrupt normal disulfide bonding in this domain. In functional assays, media containing the mutant THPO had a 2- to 3-fold reduced ability to sustain proliferation of THPO-dependent UT7-TPO cells. A sib with wildtype sequence had a normal platelet count. The authors concluded that R17C is a partial loss-of-function allele.

In 5 patients from 3 unrelated families with severe thrombocytopenia progressing to bone marrow failure, Seo et al. (2017) used exome and targeted sequencing to identify homozygous mutations in the THPO gene. Affected children in 2 families were homozygous for an R99W mutation, and the affected child in the third family was homozygous for an R157X mutation. Both mutations resulted in a lack of THPO in the patients' serum. The authors concluded that biallelic loss-of-function mutations in THPO cause bone marrow failure that does not respond to transplantation because the mechanism is extrinsic to hematopoietic cells. ⚠︎

In 3 sibs from a consanguineous Egyptian family with CAMT2, Pecci et al. (2018) identified a homozygous c.355C-T transition in the THPO gene, resulting in an arg119-to-cys (R119C) substitution. The variant was found by sequencing of MPL and THPO in the proband, and no MPL variants were found. Both parents were heterozygous. The variant was not present in the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes Project, or ExAC databases. R119 lies in helix C of the receptor-binding domain and is highly conserved, including in erythropoietin. HEK293T cells transfected with R119C or R38C expressed the mutant proteins at levels similar to or slightly higher than wildtype, and the mutant proteins had normal stability. However, THPO concentrations in the culture supernatants were about 23-fold lower for both mutants, indicating defective secretion. At equal THPO concentrations, R119C supported UT7-TPO cell proliferation at 45 to 53% of wildtype levels. Both R119C and R38C caused defective activation of STAT5, ERK1/2, and AKT downstream of MPL.

In a boy with CAMT2 born to consanguineous Palestinian parents, Capaci et al. (2023) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a homozygous c.-323C-T substitution (NM_000460.2) in a regulatory region of the THPO gene. In transcript variant 1, this change is 45 nucleotides upstream of the transcription start site. Both parents and a younger sib were heterozygous, and 2 healthy sibs were homozygous for the wildtype allele. The variant was listed in dbSNP but not in gnomAD, and the surrounding region was conserved across species. No variants were found in MPL, MECOM, HOXA11, RBM8A, or genes associated with other inherited bone marrow failure syndromes. The nucleotide was predicted to lie within consensus binding sites for ETS1 and STAT4. In HEK293T cells, the mutation reduced promoter-driven luciferase reporter activity by almost 80%. Chromatin immunoprecipitation showed reduced binding of the transcription factors to the mutant promoter, particularly STAT4. Knockdown of ETS1 and STAT4 in HepG2 cells reduced THPO mRNA and protein levels.

### Diagnosis

Pecci et al. (2018) and Capaci et al. (2023) noted that serum THPO is markedly increased in CAMT due to MPL mutations and in other forms of bone marrow aplasia. By contrast, serum THPO was normal or low in patients with THPO mutations. Both groups suggested that serum THPO measurement could help distinguish patients with THPO mutations. Capaci et al. (2023) recommended that molecular analysis of THPO, including its regulatory regions, be included in the workup of suspected CAMT.

### Clinical Management

Seo et al. (2017) found that the 2 surviving patients in their study had improved trilineage hematopoiesis after treatment with a THPO receptor agonist.

Pecci et al. (2018) treated all 3 affected sibs with romiplostim. Treatment produced trilineage hematologic responses, remission of bleeding and infections, and transfusion independence, which were maintained for up to 6.5 years. The proband had not responded to earlier treatment with cyclosporine A or oxymetholone.

Capaci et al. (2023) treated their patient with eltrombopag. After 6 months at 2.8 mg/kg/day, his platelet count had risen to 125 x 10(9)/L and his easy bruising had resolved.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Hoxha-Aliu syndrome (HXAL) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous null mutation in the ERI1 gene on chromosome 8p23. ⚠︎

### Description

Hoxha-Aliu syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic null variants in ERI1. Its main features are mild intellectual disability and anomalies of the hands and feet, such as brachydactyly, clinodactyly, short metacarpals, brachytelephalangy, dysplastic or short nails of the halluces, and toe syndactyly (Hoxha and Aliu, 2023; Guo et al., 2023). Biallelic ERI1 missense variants cause a different, more severe phenotype, spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia (Guo et al., 2023).

### Clinical Features

Choucair et al. (2017) reported a boy, born to consanguineous parents, who had intellectual disability, short stature, dysmorphic facial features, synpolydactyly, and cardiac malformations.

Szot et al. (2021) reported a girl (F5.II.1) who was homozygous for a missense variant in the KYNU gene and also carried a homozygous ERI1 nonsense variant (see MOLECULAR GENETICS). She had microcephaly, global developmental delay, and autism. Her craniofacial features included brachycephaly, a flat occiput, bitemporal narrowing, normoteloric eyes with normal eyebrows, a small nose with a normal philtrum, and cupped, prominent ears. Her limbs showed reduction defects. The right hand had a transverse terminal limb reduction with a rudimentary fifth digit. The left hand had hypoplastic, deep-set nails on digits 1, 2, 3, and 5. On the right foot, digits 1 and 5 had no nails and digits 2 to 4 were absent. On the left foot, digits 3 to 5 were absent (no bones), digits 1 and 2 had no nails, and there was syndactyly. She also had a perimembranous ventricular septal defect, pulmonary vein stenosis, and bilateral inguinal hernias. Szot et al. (2021) noted that a previously reported patient with a homozygous microdeletion including ERI1 and MFHAS1 had limb and cardiac abnormalities similar to this patient's. They suggested that the ERI1 variant might confound the contribution of the mild NAD deficiency attributed to the KYNU variant.

Hoxha and Aliu (2023) reported a girl with mild intellectual disability, eyelid ptosis, and anomalies of the hands and feet. The anomalies were brachydactyly, clinodactyly, dysplastic or short nails of the halluces, brachytelephalangy, short metacarpals, and toe syndactyly. ⚠︎

Guo et al. (2023) reported 8 affected individuals from 7 unrelated families with biallelic ERI1 variants. The individuals with biallelic null variants had mild intellectual disability and digital anomalies, without spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia. In contrast, 5 individuals with missense variants had severe spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of Hoxha-Aliu syndrome is autosomal recessive. This was shown by the homozygous ERI1 nonsense variant in the patient reported by Hoxha and Aliu (2023) and by the biallelic ERI1 null variants reported by Guo et al. (2023).

### Cytogenetics

Choucair et al. (2017) used chromosomal microarray analysis in a boy with intellectual disability, limb abnormalities, and cardiac malformation, born to consanguineous parents. They found a homozygous deletion at 8p23.1 that contained the microRNA miR-4660 and the ERI1 and MFHAS1 genes. The authors discussed how each deleted gene might contribute to the patient's features. They noted that ERI1 encodes a ribonuclease involved in RNA metabolism that is required for correct patterning of the skeleton by defining HOXC8 expression.

### Molecular Genetics

Szot et al. (2021) performed trio exome or genome sequencing in a girl (F5.II.1) with multiple malformations. She was homozygous for a missense variant in the KYNU gene (E206K). She also carried a homozygous loss-of-function variant in the ERI1 gene, a c.514C-T transition (NM_153332.4) resulting in a gln172-to-ter (Q172X) substitution. In a yeast complementation assay, the KYNU variant caused only a relatively mild reduction in NAD levels. The authors suggested that the ERI1 variant might partly explain the patient's distinct phenotype.

In a girl with mild intellectual disability, eyelid ptosis, and anomalies of the hands and feet, Hoxha and Aliu (2023) identified a homozygous nonsense variant in the ERI1 gene. ⚠︎

Guo et al. (2023) identified biallelic ERI1 variants in 8 affected individuals from 7 unrelated families. Individuals with biallelic null variants had mild intellectual disability and digital anomalies.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Guo et al. (2023) found a phenotypic dichotomy associated with biallelic ERI1 variants. Five affected individuals with missense variants had severe spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia. Individuals with biallelic null variants did not have this dysplasia and instead showed mild intellectual disability and digital anomalies. The missense variants caused loss of exoribonuclease activity, which led to defective trimming of the 3-prime end of 5.8S rRNA and decreased degradation of replication-dependent histone mRNAs. Induced pluripotent stem cells derived from affected individuals showed impaired in vitro chondrogenesis, with downregulation of genes that regulate skeletal patterning. Guo et al. (2023) concluded that, within recessive genotypes, ERI1 missense alleles had a more severe effect than null alleles.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 HOXHA-ALIU SYNDROME**  
ERI1, GLN172TER [NM_153332.4:c.514C>T; p.Gln172Ter]

Szot et al. (2021) used trio exome or genome sequencing to study a girl (F5.II.1) who had microcephaly, global developmental delay, autism, distal limb reduction defects with absent digits and nails, syndactyly, a ventricular septal defect, and pulmonary vein stenosis. She carried a homozygous c.514C-T transition (NM_153332.4) in the ERI1 gene, resulting in a gln172-to-ter (Q172X) substitution. She was also homozygous for a missense variant in the KYNU gene (E206K). The authors suggested that the ERI1 loss-of-function variant might partly explain her phenotype.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Hoxha-Aliu syndrome (HXAL) is caused by homozygous mutation in the ERI1 gene on chromosome 8p23.1.

### Description

Hoxha-Aliu syndrome (HXAL) is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic null variants in the ERI1 gene. Its main features are mild intellectual disability and anomalies of the hands and feet, including brachydactyly, clinodactyly, short metacarpals, dysplastic nails, and toe syndactyly (Hoxha and Aliu, 2023; Guo et al., 2023). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Choucair et al. (2017) reported a boy born to consanguineous parents who had intellectual disability, short stature, dysmorphic facial features, synpolydactyly, and cardiac malformations.

Szot et al. (2021) reported a girl (F5.II.1) who had microcephaly, global developmental delay, and autism. Her other features included brachycephaly, a flat occiput, bitemporal narrowing, and cupped, prominent ears. She had limb reduction defects: a transverse terminal limb reduction of the right hand with a rudimentary fifth digit, hypoplastic deep-set nails, absent toes, absent nails on the toes, and syndactyly. She also had a perimembranous ventricular septal defect, pulmonary vein stenosis, and bilateral inguinal hernias. In addition to the ERI1 variant (see MOLECULAR GENETICS), she carried a homozygous missense variant in the KYNU gene.

Hoxha and Aliu (2023) reported a female patient who had mild intellectual disability and eyelid ptosis. Her hand and foot anomalies included brachydactyly, clinodactyly, dysplastic or short nails of the halluces, brachytelephalangy, short metacarpals, and toe syndactyly.

Guo et al. (2023) reported 8 affected individuals from 7 unrelated families with biallelic ERI1 variants. The individuals with biallelic null variants had mild intellectual disability and digital anomalies but did not have spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia.

### Inheritance

Hoxha and Aliu (2023) described the disorder as autosomal recessive; their patient was homozygous for a nonsense variant in the ERI1 gene. Choucair et al. (2017) found a homozygous deletion that included ERI1 in a patient born to consanguineous parents.

### Cytogenetics

In a boy with intellectual disability, synpolydactyly, and cardiac malformations who was born to consanguineous parents, Choucair et al. (2017) used chromosomal microarray analysis and found a homozygous deletion at chromosome 8p23.1. The deletion contained the microRNA miR-4660 and the ERI1 and MFHAS1 genes.

### Molecular Genetics

Szot et al. (2021) performed trio exome or genome sequencing in a girl (F5.II.1) who had limb reduction defects, cardiac anomalies, microcephaly, and developmental delay. She was homozygous for a loss-of-function variant in the ERI1 gene, c.514C-T (NM_153332.4), which results in a gln172-to-ter (Q172X) substitution. She was also homozygous for a missense variant in the KYNU gene. The authors noted that her limb and cardiac abnormalities resembled those of the patient with a homozygous ERI1-MFHAS1 microdeletion reported by Choucair et al. (2017). They also stated that the functional link between ERI1 mutation and these defects remained to be assessed.

In a female patient with HXAL, Hoxha and Aliu (2023) identified a homozygous nonsense variant in the ERI1 gene.

Guo et al. (2023) identified biallelic ERI1 variants in 8 affected individuals from 7 unrelated families. Individuals with biallelic null variants had mild intellectual disability and digital anomalies.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Guo et al. (2023) described 2 distinct phenotypes caused by biallelic ERI1 variants. Five affected individuals with missense variants had severe spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia (SEMD). Individuals with biallelic null variants did not have SEMD; they had mild intellectual disability and digital anomalies. The missense variants caused loss of exoribonuclease activity, which led to defective trimming of the 5.8S rRNA 3-prime end and decreased degradation of replication-dependent histone mRNAs. Induced pluripotent stem cells derived from affected individuals showed impaired in vitro chondrogenesis, with downregulation of genes that regulate skeletal patterning. The authors concluded that, within recessive genotypes, the missense alleles had a more severe effect than the null alleles.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 HOXHA-ALIU SYNDROME**  
ERI1, GLN172TER [NM_153332.4:c.514C>T; p.Gln172Ter]

Szot et al. (2021) performed trio exome or genome sequencing in a girl (F5.II.1) and found that she was homozygous for a c.514C-T transition (NM_153332.4) in the ERI1 gene. The variant results in a gln172-to-ter (Q172X) substitution and is predicted to cause loss of function. She had microcephaly, global developmental delay, autism, limb reduction defects, a ventricular septal defect, pulmonary vein stenosis, and bilateral inguinal hernias. She was also homozygous for a missense variant in the KYNU gene.

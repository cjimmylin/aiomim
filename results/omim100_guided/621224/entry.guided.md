### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that ectodermal dysplasia-17 with or without limb malformations (ECTD17) is caused by heterozygous or homozygous mutation in the LEF1 gene on chromosome 4q25. ⚠︎

### Description

Ectodermal dysplasia-17 with or without limb malformations (ECTD17) is a disorder in which ectodermal dysplasia occurs with or without limb malformations. Ectodermal features include severe oligodontia of the primary and permanent teeth, hypotrichosis, and hypohidrosis (Lévy et al., 2020). Limb malformations include radial ray defects, polydactyly, and split hand/foot. Depending on the type and location of the LEF1 variant, the disorder can be inherited in an autosomal dominant or an autosomal recessive pattern (Dufour et al., 2022). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Lévy et al. (2020) reported 2 unrelated patients with a phenotype consistent with hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia. Both had severe oligodontia of the primary and permanent teeth, hypotrichosis, and hypohidrosis. Both also had taurodontism and a particular alveolar bone defect.

Dufour et al. (2022) reported 12 individuals with LEF1 variants: 11 affected individuals from 4 unrelated families and 1 affected individual from a consanguineous family. The phenotypic spectrum included ectodermal dysplasia and various limb malformations, such as radial ray defects, polydactyly, and split hand/foot.

Hassan et al. (2024) reported a family with hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia related to LEF1.

### Inheritance

Dufour et al. (2022) found that ECTD17 can be inherited in an autosomal dominant or an autosomal recessive pattern, depending on the type and location of the LEF1 variant. They identified monoallelic variants in 4 families and a biallelic variant in 1 consanguineous family.

### Cytogenetics

In 2 unrelated patients with ECTD17, Lévy et al. (2020) identified de novo deletions at chromosome 4q25 that included the LEF1 gene. They concluded that LEF1 haploinsufficiency causes ectodermal dysplasia.

### Molecular Genetics

Using high-throughput sequencing, Dufour et al. (2022) found monoallelic LEF1 variants in 11 affected individuals from 4 unrelated families with ECTD17. They found a biallelic LEF1 variant in an affected individual from a consanguineous family. The authors studied the cellular consequences using immunofluorescence, immunoblotting, pulldown assays, and/or RNA sequencing. The functional data pointed to 2 molecular mechanisms. Haploinsufficiency or loss of DNA binding was associated with a mild to moderate phenotype. Loss of beta-catenin binding caused by biallelic variants was associated with a severe phenotype. Transcriptomic studies showed altered WNT signaling.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Dufour et al. (2022) found that haploinsufficiency of LEF1 or loss of its DNA binding was associated with a mild to moderate phenotype. In contrast, biallelic variants causing loss of beta-catenin binding were associated with a severe phenotype.

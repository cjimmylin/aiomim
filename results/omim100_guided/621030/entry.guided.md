### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal dominant autoinflammation, panniculitis, and dermatosis syndrome (AIPDSA) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the OTULIN gene.

### Description

Autosomal dominant autoinflammation, panniculitis, and dermatosis syndrome (AIPDSA) is a severe autoinflammatory disorder that begins in the neonatal period or infancy. It is characterized by recurrent fever and systemic inflammation with markedly raised C-reactive protein and neutrophilia, together with sterile neutrophilic skin lesions. The skin lesions include panniculitis, pustulosis at sites of trauma, erythema nodosum, and necrotizing wounds that heal poorly. Some patients also have episodes of acute respiratory distress and growth failure. The disease has responded to corticosteroids and TNF blockade (Davidson et al., 2024; Takeda et al., 2024).

### Clinical Features

Davidson et al. (2024) reported a 22-month-old boy (P1), born to nonconsanguineous Caucasian parents at 31 weeks' gestation. At 4 days of age he developed periumbilical erythema. Surgery showed marked neutrophilic inflammation around a urachal remnant, but no bacteria were cultured. The surgical closure dehisced and left an abdominal wound that failed to close, and he later developed a spontaneous cutaneous duodenal stoma. At 8 weeks of age he had an acute respiratory deterioration with extensive body edema, hypotension, and presumed pulmonary edema, which improved with corticosteroids. He had repeated areas of swelling and erythema consistent with panniculitis. He also had pathergy-like sterile pustular abscesses at injection and cannula sites. Laboratory studies showed CRP of 100 to 200 mg/L, neutrophilia (30-50 x 10(9)/L), monocytosis, eosinophilia, and persistent thrombocytopenia, which continued until inflammation was controlled. He also had an initial hypogammaglobulinemia, which the authors noted may have been related to prematurity. A perilesional skin biopsy showed heavy acute inflammatory exudate with diffuse suppuration and necrosis.

Davidson et al. (2024) also reported a Saudi girl (P2), born at term to nonconsanguineous parents. At 7 months of age, 2 weeks after routine vaccinations, she presented with multiple tender skin lesions on the limbs, fever (38-39 degrees C), and generalized edema. She had leukocytosis (45.5 x 10(9)/L) with neutrophilia and a CRP of 264 mg/L, and cultures were negative. Some lesions progressed to cellulitis-like plaques and then to necrotizing fasciitis with ulceration, which required debridement and fasciotomy. Biopsy showed mixed infiltration of neutrophils and plasma cells with necrosis. At 12 months of age she had a second episode with fever and new tender erythematous plaques on the trunk and lower limbs, and skin biopsy showed panniculitis. She later had recurrent chest infections.

Takeda et al. (2024) reported a 9-year-old Japanese girl, born to nonconsanguineous parents at 33 weeks' gestation. Shortly after birth she developed severe inflammation with neutrophil-dominant leukocytosis and elevated CRP, and no pathogen was found in blood or CSF cultures. At 1 month of age she developed omphalitis refractory to antibiotics that extended to most of the abdominal wall, and she underwent surgical debridement. Histopathology of the debrided tissue showed massive infiltration of leukocytes, mainly neutrophils, with inflammatory granuloma formation and significant cell death. Pustulosis developed at skin puncture and surgical sites, and she had erythema nodosum. From 3 months of age she had 3 episodes of acute respiratory distress syndrome associated with pneumonia and thrombocytopenia. Febrile episodes were accompanied by leukocytosis with neutrophil predominance, thrombocytosis, and CRP levels of 100 to 500 mg/liter. Serum IL-1-beta and IL-6 were elevated, and no autoantibodies were detected. She had severely impaired physical and psychological development, which improved after remission.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous OTULIN mutations in the patients reported by Davidson et al. (2024) and Takeda et al. (2024) occurred de novo, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Davidson et al. (2024) studied 2 unrelated patients with AIPDSA. In P1, trio exome sequencing identified a de novo heterozygous c.386G-C mutation (ENST00000284274.5) in the OTULIN gene, resulting in a cys129-to-ser (C129S) substitution. In P2, whole-genome sequencing identified a de novo heterozygous c.385T-A mutation, resulting in the same C129S substitution. The variant was absent from gnomAD. Cys129 lies in the OTULIN catalytic domain. The C129S protein was as stable as wildtype, but it failed to hydrolyze M1-linked diubiquitin. Patient fibroblasts expressed wildtype and mutant OTULIN in about equal amounts. They also had normal levels of LUBAC components, but they accumulated linear ubiquitin chains. In HEK293T cells, transfection of C129S, but not of wildtype or the loss-of-function variants Y244C and N341D, caused accumulation of linear ubiquitin and induced NF-kappa-B activity, even in the presence of wildtype OTULIN. Patient fibroblasts showed more OTULIN bound to LUBAC, most of it catalytically inactive, and reduced recruitment of LUBAC to the TNFR1 signaling complex. Patient fibroblasts were also more sensitive to TNF-induced cell death, at a level similar to that of fibroblasts from patients with biallelic OTULIN mutations and unlike that of OTULIN-haploinsufficient fibroblasts. This cell death was mainly apoptotic. Patient PBMCs and fibroblasts showed increased IFNB1 and IL6 transcription and an elevated interferon-stimulated gene score. Davidson et al. (2024) concluded that the mutation acts in a dominant-negative manner. ⚠︎

In a Japanese girl with AIPDSA, Takeda et al. (2024) used trio whole-exome sequencing to identify 2 heterozygous variants in the OTULIN gene (NM_138348.6), which cDNA cloning showed were on different alleles. The first was a de novo c.917G-A transition resulting in an arg306-to-gln (R306Q) substitution. The second was a maternally inherited c.455C-T transition resulting in a pro152-to-leu (P152L) substitution. The P152L variant was also present in the patient's healthy sister. It was found in gnomAD (51 of 1,611,754 alleles) and showed deubiquitinase activity equivalent to that of wildtype. The R306Q mutation was found in 1 of 1,611,754 gnomAD alleles. It reduced deubiquitinase activity and binding to linear diubiquitin without lowering OTULIN protein levels. Takeda et al. (2024) made mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) from patient iPS cells and from base-edited iPS cells in which the P152L allele had been corrected to wildtype. Both sets of MSCs showed accumulation of linear ubiquitin and linear autoubiquitination of LUBAC after TNF stimulation, and both were more sensitive to TNF-induced cell death. In contrast, NF-kappa-B activation was not affected. In OTULIN-null cells, R306Q reversed, in a dose-dependent manner, the reduction of linear ubiquitin chains and the rescue of cell death conferred by wildtype or P152L OTULIN. Takeda et al. (2024) concluded that R306Q acts in a dominant-negative manner. ⚠︎

### Clinical Management

In the patients reported by Davidson et al. (2024), inflammation responded to corticosteroids. P1 was treated with adalimumab, which allowed the steroid dose to be reduced. P2 achieved remission on infliximab with methotrexate and was weaned off corticosteroids. Prophylactic antibiotics improved wound healing in P1 and reduced recurrent chest infections in P2. The patient reported by Takeda et al. (2024) achieved complete, steroid-free remission on etanercept, started at 2 years of age.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 AUTOINFLAMMATION, PANNICULITIS, AND DERMATOSIS SYNDROME, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
OTULIN, CYS129SER [ENST00000284274.5:c.386G>C; ENST00000284274.5:c.385T>A; p.Cys129Ser]

In 2 unrelated patients with autosomal dominant autoinflammation, panniculitis, and dermatosis syndrome (AIPDSA), Davidson et al. (2024) identified de novo heterozygous mutations in the OTULIN gene that cause a cys129-to-ser (C129S) substitution in the catalytic domain. The first patient was a Caucasian boy, in whom the mutation was a c.386G-C change found by trio exome sequencing. The second was a Saudi girl, in whom the mutation was a c.385T-A change found by whole-genome sequencing. The variant was absent from gnomAD. The mutant protein was stable but lacked deubiquitinase activity. In transfected cells, it caused accumulation of linear ubiquitin and NF-kappa-B activation despite the presence of wildtype OTULIN. Patient fibroblasts showed more catalytically inactive OTULIN bound to LUBAC, reduced recruitment of LUBAC to the TNFR1 signaling complex, and increased sensitivity to TNF-induced cell death. These findings were consistent with a dominant-negative effect.

**.0002 AUTOINFLAMMATION, PANNICULITIS, AND DERMATOSIS SYNDROME, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
OTULIN, ARG306GLN [NM_138348.6:c.917G>A; p.Arg306Gln]

In a Japanese girl with AIPDSA, Takeda et al. (2024) identified a de novo heterozygous c.917G-A transition in the OTULIN gene, resulting in an arg306-to-gln (R306Q) substitution. The mutation was found by trio whole-exome sequencing. On the other allele, the patient carried a maternally inherited P152L variant, which was also present in her healthy sister and had wildtype deubiquitinase activity. Arg306 is highly conserved and lies close to the active center and the distal ubiquitin recognition site. The R306Q protein had markedly reduced deubiquitinase activity and binding to linear diubiquitin, but normal stability and LUBAC binding. Base-edited patient-derived MSCs carrying only R306Q and a wildtype allele accumulated linear ubiquitin after TNF stimulation and were as sensitive to TNF-induced cell death as the original patient MSCs. R306Q counteracted wildtype OTULIN in a dose-dependent manner, consistent with a dominant-negative effect.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that polymicrogyria with or without vascular-type Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (PMGEDSV) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the COL3A1 gene on chromosome 2q32.

### Description

Polymicrogyria with or without vascular-type Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (PMGEDSV) is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic COL3A1 mutations. Its main feature is a structural brain malformation: bilateral frontoparietal polymicrogyria of the cobblestone type, white matter abnormalities, and cerebellar cysts. These brain findings resemble those seen in patients with GPR56 mutations. Some patients also have severe features of vascular Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, including arterial dissection or rupture, gastrointestinal rupture, thin translucent skin, and characteristic facial features. Developmental delay and seizures are present in some patients, whereas others have normal cognitive function (Jorgensen et al., 2015; Vandervore et al., 2017; Horn et al., 2017).

### Clinical Features

Plancke et al. (2009) reported a girl with recessive Ehlers-Danlos syndrome. She was born to consanguineous, healthy parents and met the criteria for laboratory testing for vascular EDS. Jorgensen et al. (2015) summarized this patient's course. She developed acute abdominal pain, and during surgery to repair a necrotic, perforated jejunum, the surgeons found extreme intestinal arterial and tissue fragility. She died at age 11 years, 6 days after the intervention, from multisystem failure. Besides features of EDS type IV, she had delayed motor development, pulmonary valve stenosis, and absence epilepsy. Brain MRI showed diffuse cortical dysplasia, predominantly frontal, with ventricular dilatation.

Jorgensen et al. (2015) reported a family with marked intrafamilial clinical variation. The male proband was born with right-sided clubfoot and later developed a unilateral hydrocele. He had crowded teeth, retrognathia, and long, slender fingers. He died unexpectedly at 15 years of age after a week of backache. Autopsy showed dissections of the thoracic aorta, the right renal artery, and the left femoral artery, with extensive bleeding and cystic medial necrosis of the aortic wall. His brain was macroscopically normal, but no brain imaging or histologic examination was done. His sister had long, slender fingers with small joint hypermobility. By age 14 years she had crowded teeth, a small lower jaw, translucent skin with visible subcutaneous vessels, and an episode of spontaneous rupture of small superficial vessels. Both sibs had pale, translucent skin and facial features characteristic of EDS type IV: small lower jaw, prominent eyes, pinched nose, and thin upper lip. At 19 years the sister had a dissection of the right internal carotid artery, followed later by a dissection flap in the ectatic left internal carotid artery, as well as stenosis of the right external iliac artery. She also had intermittent abdominal pain with occasional blood in the stool, and migraine with aura. Brain MRI showed cerebral cortical dysplasia with thickened frontoparietal cortices bilaterally and small gyri, consistent with pachy micropolygyria. Her cognitive function and neurologic examination were normal, and she had no seizures. Of the heterozygous relatives, the mother had thin skin, visible subcutaneous vessels, and reduced elasticity of the ascending aorta, but not the complete vascular EDS picture by age 60. The heterozygous father had no abnormal findings at age 63. ⚠︎

Vandervore et al. (2017) studied a family with 2 affected and 2 unaffected sibs. Brain MRI in the affected sibs showed a phenotype similar to that associated with GPR56 mutations: cobblestone-like cortical malformation, white matter changes, and cerebellar cysts. The authors also reviewed brain imaging of the 2 previously reported patients with biallelic COL3A1 mutations (Plancke et al., 2009; Jorgensen et al., 2015) and found a similar brain phenotype. ⚠︎

Horn et al. (2017) reported 2 unrelated families. The first patient was a 3-year-old girl with developmental delay. She was born with bilateral clubfoot, joint laxity, and dysmorphic facial features, and at age 2 years she had an aneurysmal brain hemorrhage. Cerebral MRI showed profound abnormalities, including bilateral frontoparietal polymicrogyria of the cobblestone variant. In the second family, 2 affected sibs had cobblestone-like cortical malformation, cerebellar cysts, white matter abnormalities, developmental delay, and seizures.

### Inheritance

Autosomal recessive inheritance was shown by Plancke et al. (2009) in a girl from a consanguineous family who was homozygous for a COL3A1 mutation; each healthy parent carried the mutation. Jorgensen et al. (2015) supported this pattern in sibs who were compound heterozygous for COL3A1 variants, each inherited from a different parent.

### Molecular Genetics

In a girl with recessive Ehlers-Danlos syndrome born to consanguineous parents, Plancke et al. (2009) sequenced the full COL3A1 cDNA. They found a homozygous 1-bp duplication (c.479dupT) that caused a premature termination codon (p.Lys161GlnfsX45). Each parent carried the mutation. Expression studies (RT-PCR, quantitative PCR, immunohistochemistry, and Western blot) showed strong mRNA decay and no type III collagen in the proband. The healthy heterozygous relatives did not show manifestations of vascular EDS.

In 2 sibs with vascular EDS, one of whom also had bilateral frontoparietal polymicrogyria, Jorgensen et al. (2015) sequenced COL3A1. Both sibs were compound heterozygous for c.1786C-T in exon 26, causing an arg596-to-ter (R596X) substitution, and c.3851G-A in exon 50, causing a gly1284-to-glu (G1284E) substitution in the carboxyl-terminal propeptide. The father carried the nonsense variant and the mother carried the missense variant, confirming that the variants were in trans. An unaffected brother carried neither variant. Several paternal relatives heterozygous for the nonsense variant had no complications or clinical features of vascular EDS. The sister's cultured dermal fibroblasts produced a reduced amount of type III procollagen, and all of its chains had abnormal electrophoretic mobility, consistent with overmodification. Fibroblasts from the father produced a reduced amount of apparently normal type III procollagen. Fibroblasts from the mother produced mostly normal type III procollagen with a small amount of abnormal, subtly overmodified procollagen.

In 2 sibs with cobblestone-like cortical malformation, white matter changes, and cerebellar cysts, Vandervore et al. (2017) used exome analysis to identify a homozygous c.145C-G variant in exon 2 of COL3A1, resulting in a pro49-to-ala (P49A) substitution. Functional assays were done on dermal fibroblasts.

In a 3-year-old girl with polymicrogyria and connective tissue abnormalities, Horn et al. (2017) identified compound heterozygous variants in COL3A1: a nonsense variant (c.1282C-T, R428X) and a frameshift variant (c.2057delC, Pro686LeufsTer105). Both variants were novel. In a second family, Horn et al. (2017) found 2 affected sibs who were homozygous for the P49A missense variant in COL3A1.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Vandervore et al. (2017) found cobblestone-like malformation in all 3 families with homozygous or compound heterozygous COL3A1 mutations reported to that point. They proposed that the variable phenotype across patients reflects genetic alterations in distinct domains of type III procollagen. Horn et al. (2017) noted that families with biallelic COL3A1 variants all had specific structural brain anomalies, whereas only some had a severe Ehlers-Danlos syndrome phenotype.

### Animal Model

Liu et al. (1997) inactivated the Col3a1 gene in mice. About 10% of homozygous mutant mice survived to adulthood, and their life span was much shorter than that of wildtype mice. The major cause of death was rupture of the major blood vessels. Ultrastructural analysis showed that type III collagen is essential for normal collagen I fibrillogenesis in the cardiovascular system and other organs.

Jeong et al. (2012) found that Col3a1-null mouse brains had a cobblestone-like cortical malformation, with breakdown of the pial basement membrane, neuronal overmigration, and marginal zone heterotopias. The pial basement membrane formed normally at embryonic day (E) 10.5. Defects with concurrent neuronal overmigration appeared from E11.5 and were present in all embryos older than E12.5; overall, 23 of 28 null embryos had ectopias, compared with none of the heterozygous or wildtype embryos. The ectopias contained neurons from both deep and superficial cortical layers. The mutant brains also showed abnormal attachment of radial glial endfeet and mispositioned Cajal-Retzius cells and interneurons. Meningeal fibroblasts appeared normal, and the expression and glycosylation of alpha-dystroglycan were unaffected.

**.0003 POLYMICROGYRIA WITH OR WITHOUT VASCULAR-TYPE EHLERS-DANLOS SYNDROME**  
COL3A1, GLY1284GLU [c.3851G>A; p.Gly1284Glu]

In 2 sibs with vascular Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, one of whom also had bilateral frontoparietal polymicrogyria, Jorgensen et al. (2015) identified compound heterozygous mutations in COL3A1. One was a c.3851G-A transition in exon 50, resulting in a gly1284-to-glu (G1284E) substitution. The other was R596X.

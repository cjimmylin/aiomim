### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that mitochondrial complex I deficiency nuclear type 7 (MC1DN7) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the NDUFV2 gene.

### Description

Mitochondrial complex I deficiency nuclear type 7 (MC1DN7) is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by mutations in NDUFV2, which encodes the 24-kD subunit of mitochondrial respiratory chain complex I. Affected individuals have shown early-onset hypertrophic cardiomyopathy with trunk hypotonia and encephalopathy, or Leigh syndrome without cardiac involvement (Benit et al., 2003; Cameron et al., 2015). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Benit et al. (2003) reported 3 affected sibs from a consanguineous family who had complex I deficiency and early-onset hypertrophic cardiomyopathy with trunk hypotonia.

Cameron et al. (2015) reported 2 sibs with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and brain atrophy who were diagnosed with complex I deficiency based on low enzyme activity in muscle and a high lactate/pyruvate ratio in fibroblasts. In a second, consanguineous family, 3 of 5 sibs were affected; they presented with Leigh syndrome but had no cardiac involvement.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of MC1DN7 in the families reported by Benit et al. (2003) and Cameron et al. (2015) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Affected sibs from consanguineous families carried homozygous mutations, and sibs in a third family carried compound heterozygous mutations. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In 3 sibs from a consanguineous family with MC1DN7, Benit et al. (2003) identified a homozygous 4-bp deletion in intron 2 of the NDUFV2 gene (IVS2+5_+8delGTAA). They detected it by denaturing high performance liquid chromatography and sequence analysis. The mutation altered the consensus splice-donor site of exon 2 and resulted in a 70% decrease in NDUFV2 protein and complex I deficiency.

In 2 sibs with MC1DN7 who had hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and brain atrophy, Cameron et al. (2015) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the NDUFV2 gene by whole-exome sequencing. One allele was the previously reported intronic mutation (c.IVS2+1delGTAA). The other was a novel insertion (c.669_670insG) predicted to cause a frameshift (Ser224ValfsTer3). Screening of 10 probands with complex I deficiency from different families revealed a second, consanguineous family in which the affected sibs were homozygous for the c.IVS2+1delGTAA mutation. These 3 affected sibs (of 5) had Leigh syndrome without cardiac involvement.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Cameron et al. (2015) noted that the same homozygous intronic NDUFV2 genotype was associated with different phenotypes in different families. Previously reported families had hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, trunk hypotonia, and encephalopathy (see Benit et al., 2003). In contrast, the affected sibs they studied presented with Leigh syndrome and no cardiac involvement.

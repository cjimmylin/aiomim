### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Pallister-Hall-like syndrome (PHLS) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the SMO gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Pallister-Hall-like syndrome is a developmental disorder whose features include hypothalamic hamartoma and polydactyly. Le et al. (2020) found that bi-allelic loss-of-function variants in SMO cause a wide range of developmental anomalies. These affect the brain (hypothalamic hamartoma and microcephaly), the heart (atrioventricular septal defect), the skeleton (postaxial polydactyly, narrow chest, and shortening of the long bones), and the enteric nervous system (aganglionosis). ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

Encha-Razavi et al. (1992) reviewed reported cases of Pallister-Hall syndrome and concluded that only skeletal dysplasia and oro-facial abnormalities were consistently present. They proposed treating these features as the minimum criteria for a distinct, possibly familial entity, which they called 'congenital hypothalamic hamartoma syndrome' (CHHS).

### Clinical Features

Marcuse et al. (1953) reported 2 cases of hypothalamic hamartoma with associated developmental defects.

Encha-Razavi et al. (1992) found congenital hypothalamic hamartomas at autopsy in 3 unrelated fetuses. In the first 2, the tumor occurred with skeletal dysplasia only. In the third, it was part of a nonrandom pattern of congenital malformations suggestive of Meckel syndrome. In one family, an earlier-born boy had died soon after birth with similar craniofacial and skeletal abnormalities. The authors noted that, to their knowledge, the combination of isolated skeletal dysplasia and congenital hypothalamic hamartoma had not been documented before.

Rubino et al. (2019) reported 2 brothers with a familial syndrome of hypothalamic hamartomas and polydactyly, which they distinguished from Pallister-Hall syndrome. One brother had a large hypothalamic hamartoma that was biopsied and subtotally resected by an endoscopic endonasal approach, and its histopathology was described.

Le et al. (2020) studied 7 individuals from 5 independent families. The individuals showed a wide range of developmental anomalies, including hypothalamic hamartoma, microcephaly, atrioventricular septal defect, postaxial polydactyly, narrow chest, shortening of the long bones, and aganglionosis of the enteric nervous system.

### Inheritance

Le et al. (2020) found bi-allelic SMO variants in affected individuals from 5 families, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Encha-Razavi et al. (1992) had earlier suggested that the disorder might be familial after a previous boy in one family died with similar abnormalities. Rubino et al. (2019) described the disorder in 2 brothers. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In 2 brothers with hypothalamic hamartomas and polydactyly, Rubino et al. (2019) identified germline mutations in the SMO gene. They described this as the first report of a familial syndrome caused by germline SMO mutations.

In 7 individuals from 5 independent families with a range of developmental anomalies, Le et al. (2020) identified bi-allelic loss-of-function variants in the SMO gene. Cells from affected individuals formed cilia normally but showed severely altered hedgehog signal transduction. This resulted either from altered trafficking in the primary cilium or from abnormal activation of the pathway downstream of SMO. Cells from affected individuals also showed hedgehog-independent accumulation of GLI2 at the tip of the primary cilium. The authors suggested that SMO may help regulate basal ciliary trafficking of GLI2 when the pathway is inactive.

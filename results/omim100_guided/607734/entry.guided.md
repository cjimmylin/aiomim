### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1F (CMT1F) is caused by heterozygous or homozygous mutation in the NEFL gene on chromosome 8p21. ⚠︎

### Description

Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1F (CMT1F) is a hereditary motor and sensory peripheral neuropathy caused by mutation in the NEFL gene. Onset is early and the clinical phenotype is often severe. Nerve conduction velocities are mildly to severely slowed (Jordanova et al., 2003; Abe et al., 2009). Missense mutations cause a dominant form of the disorder, and homozygous nonsense mutations cause a recessive form (Abe et al., 2009; Yum et al., 2009). ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

Yum et al. (2009) pointed out that the slowed nerve conduction velocities in their family with a homozygous NEFL mutation would classify the neuropathy as 'demyelinating' under the conventional 38 m/s cutoff. However, nerve biopsy suggested that the slowing was explained by the absence of large myelinated axons. The authors therefore preferred the term 'severe, early-onset axonal neuropathy' (SEOAN) and suggested the designation CMT2B5 for their patients.

### Clinical Features

Jordanova et al. (2003) reported patients with Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease and NEFL mutations. Onset was early and the clinical phenotype was often severe. On electrophysiologic examination, nerve conduction velocities were moderately to severely slowed. A nerve biopsy from a patient with a de novo missense mutation showed axonal pathology, with axonal regeneration clusters and onion bulb formations.

Abe et al. (2009) reported 6 unrelated Japanese patients with CMT and NEFL mutations. All of them had mildly to moderately delayed nerve conduction velocities, which the authors suggested might be caused by loss of large-diameter fibers.

Yum et al. (2009) reported a 2-generation Palestinian family in which 4 of 5 children of consanguineous parents (first cousins) had a severe, progressive neuropathy. Onset was at about 1.5 years, with gait difficulty, toe walking, or delayed walking. All 4 affected children had hypotonia in infancy and early childhood, mildly delayed motor milestones, slowly progressive distal atrophy and weakness of the feet and hands, and moderate glove-and-stocking sensory impairment. Ankle jerks were absent in all 4, and 3 had pes cavus. The oldest patient, a 23-year-old woman, used a walker and had proximal leg weakness, a waddling gait, and learning difficulties. Sensory responses were generally absent. Median and ulnar motor conduction velocities were severely slowed, ranging from 12 to 25 m/s, and motor amplitudes became progressively smaller with age. Peroneal and tibial responses eventually became unobtainable. EMG showed severe distal denervation. Visual evoked responses were prolonged in 3 of the 4 children, indicating subclinical central nervous system involvement. Sural nerve biopsy from a 16-year-old affected boy showed reduced myelinated axon density (1,300 compared with about 6,000 in controls). All myelinated axons were less than 3 microns in diameter and lacked intermediate filaments. There were occasional clusters of regenerated axons but no actively degenerating axons.

### Inheritance

Jordanova et al. (2003) found NEFL mutations in patients with sporadic or dominantly inherited CMT, including a de novo missense mutation. Abe et al. (2009) concluded that missense NEFL mutations cause a dominant phenotype and nonsense mutations probably cause a recessive phenotype. Yum et al. (2009) confirmed autosomal recessive inheritance in a consanguineous family in which the affected children were homozygous for a nonsense mutation and both parents were heterozygous carriers. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Jordanova et al. (2003) screened 323 patients with CMT or related peripheral neuropathies. They found 6 disease-associated missense mutations and one 3-bp in-frame deletion in the NEFL gene, clustered in functionally defined domains of the protein. One of the missense mutations had occurred de novo.

Abe et al. (2009) analyzed the NEFL gene in 223 Japanese patients with CMT who were negative for mutations in other known CMT genes. In 5 unrelated patients they found 4 heterozygous missense mutations: pro8-to-leu (P8L), glu90-to-lys (E90K), asn98-to-ser (N98S), and glu396-to-lys (E396K). Another patient was homozygous for a nonsense mutation (E140X), the first homozygous nonsense NEFL mutation to be reported.

In 4 affected sibs from a consanguineous Palestinian family with severe early-onset CMT, Yum et al. (2009) found a homozygous nonsense mutation in the NEFL gene (E210X). Both parents were heterozygous, and the unaffected sib did not carry the mutation. In transfected cells, the mutant protein did not form filaments and did not interfere with filament formation by wildtype NEFL. The authors concluded that the mutation causes a simple loss of function.

### Pathogenesis

Yum et al. (2009) found that myelinated axons in the sural nerve of a patient homozygous for the E210X NEFL mutation were small and lacked intermediate filaments. They concluded that the absence of large myelinated axons, rather than a primary Schwann cell defect, was the likely explanation for the slowed conduction velocities. They also concluded that neurofilaments are required for the maintenance of myelinated peripheral nervous system axons.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CHARCOT-MARIE-TOOTH DISEASE, DEMYELINATING, TYPE 1F**  
NEFL, GLU140TER [Glu140Stop; E140X]

In a Japanese patient with CMT and mildly to moderately delayed nerve conduction velocities, Abe et al. (2009) found a homozygous glu140-to-ter (E140X) nonsense mutation in the NEFL gene. It was the first homozygous nonsense NEFL mutation to be reported. The authors concluded that nonsense NEFL mutations probably cause a recessive phenotype.

**.0002 CHARCOT-MARIE-TOOTH DISEASE, DEMYELINATING, TYPE 1F**  
NEFL, GLU210TER [NM_006158:c.628G>T; E210X]

In 4 affected sibs from a consanguineous Palestinian family with severe, early-onset CMT, Yum et al. (2009) found a homozygous c.628G-T transversion in the NEFL gene, resulting in a glu210-to-ter (E210X) substitution. The mutation was found by sequencing all coding exons. Both parents were heterozygous, and the unaffected sib did not carry the mutation. The mutation was not found in more than 11,000 individuals tested at Athena Diagnostics. In sural nerve from a patient, axons were labeled by an antiserum against the N terminus of NEFL but not by one against the C terminus. In SW-13 vim- cells, the mutant produced an approximately 30-kD protein that accumulated poorly and did not form filaments. It did not interfere with filament formation by wildtype NEFL or with the vimentin network. These findings were consistent with a simple loss of function.

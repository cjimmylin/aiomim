### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GABRB1 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is a severe neurologic disorder with seizures that begin in infancy. Reported patients had infantile spasms with hypsarrhythmia or epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures, along with severe developmental impairment. The disorder has been caused by de novo heterozygous mutations in the GABRB1 gene, which encodes the beta-1 subunit of the GABA-A receptor (Epi4K Consortium et al., 2013; Janve et al., 2016; Burgess et al., 2019). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) studied 264 probands with infantile spasms (IS) or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome. One of them, a patient with IS, carried a de novo GABRB1 mutation. To enter the study, patients with IS had to have hypsarrhythmia on EEG and no moderate-to-severe developmental impairment before seizure onset. Janve et al. (2016) noted that this patient had a severe IS phenotype. ⚠︎

Lien et al. (2016) reported a second patient with epileptic encephalopathy and a de novo GABRB1 mutation.

Burgess et al. (2019) studied a cohort of patients with epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (EIMFS). It included 1 patient (patient 63) with a de novo GABRB1 variant. In this patient, focal seizures began at 4 months of age. At the time of study, at 2 years of age, the patient had profound to severe developmental impairment and hypotonia and had become seizure-free.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous GABRB1 mutations that caused DEE45 in the patients reported by Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013), Lien et al. (2016), and Burgess et al. (2019) arose de novo. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) performed exome sequencing on 264 trios, each a proband with IS or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome and the parents. They found a de novo mutation in the GABRB1 gene in 1 patient. The mutation was confirmed by Sanger sequencing of the proband and parents. It was 1 of 6 de novo mutations in the cohort that affected GABA-A receptor subunits: 4 in GABRB3 and 1 each in GABRA1 and GABRB1.

Janve et al. (2016) studied the GABRB1 mutation found by Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) in the patient with IS. It was a heterozygous phe246-to-ser (F246S) substitution in transmembrane domain 1, and F246 is invariant across all GABA-A receptor subunits. The authors coexpressed the mutant beta-1 subunit with alpha-1 and gamma-2L subunits in HEK293T cells. When only the mutant beta-1 subunit was expressed, peak GABA-evoked current density fell by a minor 25%. When wildtype and mutant beta-1 were coexpressed, current density did not change. The mutation slowed current deactivation. At the single-channel level, mean open time increased about 3.75-fold and burst duration about 3.2-fold, while opening frequency and conductance (about 21 pS) were reduced. The mutation also increased holding currents about 5-fold, and spontaneous open probability increased 4-fold, indicating spontaneous channel openings. Surface levels of the mutant subunit were slightly but significantly reduced, while total levels were unchanged. Janve et al. (2016) concluded that the mutation impaired GABA-A receptor function, chiefly by altering current kinetics.

Lien et al. (2016) reported a second patient with epileptic encephalopathy who carried a de novo GABRB1 mutation.

In a patient with EIMFS (patient 63), Burgess et al. (2019) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a de novo heterozygous variant in the GABRB1 gene, an ile247-to-thr (I247T) substitution (chr4:47405633T-C, GRCh37). The variant was absent from the ExAC and gnomAD databases. SIFT predicted it to be deleterious and PolyPhen predicted it to be benign. The authors classified it as likely pathogenic.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DEVELOPMENTAL AND EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY 45**  
GABRB1, PHE246SER [F246S]

Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) found a de novo heterozygous GABRB1 mutation in a patient with developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) who had infantile spasms. The mutation was identified by trio exome sequencing and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. Janve et al. (2016) identified the mutation as a phe246-to-ser (F246S) substitution at an invariant residue in transmembrane domain 1. In HEK293T cells, the mutant subunit caused a minor (25%) reduction in peak GABA-evoked current when expressed alone and no reduction when coexpressed with wildtype. It slowed current deactivation, increased single-channel mean open time (about 3.75-fold) and burst duration (about 3.2-fold), and increased spontaneous channel open probability 4-fold. ⚠︎

**.0002 DEVELOPMENTAL AND EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY 45**  
GABRB1, ILE247THR [p.I247T; Chr4:47405633T>C]

In a patient (patient 63) with DEE45 who had epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures, Burgess et al. (2019) identified a de novo heterozygous ile247-to-thr (I247T) substitution in the GABRB1 gene (chr4:47405633T-C, GRCh37) by whole-exome sequencing. The variant was absent from the ExAC and gnomAD databases. SIFT predicted it to be deleterious and PolyPhen predicted it to be benign.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that epidermolysis bullosa simplex-3 with BP230 deficiency (EBS3) is caused by homozygous or biallelic mutation in the DST gene, which encodes the epithelial isoform of bullous pemphigoid antigen-1 (BPAG1-e, also known as BP230). ⚠︎

### Description

Epidermolysis bullosa simplex-3 (EBS3) is an autosomal recessive inherited skin fragility disorder characterized by trauma-induced blisters and erosions resulting from intraepidermal separation. It is caused by mutation in the DST gene, which encodes the epithelial isoform of bullous pemphigoid antigen-1 (BPAG1-e, or BP230) (Groves et al., 2010; Ganani et al., 2021).

### Clinical Features

Groves et al. (2010) reported a 38-year-old man with lifelong generalized trauma-induced spontaneous blisters and erosions, particularly around the ankles. From the age of 37 years he also had episodic numbness in his limbs. The authors noted that these neurologic symptoms might be due to the DST mutation, but he also had a concomitant diagnosis of CADASIL (cerebral arteriopathy, autosomal dominant, with subcortical infarcts and leukoencephalopathy), which complicated interpretation of the genotype-phenotype relationship. Skin showed loss of hemidesmosomal inner plaques.

Liu et al. (2012) reported autosomal recessive epidermolysis bullosa simplex due to loss of BPAG1-e expression.

Ganani et al. (2021) reported 5 patients from 3 families with EBS due to biallelic DST mutations and reviewed 6 previously reported cases, bringing the total to 11 affected individuals. The patients had distinctive phenotypes regardless of the causative variant.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of EBS3 is autosomal recessive, as shown by the homozygous mutation in the DST gene reported by Groves et al. (2010) and the biallelic DST mutations reported by Ganani et al. (2021).

### Molecular Genetics

In a 38-year-old man with EBS3, Groves et al. (2010) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in the DST gene (gln1124-to-ter; Q1124X) located in the region encoding the coiled-coil domain of BPAG1-e. Skin immunostaining for BPAG1-e was completely absent, and labeling for plectin, the beta-4 integrin subunit, and type XVII collagen was reduced.

In 5 patients from 3 families with EBS3, Ganani et al. (2021) used whole-exome and direct sequencing to identify biallelic mutations in the DST gene, including 2 previously unreported variants, c.7097dupA (p.Tyr2366X) and c.7429delC (p.Leu2477Serfs*13).

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

In a review of 11 individuals with EBS due to DST variants, including 5 new patients, Ganani et al. (2021) found that patients had distinctive phenotypes regardless of the causative variant.

In patients with EBS3, Ganani et al. (2021) identified a previously unreported 1-bp duplication (c.7097dupA) in the DST gene, predicted to result in a premature stop codon (Tyr2366X). The variant was found as one of the biallelic DST mutations identified by whole-exome and direct sequencing in 5 patients from 3 families.

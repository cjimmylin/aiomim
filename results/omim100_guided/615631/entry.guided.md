### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type Ib (CDAN1B) is caused by homozygous mutation in the C15ORF41 gene.

### Description

The congenital dyserythropoietic anemias are a heterogeneous group of rare disorders that mainly affect erythropoiesis. They show characteristic morphologic abnormalities and a block in erythroid maturation. CDA type Ib is a form of CDA type I caused by mutation in the C15ORF41 gene, which encodes a predicted endonuclease (Babbs et al., 2013).

### Clinical Features

Sabry et al. (1997) described dysmorphic features in 3 Bedouin sibs with congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I. The features were growth retardation or short stature, congenital ptosis, abnormal tarsal bones, metatarsal duplication or hypoplasia, and hypoplasia of the nails and phalanges of the fingers and toes. The sibs also had Madelung deformity, syndactyly of the toes, and hallux valgus. Peripheral blood lymphocyte cultures from the patients had a very low mitotic index. The phenotype varied among the 3 sibs. Sabry et al. (1997) concluded that a subset of CDA type I is associated with a specific form of distal limb anomalies. They suggested that congenital ptosis and a low mitotic index could also be part of the syndrome.

### Inheritance

Babbs et al. (2013) found homozygous C15ORF41 mutations in affected members of a consanguineous family, which is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance of CDAN1B. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Ahmed et al. (2006) reported linkage and mutation analysis of the CDAN1 gene that revealed genetic heterogeneity in congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I.

Babbs et al. (2013) noted that mutations in the CDAN1 gene cause most cases of CDA type I. However, no likely pathogenic CDAN1 mutation had been found in about 20% of cases, which suggested that there was at least 1 other locus.

### Molecular Genetics

Babbs et al. (2013) studied a consanguineous family of Middle Eastern origin with CDA type I. Using whole-genome sequencing and segregation analysis, they identified a homozygous c.533T-A transversion in the C15ORF41 gene, predicted to cause a leu178-to-gln (L178Q) substitution. They then sequenced C15ORF41 in other CDA type I families without CDAN1 mutations. In 2 families of Southeast Asian origin, they found a homozygous c.281A-G transition, predicted to cause a tyr94-to-cys (Y94C) substitution. The haplotype around the c.281A-G change suggested a founder effect for this mutation in Pakistan. Detailed sequence similarity searches indicated that C15ORF41 encodes a novel restriction endonuclease belonging to the Holliday junction resolvase family of proteins.

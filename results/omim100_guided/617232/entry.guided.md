### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive limb-girdle muscular dystrophy-21 (LGMDR21) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the POGLUT1 gene on chromosome 3q.

### Description

Autosomal recessive limb-girdle muscular dystrophy-21 (LGMDR21) is a slowly progressive muscular dystrophy. Weakness is proximal and mainly affects the lower limbs, and scapular winging is common. Onset is usually in adulthood but can be congenital or infantile. Serum creatine kinase is usually normal or only mildly raised. On muscle MRI, fatty degeneration of the thigh muscles follows a distinctive 'inside-to-outside' pattern, and muscle biopsies show reduced glycosylation of alpha-dystroglycan, reduced NOTCH1 signaling, and fewer satellite cells (Servián-Morilla et al., 2016; Servián-Morilla et al., 2020).

### Nomenclature

Servián-Morilla et al. (2020) noted that this disorder, originally designated LGMD2Z, is named LGMD R21 POGLUT1-related under the reformed LGMD nomenclature and classification proposed by Straub et al. (2018). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Servián-Morilla et al. (2016) studied a consanguineous family from southern Spain in which 4 of 5 sibs had limb-girdle muscular dystrophy. Weakness was mainly in the proximal lower limbs and began in the third decade. The disease progressed to scapular winging and wheelchair confinement. Serum creatine kinase was normal in 3 patients and mildly raised in 1. Muscle biopsies ranged from very mild myopathic changes to classic dystrophic pathology, and alpha-dystroglycan was reduced. Glycosylated alpha-dystroglycan at the sarcolemma was variably reduced, with a small decrease in its molecular weight and reduced laminin binding. In contrast, agrin binding and basement membrane ultrastructure were normal, and patient skin fibroblasts showed normal alpha-dystroglycan glycosylation and laminin binding. On MRI of the legs, fatty replacement began in the internal regions of the thigh muscles and spared the external areas. This 'from inside-to-outside' pattern progressed over the years and did not match the patterns seen in other muscular dystrophies.

Servián-Morilla et al. (2020) reported 15 patients with LGMDR21 from 9 unrelated families from Spain, Bulgaria, Italy, Germany, the USA, Iran, and the United Arab Emirates. Four of the families were consanguineous, and the series included the original family. Onset was congenital in 1 patient, infantile (before 3 years of age) in 2, and in adulthood in 12. In the summary table, onset in the original family was given as the second decade for 2 sibs and the third decade for the other 2. Weakness was proximal, clearly worse in the lower limbs, and slowly progressive. Seven patients with onset in their teens were confined to a wheelchair between the third and fifth decades. Eleven of the 15 patients had mild to moderate scapular winging. Six patients developed mild to severe respiratory involvement from their mid-forties onward, and 2 of them needed ventilatory support at night. No significant bulbar, cardiac, or other systemic manifestations were found. In the text, serum CK was normal except in 4 patients with mild elevations of 2 to 6 times normal. The clinical table, however, listed raised CK in more patients, up to 10 times normal. ⚠︎

The patient with congenital onset presented with hypotonia, followed by proximal lower limb weakness after he learned to walk. He later developed mild weakness, wasting, and contractures of the upper limbs, mild facial weakness, ptosis, and a nasal voice. He also had elbow contractures, a myopathic face, and an arched palate. One patient with infantile onset had ankle contractures, calf hypertrophy, scoliosis, and hyperlordosis. Muscle imaging was available for 11 patients, and all showed the 'inside-to-outside' pattern of fatty degeneration. This pattern involved the vastus lateralis, medialis, and intermedius, the posterior thigh compartment, and the gastrocnemius. The lower leg muscles were better preserved than the thigh muscles. Muscle biopsies were studied in 9 patients. All showed reduced sarcolemmal staining for glycosylated alpha-dystroglycan with normal core protein staining, as well as reduced laminin binding, decreased NOTCH1 intracellular domain, and fewer PAX7-positive satellite cells. Few or no regenerating fibers positive for embryonic myosin heavy chain were seen. Vastus lateralis biopsies were dystrophic, whereas biceps brachii biopsies showed mild myopathic features with lobulated fibers in some cases. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Servián-Morilla et al. (2016) established autosomal recessive inheritance of LGMDR21 in a consanguineous family in which affected sibs were homozygous for a POGLUT1 mutation.

### Mapping

Servián-Morilla et al. (2016) performed homozygosity mapping with genomewide SNP arrays in the Spanish family. They identified a 14.6-Mb region of homozygosity on chromosome 3q13.13-q21.2, flanked by SNPs rs13081374 and rs2333038, that was shared by the 4 affected sibs and absent in their healthy sister.

### Molecular Genetics

In 4 sibs with LGMDR21 from a consanguineous Spanish family, Servián-Morilla et al. (2016) identified a homozygous missense mutation in the POGLUT1 gene (D233E). The mutation was found by homozygosity mapping followed by whole-exome sequencing and segregated with the disease. Patient muscle showed normal POGLUT1 protein and mRNA levels, and the mutant protein localized normally to the endoplasmic reticulum. However, the mutant enzyme had reduced O-glucosyltransferase activity toward Notch1 EGF repeats and reduced O-xylosyltransferase activity, while keeping some residual activity. Patient muscle showed reduced NOTCH1 intracellular domain, HES1 mRNA, and PAX7 mRNA, and fewer PAX7-positive cells. Wildtype human POGLUT1 rescued the muscle defects of Drosophila rumi mutants, but the D233E mutant gave only weak and variable rescue.

Servián-Morilla et al. (2020) used gene panels or exome sequencing, with Sanger confirmation, in 15 patients with LGMDR21 from 9 families. They identified 9 missense mutations and 1 nonsense mutation in the POGLUT1 gene, present in the homozygous or compound heterozygous state. Patients from 6 families were homozygous for missense mutations (I129T, R98W, Y57C, W308L, C102F, and R183W). The text described the patient with congenital onset as homozygous for R279Q, whereas the clinical table and figures described him as compound heterozygous for D233E and R279Q. Two affected sibs were compound heterozygous for Y156N and a c.552G-A transition causing a trp184-to-ter (W184X) substitution. Segregation was confirmed in all families. In functional studies, the R183W and Y57C mutant proteins were secreted normally but had much lower enzymatic activity. The I129T, R98W, C102F, and W308L mutant proteins were poorly secreted and gave little or no rescue of Notch1 EGF1-18 secretion in POGLUT1-knockout HEK293T cells. In Drosophila rumi mutants, the R183W, I129T, and Y57C mutants gave only partial rescue of myogenesis, and the C102F mutant gave no rescue.

### Pathogenesis

Servián-Morilla et al. (2016) found that primary myoblasts from patients with the D233E mutation proliferated slowly and had fewer PAX7+/MYOD- (self-renewing) cells. They also differentiated more readily, with increased myoblast fusion and myogenin expression. Overexpression of the NOTCH1 intracellular domain rescued the proliferation and differentiation defects but did not restore the number of PAX7+ cells. Muscle from patients with other muscular dystrophies, including secondary dystroglycanopathies, had higher levels of activated Notch and more PAX7+ cells. Inhibiting Notch signaling in differentiating C2C12 cells reduced alpha-dystroglycan glycosylation. The authors concluded that Notch-dependent loss of satellite cells is a key pathomechanism and that alpha-dystroglycan hypoglycosylation is a secondary consequence of reduced Notch signaling. Servián-Morilla et al. (2020) found decreased proliferation and facilitated differentiation in myoblasts from the patient with congenital onset.

### Diagnosis

Servián-Morilla et al. (2020) proposed that LGMDR21 be considered in a patient with recessive LGMD when 3 features occur together. These were alpha-dystroglycan hypoglycosylation, reduced PAX7+ cells and NOTCH1 intracellular domain levels on muscle biopsy, and the 'inside-to-outside' pattern on muscle MRI. In collagen VI-related myopathy, by contrast, the outer areas of the vastus lateralis are affected first. Most patients had normal CK levels, which the authors suggested may help distinguish LGMDR21 from other autosomal recessive LGMDs.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 MUSCULAR DYSTROPHY, LIMB-GIRDLE, AUTOSOMAL RECESSIVE 21**  
POGLUT1, ASP233GLU [c.699T>G; p.D233E]

In 4 sibs from a consanguineous Spanish family with LGMDR21, Servián-Morilla et al. (2016) identified a homozygous c.699T-G transversion in the POGLUT1 gene, resulting in an asp233-to-glu (D233E) substitution. The mutation was found by homozygosity mapping and exome sequencing and segregated with the disease. It was not present in the ExAC database or in 919 DNA samples from 57 populations. The residue lies in the CAP10 domain, 14 amino acids from the putative ERD catalytic motif, and is highly conserved in vertebrates. The mutant protein was expressed and localized normally but had reduced O-glucosyltransferase activity with residual function. It gave only weak and variable rescue of muscle defects in Drosophila rumi mutants.

Servián-Morilla et al. (2020) found D233E together with R279Q in a patient with congenital-onset LGMDR21. The clinical table and figures described him as compound heterozygous for the 2 mutations.

**.0002 MUSCULAR DYSTROPHY, LIMB-GIRDLE, AUTOSOMAL RECESSIVE 21**  
POGLUT1, ARG279GLN [p.R279Q]

In a patient (family 2) with congenital-onset LGMDR21, Servián-Morilla et al. (2020) identified an arg279-to-gln (R279Q) substitution in the POGLUT1 gene. The variant had previously been reported in the heterozygous state in a patient with Dowling-Degos disease. The text described the patient as homozygous for R279Q, but the clinical table and figures described him as compound heterozygous for D233E and R279Q. Structural analysis predicted that R279Q would interfere with donor-substrate binding. Myoblasts from the patient showed decreased proliferation and facilitated differentiation.

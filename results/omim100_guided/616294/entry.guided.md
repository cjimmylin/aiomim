### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Cole-Carpenter syndrome-2 (CLCRP2) is caused by compound heterozygous mutation in the SEC24D gene.

### Description

Cole-Carpenter syndrome-2 is a skeletal disorder with bone fragility. Features include severely disturbed ossification of the skull, multiple fractures that may begin before birth, short stature, and craniofacial malformations such as macrocephaly, frontal bossing, midface hypoplasia, and micrognathia (Garbes et al., 2015; Takeyari et al., 2018). Some patients with SEC24D mutations have been reported as having autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta (Zhang et al., 2017).

### Clinical Features

Garbes et al. (2015) reported 3 affected individuals from 2 families: a 7-year-old boy whose phenotype most closely resembled Cole-Carpenter syndrome, and 2 fetuses initially suspected to have a severe type of osteogenesis imperfecta. All 3 had severely disturbed ossification of the skull and multiple fractures with prenatal onset. The boy had short stature and craniofacial malformations including macrocephaly, midface hypoplasia, micrognathia, frontal bossing, and downslanting palpebral fissures.

Zhang et al. (2017) reported 2 Chinese probands with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta. The proband in family 1, a 23-year-old man, had recurrent fractures, dentinogenesis imperfecta, an unclosed anterior fontanel, and facial dysmorphism. CT 3-dimensional imaging of the cranium showed skull deformities with a broad ossification defect in the frontoapical area, a widened sagittal suture, and wormian bones. The proband in family 2, a 7-year-old boy, also had recurrent fractures, dentinogenesis imperfecta, and an unclosed anterior fontanel, but no obvious facial dysmorphism.

Takeyari et al. (2018) described a 15-year-old Japanese boy with short-trunk short stature and craniofacial abnormalities including ocular proptosis, marked frontal bossing, midface hypoplasia, and micrognathia, which were consistent with Cole-Carpenter syndrome. He had low bone mineral density and basilar impression.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CLCRP2 in the families reported by Garbes et al. (2015) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance, as affected individuals were compound heterozygous for SEC24D mutations. Moosa et al. (2016) and Zhang et al. (2017) reported SEC24D mutations in autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta.

### Molecular Genetics

By whole-exome sequencing, Garbes et al. (2015) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the SEC24D gene in 3 individuals from 2 families with a similar skeletal phenotype. Three mutant alleles were found: a truncating mutation, c.613C-T (Q205X), and 2 missense mutations, c.3044C-T (S1015F), located in a cargo-binding pocket, and c.2933A-C (Q978P), located in the gelsolin-like domain. Each affected individual carried 2 of these alleles; c.3044C-T was found in all 3 and lay within a 14-Mb founder haplotype that they shared. Electron and immunofluorescence microscopy of the boy's skin fibroblasts showed inefficient ER export of procollagen and dilated ER tubules. This matched the cellular phenotype of individuals with cranio-lentico-sutural dysplasia, which is caused by SEC23A mutations.

Moosa et al. (2016) reported mutations in SEC24D in autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta.

By whole-exome sequencing in 2 Chinese probands with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta, Zhang et al. (2017) identified compound heterozygous missense mutations in the SEC24D gene. The proband in family 1 carried c.2723G-A (C908Y) and c.2842T-C (S948P). The proband in family 2 carried c.938G-A (R313H) and c.875C-T (P292L).

By whole-exome sequencing, Takeyari et al. (2018) identified compound heterozygous variants in the SEC24D gene, R484X and R313H, in a 15-year-old Japanese boy with CLCRP2.

### Animal Model

Garbes et al. (2015) noted that the cellular and morphologic phenotypes in their patients were consistent with those described for the Sec24d-deficient fish mutants vbi (medaka) and bulldog (zebrafish).

**.0002 COLE-CARPENTER SYNDROME 2**  
SEC24D, SER1015PHE [c.3044C>T; p.Ser1015Phe]

Garbes et al. (2015) identified a c.3044C-T transition in the SEC24D gene, resulting in a ser1015-to-phe (S1015F) substitution in a cargo-binding pocket. It was present in all 3 affected individuals from 2 families with CLCRP2: a 7-year-old boy and 2 fetuses. Each carried S1015F in the compound heterozygous state with either Q205X or Q978P.

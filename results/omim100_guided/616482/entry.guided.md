### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that severe achondroplasia with developmental delay and acanthosis nigricans (SADDAN) is caused by mutation in the fibroblast growth factor receptor-3 gene (FGFR3).

### Description

Severe achondroplasia with developmental delay and acanthosis nigricans (SADDAN) is a skeletal dysplasia whose severity approaches that of thanatophoric dysplasia type I. Unlike thanatophoric dysplasia, it is usually compatible with survival past infancy. It is associated with progressive acanthosis nigricans, structural anomalies of the central nervous system, seizures, and severe developmental delay (Tavormina et al., 1999; Bellus et al., 1999).

### Nomenclature

Tavormina et al. (1999) named the phenotype caused by the FGFR3 lys650-to-met mutation 'severe achondroplasia with developmental delay and acanthosis nigricans' (SADDAN). They chose a separate name because the phenotype differed significantly from those caused by other known FGFR3 mutations.

### Clinical Features

Tavormina et al. (1999) reported 4 unrelated individuals with a skeletal dysplasia whose severity approached that of thanatophoric dysplasia type I. Three of the 4 developed extensive areas of acanthosis nigricans beginning in early childhood and had severe neurologic impairment. These 3 survived past infancy without prolonged life-support measures.

Bellus et al. (1999) gave a more detailed clinical account of the SADDAN phenotype. The disorder caused severe disturbances of endochondral bone growth that approached and overlapped those seen in thanatophoric dysplasia type I, but it was most often compatible with survival into adulthood. Some patients had unusual bone deformities, including femoral bowing with reverse (posterior apex) tibial and fibular bowing and 'ram's horn' bowing of the clavicle. Surviving patients had progressive acanthosis nigricans, structural anomalies of the central nervous system, seizures, and severe developmental delay. Cloverleaf skull and craniosynostosis were not present.

Zankl et al. (2008) described the prenatal and postnatal features of a patient with SADDAN. The case confirmed the severe morbidity of the disorder and added to reports of early mortality. The authors also reviewed the clinical and radiologic characteristics of all reported cases of SADDAN.

### Molecular Genetics

In 4 unrelated individuals with SADDAN, Tavormina et al. (1999) identified a novel missense mutation in the FGFR3 gene: an A1949T change resulting in a lys650-to-met (K650M) substitution. The mutation lies at the nucleotide adjacent to the thanatophoric dysplasia type II mutation (A1948G; K650E). It produces a different amino acid substitution at a highly conserved codon in the activation loop of the kinase domain. In transient transfection studies, the K650M mutation caused a dramatic increase in constitutive receptor kinase activity, about 3 times greater than that seen with the K650E mutation.

In a patient with SADDAN, Zankl et al. (2008) confirmed the clinical diagnosis by mutation analysis, which detected the FGFR3 K650M mutation. They noted that this was one of only 6 cases with molecular analysis reported in the literature.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Bellus et al. (1999) noted that the K650M mutation lies in the same FGFR3 codon as the thanatophoric dysplasia type II mutation (K650E) and has a similar effect on constitutive activation of the FGFR3 tyrosine kinase. Even so, the K650M mutation was not associated with cloverleaf skull or craniosynostosis.

### Animal Model

Iwata et al. (2001) introduced into the mouse Fgfr3 gene a K644M point mutation, which corresponds to the human K650M mutation. Heterozygous mutant mice had a phenotype similar to human SADDAN, and most of them survived the perinatal period. The authors concluded that the survival of SADDAN patients is attributable to the K650M mutation itself. The long bone abnormalities in SADDAN mice were milder than those in a mouse model of thanatophoric dysplasia type II. The rib cartilage, trachea, and nasal septum showed overgrowth of cartilaginous tissue. At low concentration, FGF ligand activated MAP kinase differently in primary chondrocyte cultures from wildtype and SADDAN mice.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that monilethrix-2 (MNLIX2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the type II hair cortex keratin gene KRT81 (hHb1), which is located in the type II keratin gene cluster on chromosome 12q13. ⚠︎

### Description

Monilethrix is a rare autosomal dominant hair disorder. Its main feature is beaded (moniliform) hair caused by periodic thinning of the hair shaft. The hair is especially prone to weathering and breaking (Winter et al., 1997). Monilethrix-2 is caused by mutation in the type II hair cortex keratin hHb1, and its severity can vary widely within a family (Winter et al., 1998).

### Clinical Features

Winter et al. (1997) studied affected members of a monilethrix family who carried a mutation in the type II hair keratin hHb1.

Winter et al. (1998) described a French family with monilethrix in which the disease varied a great deal between members. One infant had pronounced dystrophic alopecia, follicular keratosis, and clear-cut moniliform hair. A second infant had no hair loss at all, and moniliform hair could be detected only by electron microscopy. An adult family member had no symptoms that could be detected clinically or by electron microscopy, but there was clear historical proof that this person had had the disease.

### Inheritance

Winter et al. (1998) noted that monilethrix shows autosomal dominant transmission. In the French family they studied, the hHb1 mutation was transmitted across generations, and penetrance and expressivity were variable. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Winter et al. (1997) noted that several cases of monilethrix had been linked to the type II keratin gene cluster on chromosome 12q13.

### Heterogeneity

Winter et al. (1997) noted that heterozygous mutations at glu410 in the type II hair keratin hHb6 (glu410-to-lys and glu410-to-asp) had been found in monilethrix patients. In 1 of the 2 families and in the single patient they studied, they found the common hHb6 glu410-to-lys mutation. In the other family, they found a mutation in the related type II hair keratin hHb1.

### Molecular Genetics

Winter et al. (1997) studied 2 unrelated monilethrix families and 1 single patient. In affected members of 1 family, they found a glu403-to-lys substitution in the type II hair keratin hHb1. This residue in hHb1 corresponds to glu410 in hHb6, which is the site of the common hHb6 monilethrix mutation. The authors suggested that this site is a mutational hotspot in these closely related type II hair keratins. Because hHb1 and hHb6 are largely coexpressed in cortical trichocytes of the hair shaft, they concluded that monilethrix is a disease of the hair cortex.

In a French family with monilethrix, Winter et al. (1998) found a glu402-to-lys (E402K) mutation in hHb1. This mutation substitutes lysine for a highly conserved glutamic acid residue (glu106 of the 2B subdomain) in the EIATYRRLLEGEE helix termination motif. Winter et al. (1998) noted that all pathogenic mutations reported before this in hHb6 and hHb1 had affected a single highly conserved residue in this motif, which they designated glu413 (glu117 of the 2B subdomains). At that residue, the glutamic acid was replaced by lysine or, less often, by aspartic acid.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Winter et al. (1998) found that family members with the hHb1 E402K mutation had a particularly variable phenotype. It ranged from pronounced dystrophic alopecia with follicular keratosis to moniliform hair seen only by electron microscopy. One adult had no symptoms detectable clinically or by electron microscopy.

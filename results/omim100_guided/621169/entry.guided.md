### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that monilethrix-2 (MNLIX2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding the type II hair cortex keratin hHb1 (KRT81) on chromosome 12q13. ⚠︎

### Description

Monilethrix is a rare autosomal dominant hair disorder. Affected hair is beaded, or moniliform, because the hair shaft thins at regular intervals, and it weathers and breaks very easily (Winter et al., 1997). The phenotype can vary a great deal within a family. Some members have dystrophic alopecia and follicular keratosis, and others have no clinical signs at all (Winter et al., 1998).

### Clinical Features

Winter et al. (1998) studied a French family with monilethrix in which the phenotype was especially variable. One infant had pronounced dystrophic alopecia, follicular keratosis and clear-cut moniliform hair. A second infant had no hair loss, and moniliform hair could be seen only by electron microscopy. One adult had no clinical or electron microscopic signs of the disease but had clear historical evidence of having had it.

### Inheritance

Winter et al. (1997, 1998) found monilethrix to be transmitted as an autosomal dominant trait, caused by heterozygous mutations in the type II hair keratin hHb1. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Winter et al. (1997) noted that several cases of monilethrix had been linked to the type II keratin gene cluster on chromosome 12q13.

### Heterogeneity

Winter et al. (1997) studied 3 unrelated probands with monilethrix. Affected members of one family and a single sporadic patient had the glu410-to-lys mutation in the type II hair keratin hHb6. Affected members of the second family had a mutation in hHb1.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of a family with monilethrix, Winter et al. (1997) identified a heterozygous glu403-to-lys (E403K) substitution in the type II hair cortex keratin hHb1. The mutation affects the residue corresponding to glu410 of hHb6, a site at which monilethrix mutations had already been found, and the authors suggested that this position is a mutational hotspot in these closely related type II hair keratins. hHb1 and hHb6 are largely coexpressed in cortical trichocytes of the hair shaft, and the authors concluded that monilethrix is a disease of the hair cortex. ⚠︎

In a French family with variable monilethrix, Winter et al. (1998) identified a glu402-to-lys (E402K) mutation in hHb1. Glu402 (glu106 of the 2B subdomain) is a highly conserved residue in the EIATYRRLLEGEE helix termination motif. Before this report, the hHb1 and hHb6 mutations described had all affected the conserved glutamic acid that Winter et al. (1998) numbered glu413 (glu117 of the 2B subdomain), where it was replaced by lysine or, less often, by aspartic acid.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Winter et al. (1998) found that family members carrying the hHb1 E402K mutation varied widely in phenotype. Findings ranged from pronounced dystrophic alopecia with moniliform hair to moniliform hair visible only by electron microscopy, and one adult carrier had no detectable clinical or electron microscopic signs.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that axonal Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2EE (CMT2EE) is caused by homozygous mutation in the MPV17 gene.

### Description

Axonal Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2EE (CMT2EE) is an autosomal recessive axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy with juvenile or adult onset. It causes distal muscle weakness and atrophy, mainly in the lower limbs, along with distal sensory loss and absent tendon reflexes. Unlike Navajo neurohepatopathy, which is also caused by MPV17 mutations, it occurs without hepatic or brain involvement (Choi et al., 2015; Baumann et al., 2019). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Blakely et al. (2012) reported a 21-year-old Pakistani man, born to nonconsanguineous parents, whose walking difficulties began at age 14 years. Over several years these progressed to bilateral foot drop requiring orthoses, with symmetric distal numbness and coldness in the feet rising to the knees, and milder numbness in the hands. Examination showed wasting of the hands and distal lower limbs, clawed toes, a slow unsteady gait, decreased tone, and severe distal weakness. There was symmetric weakness of hip flexion, and all tendon reflexes were absent. Pinprick and light touch perception were decreased distal to the knees, vibration sense was impaired to the ankles, and joint position sense was absent at the toes. Nerve conduction studies showed an axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy. Sural nerve biopsy showed a severe chronic axonal neuropathy with only occasional surviving myelinated axons. Unlike later reported patients, this patient also had extraneural involvement. Brain MRI showed diffuse T2 hyperintensity in the cerebral white matter and cerebellum, consistent with leukoencephalopathy. CSF protein (1963 mg/L) and lactate (4.3 mmol/L) were increased. He had mild hepatomegaly, and liver biopsy showed patchy steatosis with early bridging fibrosis, although there was no clinically significant chronic liver disease. Cognition was not significantly impaired. Muscle biopsy showed ragged-red fibers, about 20% cytochrome c oxidase (COX)-deficient fibers, and subsarcolemmal accumulation of abnormal mitochondria with parking lot-like inclusions. Long-range PCR detected multiple mtDNA deletions in muscle, but mtDNA copy number was normal. His sister had died of a progressive hepatitis at age 9 years. Blakely et al. (2012) noted that the patient's phenotype resembled the classic form of Navajo neurohepatopathy. ⚠︎

Choi et al. (2015) reported 2 patients from 2 unrelated Korean families, each born to healthy nonconsanguineous parents, who had axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy without liver or brain involvement. Patient 1 was a 34-year-old woman. She first noticed distal lower limb weakness at age 9 years and began walking with short leg braces at age 12. On examination she had bilateral distal weakness and atrophy, predominantly in the lower limbs, and severe atrophy of the intrinsic hand, foot, and calf muscles. She also had flexion deformities of the interphalangeal joints, ankle joint deformity, and scoliosis. Vibration and position senses were more severely affected than pain and touch, and knee and ankle jerks were absent. Patient 2 was a 22-year-old man who first had gait and balance problems at age 13 years and began using short leg braces at age 20. He had distal weakness and atrophy, flat feet, atrophy of the intrinsic hand, foot, and calf muscles, a similar pattern of sensory disturbance, and absent deep tendon reflexes. Neither patient had growth retardation, gastrointestinal dysmotility, hepatomegaly, cognitive impairment, ophthalmoplegia, corneal scarring, or hypoglycemic attacks. Serum lactate was mildly elevated in both (2.1 and 1.9 mmol/L), but liver enzymes were normal. In both patients, electrophysiologic studies showed decreased motor conduction velocities and CMAPs in the median and ulnar nerves. Peroneal and tibial CMAPs and all sensory nerve action potentials were absent, and sural nerve responses were lost early in the disease. Visual and brainstem auditory evoked potentials were normal. Brain MRI was normal. Lower leg MRI showed severe atrophy and fatty replacement, predominantly of the soleus muscles, with relative sparing of the tibialis posterior and lateral gastrocnemius muscles; this pattern was consistent with length-dependent axonal degeneration. Sural nerve biopsy from patient 1 showed nearly complete loss of large and medium-sized myelinated fibers without demyelination or onion bulbs. Vastus lateralis biopsy from patient 2 showed a few ragged-red fibers and subsarcolemmal accumulation of enlarged mitochondria. ⚠︎

Baumann et al. (2019) reported 5 patients from 2 unrelated families with juvenile- or adult-onset axonal sensorimotor neuropathy without hepatocerebral involvement.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CMT2EE in the families reported by Choi et al. (2015) and Baumann et al. (2019) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In a 21-year-old Pakistani man with axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy, leukoencephalopathy, and liver involvement, Blakely et al. (2012) sequenced the POLG, DGUOK, and MPV17 genes and identified a homozygous missense mutation in MPV17: pro98-to-leu (P98L). The paper gives the nucleotide change as c.298C-T in a figure legend and as c.2898C-T in the text. The mutation affected a residue conserved from human to yeast and had previously been reported in a patient with mtDNA depletion in the liver. Parental samples were not available.

In 2 Korean patients from 2 unrelated families with axonal CMT2EE, Choi et al. (2015) used whole-exome sequencing and identified a homozygous c.122G-A transition in the MPV17 gene, resulting in an arg41-to-gln (R41Q) substitution at a residue conserved across vertebrates. The mutation, confirmed by Sanger sequencing, was apparently inherited from each parent. It was not found in 300 controls or in 302 in-house exomes, but was present at very low frequency in the Exome Sequencing Project (0.00008) and ExAC (0.00002471) databases. In NSC34 mouse motor neuronal cells, siRNA knockdown of MPV17 significantly reduced cell proliferation and viability against reactive oxygen species and affected OXPHOS levels. Overexpression of the R41Q mutant significantly inhibited cell proliferation. Choi et al. (2015) suggested that both loss of normal MPV17 function and a detrimental effect of the mutant protein may contribute to neuronal dysfunction.

In 5 patients from 2 unrelated families with axonal sensorimotor neuropathy without hepatocerebral involvement, Baumann et al. (2019) identified homozygous mutations in the MPV17 gene. The affected members of the first family carried the c.122G-A (R41Q) mutation. Those of the second family carried a novel near-splice mutation (c.376-9T-G), which was shown to cause an in-frame deletion of 11 amino acids.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Choi et al. (2015) noted that both R41Q and the arg50-to-gln (R50Q) mutation found in Navajo neurohepatopathy affect residues in the matrix region between the first 2 transmembrane domains of MPV17, although the 2 mutations cause quite different clinical phenotypes. In NSC34 cells, overexpression of a different substitution at the same residue (R41W) also affected cell proliferation. From these data, Choi et al. (2015) proposed that mutations at arg41 predominantly cause peripheral neuropathy. ⚠︎

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CHARCOT-MARIE-TOOTH DISEASE, AXONAL, TYPE 2EE**  
MPV17, PRO98LEU [c.298C>T; p.Pro98Leu]

In a 21-year-old Pakistani man, born to nonconsanguineous parents, Blakely et al. (2012) identified a homozygous missense mutation in the MPV17 gene that resulted in a pro98-to-leu (P98L) substitution at a residue conserved from human to yeast. The paper gives the nucleotide change as c.298C-T in a figure legend and as c.2898C-T in the text. The patient had adult-presenting axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy, leukoencephalopathy, and liver involvement, along with multiple mtDNA deletions and focal COX deficiency in skeletal muscle. The mutation had previously been reported in a patient with hepatic mtDNA depletion. Parental samples were not available.

The mutation was found by whole-exome sequencing and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. Baumann et al. (2019) identified a homozygous c.122G-A (R41Q) mutation in the MPV17 gene in the affected members of a family with axonal sensorimotor neuropathy without hepatocerebral involvement.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that adult-onset autosomal dominant leukodystrophy without amyloid angiopathy (ADLDWA) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the CST3 gene.

### Description

Adult-onset autosomal dominant leukodystrophy without amyloid angiopathy (ADLDWA) is a white matter disorder. Patients have recurrent episodes of hemiplegic migraine with transient unilateral focal deficits, and motor symptoms and cognitive decline that progress slowly from mid to older adulthood. Brain imaging shows white matter changes at typical predilection sites, including the globus pallidus. Neuropathology shows cystic degeneration of the white matter without angiopathy (Bergner et al., 2024).

### Clinical Features

Bergner et al. (2024) described 16 patients from 8 families with an adult-onset leukodystrophy. Patients had recurrent episodes of hemiplegic migraine with transient unilateral focal deficits, and motor symptoms and cognitive decline that progressed slowly in mid to older adulthood. In some cases, acute-onset clinical deterioration led to a prolonged episode of reduced consciousness, and some patients died early. On imaging, the authors described changes they considered pathognomonic. These involved the deep cerebral white matter, sparing a periventricular rim and a rim directly beneath the cortex. Changes were also seen in the middle blade of the corpus callosum, the posterior limb of the internal capsule, the middle cerebellar peduncles, the cerebral peduncles, and specifically the globus pallidus. The clinical and radiologic features differed markedly from those of Icelandic cerebral amyloid angiopathy, which is caused by the leu68-to-asn substitution in CST3.

Zhang and Wu (2024) reported Chinese patients with adult-onset leukodystrophy caused by CST3 variants.

### Inheritance

Bergner et al. (2024) found that ADLDWA is caused by dominant CST3 variants. This is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Bergner et al. (2024) studied 16 patients with ADLDWA from 8 families. Each patient carried 1 of 4 different dominant stop-gain or frameshift variants in the CST3 gene.

Zhang and Wu (2024) reported CST3 variants in Chinese patients with adult-onset leukodystrophy.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Bergner et al. (2024) noted that the stop-gain and frameshift CST3 variants in ADLDWA caused a clinical and radiologic phenotype that differed markedly from Icelandic cerebral amyloid angiopathy. That disorder is caused by the leu68-to-asn substitution in CST3.

### Pathogenesis

Bergner et al. (2024) studied 2 autopsy cases. Neither showed angiopathy. Instead, the white matter showed micro- to macrocystic degeneration. At early stages, astrocytes were activated. Later, they showed severe degeneration and loss. Despite the loss of myelin, there were elevated numbers of oligodendrocytes, some of which were apoptotic. The authors compared the structures of the variants and suggested that specific truncations of cystatin C cause abnormal function, possibly by making the protein more prone to aggregation. They noted that this proposed effect still needed to be confirmed.

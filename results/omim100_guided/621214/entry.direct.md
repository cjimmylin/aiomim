### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that adult-onset autosomal dominant leukodystrophy without amyloid angiopathy (ADLDWA) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the CST3 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Adult-onset autosomal dominant leukodystrophy without amyloid angiopathy (ADLDWA) is a white matter disorder. Patients have recurrent episodes of hemiplegic migraine with transient unilateral focal deficits, along with motor symptoms and cognitive decline that progress slowly in mid to older adulthood. Brain imaging shows characteristic white matter changes at typical predilection sites, including the globus pallidus. Neuropathology shows micro- to macrocystic white matter degeneration but no angiopathy (Bergner et al., 2024).

### Clinical Features

Bergner et al. (2024) reported 16 patients from 8 families with a novel adult-onset leukodystrophy. The clinical phenotype consisted of recurrent episodes of hemiplegic migraine associated with transient unilateral focal deficits, and slowly progressing motor symptoms and cognitive decline in mid to older adult ages. In some cases, an acute-onset clinical deterioration led to a prolonged episode with reduced consciousness and even early death. Bergner et al. (2024) considered the radiologic changes pathognomonic. These changes were found at typical predilection sites: the deep cerebral white matter, sparing a periventricular and a directly subcortical rim; the middle blade of the corpus callosum; the posterior limb of the internal capsule; the middle cerebellar peduncles; the cerebral peduncles; and specifically the globus pallidus. The clinical and radiologic features differed markedly from those of Icelandic cerebral amyloid angiopathy, which is found in patients carrying a leu68-to-asn (L68N) substitution in CST3.

Zhang and Wu (2024) reported Chinese patients with adult-onset leukodystrophy caused by CST3 variants.

### Inheritance

Bergner et al. (2024) found that the CST3 variants in families with ADLDWA acted in a dominant manner, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Bergner et al. (2024) studied 16 patients with ADLDWA from 8 families. Each patient carried 1 of 4 different dominant stop-gain or frameshift variants in the CST3 gene.

Zhang and Wu (2024) reported Chinese patients with adult-onset leukodystrophy caused by CST3 variants.

### Pathogenesis

Bergner et al. (2024) studied 2 autopsy cases. Histopathologic examination did not reveal angiopathy, but instead showed micro- to macrocystic degeneration of the white matter. Astrocytes were activated at early stages and later showed severe degeneration and loss. Although myelin was lost, the numbers of oligodendrocytes, some of them apoptotic, were elevated. In a structural comparison of the CST3 variants, Bergner et al. (2024) suggested that specific truncations of cystatin C result in abnormal protein function, possibly by making the protein more prone to aggregation. They noted that future studies were needed to confirm this effect.

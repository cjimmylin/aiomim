### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia-2 (SIDBA2) is caused by mutation in the SLC25A38 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia-2 (SIDBA2) is an autosomal recessive nonsyndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia. The sideroblastic anemias are defined by ringed sideroblasts, which are bone marrow erythroid precursors with pathologic iron deposits within mitochondria (Guernsey et al., 2009). Affected individuals have had anemia from birth that did not respond to pyridoxine and needed transfusions (Jardine et al., 1994). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Manabe et al. (1982) described a female infant with congenital refractory sideroblastic anemia that did not respond to pyridoxine therapy.

Jardine et al. (1994) reported a brother and sister, born to unaffected parents, who had transfusion-dependent, pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia from birth. Their hemoglobin levels were 4.3 and 6.4 g/dl, respectively. The mother did not have a dimorphic erythrocyte population.

### Biochemical Features

In a female infant with congenital refractory sideroblastic anemia, Manabe et al. (1982) found markedly reduced delta-aminolevulinic acid (ALA) synthetase activity in erythroblasts, both with and without pyridoxal phosphate. Erythroblast mitochondrial neutral protease activity was normal. This protease specifically inactivates the apo form of ALA synthetase, and the sensitivity of apo-ALA synthetase to the protease was also normal. The authors suggested that the reduced ALA synthetase activity resulted from impaired production of the enzyme rather than rapid destruction, which could explain why the patient did not respond to pyridoxine.

In a sister from a family with pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia, Jardine et al. (1994) measured delta-aminolevulinate synthase activity in erythroblasts from fractionated marrow at 135 pmol delta-aminolevulinate formed/10(6) erythroblasts/hour. The normal range was 110 to 650 pmol.

### Inheritance

Jardine et al. (1994) concluded that the sideroblastic anemia in the family they studied was very likely an autosomal recessive trait. The affected brother and sister had unaffected parents and had inherited different maternal alleles at the ALAS2 locus. The mother showed random X inactivation in her peripheral leukocytes, indicating that she did not carry an X-linked sideroblastic anemia mutation. Guernsey et al. (2009) established autosomal recessive inheritance of this form of nonsyndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Guernsey et al. (2009) identified the locus for autosomal recessive nonsyndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia by positional cloning.

### Heterogeneity

Jardine et al. (1994) excluded the ALAS2 gene at Xp11.21 as the cause of disease in a family with pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia. Sequencing ALAS2 in the affected son found no mutation in the coding region, the intron/exon boundaries, or the 1-kb 5-prime promoter region. They found a G-to-A transition polymorphism 220 nucleotides 3-prime of the AATAAA polyadenylation signal and used it to follow inheritance of the locus. Using mismatch PCR followed by XmnI restriction analysis of 148 alleles, they estimated the frequency of this polymorphism at 25%. The 2 affected sibs had inherited different maternal ALAS2 alleles.

### Molecular Genetics

Using positional cloning, Guernsey et al. (2009) found that autosomal recessive nonsyndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia was associated with mutations in the SLC25A38 gene. SLC25A38 encodes an erythroid-specific mitochondrial carrier family protein. The authors showed that SLC25A38 is important for heme biosynthesis in eukaryotes.

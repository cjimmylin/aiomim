## SYNDACTYLY, TYPE III; SDTY3

*Alternative titles; symbols*

SYNDACTYLY OF FINGERS IV AND V  
RING AND LITTLE FINGER SYNDACTYLY

### TEXT

This entry describes syndactyly type III (SDTY3), also called syndactyly of the ring and little fingers. In the supplied reports it was mapped to chromosome 6q22-q24. ⚠︎

### Description

Type III syndactyly is fusion between the ring and little fingers (fingers 4 and 5). It occurs as an isolated, autosomal dominant hand malformation, and expression is variable (McKiernan and McCann, 1993; De Smet et al., 1994). Type III syndactyly is also a feature of oculodentodigital dysplasia (ODD), and several authors have asked whether the 2 conditions form a single clinical spectrum (Brueton et al., 1990; Schrander-Stumpel et al., 1993; Gladwin et al., 1997).

### Clinical Features

Johnston and Kirby (1955) reported syndactyly of the ring and little fingers.

Opjordsmoen and Nyberg-Hansen (1980) reported a family from northern Norway in which 9 of 22 members across 3 generations had spastic paraplegia, neurogenic bladder disturbances, and syndactyly. The bladder disturbance was an incomplete supranuclear bladder paresis (uninhibited neurogenic bladder). It was the main complaint and appeared early in the disease. The authors thought the family probably represented an unusual form of hereditary spastic paraplegia.

Brueton et al. (1990) described a family with type III syndactyly and facial features resembling those of oculodentodigital dysplasia. None of the other characteristic findings of ODD were present.

Schrander-Stumpel et al. (1993) reported a newborn boy with complete cutaneous syndactyly of fingers 3 to 5 on the left hand and fingers 4 and 5 on the right hand. His mother and maternal grandmother were born with the same hand malformation. The mother had a thin nose and small teeth. The grandmother had very small teeth with enamel hypoplasia. Both women had normal eyes. The authors considered the family evidence of variable expression in oculodentodigital dysplasia.

McKiernan and McCann (1993) reported a pedigree with 14 cases of familial syndactyly of the ring and little fingers. Expression of the phenotype varied. Four of the affected individuals had been treated surgically.

De Smet et al. (1994) described 5 new cases of isolated syndactyly of the ring and small fingers. Brachymesophalangy of the fifth finger was a frequent association.

### Inheritance

Analysis of a large pedigree by McKiernan and McCann (1993) confirmed autosomal dominant inheritance of type III syndactyly. De Smet et al. (1994) also observed autosomal dominant inheritance. Schrander-Stumpel et al. (1993) found autosomal dominant inheritance in a 3-generation family with type III syndactyly and features of ODD.

### Mapping

Gladwin et al. (1997) carried out a genome search and mapped the oculodentodigital syndrome locus to chromosome 6q22-q24 (maximum pairwise lod score of 9.37 at theta = 0.001). Their analysis of a large family with type III syndactyly and atypical facial features suggested that isolated type III syndactyly maps to the same region.

### Heterogeneity

Brueton et al. (1990) and Schrander-Stumpel et al. (1993) discussed whether type III syndactyly and oculodentodigital dysplasia are separate genetic entities or parts of one clinical spectrum. Schrander-Stumpel et al. (1993) interpreted their family as showing variable expression of ODD. Paznekas et al. (2003) found mutations in the GJA1 (connexin-43) gene in all 17 families with ODD they screened, and noted that type III syndactyly occurs in some cases of ODD. Richardson et al. (2004) reported that expression of Gja1 correlated with the phenotype seen in oculodentodigital syndrome/type III syndactyly. ⚠︎

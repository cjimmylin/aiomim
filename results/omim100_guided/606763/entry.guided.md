### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the DNAAF3 gene on chromosome 19q13.

### Description

Primary ciliary dyskinesia is an autosomal recessive disorder characterized by chronic sinusitis, bronchiectasis, and subfertility, caused by defective ciliary function. Situs inversus occurs in about half of cases (Kartagener syndrome) (Meeks et al., 2000). CILD2 is characterized by immotile respiratory cilia that lack both outer and inner dynein arms, and it is caused by mutation in DNAAF3, a cytoplasmic factor needed to preassemble axonemal dyneins (Mitchison et al., 2012).

### Clinical Features

Meeks et al. (2000) studied 5 Arabic families with primary ciliary dyskinesia.

Mitchison et al. (2012) reported 10 patients with PCD from 3 consanguineous families. Two families were of Arabic origin and had been included in the linkage study by Meeks et al. (2000); the third was a UK-Pakistani family. One family included affected monozygotic twins. All patients had classic symptoms, including neonatal respiratory distress, recurrent cough and wheezing, nasal discharge, chest infections, sinusitis, otitis media, nasal polyps, and bronchiectasis. Four of the 10 patients had situs inversus. In nasal brushing biopsies, cilia were immotile in all affected family members. Electron microscopy of respiratory cilia showed reduced or absent inner and outer dynein arms. On immunofluorescence analysis of respiratory epithelial cells, the outer dynein arm proteins DNAH5, DNAH9, and DNAI2 and the inner dynein arm light chain DNALI1 were absent from patient cilia. In some patients, residual DNAH5 and DNALI1 were seen in the apical cytoplasm. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Meeks et al. (2000) described primary ciliary dyskinesia as an autosomal recessive condition. In the families reported by Mitchison et al. (2012), all affected individuals were homozygous for DNAAF3 mutations and unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous carriers, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Mapping

Meeks et al. (2000) performed a genome scan in 5 Arabic families with PCD. Using GENEHUNTER, they obtained a maximum multipoint lod score (HLOD) of 4.4 on chromosome 19q13.3-qter at alpha (proportion of linked families) = 0.7. Recombinations at D19S572 and D19S218 defined a 15-cM critical region. The authors stated that these data confirmed locus heterogeneity.

### Molecular Genetics

Mitchison et al. (2012) found homozygous mutations in the DNAAF3 gene in affected members of 2 of the 3 consanguineous Arabic-origin families previously linked to the CILD2 locus by Meeks et al. (2000). In both families, affected individuals had large regions of marker homozygosity across the DNAAF3 locus. Family UCL89 carried a c.323T-C transition in exon 3 resulting in a leu108-to-pro (L108P) substitution at a highly conserved residue. Family UCL66/67 carried a c.406C-T transition in exon 4 resulting in an arg136-to-ter (R136X) substitution. Neither variant was found in 148 population-matched Arabic control chromosomes. The authors then sequenced DNAAF3 in 112 more PCD families. In a UK-Pakistani family (UCL71) with first-cousin parents and evidence of linkage to chromosome 19, they found a homozygous 1-bp insertion (c.762_763insT) in exon 6. This insertion causes a frameshift and premature termination (Val255CysfsTer12). It was not found in 172 UK-Pakistani control chromosomes. In all 3 families, the mutations segregated with the disorder, and none was found in the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, or NHLBI-ESP databases. A homozygous c.973G-A variant (A325T) in a Belgian patient was considered likely to be a polymorphism: it was found in 1 of 192 Caucasian control chromosomes, it was predicted to be benign, and the residue is not conserved.

### Animal Model

Mitchison et al. (2012) found that the Chlamydomonas pf22 mutant, which has nonfunctional flagella, carries a nonsense mutation (Trp79Ter) in the ortholog of DNAAF3. The mutant failed to assemble outer dynein arms and some inner dynein arms. Tagged PF22 protein was found only in the cytoplasm. In zebrafish, morpholino knockdown of dnaaf3 caused completely static olfactory placode cilia with reduced or missing outer and inner dynein arms. Morphant embryos also showed body axis curvature, hydrocephalus, pronephric cysts, abnormal otolith number, and laterality defects; about half of dnaaf3 exon 8 morphants had reversed heart looping or no heart looping.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, LEU108PRO [c.323T>C; p.Leu108Pro]

In affected members of a consanguineous Arabic-origin family (UCL89) with primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2), Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous c.323T-C transition in exon 3 of the DNAAF3 gene, resulting in a leu108-to-pro (L108P) substitution at a residue that is highly conserved across ciliated species. The mutation segregated with the disorder, with unaffected parents and sibs being heterozygous. It was not found in 148 population-matched Arabic control chromosomes or in the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, or NHLBI-ESP databases. Patient respiratory cilia were immotile and lacked outer and inner dynein arms.

In affected members of an Arabic-origin family (UCL66/67) with primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2), Mitchison et al. The mutation segregated with the disorder and was not found in 148 population-matched Arabic control chromosomes or in the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, or NHLBI-ESP databases.

**.0003 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, 1-BP INS, 762T [c.762_763insT; p.Val255CysfsX12]

In affected members of a UK-Pakistani family (UCL71) with primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2) whose parents were first cousins, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous 1-bp insertion (c.762_763insT) in exon 6 of the DNAAF3 gene. The insertion causes a frameshift: a val255-to-cys change is followed by 11 novel amino acids and a premature stop codon (Val255CysfsTer12). The mutation segregated with the disorder and was not found in 172 UK-Pakistani control chromosomes or in the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, or NHLBI-ESP databases.

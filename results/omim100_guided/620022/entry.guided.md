### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Stickler syndrome type VI (STL6) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the COL9A3 gene on chromosome 20q13.3.

### Description

Stickler syndrome type VI (STL6) is an autosomal recessive connective tissue disorder. All reported patients have had high myopia and sensorineural hearing loss. Skeletal involvement varies and includes epiphyseal or spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia, and some patients have vitreoretinal degeneration and midface hypoplasia (Hanson-Kahn et al., 2018; Markova et al., 2021; Rad et al., 2022).

### Clinical Features

Faletra et al. (2014) described a family with autosomal recessive Stickler syndrome. Hanson-Kahn et al. (2018) summarized this family as 3 sibs with moderate to severe sensorineural hearing loss, high myopia, normal vitreous, midface hypoplasia, tibial rotation, mild irregularities of the capital femoral and metacarpal epiphyses, and pes planus. The sibs also had moderate to severe intellectual disability. According to Rad et al. (2022), the family was Moroccan, the parents were first cousins, and the sibs were 4, 11, and 16 years old.

Hanson-Kahn et al. (2018) reported a boy with STL6 whose parents were third cousins from India. He failed his newborn hearing screen. At 5 months he had moderate to severe sensorineural hearing loss on the right and severe loss on the left, and the hearing loss remained stable. CT showed mild enlargement of the vestibular aqueducts. He had no myopia at 5 months, but by 2 years his myopia measured -6.75 OD and -8 OS, and by 12 years it had progressed to -10.75 OD and -11.25 OS. No vitreous abnormalities were found. Femoral and tibial bowing were present at birth. Radiographs at 6 years showed slightly flattened proximal femoral epiphyses, flattening of the acetabula, and mild platyspondyly. Growth and intelligence were normal. Speech was initially delayed, which was attributed to the hearing loss.

Nixon et al. (2019) studied 2 patients from 1 family with recessive Stickler syndrome due to COL9A3 variants, as part of a study of 4 families with type IX collagen defects. All patients in the study were highly myopic, with congenital megalophthalmos and abnormal, hypoplastic vitreous gel, and all had sensorineural hearing loss. One patient had severe arthropathy. Markova et al. (2021) and Rad et al. (2022) noted that the older COL9A3 sib was 20 years old, had refractions up to -23 D, had severe arthropathy of the shoulders and hips with operated scoliosis, and used a wheelchair. The younger sib had none of these skeletal signs. Rad et al. (2022) stated that Nixon et al. (2019) had observed mild STL features in carrier parents.

Markova et al. (2021) reported a Russian boy with STL6 born to nonconsanguineous parents. The pregnancy was complicated by oligohydramnios and fetal growth retardation. At 1 month he had hip dysplasia with bilateral absence of ossification centers, and at 3 months he had congenital high bilateral myopia. Speech was delayed. Bilateral sensorineural hearing loss of grade 3-4 was diagnosed at 11 months. Radiographs showed spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia with spina bifida occulta of the sacral vertebrae, mild platyspondyly, kyphosis, flattened and irregular femoral heads, coxa valga, and flattened distal femoral epiphyses. Eye examination showed peripheral pigment rearrangement, small dystrophic foci, vitreous floaters, and a preretinal membrane. At 2 years and 5 months his height was in the 25th to 50th percentile. He had genu valgum, a waddling gait, pain in the lower limb joints, a flattened nasal bridge, a small nose, mild midface hypoplasia, a high palate, and planovalgus feet. The authors noted that this patient had a more severe phenotype, with early-onset arthropathy and vitreoretinal degeneration. ⚠︎

Rad et al. (2022) reported 5 patients with STL6 from 3 unrelated consanguineous Iranian families. In family 1, a 28-year-old woman had high myopia and vitreoretinal degeneration with empty vitreous, multiple lattice degenerations, and retinal pigmentary changes. She also had severe progressive sensorineural hearing loss and knee pain, but joint examination and radiographs were normal and she lacked typical craniofacial features. In family 2, 2 sibs aged 65 and 57 years had recurrent rhegmatogenous retinal detachments resulting from advanced vitreoretinal degeneration, which left them with little or no vision despite surgery. Both sibs had severe progressive sensorineural hearing loss and herniated cervical discs. In family 3, a boy aged 11.8 years and his sister aged 3.1 years had myopia, congenital moderate to severe progressive sensorineural hearing loss, spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia, pes planus, depressed nasal bridge, and anteverted nares. Midface hypoplasia and downslanting palpebral fissures were more pronounced in the sister. Radiographs of the boy showed short metacarpals, a broad big toe, genu valgum, and irregular femoral epiphyses. None of the patients had intellectual disability. Rad et al. (2022) concluded that high myopia and moderate to severe hearing loss were consistent features of STL6, whereas skeletal findings varied.

### Inheritance

Faletra et al. (2014) reported autosomal recessive inheritance of Stickler syndrome due to COL9A3 mutation. Hanson-Kahn et al. (2018) supported this pattern: the affected child of consanguineous parents was homozygous for the mutation, and both parents and an unaffected sib were heterozygous.

### Molecular Genetics

In a family with autosomal recessive Stickler syndrome, Faletra et al. (2014) identified a loss-of-function mutation in the COL9A3 gene (c.1176_1198del; p.Gln393Cysfs*25). Hanson-Kahn et al. (2018) and Rad et al. (2022) described the 3 affected sibs as homozygous for this mutation.

Exome sequencing by Hanson-Kahn et al. (2018) showed that a boy with STL6, born to consanguineous parents, was homozygous for a 1-bp duplication (c.650dupC; NM_001853.3) in exon 13 of the COL9A3 gene. The mutation was predicted to cause a frameshift, premature termination, and absence of the gene product. It was confirmed by Sanger sequencing, was not found in public SNP databases, and both parents and an unaffected sib were heterozygous carriers. Earlier testing of specific exons of COL9A1, COL9A2, and COL9A3 had been negative.

Nixon et al. (2019) identified homozygous COL9A3 variants in 2 patients from 1 family with recessive Stickler syndrome. Rad et al. (2022) listed the variant as a homozygous nonsense mutation (c.1411C-T; R471X) in exon 28.

Using targeted sequencing of 166 skeletal disorder genes, Markova et al. (2021) found that a Russian boy with STL6 was compound heterozygous for 2 nonsense mutations in the COL9A3 gene (NM_001853.4): c.268C-T (R90X) in exon 5 and c.1729C-T (R577X) in exon 30. Sanger sequencing of the parents showed that the mutations were in trans. The healthy sibs did not carry pathogenic COL9A3 variants.

Using exome sequencing in 3 unrelated consanguineous Iranian families with STL6, Rad et al. (2022) identified 3 different homozygous loss-of-function mutations in the COL9A3 gene (NM_001853.3). The mutations were a 10-bp deletion (c.107_116del; p.Pro36Argfs*49) in exon 2, a nonsense mutation (c.1204C-T; R402X) in exon 23, and a 1-bp deletion (c.355delC; p.Leu119Serfs*9) in exon 7. Segregation was studied by Sanger sequencing in available family members. The 10-bp deletion had an allele frequency of 0.00001390 in gnomAD (2 carriers) and was absent from Iranome, GME, and 1000 Genomes. The other 2 mutations had not been reported in public databases.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that neurodevelopmental disorder with hypotonia, craniofacial abnormalities, and seizures (NEDHCS) is caused by homozygous mutation in the SNIP1 gene (608241) on chromosome 1p34.3. ⚠︎

### Description

Neurodevelopmental disorder with hypotonia, craniofacial abnormalities, and seizures is an autosomal recessive disorder. Its main features are severe psychomotor retardation, hypotonia, seizures, and craniofacial dysmorphism. Puffenberger et al. (2012) described the disorder in Old Order Amish children.

### Clinical Features

Puffenberger et al. (2012) studied Old Order Amish children with severe psychomotor retardation, hypotonia, epilepsy, and craniofacial dysmorphism. They used genetic mapping and exome sequencing to study this disorder and several other new recessive disorders in Plain communities.

### Inheritance

Puffenberger et al. (2012) found homozygous SNIP1 mutations in affected children from Amish families, which is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected Old Order Amish children, Puffenberger et al. (2012) used homozygosity mapping and exome sequencing to identify a homozygous missense mutation in the SNIP1 gene (glu366-to-gly; E366G). For the disorders in their study, Puffenberger et al. (2012) stated that sibs and parents were also sequenced, when available, to confirm segregation of the allele within the family. They did not give segregation data specific to SNIP1.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that adult-onset autosomal dominant leukodystrophy without amyloid angiopathy (ADLDWA) is caused by heterozygous (dominant) stop-gain or frameshift mutation in the CST3 gene, which encodes cystatin C.

### Description

Adult-onset autosomal dominant leukodystrophy without amyloid angiopathy is a white matter disorder characterized by recurrent episodes of hemiplegic migraine with transient unilateral focal deficits, and by slowly progressive motor symptoms and cognitive decline in mid to older adulthood. Some patients have acute clinical deterioration with a prolonged episode of reduced consciousness, which can lead to early death. Brain MRI shows characteristic involvement of the deep cerebral white matter with sparing of a periventricular and directly subcortical rim, together with involvement of the globus pallidus and other predilection sites (Bergner et al., 2024).

### Clinical Features

Bergner et al. (2024) described 16 patients from 8 families with a novel adult-onset leukodystrophy. The clinical phenotype consisted of recurrent episodes of hemiplegic migraine associated with transient unilateral focal deficits, and slowly progressing motor symptoms and cognitive decline in mid to older adult ages. In some cases, acute-onset clinical deterioration led to a prolonged episode with reduced consciousness and even early death. Radiologically, pathognomonic changes were found at typical predilection sites involving the deep cerebral white matter, sparing a periventricular and directly subcortical rim, as well as the middle blade of the corpus callosum, the posterior limb of the internal capsule, the middle cerebellar peduncles, the cerebral peduncles, and specifically the globus pallidus. Bergner et al. (2024) noted that the clinical and radiologic features differed markedly from those of the previously described Icelandic cerebral amyloid angiopathy found in patients carrying the leu68-to-asn substitution in CST3.

Zhang and Wu (2024) reported Chinese patients with adult-onset leukodystrophy caused by CST3 variants.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of adult-onset leukodystrophy without amyloid angiopathy in the 8 families reported by Bergner et al. (2024) was consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In 16 patients from 8 families with adult-onset leukodystrophy without amyloid angiopathy, Bergner et al. (2024) identified 1 of 4 different dominant stop-gain or frameshift variants in the CST3 gene.

Zhang and Wu (2024) reported CST3 variants in Chinese patients with adult-onset leukodystrophy.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Bergner et al. (2024) noted that the clinical and radiologic features of the leukodystrophy caused by stop-gain or frameshift CST3 variants differed markedly from those of Icelandic cerebral amyloid angiopathy caused by the leu68-to-asn substitution in CST3, and that their 2 autopsy cases did not show angiopathy.

### Pathogenesis

Histopathologic characterization of 2 autopsy cases by Bergner et al. (2024) did not reveal angiopathy, but instead showed micro- to macrocystic degeneration of the white matter. Astrocytes were activated at early stages and later displayed severe degeneration and loss. Despite the loss of myelin, elevated numbers of partly apoptotic oligodendrocytes were observed. A structural comparison of the CST3 variants suggested that specific truncations of cystatin C result in an abnormal function, possibly by rendering the protein more prone to aggregation; Bergner et al. (2024) stated that future studies were required to confirm the assumed effect on the protein.

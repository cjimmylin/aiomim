### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that brain small vessel disease-4 (BSVD4) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the NIT1 gene.

### Description

Brain small vessel disease-4 (BSVD4) is an autosomal recessive cerebral small vessel disease caused by loss of NIT1 function. It is characterized by a triad of movement disorders, massively dilated basal ganglia perivascular spaces, and intracerebral hemorrhage, with onset in mid-adulthood (Rutten et al., 2024). Neuropsychiatric features, including psychosis and cognitive deterioration, cystic basal ganglia lesions, and a positive urine sulfite test reflecting urinary accumulation of deaminated glutathione have also been reported (Rendu et al., 2019). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Rendu et al. (2019) reported 3 brothers with neuropsychiatric disorders and cystic lesions of the basal ganglia. The index patient, a 34-year-old man with a history of nonsevere mental retardation and an acute psychotic episode followed by generalized seizures 2 years earlier, presented with a sudden pure motor left hemiparesis. CT and MRI showed bilateral small cystic lesions affecting the basal ganglia; arteriography, EEG, EMG, Doppler ultrasound, and cardiac echocardiography were normal. A dipstick urine test for sulfites was positive. Over a 20-year period his condition progressively worsened, with severe neuropsychological deterioration and spastic tetraplegia, and he died at age 57 years. A brother, a 58-year-old man with diabetes mellitus who had lived an active professional life, was admitted for sudden coma with cardiovascular collapse; CT and MRI showed the same abnormalities, and the urine sulfite test was positive. He recovered progressively but had neuropsychological deterioration (BREF 14/18, MMSE 22/30). A third brother, institutionalized for schizophrenia diagnosed 40 years earlier, had a normal clinical examination but the same brain lesions on CT and MRI and a positive urine sulfite test. Rendu et al. (2019) noted that the cavities differed from cavitary lesions commonly reported in adults because of their basal ganglia location, and that sulfite oxidase deficiency and molybdenum cofactor deficiency had been excluded.

Rutten et al. (2024) studied 7 patients with NIT1-related small vessel disease from 5 unrelated pedigrees, including 3 sibs and 4 single cases. Patients presented in mid-adulthood with movement disorders. All had striking brain MRI abnormalities, with numerous and massively dilated basal ganglia perivascular spaces. Three patients had nonlobar intracerebral hemorrhage between ages 45 and 60 years, which was fatal in 2. Brain autopsy showed large electron-dense deposits in the walls of small and medium-sized cerebral arteries.

### Biochemical Features

Rendu et al. (2019) found that the 3 affected brothers had a positive urine sulfite dipstick test, whereas the mother and unaffected sibs tested were negative. Urinary organic acid analysis by GC/MS detected a compound identified as a deaminated glutathione (dGSH) derivative, with a mass spectrum similar to that found in the urine of Nit1-knockout mice, in the 3 affected brothers but not in the other sibs tested or the mother. Rendu et al. (2019) suggested that dGSH accumulation was probably responsible for the positive urine sulfite test. In fibroblasts from 1 patient, cytoplasmic Cu/Zn SOD activity was unchanged, whereas mitochondrial MnSOD activity was increased about 2-fold compared with controls.

In patients studied by Rutten et al. (2024), metabolic analysis of urine confirmed loss of NIT1 enzymatic function.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of BSVD4 in the families reported by Rendu et al. (2019) and Rutten et al. (2024) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

In 2 affected brothers from a family with cystic basal ganglia lesions and neuropsychiatric disorders, Rendu et al. (2019) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the NIT1 gene: a c.457G-A transition, resulting in a gly153-to-arg (G153R) substitution, and a 1-bp duplication (c.670dupA), resulting in a frameshift and premature termination (Thr224AsnfsTer41). The mutations were found by exome sequencing. Cloning of a PCR fragment from 1 patient showed that the variants were on different alleles. Asymptomatic sibs whose DNA was available had neither variant; according to the text, the c.670dupA variant was inherited from the asymptomatic mother, although the accompanying table listed the mother as heterozygous for c.457G-A and negative for c.670dupA. The father's DNA was unavailable, and the index patient was not genotyped. The c.457G-A variant affects the last nucleotide of exon 4, and sequencing of the NIT1 transcript from a patient skin biopsy showed that it affected splicing. NIT1 protein was undetectable on Western blot of patient fibroblasts.

Using exome sequencing, Rutten et al. (2024) identified biallelic NIT1 mutations in 7 patients from 5 unrelated pedigrees. Three sibs were compound heterozygous for a c.727C-T transition, resulting in an arg243-to-trp (R243W) substitution, and a 2-bp deletion (c.198_199del), resulting in a premature termination codon (Ala68Ter). The 4 other patients, single cases from 4 unrelated pedigrees, were homozygous for the R243W mutation. Western blot of patient fibroblasts showed absence of NIT1 protein.

### Pathogenesis

Rendu et al. (2019) noted that NIT1 encodes an amidase required for recycling deaminated glutathione (dGSH) into 2-ketoglutarate and cysteinylglycine, and that Nit1-knockout mice accumulate dGSH and excrete it in urine. They proposed that loss of NIT1 function in patients leads to dGSH accumulation and oxidative stress at the cellular level, supported by increased mitochondrial SOD activity in patient fibroblasts, and that further studies were needed to determine whether dGSH toxicity and/or oxidative stress underlie the neuronal dysfunction.

### Diagnosis

Rendu et al. (2019) suggested that the urine sulfite test could easily be used to screen for the disorder in cohorts of psychiatric patients.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 BRAIN SMALL VESSEL DISEASE 4**  
NIT1, GLY153ARG [c.457G>A; p.Gly153Arg]

In 2 brothers with BSVD4, Rendu et al. (2019) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the NIT1 gene: a c.457G-A transition, resulting in a gly153-to-arg (G153R) substitution at a highly conserved residue, and a 1-bp duplication (c.670dupA). The c.457G-A change affects the last nucleotide of exon 4 and was shown by transcript sequencing from a patient skin biopsy to affect splicing. NIT1 protein was undetectable in patient fibroblasts.

**.0002 BRAIN SMALL VESSEL DISEASE 4**

The variants were shown to be on different alleles, and according to the text the c.670dupA variant was inherited from the asymptomatic mother.

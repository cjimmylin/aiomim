### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive limb-girdle muscular dystrophy-21 (LGMDR21) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the POGLUT1 gene (615618) on chromosome 3q13.

### Description

Autosomal recessive limb-girdle muscular dystrophy-21 (LGMDR21) is a progressive myopathy affecting mainly the proximal muscles of the limbs. Muscle biopsies show dystrophic changes, fewer satellite cells, and reduced glycosylation of alpha-dystroglycan. The disorder is caused by loss of the protein O-glucosyltransferase POGLUT1, which glucosylates Notch receptors, and is associated with reduced Notch signaling in muscle. ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

This disorder was first designated limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 2Z (LGMD2Z). Under the revised LGMD nomenclature it is LGMDR21. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Servian-Morilla et al. (2016) reported a consanguineous family with an autosomal recessive limb-girdle muscular dystrophy that began in adulthood. Affected members had progressive proximal muscle weakness. Muscle biopsies showed dystrophic features, reduced alpha-dystroglycan glycosylation, and fewer satellite cells.

Servian-Morilla et al. (2020) reported more patients with biallelic POGLUT1 mutations. These patients had a myopathy with variable age at onset, mainly affecting the limb-girdle muscles. Muscle MRI showed a distinctive pattern of involvement: inner (medial) parts of the thigh muscles were affected, while outer parts were relatively spared. Biopsies again showed fewer satellite cells and alpha-dystroglycan hypoglycosylation. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of LGMDR21 in the family reported by Servian-Morilla et al. (2016) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of a consanguineous family with LGMD, Servian-Morilla et al. (2016) found a homozygous missense mutation in the POGLUT1 gene, asp233-to-glu (D233E). In functional studies, the mutant enzyme had reduced O-glucosyltransferase activity on Notch. Patient muscle and cells showed reduced NOTCH1 signaling, fewer satellite cells, and abnormal myogenic differentiation. These findings suggested that impaired Notch signaling and loss of satellite cells contribute to the disease. ⚠︎

Servian-Morilla et al. (2020) found homozygous or compound heterozygous POGLUT1 mutations in further patients with LGMD.

### Pathogenesis

Servian-Morilla et al. (2016) proposed that reduced O-glucosylation of Notch by mutant POGLUT1 lowers Notch signaling. This would deplete the muscle satellite cell pool and impair muscle regeneration. They also observed reduced glycosylation of alpha-dystroglycan in patient muscle.

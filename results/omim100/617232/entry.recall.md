### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive limb-girdle muscular dystrophy-21 (LGMDR21) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the POGLUT1 gene (615618) on chromosome 3q13.33.

### Description

Autosomal recessive limb-girdle muscular dystrophy-21 (LGMDR21) is a progressive myopathy affecting mainly the proximal muscles of the limbs (Servián-Morilla et al., 2016). Muscle biopsies show dystrophic changes, fewer satellite cells, and reduced glycosylation of alpha-dystroglycan. Servián-Morilla et al. (2016) found that the disorder results from mutation in POGLUT1, a protein O-glucosyltransferase that adds O-glucose to EGF repeats found in Notch receptors. The mutation they identified greatly reduced the O-glucosyltransferase activity of POGLUT1 and was associated with reduced Notch signaling in patient muscle.

### Nomenclature

Servián-Morilla et al. (2020) noted that this disorder has been named both limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 2Z (LGMD2Z) and LGMD R21 POGLUT1-related.

### Clinical Features

Servián-Morilla et al. (2016) reported a consanguineous family with an autosomal recessive limb-girdle muscular dystrophy that began in adulthood, during the third decade. Affected members had progressive proximal muscle weakness. Muscle biopsies showed dystrophic features, reduced alpha-dystroglycan glycosylation, and fewer satellite cells.

Servián-Morilla et al. (2020) reported additional patients with biallelic POGLUT1 mutations. All of them had limb-girdle muscle weakness, and age at onset varied, ranging from congenital to adult onset. On muscle imaging, fatty degeneration followed an 'inside-to-outside' pattern, with the inner region of each muscle in the anterior and posterior compartments of the thigh most affected. Muscle biopsies showed fewer satellite cells and alpha-dystroglycan hypoglycosylation.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of LGMDR21 in the family reported by Servián-Morilla et al. (2016) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Servián-Morilla et al. (2016) studied affected members of a consanguineous family with LGMD and found a homozygous missense mutation in the POGLUT1 gene, asp233-to-glu (D233E). In functional studies, the mutant enzyme had reduced O-glucosyltransferase activity toward EGF repeats from Notch1. Patient muscle showed reduced Notch1 signaling and fewer satellite cells. Patient myoblasts showed abnormal myogenic differentiation. Servián-Morilla et al. (2016) suggested that a key disease mechanism is Notch-dependent loss of satellite cells.

Servián-Morilla et al. (2020) found homozygous or compound heterozygous POGLUT1 mutations in additional patients with LGMD. The disease segregated with the homozygous or compound heterozygous state in affected and unaffected relatives.

### Pathogenesis

Servián-Morilla et al. (2016) proposed that reduced O-glucosylation of Notch by mutant POGLUT1 lowers Notch signaling, which depletes the muscle satellite cell pool and impairs muscle regeneration. They also observed reduced alpha-dystroglycan glycosylation in patient muscle.

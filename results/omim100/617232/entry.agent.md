### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive limb-girdle muscular dystrophy-21 (LGMDR21) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the POGLUT1 gene on chromosome 3q.

### Description

Autosomal recessive limb-girdle muscular dystrophy-21 (LGMDR21) is a muscular dystrophy with proximal limb weakness that predominates in the lower limbs. Onset is usually in adulthood but can be infantile or congenital. Serum creatine kinase is usually normal. Servián-Morilla et al. (2020) found that muscle imaging showed a distinctive 'inside-to-outside' pattern of fatty degeneration in the thigh muscles. Muscle biopsies showed reduced glycosylation of alpha-dystroglycan, reduced NOTCH1 intracellular domain, and fewer satellite cells.

### Nomenclature

At the 229th ENMC international workshop, Straub et al. (2018) reformed the nomenclature and classification of the limb-girdle muscular dystrophies. Servián-Morilla et al. (2020) noted that the POGLUT1-related disorder had been named LGMD2Z, or LGMD R21 POGLUT1-related.

### Clinical Features

Servián-Morilla et al. (2016) studied a consanguineous family from southern Spain with 17 members across 3 generations. Four of 5 sibs in generation II had limb-girdle muscular dystrophy, with weakness mainly in the proximal lower limbs and onset in the third decade. The disease progressed, leading to scapular winging and wheelchair confinement. Serum creatine kinase was normal in 3 patients and mildly elevated in 1. Muscle biopsies from all 4 affected sibs ranged from very mild myopathic changes to classic dystrophic pathology. Of the proteins typically affected in myopathies, only alpha-dystroglycan was reduced. Leg muscle MRI showed early fatty replacement of the internal regions of the thigh muscles, sparing the external areas. This 'from inside-to-outside' degeneration progressed over the years and did not match patterns seen in other muscular dystrophies. Transmission electron microscopy showed normal muscle ultrastructure and normal basement membrane compaction. The patients had no skin abnormalities.

Servián-Morilla et al. (2020) reported 15 patients from 9 unrelated families, including the original family. The families came from Spain, Bulgaria, Italy, Germany, the USA, Iran, and the United Arab Emirates, and 4 of the 9 families were consanguineous. One patient had a congenital presentation, 2 had infantile onset before 3 years of age, and 12 had adult onset. Of the adult-onset patients, 58.3% began in the second decade, 25% in the third, and 16.6% in the fourth to fifth decades. Weakness was proximal, clearly predominant in the lower limbs, and slowly progressive. Seven patients (46.6%) with onset in their teens used a wheelchair between the third and fifth decades. Mild to moderate scapular winging was present in 11 of the 15 patients. The congenital case presented with hypotonia and proximal lower limb weakness after gait acquisition. He later developed mild weakness, wasting, and contractures of the upper limbs, with mild facial weakness, ptosis, and nasal voice. Six patients had mild to severe respiratory involvement from the mid-forties onward, and 2 of them needed nighttime ventilatory support. No significant bulbar, cardiac, or other systemic manifestations were found. In the text, serum CK was normal except in 4 patients with mild elevation (2 to 6 times normal). However, the clinical table listed elevated CK in more patients, up to 10 times normal. The table also noted a patient with infantile onset (family 9) who had nystagmus and retinitis pigmentosa. For 2 of the 4 originally reported sibs, the table gave onset in the second decade. ⚠︎

Muscle MRI or CT of the lower limbs was available for 11 of the 15 patients reported by Servián-Morilla et al. (2020), and all showed the 'from inside-to-outside' pattern of fatty degeneration. The pattern specifically involved the vastus lateralis, medialis, and intermedius, the posterior thigh compartment, and the gastrocnemius. It was clearest in early disease but could still be seen in the vastus lateralis at end stage. The lower leg muscles were better preserved than the thigh muscles. Biopsies from the vastus lateralis were dystrophic. Biopsies from the biceps brachii showed mild myopathic features, with lobulated fibers in some cases.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of LGMDR21 is autosomal recessive. Servián-Morilla et al. (2016) found a homozygous mutation in affected sibs from a consanguineous family. Servián-Morilla et al. (2020) found homozygous or compound heterozygous POGLUT1 variants that segregated with the disease in 9 families, and no asymptomatic carriers were identified.

### Mapping

In the Spanish family, Servián-Morilla et al. (2016) carried out genomewide SNP genotyping. They found a single region of homozygosity identical by descent, over 500 kb, that was shared by the 4 affected sibs and absent in their healthy sister. The region spanned 14.6 Mb on chromosome 3q13.13-q21.2 and contained 112 protein-coding genes, including POGLUT1.

### Molecular Genetics

Servián-Morilla et al. (2016) performed whole-exome sequencing in 2 affected sibs and their healthy sister from the consanguineous Spanish family. In the POGLUT1 gene they found a homozygous c.699T-G transversion, resulting in an asp233-to-glu (D233E) substitution, and the variant segregated with the disease. It was absent from the ExAC database (60,698 individuals) and from 919 DNA samples from 57 populations. The mutation did not affect POGLUT1 expression in muscle or its localization in the endoplasmic reticulum. The purified mutant enzyme had much lower O-glucosyltransferase activity than wildtype toward an EGF repeat from human factor IX and toward 5 EGF repeats from mouse Notch1. It also had lower O-xylosyltransferase activity but kept some residual enzymatic activity. In Drosophila lacking Rumi (the fly POGLUT1) enzymatic activity, wildtype human POGLUT1 rescued the muscle defects, whereas the D233E mutant gave only weak and variable rescue.

Servián-Morilla et al. (2020) studied 15 patients with LGMDR21 from 9 families. Sequencing used gene panels or whole-exome sequencing, and all variants were confirmed by Sanger sequencing. Across the cohort they identified 9 missense variants and 1 nonsense variant in POGLUT1, in homozygous or compound heterozygous state. Homozygous missense variants were found in patients from 6 families: I129T, R98W, Y57C, W308L, and C102F (all novel), and R183W, previously described as a Dowling-Degos disease mutation. Two affected sibs in family 8 were compound heterozygous for Y156N and a c.552G-A transition that creates a stop codon (W184X). In public databases, the novel variants were either absent or seen in the heterozygous state in 1 or 2 individuals out of more than 60,000 controls. ⚠︎

Servián-Morilla et al. (2020) gave conflicting genotypes for patient II.1 of family 2, who had the congenital form. The results text said this patient was homozygous for R279Q, a variant previously described in the heterozygous state in a patient with Dowling-Degos disease. The clinical table and the myoblast studies instead gave him as compound heterozygous for D233E and R279Q.

Servián-Morilla et al. (2020) tested the variants functionally. In HEK293T cells, the R183W and Y57C mutants were secreted normally, but the purified proteins had much lower O-glucosyltransferase activity than wildtype. The I129T, R98W, C102F, and W308L mutants were poorly secreted, suggesting that they destabilize the protein. These 4 mutants gave little or no rescue of Notch1 EGF1-18 secretion from POGLUT1-knockout HEK293T cells. In the Drosophila rumi myogenesis rescue assay, R183W, I129T, and Y57C gave partial rescue, similar to D233E, and C102F gave no rescue. Y156N was not tested. The authors predicted that W184X would be inactive because it removes the substrate-binding site.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

In the cohort reported by Servián-Morilla et al. (2020), there was no relevant variability in clinical features within or between families in the adult-onset group. The main variation across the whole cohort was in age at onset, which ranged from the first to the fifth decade. The congenital case, who carried D233E/R279Q, had a similar distribution of weakness but faster and more severe progression. He also had features not seen in the adult forms: facial involvement, ptosis, and mild contractures.

### Pathogenesis

Servián-Morilla et al. (2016) studied muscle from patients homozygous for the D233E mutation. Compared with controls, it showed reduced NOTCH1 intracellular domain, lower HES1 and PAX7 mRNA levels, and fewer PAX7-positive satellite cells. These changes were not found in age-matched patients with other muscular dystrophies or with secondary dystroglycanopathies. Patient muscle showed reduced glycosylation of alpha-dystroglycan and reduced laminin binding, but agrin binding was normal. In patient skin fibroblasts, alpha-dystroglycan glycosylation and laminin binding were normal. Primary myoblasts from patients proliferated slowly, had fewer self-renewing PAX7-positive cells, and differentiated more readily. Overexpressing the NOTCH1 intracellular domain in immortalized patient myoblasts reversed the proliferation defect and the facilitated differentiation, but did not restore the number of PAX7-positive cells. Blocking Notch signaling in differentiating C2C12 cells reduced glycosylated alpha-dystroglycan. The authors concluded that loss of satellite cells due to reduced Notch signaling was a key disease mechanism. They proposed that the alpha-dystroglycan hypoglycosylation was secondary.

Servián-Morilla et al. (2020) found reduced glycosylated alpha-dystroglycan at the sarcolemma in all muscle biopsies tested, with reduced laminin binding. Patient muscle also had less NOTCH1 intracellular domain and fewer PAX7-positive cells than controls. Muscle from patients with POGLUT1 mutations showed no or only a few scattered fibers positive for embryonic myosin heavy chain, whereas disease controls had many. Primary myoblasts from the D233E/R279Q patient proliferated more slowly than control cells, with fewer proliferating and self-renewing cells. They also differentiated more readily, with higher myogenin expression and a higher fusion index.

### Diagnosis

Servián-Morilla et al. (2020) suggested considering LGMDR21 and sequencing POGLUT1 in any patient with recessive LGMD who has 3 findings together: alpha-dystroglycan hypoglycosylation on muscle biopsy, reduced PAX7-positive cells and NOTCH1 intracellular domain levels, and the inside-to-outside pattern on muscle MRI. They noted that normal CK in most cases could help distinguish LGMDR21 from other autosomal recessive LGMDs. They also cautioned against confusing it with collagen VI-related myopathies, in which the earliest degeneration of the vastus lateralis appears in the outer areas.

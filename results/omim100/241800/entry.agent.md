### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Pallister-Hall-like syndrome (PHLS) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous (bi-allelic) mutation in the SMO gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Pallister-Hall-like syndrome is a developmental disorder caused by loss of SMO function. Its features vary widely. They include hypothalamic hamartoma and microcephaly; heart defects such as atrioventricular septal defect; skeletal anomalies including postaxial polydactyly, narrow chest and shortening of the long bones; and aganglionosis of the enteric nervous system (Le et al., 2020). Hypothalamic hamartoma with polydactyly in this condition resembles Pallister-Hall syndrome, which is caused by GLI3 mutation, but PHLS is a separate familial syndrome (Rubino et al., 2019). ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

Encha-Razavi et al. (1992) proposed the name 'congenital hypothalamic hamartoma syndrome' (CHHS) for a possibly familial entity. In their definition, the minimum criteria were skeletal dysplasia and orofacial abnormalities occurring with congenital hypothalamic hamartoma.

### Clinical Features

Marcuse et al. (1953) reported 2 cases of hypothalamic hamartoma with associated developmental defects. Only the title of this report was available.

Encha-Razavi et al. (1992) found congenital hypothalamic hamartomas at autopsy in 3 unrelated fetuses. In the first 2, the tumor occurred with skeletal dysplasia alone. In the third, it was part of a nonrandom pattern of congenital malformations suggestive of Meckel syndrome. In 1 family, an earlier-born boy had died soon after birth with similar craniofacial and skeletal abnormalities. The authors reviewed published cases of Pallister-Hall syndrome and found that only skeletal dysplasia and orofacial abnormalities were consistently present. They proposed these features as the minimum criteria for a distinct, possibly familial entity, which they called 'congenital hypothalamic hamartoma syndrome' (CHHS).

Rubino et al. (2019) described 2 brothers with a familial syndrome of hypothalamic hamartomas and polydactyly. In 1 brother, a large hypothalamic hamartoma underwent endoscopic endonasal biopsy and subtotal resection.

Le et al. (2020) reported 7 affected individuals from 5 independent families. Their developmental anomalies spanned a wide range. Brain findings were hypothalamic hamartoma and microcephaly, and the heart defect was atrioventricular septal defect. Skeletal findings were postaxial polydactyly, narrow chest and shortening of the long bones. The enteric nervous system showed aganglionosis. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Le et al. (2020) found bi-allelic SMO mutations in affected individuals from 5 families, which is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Rubino et al. (2019) had earlier described the disorder in 2 affected brothers. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Rubino et al. (2019) found germline mutations in the SMO gene in 2 brothers with hypothalamic hamartomas and polydactyly. The authors stated that this was the first report of a familial syndrome caused by germline SMO mutations. The abstract did not give the specific variants or their zygosity.

Le et al. (2020) identified bi-allelic loss-of-function mutations in the SMO gene in 7 individuals from 5 independent families. Cells derived from affected individuals formed cilia normally, but hedgehog signal transduction was severely altered. This resulted either from altered trafficking at the primary cilium or from abnormal activation of the pathway downstream of SMO. In cells from affected individuals, GLI2 also accumulated at the tip of the primary cilium independently of hedgehog. This finding suggested that SMO may regulate basal ciliary trafficking of GLI2 when the pathway is off.

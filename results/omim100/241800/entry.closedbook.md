### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Pallister-Hall-like syndrome (PHLS) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the SMO gene (601500) on chromosome 7q32. ⚠︎

### Description

Pallister-Hall-like syndrome is an autosomal recessive developmental disorder that resembles Pallister-Hall syndrome. Features vary but include developmental delay and intellectual disability, polydactyly, short stature, and malformations of other organ systems, including the heart and genitalia. Hypothalamic hamartoma has been seen in some patients. The disorder results from defective Hedgehog signaling. ✗

### Inheritance

The finding of biallelic SMO mutations in affected individuals, including those from consanguineous families, by Le et al. (2020) indicated that Pallister-Hall-like syndrome is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Somatic mosaic activating mutation in SMO causes Curry-Jones syndrome, which is a distinct disorder (Twigg et al., 2016). ✗

### Molecular Genetics

Le et al. (2020) used exome sequencing in several unrelated families with a wide range of developmental anomalies overlapping Pallister-Hall syndrome. They found homozygous or compound heterozygous mutations in the SMO gene in affected individuals. Functional studies showed that the variants impaired Hedgehog signaling. The authors proposed calling the disorder Pallister-Hall-like syndrome. ✗

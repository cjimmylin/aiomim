### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Pallister-Hall-like syndrome (PHLS) is caused by biallelic mutation in the SMO gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Pallister-Hall-like syndrome is a developmental disorder characterized by hypothalamic hamartoma and polydactyly. Le et al. (2020) described a wide spectrum of anomalies affecting the brain (hypothalamic hamartoma and microcephaly), the heart (atrioventricular septal defect), the skeleton (postaxial polydactyly, narrow chest, and shortening of long bones), and the enteric nervous system (aganglionosis). Rubino et al. (2019) noted that the disorder differs from Pallister-Hall syndrome, which is autosomal dominant and caused by truncation of GLI3.

### Nomenclature

Encha-Razavi et al. (1992) reviewed reported cases of Pallister-Hall syndrome and found that only skeletal dysplasia and orofacial abnormalities were consistently present. These features resembled the findings in their first 2 cases. They proposed them as the minimum criteria for a distinct, possibly familial entity, and suggested calling it 'congenital hypothalamic hamartoma syndrome' (CHHS).

### Clinical Features

Marcuse et al. (1953) reported 2 cases of hamartoma of the hypothalamus with associated developmental defects.

Encha-Razavi et al. (1992) reported congenital hypothalamic hamartomas found at autopsy in 3 unrelated fetuses. In 2 of the fetuses, the tumor was associated only with skeletal dysplasia. In the third fetus, it was part of a nonrandom association of congenital malformations suggestive of Meckel syndrome. In one family, a previous boy had died soon after birth with similar craniofacial and skeletal abnormalities.

Rubino et al. (2019) described 2 brothers with a familial syndrome of hypothalamic hamartomas and polydactyly that differed from Pallister-Hall syndrome. One brother had a large hypothalamic hamartoma that was biopsied and subtotally resected by an endoscopic endonasal approach, and the authors reported its histopathologic findings.

Le et al. (2020) reported 7 individuals from 5 independent families with a wide spectrum of developmental anomalies. These affected the brain (hypothalamic hamartoma and microcephaly), the heart (atrioventricular septal defect), the skeleton (postaxial polydactyly, narrow chest, and shortening of long bones), and the enteric nervous system (aganglionosis). ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Encha-Razavi et al. (1992) suggested that congenital hypothalamic hamartoma syndrome may be familial. In one of their families, a previous boy had died soon after birth with similar craniofacial and skeletal abnormalities.

Rubino et al. (2019) reported the disorder in 2 brothers. Le et al. (2020) identified biallelic SMO variants in affected individuals from 5 families, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Heterogeneity

Rubino et al. (2019) contrasted the syndrome in their family, which they attributed to germline SMO mutations, with Pallister-Hall syndrome. They noted that Pallister-Hall syndrome is autosomal dominant and is caused by truncation of GLI3, a downstream effector in the sonic hedgehog pathway.

Craig et al. (2008) noted that hypothalamic hamartomas are rare, benign congenital tumors associated with intractable epilepsy. Most cases are sporadic and nonsyndromic, and about 5% are associated with Pallister-Hall syndrome. Craig et al. (2008) studied 55 patients with sporadic, nonsyndromic hypothalamic hamartoma and intractable epilepsy, comparing genomic DNA from peripheral blood with DNA from surgically resected hamartoma tissue. Resequencing of GLI3 identified no causative germline mutations. However, they found somatic (tumor-only) chromosomal abnormalities involving the GLI3 locus in 8 of 55 (15%) resected tissue samples. Copy-number analysis on Affymetrix 10K SNP arrays identified a somatic abnormality on chromosome 7p in 1 sample. GLI3 resequencing showed loss of heterozygosity (LOH) within GLI3 in 3 samples, and genotyping of 28 SNPs in and around GLI3 found LOH in 5 more. The authors concluded that these somatic GLI3 abnormalities are associated with the pathogenesis of hypothalamic hamartoma in sporadic, nonsyndromic patients, and noted that the abnormalities appeared highly variable.

### Molecular Genetics

Rubino et al. (2019) attributed the familial syndrome of hypothalamic hamartomas and polydactyly in 2 brothers to germline mutations in the SMO gene. They stated that this was the first familial syndrome reported to be caused by germline SMO mutations. They also stated that it was the first familial syndrome other than Pallister-Hall syndrome to be associated with hypothalamic hamartomas.

In 7 affected individuals from 5 independent families with developmental anomalies, Le et al. (2020) identified biallelic loss-of-function variants in the SMO gene. Cells from affected individuals showed normal ciliogenesis but severely altered hedgehog signal transduction.

### Pathogenesis

Le et al. (2020) found that cells from individuals with biallelic SMO variants had normal ciliogenesis but severely altered hedgehog signal transduction. This resulted either from altered trafficking at the primary cilium or from abnormal activation of the pathway downstream of SMO. GLI2 accumulated at the ciliary tip independently of hedgehog stimulation. The authors suggested that this points to a role for SMO in regulating basal ciliary trafficking of GLI2 when the pathway is off.

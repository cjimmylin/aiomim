### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spinal muscular atrophy with congenital bone fractures-2 (SMABF2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the ASCC1 gene (614215) on chromosome 10q22.1.

### Description

Spinal muscular atrophy with congenital bone fractures-2 is a severe autosomal recessive neuromuscular disorder (Oliveira et al., 2017) that begins before birth (Knierim et al., 2016). Its features include arthrogryposis multiplex congenita, respiratory distress, and congenital bone fractures (Knierim et al., 2016), with prenatal fractures of the long bones (Giuffrida et al., 2020).

### Clinical Features

Knierim et al. (2016) reported patients with prenatal-onset spinal muscular atrophy, multiple congenital contractures (arthrogryposis multiplex congenita), respiratory distress, and congenital bone fractures.

Oliveira et al. (2017) reported a patient who had generalized hypotonia and a lack of spontaneous movements as a newborn. She died within the first days of life.

### Inheritance

Knierim et al. (2016) reported recessive loss-of-function mutations in ASCC1 and TRIP4 in patients with this disorder. Oliveira et al. (2017) also described the disorder as recessive.

### Heterogeneity

Along with ASCC1 mutations, Knierim et al. (2016) found mutations in the TRIP4 gene in patients with prenatal-onset spinal muscular atrophy and congenital bone fractures. TRIP4 and ASCC1 both encode subunits of the nuclear activating signal cointegrator-1 (ASC-1) complex.

### Molecular Genetics

In patients with prenatal-onset spinal muscular atrophy and congenital bone fractures, Knierim et al. (2016) identified homozygous and compound heterozygous loss-of-function mutations in the ASCC1 and TRIP4 genes. Both genes encode subunits of the ASC-1 complex. Knierim et al. (2016) concluded that dysfunction of this transcriptional coactivator complex can cause a clinical syndrome affecting the neuromuscular system.

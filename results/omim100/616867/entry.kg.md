### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spinal muscular atrophy with congenital bone fractures-2 (SMABF2) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the ASCC1 gene (614215) on chromosome 10q22.1. ⚠︎

### Description

Spinal muscular atrophy with congenital bone fractures-2 (SMABF2) is a severe neuromuscular disorder that begins before birth. Affected individuals have prenatal-onset muscle weakness or spinal muscular atrophy, neonatal hypotonia, multiple congenital contractures (arthrogryposis multiplex congenita), respiratory distress, and congenital bone fractures (Knierim et al., 2016; Bohm et al., 2019).

### Clinical Features

Knierim et al. (2016) studied 4 families in which affected individuals presented with prenatal-onset spinal muscular atrophy, multiple congenital contractures (arthrogryposis multiplex congenita), respiratory distress, and congenital bone fractures.

Oliveira et al. (2017) described a female newborn with a severe neuromuscular disorder. She had generalized hypotonia, congenital bone fractures, no spontaneous movements, and poor respiratory effort, and she died within the first days of life. A male sib born later had the same clinical presentation. Karyotyping and screening of several neuromuscular disease genes were negative.

Bohm et al. (2019) gave a clinical, histologic, and genetic description of 3 families with ASCC1-related disorder. All patients had severe prenatal-onset muscle weakness, neonatal hypotonia, arthrogryposis, and congenital bone fractures. Muscle biopsies from affected infants showed intense oxidative rims beneath the sarcolemma and scattered remnants of sarcomeres with enlarged Z-bands. The authors suggested that these changes could be a histopathologic hallmark of the disorder.

Giuffrida et al. (2020) described the first case of SMABF2 diagnosed in a stillbirth. The course of the disease was documented during the prenatal period. Arthrogryposis and hydrops were present, and these findings led to chromosomal microarray analysis.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of SMABF2 is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Knierim et al. (2016) found homozygous and compound heterozygous mutations that cosegregated with the disease. Oliveira et al. (2017) found a homozygous mutation in a family with 2 affected sibs, and Giuffrida et al. (2020) found compound heterozygous mutations. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Knierim et al. (2016) found that the phenotype of prenatal-onset spinal muscular atrophy with congenital bone fractures was associated with mutations in either of 2 genes, TRIP4 or ASCC1. Both genes encode subunits of the activating signal cointegrator 1 (ASC-1) complex.

### Molecular Genetics

Knierim et al. (2016) studied affected individuals from 4 families with prenatal-onset spinal muscular atrophy and congenital bone fractures. Using autozygosity mapping and whole-exome sequencing, they identified homozygous and compound heterozygous nonsense and frameshift mutations in the TRIP4 and ASCC1 genes, which encode subunits of the nuclear ASC-1 complex. The mutations cosegregated with the disease and led to truncation or complete absence of the respective proteins. ASCC1-mutant patient fibroblasts showed downregulation of genes involved in neurogenesis and bone development (see PATHOGENESIS).

Oliveira et al. (2017) performed whole-exome sequencing under a recessive model in a female newborn with SMABF2, whose male sib had the same presentation. They first found no disease-causing variants in known neuromuscular genes. They then identified a homozygous frameshift mutation in the ASCC1 gene (c.157dupG, p.Glu53GlyfsTer19) in the proband. The authors concluded that this second known case confirmed ASCC1 involvement in a severe neuromuscular disease within the spinal muscular atrophy or primary muscle disease spectrum.

Bohm et al. (2019) sequenced affected individuals from 3 families with prenatal-onset muscle weakness, arthrogryposis, and congenital bone fractures. They identified recessive nonsense or frameshift mutations in the ASCC1 gene, including 2 novel mutations.

Giuffrida et al. (2020) found compound heterozygous mutations in the ASCC1 gene in a stillborn fetus with SMABF2. Microarray analysis first detected a microdeletion of about 64 kb that involved 4 exons of ASCC1. Exome analysis then identified a c.1027C-T nonsense mutation (Arg343Ter) that introduces a premature termination codon. The authors stated that this was the first reported case of ASCC1 compound heterozygosity caused by an exonic microdeletion together with a nucleotide variant.

### Pathogenesis

Knierim et al. (2016) found that ASCC1-mutant patient fibroblasts downregulated genes associated with neurogenesis, neuronal migration, and pathfinding (SERPINF1, DAB1, SEMA3D, SEMA3A) and genes associated with bone development (TNFRSF11B, RASSF2, STC1). Immunoprecipitation of the ASC-1 complex consistently copurified the transcriptional cofactor CSRP1. The authors concluded that dysfunction of a transcriptional coactivator complex can cause a syndrome that affects the neuromuscular system.

In mouse embryos at embryonic day 17.5, Knierim et al. (2016) found identical expression patterns for Trip4 and Ascc1. Both genes were highly expressed in spinal cord, brain, paraspinal ganglia, thyroid, and submandibular glands.

### Animal Model

Knierim et al. (2016) found that morpholino knockdown of either trip4 or ascc1 in zebrafish disrupted alpha-motoneuron outgrowth and the formation of myotomes and neuromuscular junctions. The knockdown also caused a swimming defect in larvae.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 SPINAL MUSCULAR ATROPHY WITH CONGENITAL BONE FRACTURES 2**  
ASCC1, 1-BP DUP, 157G [c.157dupg; p.E53G]

Oliveira et al. (2017) studied a female newborn with SMABF2 who died within the first days of life; a later-born male sib had the same presentation. In the proband they identified a homozygous 1-bp duplication (c.157dupG) in the ASCC1 gene, which causes a frameshift (Glu53GlyfsTer19). The variant was found by whole-exome sequencing under a recessive model after no disease-causing variants were found in known neuromuscular genes.

**.0002 SPINAL MUSCULAR ATROPHY WITH CONGENITAL BONE FRACTURES 2**  
ASCC1, ARG343TER [c.1027C>T; p.R343*]

Exome analysis by Giuffrida et al. (2020) identified a c.1027C-T transition in the ASCC1 gene in a stillborn fetus with SMABF2, arthrogryposis, and hydrops. The change produces an arg343-to-ter (R343X) substitution and introduces a premature termination codon. The fetus was compound heterozygous for this mutation and a microdeletion of about 64 kb involving 4 exons of ASCC1.

**.0003 SPINAL MUSCULAR ATROPHY WITH CONGENITAL BONE FRACTURES 2**  
ASCC1, 64-KB DEL

Giuffrida et al. (2020) used chromosomal microarray analysis to detect a microdeletion of about 64 kb involving 4 exons of the ASCC1 gene in a stillborn fetus with SMABF2. The fetus was compound heterozygous for this deletion and a nonsense mutation (R343X). The authors stated that this was the first reported case of ASCC1 compound heterozygosity caused by an exonic microdeletion together with a nucleotide variant.

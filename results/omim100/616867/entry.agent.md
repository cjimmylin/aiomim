### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spinal muscular atrophy with congenital bone fractures-2 (SMABF2) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the ASCC1 gene, which encodes a subunit of the activating signal cointegrator 1 (ASC-1) complex. ⚠︎

### Description

Spinal muscular atrophy with congenital bone fractures-2 (SMABF2) is a rare autosomal recessive neuromuscular disorder characterized by arthrogryposis multiplex congenita and prenatal fractures of the long bones, with poor prognosis (Giuffrida et al., 2020). Affected individuals present with prenatal-onset muscle weakness, neonatal hypotonia, respiratory distress, and congenital bone fractures (Knierim et al., 2016; Bohm et al., 2019).

### Clinical Features

Knierim et al. (2016) studied 4 families in which affected individuals presented with prenatal-onset spinal muscular atrophy, multiple congenital contractures (arthrogryposis multiplex congenita), respiratory distress, and congenital bone fractures. According to Oliveira et al. (2017), 3 of these families carried TRIP4 mutations and 1 carried ASCC1 mutations. ⚠︎

Oliveira et al. (2017) reported a female newborn with a severe neuromuscular disorder who had generalized hypotonia, congenital bone fractures, no spontaneous movements, and poor respiratory effort; she died within the first days of life. A male sib born later had the same clinical presentation. Karyotyping and screening of several genes associated with neuromuscular disease were negative.

Bohm et al. (2019) provided clinical, histologic, and genetic descriptions of 3 additional families. All patients had severe prenatal-onset muscle weakness, neonatal hypotonia, arthrogryposis, and congenital bone fractures. Muscle biopsies from the affected infants showed intense oxidative rims beneath the sarcolemma and scattered remnants of sarcomeres with enlarged Z-bands, which the authors suggested might be a histopathologic hallmark of the disorder.

Giuffrida et al. (2020) described a stillbirth with SMABF2, the first case in which the evolution of the disease was documented during the prenatal period. Microarray analysis had been requested because of arthrogryposis and hydrops. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of SMABF2 in the families reported by Knierim et al. (2016) and Oliveira et al. (2017) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Knierim et al. (2016) found that the mutations cosegregated with the disease, and Oliveira et al. (2017) found a homozygous mutation in a patient who had an affected sib.

### Heterogeneity

Knierim et al. (2016) found that recessive loss-of-function mutations in TRIP4, which encodes another subunit of the ASC-1 complex, were associated with a similar phenotype of prenatal spinal muscular atrophy and congenital bone fractures. According to Oliveira et al. (2017), TRIP4 mutations were found in 3 of the 4 families in that study. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Knierim et al. (2016) used autozygosity mapping and whole-exome sequencing in 4 families with prenatal-onset spinal muscular atrophy and congenital bone fractures. They identified homozygous and compound heterozygous nonsense and frameshift mutations in the TRIP4 and ASCC1 genes, which encode subunits of the nuclear ASC-1 complex. The mutations caused truncation or complete absence of the respective proteins and cosegregated with the disease. ASCC1-mutant fibroblasts showed downregulation of genes associated with neurogenesis, neuronal migration, and pathfinding (SERPINF1, DAB1, SEMA3D, SEMA3A) and with bone development (TNFRSF11B, RASSF2, STC1). Immunoprecipitation of the ASC-1 complex consistently copurified the transcriptional cofactor CSRP1.

In a female newborn with SMABF2, Oliveira et al. (2017) used whole-exome sequencing and found a homozygous 1-bp duplication in the ASCC1 gene (c.157dupG), resulting in a frameshift (Glu53GlyfsTer19). Filtering of genes associated with congenital myopathies, muscle diseases, and other neuromuscular disorders had not identified a disease-causing variant. The authors described this as the second known case of ASCC1 involvement.

Bohm et al. (2019) noted that a single homozygous ASCC1 mutation had been reported in 2 families. In patients from 3 additional families with SMABF2, they identified recessive nonsense or frameshift mutations in ASCC1, 2 of which were novel.

In a stillbirth with SMABF2, Giuffrida et al. (2020) found compound heterozygous mutations in the ASCC1 gene. Microarray analysis first detected a deletion copy number variant of about 64 kb that involved 4 exons of ASCC1. Exome analysis then identified a nonsense mutation (c.1027C-T; Arg343Ter) on the other allele. The authors described this as the first case of ASCC1 compound heterozygosity involving an exonic microdeletion and a nucleotide variant.

### Animal Model

Knierim et al. (2016) found that Trip4 and Ascc1 had identical expression patterns in 17.5-day-old mouse embryos, with high expression in the spinal cord, brain, paraspinal ganglia, thyroid, and submandibular glands. Morpholino knockdown of either trip4 or ascc1 in zebrafish disrupted alpha-motoneuron outgrowth and the formation of myotomes and neuromuscular junctions, and caused a swimming defect in the larvae.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 SPINAL MUSCULAR ATROPHY WITH CONGENITAL BONE FRACTURES 2**  
ASCC1, 1-BP DUP, 157G [c.157dupG; p.Glu53Glyfs*19]

In a female newborn with SMABF2 who died within the first days of life, Oliveira et al. (2017) identified a homozygous 1-bp duplication (c.157dupG) in the ASCC1 gene by whole-exome sequencing. The duplication results in a frameshift and premature termination (Glu53GlyfsTer19). A male sib had the same clinical presentation.

**.0002 SPINAL MUSCULAR ATROPHY WITH CONGENITAL BONE FRACTURES 2**  
ASCC1, ARG343TER [c.1027C>T; p.Arg343*]

In a stillbirth with SMABF2, arthrogryposis, and hydrops, Giuffrida et al. (2020) found compound heterozygous mutations in the ASCC1 gene. Exome analysis found a c.1027C-T transition, resulting in an arg343-to-ter (R343X) substitution that introduces a premature termination codon. Microarray analysis had earlier found an approximately 64-kb deletion involving 4 exons of ASCC1 on the other allele.

**.0003 SPINAL MUSCULAR ATROPHY WITH CONGENITAL BONE FRACTURES 2**  
ASCC1, 64-KB DEL

In a stillbirth with SMABF2, Giuffrida et al. (2020) used microarray analysis to identify a deletion of about 64 kb that involved 4 exons of the ASCC1 gene. The deletion was present in compound heterozygous state with a nonsense mutation (R343X).

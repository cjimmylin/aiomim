### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spinal muscular atrophy with congenital bone fractures-2 (SMABF2) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the ASCC1 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Spinal muscular atrophy with congenital bone fractures-2 (SMABF2) is a severe autosomal recessive neuromuscular disorder. Its main features are prenatal-onset muscle weakness or spinal muscular atrophy, arthrogryposis multiplex congenita, prenatal or congenital fractures of the long bones, neonatal hypotonia and respiratory distress, and it has a poor prognosis (Knierim et al., 2016; Bohm et al., 2019; Giuffrida et al., 2020). The disorder is caused by loss-of-function mutations in ASCC1, which encodes a subunit of the activating signal cointegrator-1 (ASC-1) transcriptional coactivator complex (Knierim et al., 2016). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Knierim et al. (2016) studied 4 families with a disorder caused by mutations in genes that encode subunits of the ASC-1 complex. Affected individuals had prenatal-onset spinal muscular atrophy (SMA), multiple congenital contractures (arthrogryposis multiplex congenita), respiratory distress, and congenital bone fractures.

Oliveira et al. (2017) reported a female newborn with a severe neuromuscular disorder. She had generalized hypotonia, congenital bone fractures, no spontaneous movements, and poor respiratory effort, and she died within the first days of life. Karyotyping and screening of several genes related to neuromuscular diseases were negative. A male sib was later born with the same clinical presentation.

Bohm et al. (2019) gave a clinical, histologic, and genetic description of 3 families with ASCC1-related disease. All patients had severe prenatal-onset muscle weakness, neonatal hypotonia, arthrogryposis, and congenital bone fractures. Muscle biopsies from the affected infants showed intense oxidative rims beneath the sarcolemma and scattered remnants of sarcomeres with enlarged Z-bands. Bohm et al. (2019) suggested that these changes might be a histopathologic hallmark of the disorder.

Giuffrida et al. (2020) reported a stillbirth with SMABF2 in which the evolution of the disease was documented during the prenatal period. Arthrogryposis and hydrops were the reasons for requesting microarray analysis.

### Inheritance

Knierim et al. (2016) found that SMABF2 follows a recessive pattern of inheritance: affected individuals carried homozygous or compound heterozygous mutations in ASCC1, and these mutations cosegregated with the disease. Later reports of homozygous and compound heterozygous ASCC1 mutations were consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance (Oliveira et al., 2017; Bohm et al., 2019; Giuffrida et al., 2020). ⚠︎

### Mapping

Knierim et al. (2016) used autozygosity mapping together with whole-exome sequencing to search for pathogenic mutations in 4 families with prenatal-onset SMA and congenital bone fractures.

### Heterogeneity

Knierim et al. (2016) found recessive loss-of-function mutations in 2 genes that encode subunits of the ASC-1 complex, TRIP4 and ASCC1, in families with prenatal-onset SMA, arthrogryposis multiplex congenita, respiratory distress, and congenital bone fractures. As summarized by Oliveira et al. (2017), 3 of these families had TRIP4 mutations and 1 family had ASCC1 mutations. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Knierim et al. (2016) performed autozygosity mapping and whole-exome sequencing in 4 families with prenatal-onset SMA and congenital bone fractures. They identified homozygous and compound heterozygous nonsense and frameshift mutations in TRIP4 and ASCC1. The mutations caused truncation or complete absence of the corresponding proteins and cosegregated with the disease. Fibroblasts with ASCC1 mutations showed downregulation of genes associated with neurogenesis, neuronal migration, and pathfinding (SERPINF1, DAB1, SEMA3D, SEMA3A) and with bone development (TNFRSF11B, RASSF2, STC1). Morpholino knockdown of ascc1 in zebrafish disrupted alpha-motoneuron outgrowth and the formation of myotomes and neuromuscular junctions, and the larvae had a swimming defect. Immunoprecipitation of the ASC-1 complex consistently copurified the transcriptional cofactor CSRP1.

Oliveira et al. (2017) performed whole-exome sequencing in a female newborn with a severe neuromuscular disorder and filtered variants assuming a recessive model. No disease-causing variants were found in genes known to cause congenital myopathies, muscle diseases, or other neuromuscular disorders. The patient was then found to be homozygous for a frameshift mutation in ASCC1 (c.157dupG; Glu53GlyfsTer19). The authors stated that this was the second known case and that it confirmed the involvement of ASCC1. Bohm et al. (2019) noted that the same single homozygous ASCC1 mutation had been reported in 2 families.

In patients from 3 families with prenatal-onset muscle weakness, arthrogryposis, and congenital bone fractures, Bohm et al. (2019) identified recessive nonsense or frameshift mutations in ASCC1, including 2 novel mutations.

In a stillbirth with SMABF2, Giuffrida et al. (2020) identified compound heterozygous mutations in ASCC1. Microarray analysis first detected a microdeletion of about 64 kb that included 4 exons of ASCC1. Exome analysis then found a nonsense mutation (c.1027C-T; Arg343Ter) on the other allele. The authors stated that this was the first reported case of ASCC1 compound heterozygosity in which one allele was an exonic microdeletion and the other a nucleotide variant.

### Animal Model

Knierim et al. (2016) showed that Trip4 and Ascc1 had identical expression patterns in 17.5-day-old mouse embryos, with high levels in the spinal cord, brain, paraspinal ganglia, thyroid, and submandibular glands. In zebrafish, antisense morpholino knockdown of either trip4 or ascc1 disrupted the patterned, coordinated outgrowth of alpha-motoneurons and the formation of myotomes and neuromuscular junctions. The larvae had a swimming defect.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 SPINAL MUSCULAR ATROPHY WITH CONGENITAL BONE FRACTURES 2**  
ASCC1, 1-BP DUP, 157G [c.157dupG; p.Glu53Glyfs*19]

Oliveira et al. (2017) identified a homozygous 1-bp duplication in the ASCC1 gene (c.157dupG) in a female newborn with SMABF2. The duplication causes a frameshift (Glu53GlyfsTer19). She had generalized hypotonia, congenital bone fractures, no spontaneous movements, and poor respiratory effort, and she died within the first days of life. The mutation was found by whole-exome sequencing with filtering under a recessive model. A male sib had the same clinical presentation. Bohm et al. (2019) noted that this single homozygous ASCC1 mutation had been reported in 2 families. ⚠︎

**.0002 SPINAL MUSCULAR ATROPHY WITH CONGENITAL BONE FRACTURES 2**  
ASCC1, ARG343TER [c.1027C>T; p.Arg343*]

In a stillbirth with SMABF2, arthrogryposis, and hydrops, Giuffrida et al. (2020) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the ASCC1 gene. The first, a c.1027C-T transition found by exome analysis, results in an arg343-to-ter (R343X) substitution and introduces a premature termination codon. The second, found earlier by microarray analysis, was a microdeletion of about 64 kb that included 4 exons of ASCC1.

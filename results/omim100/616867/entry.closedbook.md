### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spinal muscular atrophy with congenital bone fractures-2 (SMABF2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the ASCC1 gene on chromosome 10q22. ⚠︎

### Description

Spinal muscular atrophy with congenital bone fractures-2 is a severe autosomal recessive neuromuscular disorder that begins before birth. It is characterized by fetal hypokinesia, generalized hypotonia and muscle weakness, arthrogryposis, and congenital fractures of the long bones. Affected infants have respiratory insufficiency and usually die early. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Knierim et al. (2016) reported patients with prenatal-onset spinal muscular atrophy and congenital bone fractures. Features included reduced fetal movements, severe hypotonia at birth, arthrogryposis, congenital fractures, and respiratory failure resulting in early death. ✗

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of SMABF2 in the family reported by Knierim et al. (2016) fit autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Spinal muscular atrophy with congenital bone fractures-1 (SMABF1) is caused by mutation in the TRIP4 gene, which encodes another subunit of the activating signal cointegrator-1 (ASC-1) complex. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Knierim et al. (2016) found homozygous loss-of-function mutations in genes encoding subunits of the ASC-1 complex in patients with prenatal spinal muscular atrophy and congenital bone fractures. The patients with SMABF2 carried mutations in the ASCC1 gene, and the patients with SMABF1 carried mutations in TRIP4. Knierim et al. (2016) linked the disorder to impaired function of the ASC-1 transcriptional coactivator complex. ⚠︎

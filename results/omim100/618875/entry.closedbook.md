### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that early-onset seizures with neurodegeneration and brain calcifications (SENEBAC) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the NRROS gene on chromosome 3q29. ⚠︎

### Description

SENEBAC is an autosomal recessive neurodegenerative disorder. It begins in infancy with seizures and developmental regression, often after early development that seemed normal. Brain imaging shows intracranial calcifications and white matter abnormalities, a picture that can resemble the type I interferonopathies, such as Aicardi-Goutieres syndrome. The disease is progressive and is often fatal in early childhood. It is thought to be a microgliopathy caused by loss of NRROS-dependent activation of TGF-beta-1 in microglia. ✗

### Clinical Features

Smith et al. (2020) and Dong et al. (2020) reported patients from unrelated families with biallelic NRROS mutations. Seizures began in infancy and were followed by loss of developmental milestones, progressive neurologic deterioration and early death. Brain imaging showed intracranial calcifications and white matter changes. Neuropathologic examination showed loss of homeostatic microglial markers, such as P2RY12 and TMEM119, together with neurodegeneration. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The reported patients carried homozygous or compound heterozygous NRROS mutations, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Smith et al. (2020) and Dong et al. (2020) used exome sequencing to find biallelic loss-of-function mutations in the NRROS gene in affected children. The mutations segregated with the disorder in the families studied. Functional studies suggested that the mutations impaired the ability of NRROS to anchor latent TGF-beta-1 at the cell surface and support its activation. ✗

### Animal Model

In mice, Nrros deficiency disrupted microglial TGF-beta-1 signaling. Microglia lost their homeostatic signature, and the mice developed neurologic disease with neurodegeneration (Qin et al., 2018). ⚠︎

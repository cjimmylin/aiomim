### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that early-onset seizures with neurodegeneration and brain calcifications (SENEBAC) is caused by homozygous mutation in the NRROS gene (615322) on chromosome 3q29.

### Description

SENEBAC is an autosomal recessive neurodegenerative disorder caused by biallelic mutations in NRROS (Smith et al., 2020). Seizures begin within the first year of life (Dong et al., 2020). Smith et al. (2020) reported normal early development, followed by refractory seizures and neurodegeneration. Brain imaging shows intracranial calcification and, later, white matter signal changes. The disease is progressive and led to death in early childhood. Smith et al. (2020) called the disorder a microgliopathy and suggested that NRROS functions in TGF-beta and reactive oxygen species regulation may be important in its pathogenesis. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Smith et al. (2020) reported affected children from 2 unrelated families. Neuroimaging showed calcification. Later imaging showed diffuse signal changes in the cerebral and cerebellar white matter. Follow-up showed rapidly progressive cortical and subcortical atrophy, and the patients died between 27 and 36 months of age. On neuropathologic examination, P2Y12 staining was lost and TMEM119 immunoreactivity was weak, indicating marked parenchymal microglial abnormalities. There was also widespread neuronal loss with reactive gliosis throughout the grey matter. ⚠︎

Dong et al. (2020) reported 6 individuals from 4 families with biallelic NRROS variants. Seizures began within the first year of life and were followed by developmental regression.

### Inheritance

Smith et al. (2020) found homozygous NRROS mutations in the affected children from both families they studied. Biallelic mutations are consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Smith et al. (2020) used exome sequencing to identify homozygous NRROS mutations in the affected children from 2 families. Both mutations were predicted to truncate the protein.

Dong et al. (2020) identified biallelic loss-of-function NRROS variants in affected individuals. These variants resulted in aberrant NRROS proteins with an impaired ability to anchor latent TGF-beta-1 on the cell surface. Dong et al. (2020) noted that this anchoring is required for activation of TGF-beta-1 in macrophages and microglia.

### Animal Model

Smith et al. (2020) noted that Nrros -/- mice lost the homeostatic microglial gene expression profile, including suppression of P2ry12 and Tmem119 expression. The mice developed progressive neurologic decline, and their neuropathology included neuronal loss.

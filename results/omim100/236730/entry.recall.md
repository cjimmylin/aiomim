A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that urofacial syndrome-1 (UFS1) is caused by homozygous mutation in the HPSE2 gene (613469) on chromosome 10q24.2.

### Description

Urofacial (Ochoa) syndrome is a rare autosomal recessive disorder (Wang et al., 1999). It is characterized by facial grimacing when attempting to smile and by failure of the urinary bladder to void completely, despite a lack of anatomical bladder outflow obstruction or overt neurological damage (Daly et al., 2010). Patients have an unusual 'inverted' facial expression and appear to be crying when they smile (Derbent et al., 2009). Affected individuals often have reflux of infected urine from the bladder to the upper renal tract, with a risk of kidney damage and renal failure (Daly et al., 2010). The urinary problems usually result in urinary tract infection and hydronephrosis (Derbent et al., 2009). Constipation may also occur (Ochoa and Gorlin, 1987).

### Clinical Features

Ochoa and Gorlin (1987) drew attention to the association of distortion of facial expression with neuropathic bladder and resultant damage to the genitourinary tract. They recommended that individuals with 'inversion' of facial expression undergo urological evaluation. About two-thirds of their patients also had moderate to severe constipation.

### Inheritance

Urofacial syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder (Wang et al., 1999). Ochoa and Gorlin (1987) concluded that the occurrence of the disorder in multiple sibs and increased parental consanguinity indicated autosomal recessive inheritance. The disease gene was localized by homozygosity mapping (Wang et al., 2003), and Daly et al. (2010) confirmed HPSE2 as the causative gene by identifying homozygous mutations.

### Mapping

Wang et al. (1997) used homozygosity and linkage disequilibrium mapping to map the urofacial syndrome gene to a small interval on chromosome 10q23-q24.

### Molecular Genetics

Pang et al. (2010) identified loss-of-function mutations in the HPSE2 gene, which encodes heparanase-2, in patients with urofacial syndrome.

In families with urofacial syndrome, Daly et al. (2010) identified homozygous mutations in the HPSE2 gene, including deletions.

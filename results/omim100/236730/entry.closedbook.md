### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that urofacial syndrome-1 (UFS1) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the HPSE2 gene (613469) on chromosome 10q24. ⚠︎

### Description

Urofacial syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder. Its main features are an unusual facial expression and bladder dysfunction. When affected individuals try to smile, their face shows a grimace that looks like crying or sadness (an 'inverted smile'). The bladder dysfunction is neuropathic but has no detectable neurologic lesion. It causes incomplete bladder emptying, vesicoureteral reflux, hydronephrosis and recurrent urinary tract infections, which can progress to renal failure if untreated. Bowel dysfunction, including constipation, may also occur. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Ochoa and colleagues described the syndrome in Colombia. They drew attention to the characteristic facial grimace on smiling in children with severe voiding dysfunction and upper urinary tract dilatation, and recommended that the facial sign be used to recognize the urologic disorder early. ✗

### Inheritance

Urofacial syndrome shows autosomal recessive inheritance. This is supported by affected sibs, parental consanguinity in many families, and homozygosity mapping. It was confirmed by the identification of biallelic HPSE2 mutations. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Wang et al. (1997) studied Colombian families with urofacial syndrome. Using homozygosity and linkage disequilibrium mapping, they mapped the gene to a small interval on chromosome 10q23-q24. ✗

### Heterogeneity

Urofacial syndrome-2 (UFS2) is caused by mutation in the LRIG2 gene. ✗

### Molecular Genetics

Daly et al. (2010) and Pang et al. (2010) independently identified biallelic mutations in the HPSE2 gene in families with urofacial syndrome. HPSE2 encodes heparanase-2. The mutations included deletions and other variants predicted to cause loss of function. The HPSE2 protein was detected in the pelvic ganglia and nerves that supply the bladder, which suggested a peripheral neural basis for the bladder dysfunction. ✗

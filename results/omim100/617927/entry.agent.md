### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII (OFD18) is caused by homozygous mutation in the IFT57 gene.

### Description

Orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII is an oral-facial-digital syndrome with short stature, skeletal dysplasia, and brachymesophalangia. Thevenon et al. (2016) described oral anomalies (supernumerary frenulae, missing incisors), medial clefting of the upper lip, and pre- and postaxial polydactyly. The phenotype overlaps with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome, but affected individuals did not have short ribs or a narrow thorax (Phadke et al., 1999; Thevenon et al., 2016).

### Clinical Features

Phadke et al. (1999) reported a consanguineous family with a new variety of oral-facial-digital syndrome associated with acromelic short stature and genu valgum, with features that overlapped with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome. One of the sibs had urinary incontinence and growth hormone deficiency, which the authors noted had not previously been reported in any type of OFDS.

Thevenon et al. (2016) reassessed the family reported by Phadke et al. (1999), which had 3 affected sibs born to healthy consanguineous parents and had been followed for 15 years. One sib (II-3) died at 17 years of age of a complicated pulmonary infection. Subject II-1, a man examined at 25 years of age, had normal intelligence and short stature, with a final height of 155 cm (-3 SD), weight 45 kg (less than -3 SD), and occipitofrontal circumference 50 cm (mean). He had a square face with a short forehead, upslanting palpebral fissures, a high nasal bridge, a pyriform nose, a short philtrum, and a medial cleft of the upper lip. Oral anomalies included supernumerary frenulae and missing incisors. He had pronounced brachydactyly and postaxial polydactyly of the upper limbs. X-rays showed severe mesophalangeal shortening, and duplication of the first metatarsal was diagnosed as preaxial polydactyly. He also had bilateral short femoral necks with varus femurs and symmetric genu valgum. There was no thoracic deformity, and heart, renal, and abdominal ultrasound examinations were normal.

Subject II-2 in the family studied by Thevenon et al. (2016), a woman examined at 22 years of age, had normal intelligence and short stature, with a final height of 122 cm (-5 SD), weight 37 kg (-2 SD), and occipitofrontal circumference 51 cm (mean). She had a square face with a narrow forehead, enophthalmia, upslanting palpebral fissures, a broad nasal bridge, bilateral hypopigmented pseudoclefts with medial labial clefting, missing incisors, and supernumerary frenulae. She had marked brachydactyly and postaxial polydactyly of all 4 limbs, which had been surgically removed in infancy. Puberty was not delayed. Hand x-rays showed mesophalangeal shortening and residual postaxial polydactyly of the right hand, and foot x-rays showed duplication of the first metatarsal and absent intermediate phalanges, suggesting preaxial polydactyly. She also had abnormal vertebral bodies with medial notches but without platyspondyly, bilateral cervical ribs without a narrow thorax, gracile long bones, and shortened femoral necks. Brain MRI and abdominal and heart ultrasounds were normal. Thevenon et al. (2016) concluded that the phenotype was an unclassified OFDS associated with skeletal dysplasia rather than Ellis-van Creveld syndrome.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OFD18 in the family reported by Phadke et al. (1999) and Thevenon et al. (2016) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance: 3 sibs born to healthy consanguineous parents were affected, and all were homozygous for a mutation in the IFT57 gene (Thevenon et al., 2016).

### Mapping

By homozygosity mapping in 2 affected sibs from the consanguineous family originally reported by Phadke et al. (1999), Thevenon et al. (2016) found 11 Mb of shared runs of homozygosity, on chromosome 3 (chr3:104,822,019-111,671,862, GRCh37) and chromosome 10 (chr10:1,372,501-6,029,726). None of the genes in these intervals had previously been associated with a recessive ciliopathy. Segregation analysis in the third affected sib excluded the chromosome 10 interval.

### Molecular Genetics

In 3 affected sibs from the consanguineous family originally reported by Phadke et al. (1999), Thevenon et al. (2016) identified a homozygous synonymous c.777G-A transition (K259K) in the last base of exon 6 of the IFT57 gene. The mutation was found by homozygosity mapping followed by exome sequencing and was confirmed by Sanger sequencing. It was absent from the ExAC database and from 50 healthy individuals from northern India. Analysis of patient fibroblasts showed a complex splicing pattern: normal transcript was present, along with 3 abnormal transcripts with skipping of exon 6, exons 5 and 6, or exons 4 through 6, which were detected only after NMD was inhibited. Patient fibroblasts had significantly less IFT57 mRNA than controls. Primary cilia number and length were normal. However, IFT57 staining at the ciliary tip was significantly reduced (90% vs 35%; p less than 0.001), and staining at the basal body was increased, indicating defective anterograde ciliary transport. Stimulation with a Smoothened agonist poorly induced GLI1 expression in patient fibroblasts. siRNA knockdown of IFT57 in control fibroblasts abolished GLI1 induction, indicating impaired sonic hedgehog signaling. The authors considered the mutation hypomorphic. Sanger sequencing of IFT57 in 13 OFDS patients and 12 Ellis-van Creveld syndrome patients, and review of exome or ciliome data from 172 patients with various ciliopathies, found no further causative mutations.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 OROFACIODIGITAL SYNDROME XVIII**  
IFT57, 777G-A [NM_018010.3:c.777G>A; p.Lys259Lys; chr3:g.107910368C>T]

In 3 affected sibs from a consanguineous family with orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII (OFD18), originally reported by Phadke et al. (1999), Thevenon et al. (2016) identified a homozygous c.777G-A transition in the last base of exon 6 of the IFT57 gene. The change is synonymous at the protein level (lys259 to lys; K259K) but alters splicing. The mutation was found by homozygosity mapping and exome sequencing and was confirmed by Sanger sequencing in all 3 affected sibs. It was absent from ExAC and from 50 healthy individuals from northern India. In patient fibroblasts it produced normal transcript together with abnormal transcripts with skipping of exon 6, exons 5 and 6, or exons 4 through 6. It also reduced IFT57 mRNA levels, impaired anterograde ciliary transport of IFT57, and reduced sonic hedgehog pathway activation.

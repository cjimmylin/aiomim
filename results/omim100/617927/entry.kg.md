### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII (OFD18) is caused by homozygous mutation in the IFT57 gene (606621) on chromosome 3q13.

### Description

Orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII is a type of oral-facial-digital syndrome (OFDS) with short stature and brachymesophalangia. It overlaps with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome (EVCS). Thevenon et al. (2016) found that the disorder was caused by a homozygous hypomorphic mutation in IFT57 and called it the first description of a ciliary transport defect in OFDS.

### Clinical Features

Phadke et al. (1999) reported sibs from a consanguineous family who had a new variety of oral-facial-digital syndrome. In addition to oral, facial, and digital malformations, the sibs had acromelic short stature and genu valgum. The authors noted that these features overlap with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome. One sib also had urinary incontinence and growth hormone deficiency. The authors stated that neither feature had been reported before in any type of OFDS.

Thevenon et al. (2016) restudied the consanguineous Indian family first reported by Phadke et al. (1999). It included 3 affected sibs who had been followed for 15 years. One sib (II-3) died at 17 years of a complicated pulmonary infection. Subject II-1 was a male examined at 25 years. He had normal intelligence and short stature (155 cm, -3 SD). His facial and oral features were a square face with a short forehead, upslanting palpebral fissures, a high nasal bridge, a pyriform nose, a short philtrum, a medial cleft of the upper lip, supernumerary frenulae, and missing incisors. His limb features were pronounced brachydactyly, postaxial polydactyly of the upper limbs, and preaxial polydactyly of the feet due to duplication of the first metatarsal. X-rays showed severe mesophalangeal shortening, short femoral necks with varus femurs, and genu valgum. There was no thoracic deformity and there were no vertebral anomalies. Heart, renal, and abdominal ultrasounds were normal.

Subject II-2 in the family reported by Thevenon et al. (2016) was a female examined at 22 years. She had normal intelligence and short stature (122 cm, -5 SD). Her facial and oral features were a square face, a narrow forehead, enophthalmos, upslanting palpebral fissures, a broad nasal bridge, bilateral hypopigmented pseudoclefts, a medial labial cleft, missing incisors, and supernumerary frenulae. She had marked brachydactyly and postaxial polydactyly of all 4 limbs. X-rays showed: - mesophalangeal shortening, fused proximal phalanges, and bifid distal phalanges - duplication of the first metatarsal with absent middle phalanges (preaxial polydactyly) - vertebral bodies with medial notches, without platyspondyly - bilateral cervical ribs - gracile long bones and short femoral necks She had no thoracic deformity. Brain MRI and abdominal and heart ultrasounds were normal.

After reviewing the family, Thevenon et al. (2016) concluded that it had an unclassified type of OFDS with skeletal dysplasia rather than EVCS. The patients did not have short ribs or a narrow thorax, but they did have short stature, brachymesophalangia, and stocky femoral necks. The affected individuals had no visual complaints, although retinopathy was not excluded.

### Inheritance

Phadke et al. (1999) described this OFDS variant in sibs born to consanguineous parents. Thevenon et al. (2016) found a homozygous IFT57 mutation in the affected sibs of this family, which is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Mapping

Thevenon et al. (2016) performed homozygosity mapping in 2 affected sibs of the consanguineous family originally reported by Phadke et al. (1999). The sibs shared runs of homozygosity totaling 11 Mb on chromosome 3 (chr3:104822019-111671862) and chromosome 10 (chr10:1372501-6029726). Segregation in the third affected sib excluded the chromosome 10 interval.

### Heterogeneity

Thevenon et al. (2016) used Sanger sequencing to screen 13 OFDS patients with polydactyly and cleft lip/palate and 12 EVCS patients who were negative for EVC/EVC2 mutations. None had a causative IFT57 mutation. Exome or ciliome data from 172 patients with various ciliopathies also showed no biallelic causative IFT57 mutations.

### Molecular Genetics

Thevenon et al. (2016) studied 3 sibs from a consanguineous Indian family with an unclassified OFDS with short stature and brachymesophalangia, originally reported by Phadke et al. (1999). Using homozygosity mapping and exome sequencing, they identified a homozygous synonymous variant in the IFT57 gene: a c.777G-A transition (p.Lys259Lys; NM_018010.3) affecting the last base of exon 6. Sanger sequencing confirmed that all 3 affected sibs were homozygous. The variant was not found in the ExAC database or in 50 healthy northern Indian controls. In patient fibroblasts, the variant caused a complex splicing pattern. Normal transcript was present along with 3 aberrant transcripts: - in-frame skipping of exon 6 - in-frame skipping of exons 5 and 6 - out-of-frame skipping of exons 4 through 6 The aberrant transcripts were detected only after inhibition of nonsense-mediated decay. IFT57 mRNA levels were reduced. These findings were consistent with a hypomorphic allele.

### Pathogenesis

Thevenon et al. (2016) found that fibroblasts from the patients had a normal number and length of primary cilia but reduced anterograde ciliary transport. IFT57 staining at the ciliary tip was significantly decreased (90% vs 35%), and staining at the basal body was increased. Stimulation with SAG produced poor induction of GLI1 mRNA in patient fibroblasts. siRNA knockdown of IFT57 in control fibroblasts abolished GLI1 induction. These results indicated defective sonic hedgehog signaling, and the authors suggested that this defect underlies many of the phenotypes.

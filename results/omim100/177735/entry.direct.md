### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal dominant pseudohypoaldosteronism type I (PHA1A) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding the mineralocorticoid receptor (NR3C2).

### Description

Pseudohypoaldosteronism type I is characterized by neonatal renal salt wasting with dehydration, hypotension, hyperkalemia, and metabolic acidosis despite elevated aldosterone levels (Geller et al., 1998). The autosomal dominant or sporadic form is milder than the autosomal recessive form and remits with age (Geller et al., 1998; Viemann et al., 2001). Hanukoglu (1991) concluded that type I PHA includes 2 clinically and genetically distinct entities, one with a renal defect and one with defects in multiple target organs.

### Clinical Features

Hanukoglu (1991) studied 2 kindreds with a total of 9 patients with PHA. In kindred I, the proband had renal salt wasting in infancy, with vomiting, failure to thrive, short stature, hyponatremia, and hyperkalemia, and responded dramatically to a high salt diet (2.5 g/day). Sodium supplementation was stopped at age 2 years. In 7 more family members from 3 generations, clinical expression of PHA ranged from asymptomatic to moderate. In affected members (the proband, mother, and 2 brothers), hyperaldosteronism persisted over 13 years, but plasma renin activity (PRA) fell gradually to near normal values. Hanukoglu (1991) interpreted the persistent hyperaldosteronism with falling PRA as tertiary hyperaldosteronism due to an autonomously functioning zona glomerulosa. In kindred II, the proband, born of a consanguineous marriage, developed severe renal salt loss at age 9 days. She also had increased salivary and sweat electrolytes, consistent with unresponsiveness to mineralocorticoids in multiple organs. Life-threatening episodes of salt wasting recurred after age 2 years, and at age 5 years she still needed high-dose salt supplements (14 g/day). A sister died at 9 days of age with PHA symptoms.

Tajima et al. (2000) studied a Japanese family with a renal form of PHA1.

Viemann et al. (2001) studied a sporadic case and 5 affected patients from 2 families with autosomal dominant PHA1. The family members of the sporadic patient were asymptomatic.

Sartorato et al. (2003) described autosomal dominant or sporadic PHA1 in 14 families as a rare form of mineralocorticoid resistance characterized by neonatal renal salt wasting and failure to thrive.

### Biochemical Features

Armanini et al. (1985) studied 3 patients with pseudohypoaldosteronism: a 28-year-old index case in Melbourne and 2 sibs in Munich, aged 8 and 2 years. The younger Munich sib was less severely affected than his sister. Percoll-separated control monocytes bound [3H]aldosterone with high affinity (Kd approximately 3 nM) and limited capacity (150 to 600 sites per cell). On repeated testing, monocytes from the index case and the older Munich sib showed no [3H]aldosterone binding. The younger sib had 62 sites per cell, more than 2 SD below control values. The parents of the Munich sibs were first cousins and had normal binding. Armanini et al. (1985) concluded that pseudohypoaldosteronism is caused by a defect in the type I (mineralocorticoid) receptor, that the defect may be complete or partial, and that transmission may be autosomal recessive.

### Inheritance

Hanukoglu (1991) found that the pedigree of kindred I fit autosomal dominant transmission with variable expression. The pedigree of kindred II, in which PHA involved multiple organs, fit autosomal recessive inheritance.

Geller et al. (1998) found heterozygous NR3C2 mutations that segregated with PHA1 in 4 autosomal dominant kindreds. They also found a de novo mutation in a sporadic case. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Geller et al. (1998) found no mutations in the genes for the epithelial sodium channel (ENaC) in 6 dominant and 7 sporadic PHA1 kindreds. ENaC mutations cause the autosomal recessive form. They found NR3C2 mutations in only 5 of these 13 kindreds.

Viemann et al. (2001) found no NR3C2 mutation in 2 kindreds with autosomal dominant PHA1. They concluded that autosomal dominant or sporadic PHA1 is genetically heterogeneous and involves other, as yet unidentified, genes.

Sartorato et al. (2003) found NR3C2 mutations in 70% of their familial cases of autosomal dominant PHA1. They suggested that the remaining families might have mutations in other genes.

Pujo et al. (2007) noted that a genocopy was possible in 3 PHA1 kindreds without NR3C2 mutations. They suggested that other patients without NR3C2 mutations might have different diseases that resemble PHA1 in the neonatal period.

### Molecular Genetics

Geller et al. (1998) screened the NR3C2 gene in 6 dominant and 7 sporadic PHA1 kindreds. They found heterozygous mutations in 1 sporadic and 4 dominant kindreds: 2 frameshift mutations (1 of them de novo), 2 premature termination codons, and 1 splice donor mutation. The mutations segregated with PHA1 and were not found in unaffected subjects.

Tajima et al. (2000) used PCR and direct sequencing to find a heterozygous missense mutation in the NR3C2 gene (L924P) in all affected members of a Japanese family with renal PHA1. In transfected COS-1 cells, aldosterone increased reporter activity in cells expressing wildtype receptor but had no effect in cells expressing the mutant receptor. The authors stated that this was the first missense mutation in the NR3C2 gene reported in renal PHA1.

In a patient with sporadic PHA1, Viemann et al. (2001) identified a frameshift mutation (2871insC) in exon 9 of the NR3C2 gene. The asymptomatic family members did not carry the mutation. The authors noted that this was the first mutation described in exon 9 and that it impaired the last 27 amino acids of the hormone-binding domain.

Sartorato et al. (2003) analyzed the NR3C2 gene in 14 families with autosomal dominant or sporadic PHA1 and found 6 heterozygous mutations. Two frameshift mutations in exon 2 (insT1354 and del8bp537) and a nonsense mutation in exon 4 (C645X) were predicted to produce truncated proteins. Three missense mutations (G633R, Q776R, and L979P) affected receptor function in different ways. The authors suggested that L979P has a transdominant negative effect on wildtype receptor activity, whereas G633R and Q776R probably result in haploinsufficiency.

Riepe et al. (2006) found 6 heterozygous NR3C2 mutations in 6 patients with PHA1. These were 2 frameshift mutations (c.1131dupT, previously reported, and c.2871dupC), 2 novel nonsense mutations that produce a truncated receptor protein, and 2 missense mutations (S818L and E972G). Functional studies included aldosterone binding, transactivation, subcellular translocation, proteolysis experiments, and 3-dimensional modeling.

Pujo et al. (2007) screened coding regions, intron-exon junctions, untranslated exons, and large deletions of the NR3C2 gene. They found a total of 22 NR3C2 abnormalities in 33 patients and showed that NR3C2 mutations are very frequent in PHA1 patients classified by aldosterone and potassium levels.

### History

Early reports of salt-losing syndromes in infancy included those of Cheek and Perry (1958) and Raine and Roy (1962). Donnell et al. (1959) described 'pseudohypo-adrenalocorticism,' with renal sodium loss, hyponatremia, and hyperkalemia due to renal tubular insensitivity to mineralocorticoids. Hanukoglu et al. (1978) addressed the inheritance of pseudohypoaldosteronism.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 PSEUDOHYPOALDOSTERONISM, TYPE I, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
NR3C2, LEU924PRO [L924P; CTG-to-CCG at codon 924]

In all affected members of a Japanese family with renal PHA1, Tajima et al. (2000) identified a heterozygous mutation in the NR3C2 gene that changes codon 924 from CTG to CCG, resulting in a leu924-to-pro (L924P) substitution. In transfected COS-1 cells, aldosterone increased reporter activity with wildtype receptor but had no effect with the mutant receptor.

**.0002 PSEUDOHYPOALDOSTERONISM, TYPE I, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
NR3C2, 1-BP INS, 2871C [Ins2871C; c.2871dupC]

In a patient with sporadic PHA1, Viemann et al. (2001) identified a frameshift mutation, a 1-bp insertion (2871insC) in exon 9 of the NR3C2 gene. The mutation impaired the last 27 amino acids of the hormone-binding domain and was absent in the asymptomatic family members.

Riepe et al. (2006) found that this mutation, which they designated c.2871dupC, abolished aldosterone binding and transactivation because of a major change in receptor conformation.

**.0003 PSEUDOHYPOALDOSTERONISM, TYPE I, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
NR3C2, GLY633ARG [G633R]

Sartorato et al. (2003) identified a heterozygous gly633-to-arg (G633R) mutation in the DNA-binding domain of NR3C2 in a family with autosomal dominant or sporadic PHA1. The mutant had reduced maximal transactivation, although its binding characteristics and ED50 of transactivation were comparable to wildtype. The authors suggested that the mutation probably causes haploinsufficiency. ⚠︎

**.0004 PSEUDOHYPOALDOSTERONISM, TYPE I, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
NR3C2, CYS645TER [C2157A; Cys645stop]

In a family with autosomal dominant or sporadic PHA1, Sartorato et al. (2003) identified a heterozygous 2157C-A transversion in exon 4 of the NR3C2 gene, resulting in a cys645-to-ter (C645X) substitution that generates a truncated protein. ⚠︎

**.0005 PSEUDOHYPOALDOSTERONISM, TYPE I, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
NR3C2, GLN776ARG [Q776R]

Sartorato et al. (2003) identified a heterozygous gln776-to-arg (Q776R) mutation in the ligand-binding domain of NR3C2 in a family with autosomal dominant or sporadic PHA1. The mutant showed reduced aldosterone binding and reduced ligand-dependent transactivation, and the authors suggested that the mutation probably causes haploinsufficiency. ⚠︎

**.0006 PSEUDOHYPOALDOSTERONISM, TYPE I, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
NR3C2, LEU979PRO [L979P]

Sartorato et al. (2003) identified a heterozygous leu979-to-pro (L979P) mutation in the ligand-binding domain of NR3C2 in a family with autosomal dominant or sporadic PHA1. The mutant showed no aldosterone binding or ligand-dependent transactivation and had a transdominant negative effect on wildtype receptor activity.

**.0007 PSEUDOHYPOALDOSTERONISM, TYPE I, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
NR3C2, SER818LEU [S818L]

In a patient with PHA1, Riepe et al. (2006) identified a heterozygous ser818-to-leu (S818L) mutation in the NR3C2 gene. The mutant receptor did not bind aldosterone, activate transcription, or translocate into the nucleus. Modeling showed a major displacement of several residues involved in aldosterone binding.

**.0008 PSEUDOHYPOALDOSTERONISM, TYPE I, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT**  
NR3C2, GLU972GLY [E972G]

In a patient with PHA1, Riepe et al. (2006) identified a heterozygous glu972-to-gly (E972G) mutation in the NR3C2 gene. The mutant had significantly lower ligand-binding affinity and only 9% of wildtype transcriptional activity, which the authors attributed to major changes in receptor conformation.

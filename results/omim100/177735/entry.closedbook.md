### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal dominant pseudohypoaldosteronism type I (PHA1A) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the mineralocorticoid receptor gene (NR3C2; 600983) on chromosome 4q31.

### Description

Autosomal dominant pseudohypoaldosteronism type I is a form of mineralocorticoid resistance that affects only the kidney. In the neonatal period it causes renal salt wasting, dehydration, hyperkalemia and metabolic acidosis, even though plasma renin activity and aldosterone are markedly raised. It is milder than the autosomal recessive multisystem form and usually improves with age. Sodium supplementation can often be stopped in childhood, and some adult carriers have no symptoms apart from high aldosterone levels. ✗

### Nomenclature

Hanukoglu (1991) distinguished 2 forms of PHA I. One is a renal form, inherited as an autosomal dominant trait or occurring sporadically, in which salt wasting is limited to the kidney and improves with age. The other is a more severe, autosomal recessive multisystem form, in which mineralocorticoid resistance also affects the sweat glands, salivary glands and colon. The recessive form is caused by mutations in subunits of the epithelial sodium channel. ✗

### Clinical Features

Cheek and Perry (1958) are generally credited with the first description of pseudohypoaldosteronism: an infant with renal salt wasting who did not respond to mineralocorticoid. ✗

Hanukoglu (1991) reviewed families with the renal form. Affected infants presented with salt wasting and failure to thrive. Their electrolytes became normal on salt supplementation, and with age they became clinically well. Clinically unaffected relatives often had raised plasma aldosterone, suggesting variable expressivity of a dominant trait. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The renal form of PHA I is transmitted as an autosomal dominant trait, and sporadic cases also occur. Clinical expression is variable: some heterozygous carriers have only biochemical abnormalities such as raised aldosterone. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Geller et al. (1998) identified heterozygous mutations in the mineralocorticoid receptor gene (NR3C2) in kindreds with autosomal dominant PHA I. The mutations included frameshift, premature termination and splice-site changes, consistent with loss of function and haploinsufficiency of the receptor. Within families the mutations segregated with the phenotype, including with raised aldosterone in clinically unaffected carriers. ✗

Later studies found further heterozygous NR3C2 mutations, including missense mutations, in patients with the renal form of PHA I. Some of these mutations were de novo in sporadic cases. In some patients with the clinical phenotype, no NR3C2 mutation was found.

### Clinical Management

Treatment is sodium chloride supplementation in infancy. Because mineralocorticoid sensitivity improves with age, supplementation can usually be tapered and stopped during childhood. ✗

### Animal Model

Mice lacking the mineralocorticoid receptor developed severe salt wasting in the neonatal period, with hyperkalemia and markedly raised renin and aldosterone, and died without salt supplementation. This model resembles human PHA I. ✗

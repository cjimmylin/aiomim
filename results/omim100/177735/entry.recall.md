### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal dominant pseudohypoaldosteronism type I (PHA1A) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the mineralocorticoid receptor gene (NR3C2; 600983) on chromosome 4q31.23.

### Description

Autosomal dominant pseudohypoaldosteronism type I is a form of mineralocorticoid resistance limited to the kidney (Hanukoglu, 1991). In the dominant kindred reported by Hanukoglu (1991), the disorder presented in infancy with renal salt wasting and hyperkalemia, and hyperaldosteronism was documented. Plasma renin activity was raised at first but fell gradually to near-normal values. Geller et al. (1998) described autosomal dominant PHA1 as a milder disease that remits with age.

### Nomenclature

Hanukoglu (1991) distinguished 2 clinically and genetically distinct forms of PHA I. One is a renal form, limited to the kidney, in a kindred whose pedigree was consistent with autosomal dominant transmission. In the proband of this kindred, sodium supplementation was stopped at age 2 years. The other is a more severe form, in a kindred whose pedigree was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. In that form, multiple target organs do not respond to mineralocorticoids, as shown by increased salivary and sweat electrolytes.

### Clinical Features

Cheek and Perry (1958) described a salt-wasting syndrome in infancy.

Hanukoglu (1991) studied 2 kindreds with a total of 9 patients with PHA. One kindred had the renal form. The proband presented in infancy with renal salt wasting, failure to thrive and hyperkalemia. He responded dramatically to a high-salt diet (2.5 g/day), and sodium supplementation was stopped at age 2 years. In other family members, clinical expression varied from asymptomatic to moderate. In the affected members (the proband, his mother and 2 brothers), hyperaldosteronism persisted for 13 years, while plasma renin activity fell gradually to near-normal values. The pedigree was consistent with autosomal dominant transmission with variable expression. The dominant kindred ranged from asymptomatic to moderately affected, whereas the recessive case was severe.

### Inheritance

Hanukoglu (1991) found that the renal form of PHA I was transmitted as an autosomal dominant trait with variable expression. Clinical expression ranged from asymptomatic to moderate, and hyperaldosteronism persisted in affected members. Tajima et al. (2000) noted that sporadic cases also occur.

### Molecular Genetics

Geller et al. (1998) identified heterozygous mutations in the mineralocorticoid receptor gene (NR3C2) in kindreds with autosomal dominant PHA I. The mutations included frameshift, premature termination and splice donor mutations, and they segregated with PHA1.

In a family with renal PHA I, Tajima et al. (2000) identified a heterozygous missense mutation in the NR3C2 gene in all affected members. To their knowledge, it was the first missense mutation of NR3C2 reported in renal PHA1.

In a sporadic patient with renal PHA I, Viemann et al. (2001) identified a frameshift mutation in the NR3C2 gene. Family members were asymptomatic and did not carry the mutation, which indicated de novo occurrence. In 2 kindreds with autosomal dominant PHA1, Viemann et al. (2001) found no NR3C2 mutation.

### Clinical Management

Hanukoglu (1991) treated the proband of a dominant kindred in infancy with salt supplementation (a high-salt diet). Sodium supplementation was stopped at age 2 years.

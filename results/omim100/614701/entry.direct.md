### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Cornelia de Lange syndrome-4 with or without midline brain defects (CDLS4) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the RAD21 gene (606462) on chromosome 8q24.11.

### Description

Cornelia de Lange syndrome-4 (CDLS4) is a cohesinopathy characterized by growth retardation, minor skeletal anomalies, and facial features that overlap those of Cornelia de Lange syndrome (CdLS). Cognitive impairment is much milder than in patients with classical CdLS caused by mutation in NIPBL, SMC1A, or SMC3 (Deardorff et al., 2012). Overall, affected individuals have an attenuated CdLS phenotype. Facial dysmorphism, limb anomalies, and cognitive and behavioral involvement are all less marked, and there is striking interfamilial and intrafamilial variability (Krab et al., 2020). Some patients have holoprosencephaly, with or without CdLS characteristics (Kruszka et al., 2019; Krab et al., 2020).

### Clinical Features

Deardorff et al. (2012) reported children with RAD21 mutations who had growth retardation, minor skeletal anomalies, and facial features that overlapped findings in individuals with CdLS. Their cognitive impairment was much milder than that of individuals with classical CdLS caused by mutations in NIPBL, SMC1A, or SMC3.

Minor et al. (2014) reported 2 patients with atypical CdLS. The first had developmental delay, hypospadias, inguinal hernia, and dysmorphic features. The second had developmental delay, characteristic facial features, hirsutism, and hand and foot anomalies. The first patient was less severely affected than the second. The first patient's mother, from whom he had inherited the RAD21 mutation, had a history of melanoma and other unspecified medical problems. Minor et al. (2014) discussed the variable clinical presentation within families and the low penetrance of mutations.

Boyle et al. (2017) reported a patient (IV.16) who could be classified as having mild CdLS. The same RAD21 variant was present in the patient's mother and 2 aunts. None of them had been suspected of having CdLS or a cohesinopathy before the study, and none fulfilled the diagnostic criteria for CdLS. Boyle et al. (2017) suggested that RAD21 variants are associated with incomplete penetrance and that carriers may therefore be underdiagnosed.

Gudmundsson et al. (2019) reported a 15-month-old boy with developmental delay, distinct CdLS-like facial features, gastrointestinal reflux in early infancy, testis retention, prominent digit pads, and diaphragmatic hernia. His parents were healthy.

Kruszka et al. (2019) reported holoprosencephaly in individuals with variants in cohesin complex genes. These included 4 individuals with variants in the non-X-linked genes SMC3 and RAD21. Krab et al. (2020) noted that some of the individuals with loss-of-function cohesin variants and holoprosencephaly reported by Kruszka et al. (2019) also had features of CdLS.

Dorval et al. (2020) reported a boy with a mild CdLS presentation and compared his clinical features with those of previously described patients carrying RAD21 intragenic mutations.

Goel and Parasivam (2020) reported another case of holoprosencephaly associated with a RAD21 loss-of-function variant.

Krab et al. (2020) reported 49 individuals from 33 families with RAD21 alterations, 24 of whom had not been reported before. The patients came from Australia, Belgium, Canada, Denmark, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, Turkey, the United Kingdom, and the United States. Full clinical information was available for 29 individuals from 22 families (cohort A). Their ages ranged from 0 to 61 years (median, 9 years), and 15 were male. All patients in cohort A had CdLS clinical scores of at least 5, which is sufficient to warrant molecular testing for CdLS. CdLS had been suspected before testing in 13 of 21 index cases.

Compared with NIPBL and SMC1A cohorts, the patients reported by Krab et al. (2020) had an attenuated CdLS phenotype. Common facial features included: - long and/or smooth philtrum (90%) - short nose (88%) - concave nasal ridge (83%) - thick eyebrows (83%) - long eyelashes (81%) - thin upper lip vermilion (79%) - upturned nasal tip (70%) - synophrys (68%) - arched eyebrows (67%) Other findings were: - short fifth finger (82%) - postnatal head circumference below -2 SD (57%) - clinodactyly of the fifth finger (54%) - gastroesophageal reflux (52%) - dislocated elbow or abnormal elbow extension (46%) - ptosis (42%) - heart anomalies (39%) - hirsutism (38%) - postnatal height below -2 SD (37%) - hearing loss (33%) - cleft or submucous cleft palate (24%) - seizures (9%) No patient had a major limb malformation or visual impairment. Hands and feet were generally of normal size. Growth at birth was generally less impaired than in the other cohorts, and short stature and microcephaly developed postnatally. Unusual anomalies included vertebral anomalies (clefts and hemivertebrae) and relatively frequent malformations of structures derived from the embryonic foregut. Diaphragmatic hernia, anal atresia, and choanal atresia occurred occasionally.

Cognitive function in the cohort reported by Krab et al. (2020) was normal in 3 of 29 patients and mildly impaired in 13 of 29. Four patients had moderate disability, and none had severe or profound disability. All patients aged 3 years and above used some words. Development showed more language-based than motor-based problems. Fourteen of 25 patients had behavioral problems, mainly anxiety, ADHD, autism spectrum disorder, and obsessive-compulsive behavior. Aggression and self-injurious behavior were each found in only 1 patient. Some affected individuals had children of their own and were diagnosed only after a more severely affected child was recognized. Additional phenotypes seen in individuals with limited clinical information (cohort B) included holoprosencephaly, with or without CdLS characteristics. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

CDLS4 shows autosomal dominant inheritance. It may result from de novo mutations (Deardorff et al., 2012; Gudmundsson et al., 2019) or from mutations transmitted from mildly affected or apparently unaffected parents, with variable expressivity and incomplete penetrance (Minor et al., 2014; Boyle et al., 2017). In the series of Krab et al. (2020), 9 of the 21 index cases for whom inheritance could be established were familial. ⚠︎

### Cytogenetics

Wuyts et al. (2002) described a boy with multiple exostoses, hypertrichosis, mental retardation, and epilepsy due to a de novo deletion on chromosome 8q24. The deletion interval overlapped the Langer-Giedion syndrome region. It involved the EXT1 gene and additional genes located distal to EXT1, but probably did not include TRPS1, which lies proximal to EXT1. Krab et al. (2020) excluded this case from their series because other disease genes were involved in the deletion.

McBrien et al. (2008) reported a boy with a small 8q24 microdeletion characterized by oligo array CGH. They described the deletion as involving EXT1 alone. The boy had a mild Langer-Giedion-like phenotype and unusual features, including prominent toe pads and fat pads on the soles of his feet. Krab et al. (2020) listed this patient, who was also patient 2 of Deardorff et al. (2012), as having a de novo 1.7-Mb deletion at 8q23.3q24.12 that included the whole RAD21 gene.

Among the 33 families reported by Krab et al. (2020), 7 had unique deletions that included RAD21, and 6 of these deletions also included other genes. One patient with a CdLS clinical score of 12 had a 27-kb deletion that included exons 1 through 9 of RAD21. Krab et al. (2020) noted that RAD21 lies at 8q24.11 between TRPS1 and EXT1. Microdeletions involving neighboring genes therefore make it harder to attribute the phenotype to RAD21. Exostoses due to EXT1 haploinsufficiency likely caused the upper limb anomalies in patients with microdeletions.

### Molecular Genetics

Deardorff et al. (2012) identified mutations in the RAD21 gene in children with a CdLS-like phenotype. The mutations acted at the interface of RAD21 with the cohesin proteins STAG2 and SMC1A. They impaired the cellular DNA damage response and disrupted transcription in a zebrafish model. According to Krab et al. (2020), these patients included 2 with de novo heterozygous missense mutations, P376R and C585R, and 2 with deletions encompassing the whole RAD21 gene.

In 2 patients with atypical CdLS, Minor et al. (2014) reported the first intragenic deletion and frameshift mutations identified in RAD21. One patient had an in-frame deletion of exon 13 inherited from his mother. The other had a c.592_593dup frameshift mutation. Krab et al. (2020) specified these mutations as a 665-bp in-frame deletion (c.1621-388_1704+193del; Asp541_Gln568del) and c.592_593dupAG (Ser198ArgfsTer6).

In a patient with mild CdLS, Boyle et al. (2017) identified a heterozygous 1-bp deletion (c.704delG) in the RAD21 gene, predicted to result in a frameshift and premature termination (Ser235IlefsTer19). The deletion was also present in the patient's mother and 2 maternal aunts, who did not fulfill the diagnostic criteria for CdLS. ⚠︎

In a 15-month-old boy with CDLS4, Gudmundsson et al. (2019) used exome sequencing to identify a de novo heterozygous 3-bp in-frame deletion (c.1774_1776del) in the RAD21 gene, resulting in deletion of gln592 (Gln592del). An in silico structural model predicted that the variant breaks the contacts between RAD21 lys591 and SMC1A residues glu1191 and glu1192, altering the RAD21-SMC1A interface. ⚠︎

Kruszka et al. (2019) identified variants in the cohesin complex genes SMC3 and RAD21 in 4 individuals with holoprosencephaly. According to Krab et al. (2020), the RAD21 variants included a de novo 8-bp deletion (c.1217_1224del; Lys406ArgfsTer4) in a patient with a CdLS clinical score of 13. They also included a paternally inherited 1-bp duplication (c.1550dupC; Glu518ArgfsTer19) in a patient who had also been reported by Yuan et al. (2019).

Dorval et al. (2020) identified a RAD21 mutation in a boy with a mild CdLS presentation. Krab et al. (2020) specified this mutation as a de novo 4-bp deletion (c.943_946del) predicted to cause a frameshift (Glu315GlnfsTer9).

Krab et al. (2020) reported 31 different RAD21 alterations in 33 families: 7 unique deletions and 24 intragenic sequence variants. Two variants were recurrent: C585R and R586X were each found in 2 families. Of the 24 sequence variants, 13 were predicted to be truncating: 2 nonsense, 2 splice site, and 9 frameshift variants, scattered throughout the gene. Three were in-frame deletions, and the missense variants tended to cluster in the functional domains of the protein. Krab et al. (2020) used protein modeling and molecular dynamics to study 12 variants. They concluded that at least 6 were likely pathogenic: S345P, P376R, C585R, R586Q, Q592del, and L603P. In a family with Mungan syndrome, affected individuals carried biallelic A622T variants, and heterozygous family members had some features of the CdLS spectrum. Whether this variant causes a phenotype in the heterozygous state remained uncertain.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Deardorff et al. (2012) suggested that dominant missense mutations in RAD21 cause more severe functional defects and worse structural and cognitive findings than loss-of-function mutations.

Krab et al. (2020) did not find clear differences between genotype groups. Patients with microdeletions tended to have higher CdLS scores and more often had impaired growth parameters than patients with intragenic variants. However, the 2 groups did not appear to differ in the frequency of major malformations or of cognitive and behavioral problems. CdLS scores and growth parameters did not differ between patients with truncating variants (median score 10; range, 9-13) and those with non-truncating sequence variants (median score 9.5; range, 5-12). In 12 of 15 patients with intragenic variants and major malformations or health problems, the variant lay in a protein-binding domain, but the authors noted that this association was uncertain. Intrafamilial variation was considerable, especially in cognition and behavior, and could not be explained by mosaicism in most families. ⚠︎

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CORNELIA DE LANGE SYNDROME 4**  
RAD21, PRO376ARG [c.1127C>G; p.Pro376Arg]

In a patient with CDLS4, Krab et al. (2020) listed a de novo heterozygous c.1127C-G transversion in exon 9 of the RAD21 gene, resulting in a pro376-to-arg (P376R) substitution in the STAG-binding domain. The patient had a CdLS clinical score of at least 10. Molecular dynamics modeling predicted that the substitution forms a new salt bridge between RAD21 and STAG2, over-stabilizing the interaction between the 2 proteins.

**.0002 CORNELIA DE LANGE SYNDROME 4**  
RAD21, CYS585ARG [c.1753T>C; p.Cys585Arg]

In a patient with CDLS4, Krab et al. (2020) listed a de novo heterozygous c.1753T-C transition in exon 14 of the RAD21 gene, resulting in a cys585-to-arg (C585R) substitution in the SMC1A-binding domain. Krab et al. (2020) also found the same variant in 2 affected members of a second family. Molecular dynamics modeling showed that the variant causes the adjacent arg586 to lose its interaction with glu577, destabilizing the RAD21-SMC1A domain.

**.0003 CORNELIA DE LANGE SYNDROME 4**  
RAD21, 665-BP DEL, EX13 [c.1621-388_1704+193del; p.Asp541_Gln568del]

In a patient with atypical CdLS, Minor et al. (2014) identified a heterozygous in-frame deletion of exon 13 of the RAD21 gene, inherited from his mother. The patient had developmental delay, hypospadias, inguinal hernia, and dysmorphic features. His mother had a history of melanoma and other unspecified medical problems. Krab et al. (2020) specified the variant as a 665-bp deletion (c.1621-388_1704+193del) removing asp541 through gln568 (Asp541_Gln568del). They reported CdLS clinical scores of 8 for the patient and at least 5 for his mother. ⚠︎

**.0004 CORNELIA DE LANGE SYNDROME 4**

In a patient with atypical CdLS, Minor et al. (2014) identified a heterozygous 2-bp duplication (c.592_593dup) in the RAD21 gene, resulting in a frameshift. The patient had developmental delay, characteristic facial features, hirsutism, and hand and foot anomalies. Krab et al. (2020) specified the variant as c.592_593dupAG in exon 6, predicted to result in Ser198ArgfsTer6, and reported a CdLS clinical score of 12 for this patient. ⚠︎

**.0005 CORNELIA DE LANGE SYNDROME 4**

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Cornelia de Lange syndrome-4 with or without midline brain defects (CDLS4) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the RAD21 gene (606462) on chromosome 8q24.11. ⚠︎

### Description

Cornelia de Lange syndrome-4 (CDLS4) is a cohesinopathy caused by mutation in RAD21, which encodes a structural protein of the cohesin complex. Affected individuals have growth retardation, minor skeletal anomalies, and facial features overlapping those of Cornelia de Lange syndrome (CdLS), but cognitive impairment is much milder than in classic CdLS (Deardorff et al., 2012). Krab et al. (2020) described the phenotype as an attenuated form of CdLS compared with that caused by NIPBL or SMC1A variants, especially for facial morphology, limb anomalies, cognition, and behavior. Some patients have holoprosencephaly (Kruszka et al., 2019; Goel and Parasivam, 2020). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Deardorff et al. (2012) reported that children with RAD21 mutations had growth retardation, minor skeletal anomalies, and facial features overlapping those of CdLS. Unlike individuals with NIPBL, SMC1A, or SMC3 mutations, they had much milder cognitive impairment than is seen in classic CdLS.

Minor et al. (2014) reported 2 patients with an atypical, mild CdLS-like presentation. The first patient had developmental delay, hypospadias, inguinal hernia, and dysmorphic features. The second patient had developmental delay, characteristic facial features, hirsutism, and hand and foot anomalies. The first patient was more mildly affected than the second.

Boyle et al. (2017) described a family with intrafamilial phenotypic variation. The index patient was classified as having mild CdLS. The patient's mother and 2 aunts did not meet diagnostic criteria for CdLS, and none of them had been suspected of having CdLS or a cohesinopathy before the study.

Gudmundsson et al. (2019) reported a 15-month-old boy with CDLS4 who had developmental delay, distinct CdLS-like facial features, gastrointestinal reflux in early infancy, testis retention, prominent digit pads, and diaphragmatic hernia. The authors stated that the patient expanded the clinical description of CDLS4.

Kruszka et al. (2019) reported holoprosencephaly in 4 individuals with variants in the non-X-linked cohesin complex genes SMC3 and RAD21.

Dorval et al. (2020) reported a boy with a mild presentation of CdLS and compared his clinical features with those of previously reported patients who carried other RAD21 intragenic mutations. The authors noted that RAD21 is associated with a milder phenotype.

Krab et al. (2020) reported 49 individuals from 33 families with RAD21 alterations, 24 of whom had not been reported previously. The patients came from Australia, Europe, Turkey, Canada, and the United States. Full clinical information was available for 29 patients from 22 families (cohort A). Their phenotype was an attenuated form of CdLS compared with that caused by NIPBL or SMC1A variants, especially for facial morphology, limb anomalies, cognition, and behavior. The 29 patients in cohort A were aged 0 to 61 years, and 15 were male. All had CdLS scores of at least 5. Common features included short nose (23/26), long and/or smooth philtrum (26/29), concave nasal ridge (24/29), thick eyebrows (20/24), long eyelashes (21/26), synophrys (19/28), short fifth finger (23/28), and postnatal microcephaly (16/28). No patient had a major limb malformation. Small hands (5/27), small feet (3/27), hirsutism (10/26), seizures (2/22), and visual impairment (0/24) were less frequent than in the NIPBL and SMC1A cohorts. Hearing loss occurred in 8 of 24 patients, heart anomalies in 9 of 23, gastroesophageal reflux in 13 of 25, and cleft palate in 6 of 25. ⚠︎

Cognition and behavior were attenuated in the RAD21 patients compared with NIPBL and SMC1A patients (Krab et al., 2020). Of the 29 patients, 16 (55%) had normal cognition or mild intellectual disability, 4 had moderate disability, and none had severe or profound disability. All patients aged 3 years or older used some words. Fourteen of 25 had behavioral problems, mainly anxiety, ADHD, features of autism spectrum disorder, and obsessive-compulsive behavior. Self-injurious behavior and aggression were each seen in only 1 patient. ⚠︎

Krab et al. (2020) summarized the natural history. Pregnancy and birth usually progressed normally, and prenatal growth retardation was uncommon. About half of the patients had congenital anomalies such as cleft palate or cardiac anomalies. Diaphragmatic hernia, anal atresia, and choanal atresia occurred occasionally. Short stature and microcephaly developed postnatally. Vertebral anomalies (clefts and hemivertebrae) and malformations of structures derived from the embryonic foregut were unusual findings, but they were relatively frequent in this series. ⚠︎

Goel and Parasivam (2020) reported another case of holoprosencephaly associated with a loss-of-function variant in the RAD21 gene.

### Other Features

Clinical information on the remaining patients (cohort B) of Krab et al. (2020) was limited. The authors noted that it suggested that biallelic RAD21 variants can cause Mungan syndrome and that monoallelic variants can cause holoprosencephaly, as in one cohort A case. They also suggested a possible link with schizophrenia, but noted that this association may be coincidental. On follow-up, members of the family with Mungan syndrome showed CdLS characteristics that had not been mentioned in the original publication.

### Inheritance

CDLS4 is transmitted as an autosomal dominant trait. Mutations can arise de novo (Gudmundsson et al., 2019; Krab et al., 2020) or be inherited. Minor et al. (2014) reported the first familial case, in which a RAD21 deletion was inherited from the mother, and discussed variable clinical presentation within families and low penetrance. Boyle et al. (2017) found a RAD21 deletion in an index patient with mild CdLS as well as in the patient's mother and 2 aunts, who did not meet CdLS diagnostic criteria. They suggested that RAD21 variants are associated with incomplete penetrance and that carriers may therefore be underdiagnosed. Krab et al. (2020) found that RAD21 variants were frequently familial: this was the case in 9 of 21 index cases in which inheritance could be determined. ⚠︎

### Cytogenetics

Wuyts et al. (2002) identified a de novo submicroscopic interstitial deletion of chromosome 8q24 in a boy with multiple exostoses, hypertrichosis, mental retardation, epilepsy, and brain abnormalities. The deletion overlapped the Langer-Giedion syndrome region and included the EXT1 gene and additional genes distal to EXT1. The authors concluded that it probably did not include the TRPS1 gene, which lies proximal to EXT1.

McBrien et al. (2008) used oligo array CGH to characterize a small 8q24.1 microdeletion in a boy with a mild Langer-Giedion-like phenotype. The deletion involved EXT1 alone and not TRPS1, and the authors suggested that this pointed to a functional disturbance of TRPS1. The boy also had unusual features, including prominent toe pads and fat pads on the soles of the feet, similar to those described in Pierpont syndrome.

Krab et al. (2020) identified 7 deletions that included RAD21. Six also removed neighboring genes. The seventh was a 27-kb deletion confined to RAD21 exons 1 through 9 (8q24.11). Patients with microdeletions tended to have higher CdLS scores and more frequent growth impairment than those with intragenic variants, but major malformations and cognitive and behavioral problems were not more frequent. Exostoses related to EXT1 haploinsufficiency likely caused upper limb anomalies in some patients with deletions.

### Molecular Genetics

Deardorff et al. (2012) identified mutations in RAD21 in children with a congenital cohesinopathy phenotype. RAD21 encodes an integral structural protein of the cohesin complex. The mutations acted at the interface of RAD21 with the cohesin proteins STAG2 and SMC1A.

In a patient with mild atypical CdLS, Minor et al. (2014) identified an in-frame intragenic deletion of exon 13 of the RAD21 gene. This was the first intragenic deletion reported in RAD21. The deletion was inherited from the mother, who had a history of melanoma and other unspecified medical problems. In a second patient with atypical CdLS, they identified a frameshift mutation in RAD21 (c.592_593dup).

In a patient with mild CdLS, Boyle et al. (2017) identified a novel 1-bp deletion in the RAD21 gene (c.704delG). The deletion was predicted to cause a frameshift resulting in a premature stop codon (Ser235IlefsTer19) and was predicted to be deleterious. It was also present in the patient's mother and 2 aunts. The authors noted that RAD21 variants had previously been described in only 5 patients with cohesinopathies.

In a boy with CDLS4, Gudmundsson et al. (2019) used exome sequencing to identify a de novo heterozygous 3-bp deletion in the RAD21 gene (c.1774_1776del), resulting in an in-frame deletion of gln592 (Gln592del). The variant was absent in both healthy parents and was predicted to be disease-causing by bioinformatic analysis. In silico structural modeling predicted that the deletion disrupts contact between RAD21 lys591 and SMC1A residues glu1191 and glu1192, changing the RAD21-SMC1A interface. ⚠︎

Kruszka et al. (2019) reported holoprosencephaly in 4 individuals with variants in SMC3 and RAD21. They also reported holoprosencephaly in 11 individuals with variants in the X-linked cohesin genes STAG2 and SMC1A; 10 of these were loss-of-function variants and 1 was a missense variant.

Dorval et al. (2020) identified a novel RAD21 mutation in a boy with mild CdLS. They noted that very few RAD21 intragenic mutations had been identified.

In 33 families, Krab et al. (2020) identified 31 different RAD21 alterations: 7 unique deletions, 6 of which also included other genes, and 24 intragenic sequence variants, 2 of which were recurrent. Thirteen of the sequence variants were predicted to be truncating (2 nonsense, 2 splice site, and 9 frameshift) and were scattered throughout the gene. Missense variants, by contrast, tended to cluster in the functional domains. The recurrent variants were a missense variant (C585R) and a nonsense variant (R586X). Each was found in 2 families. Molecular dynamics modeling predicted structural effects for several variants located in the SMC1A-binding and STAG-binding domains. The authors also updated data on the family originally reported by Boyle et al. (2017) and on a family with an in-frame deletion of exon 13.

Goel and Parasivam (2020) reported a loss-of-function variant in the RAD21 gene in a patient with holoprosencephaly.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Deardorff et al. (2012) suggested that dominant missense mutations in RAD21 cause more severe functional defects than loss-of-function mutations and lead to worse structural and cognitive clinical findings.

Krab et al. (2020) found no difference in CdLS scores or growth parameters between patients with truncating and those with nontruncating RAD21 sequence variants. Twelve of 15 patients with intragenic variants and major malformations or health problems had a variant in a protein-binding domain, but the authors noted that it was uncertain whether this was a true association. Variability within and between families was striking, especially for cognition and behavior, and in most families it could not be explained by mosaicism. Patients with microdeletions tended to have higher CdLS scores and more frequent growth impairment than those with intragenic variants.

### Pathogenesis

Deardorff et al. (2012) found that the RAD21 mutations impaired the cellular DNA damage response and disrupted transcription in a zebrafish model.

Kruszka et al. (2019) concluded that the cohesin complex is an important regulator of median forebrain development. In human neural stem cells, shRNA knockdown of STAG2 and SMC1A caused aberrant expression of the holoprosencephaly-associated genes ZIC2, GLI2, SMAD3, and FGFR1.

Krab et al. (2020) suggested that the relatively mild phenotype indicates that, unlike other CdLS genes, RAD21 is not highly intolerant to loss of function. They speculated that RAD21 haploinsufficiency may be compensated more effectively than that of other cohesin genes. However, patients with microdeletions or truncating variants did not differ markedly from those with missense variants. Modeling suggested that pathogenic variants in the SMC1A-binding domain disturb the ATPase reaction that is pivotal to opening of the cohesin ring. ⚠︎

### Diagnosis

Krab et al. (2020) found that applying the CdLS consensus criteria for molecular testing would not have missed any RAD21 variant, and they concluded that the CdLS score remains a reliable tool. CdLS was suspected before testing in 13 of 21 index cases. Because familial occurrence was frequent and variability within families was marked, the authors recommended testing parents even when they appear unaffected.

Arg376 was predicted to form a new salt bridge with STAG2, over-stabilizing the interaction between the 2 proteins.

**.0006 CORNELIA DE LANGE SYNDROME 4 WITH OR WITHOUT MIDLINE BRAIN DEFECTS**

Krab et al. (2020) found a recurrent heterozygous c.1753T-C transition in the RAD21 gene, resulting in a cys585-to-arg (C585R) substitution, in 2 families. The mutation arose de novo in 1 patient. In the other family it was present in 2 relatives, whose parents were not tested. Molecular dynamics modeling predicted that Cys585Arg causes the adjacent arg586 to lose its salt bridge with glu577, destabilizing the RAD21-SMC1A domain.

**.0015 MUNGAN SYNDROME (HOMOZYGOUS); CDLS-SPECTRUM FEATURES IN HETEROZYGOTES, PATHOGENICITY UNCERTAIN**  
RAD21, ALA622THR [c.1864G>A; p.A622T]

Krab et al. (2020) updated the data on a family with Mungan syndrome previously reported by Bonora et al. (2015). In this family, 3 affected sibs were homozygous for a c.1864G-A transition in the RAD21 gene, resulting in an ala622-to-thr (A622T) substitution. Three further family members were heterozygous and had some clinical features of the CdLS spectrum. However, it remained uncertain whether they had a full CdLS phenotype and whether the variant causes disease in the heterozygous state. Modeling showed no structural change, but the authors noted that ala622 lies next to thr623, a PLK1 phosphorylation site. ⚠︎

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Cornelia de Lange syndrome-4 with or without midline brain defects (CDLS4) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the RAD21 gene (606462) on chromosome 8q24.11.

### Description

Cornelia de Lange syndrome-4 (CDLS4) is a cohesinopathy caused by mutation in RAD21, which encodes a structural protein of the cohesin complex. Affected individuals have growth retardation, minor skeletal anomalies, and facial features overlapping those of Cornelia de Lange syndrome (CdLS), but much milder cognitive impairment than is seen in classic CdLS (Deardorff et al., 2012). Krab et al. (2020) described the phenotype as an attenuated form of CdLS compared with that caused by NIPBL or SMC1A variants, particularly for facial morphology, limb anomalies, cognition, and behavior. Some patients have holoprosencephaly (Kruszka et al., 2019; Krab et al., 2020).

### Clinical Features

Deardorff et al. (2012) reported that children with RAD21 mutations had growth retardation, minor skeletal anomalies, and facial features overlapping those of Cornelia de Lange syndrome. Unlike individuals with NIPBL, SMC1A, or SMC3 mutations, they had much milder cognitive impairment than is seen in classic CdLS.

Minor et al. (2014) reported 2 unrelated patients with an atypical, mild CdLS-like presentation. The first patient had developmental delay, hypospadias, inguinal hernia, and dysmorphic features. The second had developmental delay, characteristic facial features, hirsutism, and anomalies of the hands and feet. The first patient was more mildly affected than the second. The mother of the first patient carried the same RAD21 deletion and had a history of melanoma and other unspecified medical problems. ⚠︎

Boyle et al. (2017) described a family with phenotypic variation among relatives carrying the same RAD21 deletion. The index patient was classified as having mild CdLS. The patient's mother and 2 aunts also carried the deletion but did not meet the diagnostic criteria for CdLS. None of them had been suspected of having CdLS or a cohesinopathy before the study. ⚠︎

Gudmundsson et al. (2019) reported a 15-month-old boy with CDLS4. He had developmental delay, distinct CdLS-like facial features, gastrointestinal reflux in early infancy, testis retention, prominent digit pads, and diaphragmatic hernia. The authors stated that this patient expanded the clinical description of CDLS4.

Kruszka et al. (2019) reported holoprosencephaly in 4 individuals with variants in the non-X-linked cohesin complex genes SMC3 and RAD21.

Dorval et al. (2020) reported a boy with a mild presentation of CdLS and a molecularly confirmed novel RAD21 mutation. They compared his clinical features with those of previously reported patients carrying other intragenic RAD21 mutations. They noted that RAD21 is associated with a milder phenotype and that very few intragenic RAD21 mutations had been identified.

Krab et al. (2020) reported 49 individuals from 33 families with RAD21 alterations. The patients came from Australia, Europe, Turkey, Canada, and the United States, and 24 of the cases were previously unpublished. Full clinical information was available for 29 patients from 22 families (cohort A), aged 0 to 61 years, of whom 15 were male. All cohort A patients had CdLS scores of at least 5. Common features included: - short nose (23/26) - long and/or smooth philtrum (26/29) - concave nasal ridge (24/29) - thick eyebrows (20/24) - long eyelashes (21/26) - synophrys (19/28) - short fifth finger (23/28) - postnatal microcephaly (16/28) No patient had a major limb malformation. The following were less frequent than in the NIPBL and SMC1A cohorts: small hands (5/27), small feet (3/27), hirsutism (10/26), seizures (2/22), and visual impairment (0/24). Other findings were hearing loss (8 of 24), heart anomalies (9 of 23), gastroesophageal reflux (13 of 25), and cleft palate (6 of 25).

Krab et al. (2020) found that cognition and behavior were attenuated in RAD21 patients compared with NIPBL and SMC1A patients. Of the 29 cohort A patients, 16 (55%) had normal cognition or mild intellectual disability, 4 had moderate disability, and none had severe or profound disability. All patients aged 3 years or older used some words. Fourteen of 25 had behavioral problems, mainly anxiety, ADHD, features of autism spectrum disorder, and obsessive-compulsive behavior. Self-injurious behavior and aggression were each seen in only 1 patient. ⚠︎

Krab et al. (2020) also summarized the natural history. Pregnancy and birth usually progressed normally, and prenatal growth retardation was uncommon. About half of the patients had congenital anomalies such as cleft palate or cardiac anomalies. Diaphragmatic hernia, anal atresia, and choanal atresia occurred occasionally. Short stature and microcephaly developed postnatally. Two unusual findings were relatively frequent in this series: vertebral anomalies (clefts and hemivertebrae) and malformations of structures derived from the embryonic foregut. ⚠︎

Goel and Parasivam (2020) reported another case of holoprosencephaly associated with a loss-of-function variant in the RAD21 gene.

### Other Features

Krab et al. (2020) noted that the limited clinical information on their cohort B suggested two things: biallelic RAD21 variants can cause Mungan syndrome, and monoallelic variants can cause holoprosencephaly, as in one cohort A case. They also suggested a possible link with schizophrenia but noted that this association may be coincidental. On follow-up, members of the family with Mungan syndrome showed CdLS characteristics that had not been mentioned in the original publication.

### Inheritance

CDLS4 is inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern. Minor et al. (2014) described the first familial case, in which a RAD21 deletion was inherited from the mother. Boyle et al. (2017) found the same RAD21 deletion in an index patient and in her mother and 2 aunts, who did not meet the diagnostic criteria for CdLS. Based on their study and previous reports, Boyle et al. (2017) suggested that RAD21 variants are associated with incomplete penetrance and that carriers may therefore be underdiagnosed. Krab et al. (2020) found that variants were familial in 9 of 21 index cases in which inheritance could be determined. De novo mutations have also been reported (Gudmundsson et al., 2019; Krab et al., 2020). ⚠︎

### Cytogenetics

Wuyts et al. (2002) studied a boy with multiple exostoses, hypertrichosis, mental retardation, epilepsy, and brain abnormalities. He had a de novo submicroscopic interstitial deletion of chromosome 8q24. Molecular analysis showed that the deletion overlapped the Langer-Giedion syndrome region and included the EXT1 gene and additional genes distal to EXT1. The authors concluded that it probably did not include the TRPS1 gene, which lies proximal to EXT1.

McBrien et al. (2008) reported a boy with a mild Langer-Giedion-like phenotype and a small 8q24.1 microdeletion. Oligo array CGH showed that the deletion involved EXT1 alone and not TRPS1. The authors suggested that this pointed to a functional disturbance of TRPS1. The boy also had unusual features, including prominent toe pads and fat pads on the soles of his feet, similar to those described in Pierpont syndrome.

Krab et al. (2020) identified 7 deletions that included RAD21. Six also removed neighboring genes. The seventh was a 27-kb deletion confined to RAD21 exons 1 through 9 (8q24.11). Patients with microdeletions tended to have higher CdLS scores and more frequent growth impairment than those with intragenic variants. However, the two groups did not differ in the frequency of major malformations or of cognitive and behavioral problems. In some patients with deletions, upper limb anomalies were likely caused by exostoses related to EXT1 haploinsufficiency.

### Molecular Genetics

In children with a congenital cohesinopathy phenotype, Deardorff et al. (2012) identified mutations in the RAD21 gene, which encodes an integral structural protein of the cohesin complex. The mutations acted at the interface of RAD21 with the cohesin proteins STAG2 and SMC1A.

Minor et al. (2014) studied 2 patients with mild, atypical CdLS. In the first, they identified an in-frame intragenic deletion of exon 13 of the RAD21 gene, the first intragenic deletion reported in RAD21. It was inherited from the mother, which the authors described as the first familial case, and they discussed variable presentation within families and low penetrance. In the second patient, they identified a frameshift mutation (c.592_593dup) in RAD21.

In a patient with mild CdLS, Boyle et al. (2017) identified a novel 1-bp deletion in the RAD21 gene (c.704delG). It was predicted to cause a frameshift and premature stop codon (S235IfsTer19) and to be deleterious. The patient's mother and 2 aunts also carried the deletion.

In a boy with CDLS4, Gudmundsson et al. (2019) used exome sequencing to identify a de novo heterozygous 3-bp deletion in the RAD21 gene (c.1774_1776del). The deletion removes glutamine-592 in frame (gln592del) and was absent from both healthy parents. ⚠︎

Kruszka et al. (2019) reported holoprosencephaly in 4 individuals with variants in the non-X-linked cohesin genes SMC3 and RAD21. They also reported it in 11 individuals with variants in the X-linked cohesin genes STAG2 and SMC1A, of which 10 were loss-of-function variants and 1 was a missense variant.

In 33 families, Krab et al. (2020) identified 31 different RAD21 alterations: 7 unique deletions (6 of which also included other genes) and 24 intragenic sequence variants. Thirteen of the sequence variants were predicted to be truncating: 2 nonsense, 2 splice site, and 9 frameshift. The truncating variants were scattered throughout the gene, whereas missense variants tended to cluster in the functional domains. Two variants were recurrent: c.1753T-C (C585R) and c.1756C-T (R586X), each found in 2 families. Several variants arose de novo, and others were inherited.

Goel and Parasivam (2020) reported a patient with holoprosencephaly associated with a loss-of-function variant in the RAD21 gene.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Deardorff et al. (2012) suggested that dominant missense mutations in RAD21 cause more severe functional defects than loss-of-function mutations and lead to worse structural and cognitive findings.

Krab et al. (2020) found no difference in CdLS scores or growth parameters between patients with truncating and nontruncating RAD21 sequence variants. Of 15 patients with intragenic variants and major malformations or health problems, 12 had a variant in a protein-binding domain, but the authors were uncertain whether this was a true association. Variability within and between families was striking, especially for cognition and behavior, and in most families could not be explained by mosaicism. Patients with microdeletions or truncating variants did not differ markedly from those with missense variants.

### Pathogenesis

Deardorff et al. (2012) found that the RAD21 mutations impaired the cellular DNA damage response and disrupted transcription in a zebrafish model.

Kruszka et al. (2019) concluded that the cohesin complex is an important regulator of median forebrain development. In human neural stem cells, shRNA knockdown of STAG2 and SMC1A caused aberrant expression of the holoprosencephaly-associated genes ZIC2, GLI2, SMAD3, and FGFR1.

Krab et al. (2020) suggested that the relatively mild phenotype indicates that, unlike other CdLS genes, RAD21 is not highly intolerant to loss of function. They speculated that RAD21 haploinsufficiency may be compensated more effectively than that of other cohesin genes. Molecular modeling suggested that pathogenic variants in the SMC1A-binding domain disturb the ATPase reaction that is pivotal to opening of the cohesin ring.

### Diagnosis

Krab et al. (2020) found that applying the CdLS consensus criteria for molecular testing would not have missed any RAD21 variant, and concluded that the CdLS score remains a reliable tool. CdLS was suspected before testing in 13 of 21 index cases. Because familial occurrence was frequent and variability within families was marked, the authors recommended testing parents even when they appear unaffected.

**.0004 CORNELIA DE LANGE SYNDROME 4 WITH OR WITHOUT MIDLINE BRAIN DEFECTS**  
RAD21, 3-BP DEL, 1774CAG [c.1774_1776del; p.Q592del]

In a 15-month-old boy with CDLS4, Gudmundsson et al. (2019) used exome sequencing to identify a de novo heterozygous 3-bp deletion (c.1774_1776del) in the RAD21 gene. The deletion removes glutamine-592 in frame (gln592del). It was absent from both healthy parents and was predicted to be disease-causing by bioinformatic analysis. In silico structural modeling predicted that the deletion disrupts contact between RAD21 lys591 and SMC1A residues glu1191 and glu1192, changing the RAD21-SMC1A interface. ⚠︎

Krab et al. (2020) reported a patient with the de novo c.1774_1776del (gln592del) deletion. Molecular dynamics modeling predicted that gln592del moves lys605 and thereby disturbs the positioning of SMC1A asn35 at ATPase site 1.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Cornelia de Lange syndrome-4 with or without midline brain defects (CDLS4) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the RAD21 gene (606462) on chromosome 8q24.

### Description

Cornelia de Lange syndrome-4 (CDLS4) is a cohesinopathy caused by heterozygous mutation in RAD21, which encodes a core subunit of the cohesin complex. Affected individuals have features that overlap classic Cornelia de Lange syndrome, including growth retardation, characteristic facial features, and minor limb or skeletal anomalies. The phenotype is generally milder than classic CdLS, particularly with respect to cognitive impairment. Some patients have midline brain defects that fall within the holoprosencephaly spectrum.

### Clinical Features

Deardorff et al. (2012) described individuals with heterozygous RAD21 mutations or deletions. Their features overlapped with Cornelia de Lange syndrome and included growth retardation, minor skeletal anomalies, and facial features suggesting CdLS. Cognitive impairment was milder than in typical CdLS caused by NIPBL mutation. ⚠︎

Kruszka et al. (2019) found variants in cohesin complex genes, including RAD21, in patients with holoprosencephaly. This extended the RAD21-associated phenotype to include midline brain defects. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

CDLS4 is transmitted as an autosomal dominant trait. Heterozygous RAD21 mutations have arisen de novo in some patients and have been inherited in others (Deardorff et al., 2012). ✗

### Heterogeneity

Cornelia de Lange syndrome is genetically heterogeneous. Other forms are caused by mutation in other genes encoding cohesin components or cohesin regulators, including NIPBL, SMC1A, SMC3, and HDAC8.

### Molecular Genetics

Deardorff et al. (2012) identified heterozygous mutations in the RAD21 gene, as well as genomic deletions including RAD21, in patients with a CdLS-like phenotype. They showed by functional studies that the missense mutations impaired RAD21 interactions within the cohesin complex. Cells from patients showed defects in the cellular response to DNA damage. ✗

Kruszka et al. (2019) identified heterozygous RAD21 variants in patients with holoprosencephaly, in a study of cohesin-associated holoprosencephaly. ⚠︎

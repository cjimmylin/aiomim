### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that malonyl-CoA decarboxylase deficiency is caused by homozygous mutation in the gene encoding malonyl-CoA decarboxylase (MLYCD; 606761).

### Description

Malonyl-CoA decarboxylase deficiency is an extremely rare inborn error of metabolism (de Wit et al., 2006). Wightman et al. (2003) described it as an autosomal recessive disorder characterized by malonic aciduria, developmental delay, seizure disorder, hypoglycemia, and cardiomyopathy. It presents with metabolic acidosis, hypoglycemia, and/or cardiomyopathy (de Wit et al., 2006), and has also been associated with developmental delay (Sacksteder et al., 1999), hypotonia (Gao et al., 1999), and vomiting (Haan et al., 1986). Sacksteder et al. (1999) noted that cytoplasmic malonyl-CoA is a potent inhibitor of mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation and suggested that malonyl-CoA decarboxylase regulates mitochondrial fatty acid uptake and oxidation, a hypothesis supported by the resemblance of the deficiency phenotypes to those of mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation disorders.

### Nomenclature

The disorder has also been called MLYCD deficiency (Wightman et al., 2003).

### Clinical Features

Brown et al. (1984) described a patient with deficiency of malonyl-CoA decarboxylase, an inborn error of metabolism not recognized previously. The child had repeated episodes of vomiting, some requiring hospitalization.

### Biochemical Features

Malonic aciduria is the characterizing biochemical feature of the disorder (Wightman et al., 2003). In a patient reported by Haan et al. (1986), the urine contained increased amounts of malonic and methylmalonic acids, and malonyl-CoA decarboxylase activity in cultured fibroblast extracts was deficient (4% of the mean control value). ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Wightman et al. (2003) described malonyl-CoA decarboxylase deficiency as an autosomal recessive disorder. FitzPatrick et al. (1999) identified homozygous MLYCD mutations in 2 consanguineous Scottish patients.

### Molecular Genetics

FitzPatrick et al. (1999) identified 2 different homozygous mutations in the MLYCD gene by RT-PCR analysis of fibroblast RNA from 2 previously reported consanguineous Scottish patients with malonyl-CoA decarboxylase deficiency.

Sacksteder et al. (1999) found a severe mutation in the MLYCD gene (c.947-948delTT) in a patient with malonyl-CoA decarboxylase deficiency.

### Clinical Management

Haan et al. (1986) found that their patient excreted only relatively small quantities of malonic, methylmalonic, and succinic acid in the urine when fed an isocaloric low-fat, high-carbohydrate diet.

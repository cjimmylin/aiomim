### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that malonyl-CoA decarboxylase deficiency is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding malonyl-CoA decarboxylase (MLYCD; 606761) on chromosome 16q23. ✗

### Description

Malonyl-CoA decarboxylase deficiency is a rare autosomal recessive inborn error of metabolism. Its biochemical hallmark is increased urinary excretion of malonic acid (malonic aciduria). Clinical features vary. They include developmental delay, hypotonia, seizures, hypoglycemia, metabolic acidosis, vomiting and diarrhea, and cardiomyopathy. Malonyl-CoA inhibits carnitine palmitoyltransferase I, so its accumulation is thought to impair mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation. ✗

### Nomenclature

The disorder has also been called malonic aciduria and MLYCD deficiency. ✗

### Clinical Features

Brown et al. (1984) were among the first to describe malonic aciduria with deficient malonyl-CoA decarboxylase activity, in a child with developmental delay and episodes of metabolic decompensation. ✗

Salomons et al. (2007) described the clinical, enzymatic and molecular findings in additional patients with MLYCD deficiency. They emphasized how variable the phenotype is: findings included developmental delay, hypotonia, seizures, hypoglycemia and cardiomyopathy. ✗

### Biochemical Features

Affected individuals excrete increased amounts of malonic acid in urine. Some also have mildly increased methylmalonic acid. Malonylcarnitine is raised in blood, so the disorder can be detected by acylcarnitine analysis, including newborn screening. Malonyl-CoA decarboxylase activity is deficient in cultured fibroblasts. ✗

### Inheritance

Malonyl-CoA decarboxylase deficiency is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. Patients carry homozygous or compound heterozygous MLYCD mutations. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Sacksteder et al. (1999) and FitzPatrick et al. (1999) identified mutations in the MLYCD gene in patients with malonyl-CoA decarboxylase deficiency. ⚠︎

Salomons et al. (2007) identified homozygous or compound heterozygous MLYCD mutations in further patients with the deficiency. ✗

### Clinical Management

Reported treatments include a low-fat, high-carbohydrate diet and carnitine supplementation. Some patients with cardiomyopathy improved on this treatment. ✗

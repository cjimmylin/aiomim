### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that common variable immunodeficiency-15 (CVID15) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the SEC61A1 gene (609213) on chromosome 3q21.

### Description

Common variable immunodeficiency-15 (CVID15) is a primary antibody deficiency. It is characterized by low serum IgM, IgG and IgA and by severe recurrent bacterial infections, mostly beginning in the first year of life. These include tonsillitis, otitis, sinusitis, pneumonia and gastrointestinal infections. Peripheral B-cell numbers are normal, but plasmablast and plasma cell differentiation is defective (Schubert et al., 2018).

### Clinical Features

Schubert et al. (2018) studied 10 affected individuals in a family (Family I). They had deficiencies of the IgM, IgG and IgA isotypes and severe recurrent bacterial infections, including tonsillitis, otitis, sinusitis, pneumonia and gastrointestinal infections. Onset was mostly in the first year of life. Affected individuals did not respond to polysaccharide vaccination and had variable responses to tetanus toxoid vaccination. After IVIG replacement therapy was started, infections became markedly fewer and less severe.

Schubert et al. (2018) also described a second family (Family II). The index patient (II.P3) was an 8-year-old boy who had had hypogammaglobulinemia since birth. On IVIG treatment he had no severe or recurrent infections. Repeated attempts to withdraw IVIG failed because his immunoglobulin levels stayed low. His grandmother, a mutation carrier, had more frequent upper respiratory infections and recurrent bilateral bacterial otitis media in childhood. Her serum immunoglobulin levels were normal at age 66 years.

In patients from both families, Schubert et al. (2018) found normal B-, T- and NK-cell ratios. T-cell subsets, regulatory T cells and recent thymic emigrants were normal. Peripheral B-cell subpopulations were also normal, including naive, unswitched memory, switched memory, transitional and CD21-low B cells. The only abnormality was an apparent reduction in the very small CD27+CD38-high plasmablast population in Family I. The patients had no clinical symptoms other than infections. Serum levels of liver- and pancreas-secreted proteins were normal, as were the laboratory values that are altered in SEC61A1-associated autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease (ADTKD).

### Inheritance

In the families reported by Schubert et al. (2018), the heterozygous SEC61A1 mutation segregated with disease, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. Penetrance was complete in Family I. In Family II, expressivity was variable and penetrance incomplete.

### Mapping

Schubert et al. (2018) performed genome-wide linkage analysis with microsatellite markers in Family I. They found linkage to chromosome 3 between markers D3S3697 and D3S3684, with a peak LOD score of 3.31.

### Molecular Genetics

Schubert et al. (2018) performed whole-exome sequencing on 4 affected and 2 unaffected members of Family I. They identified a heterozygous c.254T-A transversion in the SEC61A1 gene (609213), predicting a val85-to-asp (V85D) substitution. SEC61A1 was the only candidate gene in the chromosome 3 linkage region. Sanger sequencing confirmed that the mutation segregated perfectly with the disease. V85 is highly conserved and is one of the 6 pore-ring residues of the Sec61 channel. In HeLa cells, the V85D mutant impaired cotranslational translocation of preprolactin. Its overexpression lowered the ER calcium load, which the authors concluded was a dominant effect.

Schubert et al. (2018) then performed targeted next-generation sequencing in more than 200 additional patients with primary antibody deficiency. They identified a heterozygous c.1325G-T nonsense mutation in SEC61A1 (E381X) in the index patient of Family II. His grandmother and mother also carried the mutation. No premature stop codons in SEC61A1 were present in the gnomAD database. SEC61A1 protein was reduced in the patients' naive B cells, and no truncated protein was detected. The authors suggested that the mutation causes nonsense-mediated mRNA decay and haploinsufficiency, with variable expressivity and incomplete penetrance.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Schubert et al. (2018) suggested that SEC61A1 haploinsufficiency causes possibly transient hypogammaglobulinemia, with variable expressivity and incomplete penetrance, in Family II. In contrast, they proposed that the dominant-negative V85D missense mutation causes the fully penetrant disease in Family I. The phenotype in Family I ranged from transient hypogammaglobulinemia and specific antibody deficiency to CVID.

### Pathogenesis

Schubert et al. (2018) found that, after both T cell-dependent and T cell-independent in vitro stimulation, B cells from mutation carriers in both families generated significantly fewer plasmablasts and plasma cells and produced less IgG and IgA. EBV-transformed B-cell lines from patients had reduced SEC61A1 levels. These lines lacked a CD38-high/CD138+ plasma cell subpopulation and secreted less immunoglobulin.

Schubert et al. (2018) found that overexpression of SEC61A1-V85D depleted the ER calcium store in HeLa cells, suggesting increased Ca(2+) leakage. In multiple myeloma cell lines, stable expression of the mutant led to loss of the transduced cells. It also activated the terminal unfolded protein response, with upregulation of IRE1A, PERK, XBP1s, ATF4 and CHOP. Other cell lines were not affected. In thapsigargin-treated HEK293T cells, the V85D mutant had a dominant effect on XBP1s induction. The authors concluded that the mutation selectively impairs plasma cell survival.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that common variable immunodeficiency-15 (CVID15) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the SEC61A1 gene on chromosome 3.

### Description

Common variable immunodeficiency-15 (CVID15) is a primary antibody deficiency characterized by early-onset hypogammaglobulinemia involving IgM, IgG, and IgA and severe recurrent bacterial infections of the respiratory tract, with normal numbers and subsets of peripheral B and T cells. B cells show an intrinsic defect in differentiation into plasma cells. Expressivity is variable, ranging from transient hypogammaglobulinemia and specific antibody deficiency to CVID (Schubert et al., 2018).

### Clinical Features

Schubert et al. (2018) studied 11 patients from 2 unrelated families with primary antibody deficiency. In family I, 10 individuals had antibody isotype deficiencies involving IgM, IgG, and IgA and suffered from severe recurrent bacterial infections such as tonsillitis, otitis, sinusitis, pneumonias, and gastrointestinal infections. Disease onset was mostly in the first year of life. Affected individuals did not respond to polysaccharide vaccination and responded variably to tetanus toxin vaccination. The number and severity of infections decreased markedly after initiation of intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) replacement therapy. The patients did not present clinical symptoms other than infections, and serum levels of proteins secreted by the liver and pancreas were normal.

The index patient of family II reported by Schubert et al. (2018) was an 8-year-old boy who had had hypogammaglobulinemia since birth; owing to IVIG treatment he had not experienced severe or recurrent infections, and attempts to withdraw IVIG repeatedly failed because of persistently low serum immunoglobulin levels. His grandmother, also a mutation carrier, reported increased frequency of upper respiratory infections in childhood with recurrent bilateral bacterial otitis media, but at age 66 years had normal serum immunoglobulin levels. His mother was also a mutation carrier but was not included in the study because she had a secondary immunodeficiency.

In patients from both families, Schubert et al. (2018) found normal ratios of B, T, and NK cells, normal T-cell subsets including regulatory T cells and recent thymic emigrants, and normal peripheral B-cell subpopulations, including naive, unswitched memory, switched memory, IgA+, IgG+, transitional, and CD21-low B cells. Only the very small population of peripheral CD27+CD38-high plasmablasts appeared to be reduced in patients of family I.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CVID15 in the families reported by Schubert et al. (2018) was consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. The heterozygous missense mutation was fully penetrant in family I, whereas the authors suggested that haploinsufficiency due to the nonsense mutation in family II caused hypogammaglobulinemia with variable expressivity and incomplete penetrance. ⚠︎

### Mapping

By genomewide linkage analysis using microsatellite markers in family I, Schubert et al. (2018) identified a single region on chromosome 3 with LOD scores greater than 2; fine mapping gave a peak single- and multiple-marker LOD score of 3.31, and the candidate interval lay between markers D3S3697 and D3S3684.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of family I with CVID15, Schubert et al. (2018) identified a heterozygous missense mutation (c.254T-A; V85D) in the SEC61A1 gene. Whole-exome sequencing of 4 affected and 2 unaffected family members revealed 3 novel shared variants, of which only the SEC61A1 variant lay in the linkage region on chromosome 3; Sanger sequencing confirmed that it segregated perfectly with the disease in the family. V85 is a highly conserved residue in the pore ring of the Sec61 channel. In HeLa cells, cotranslational transport of preprolactin through the V85D channel was significantly decreased, and overexpression of the mutant reduced the ER calcium load, which the authors concluded was a dominant effect. Stable expression of V85D led to the loss of transduced multiple myeloma cells and induced the terminal unfolded protein response in these plasma cell lines, whereas other cell lines were unaffected. ⚠︎

By targeted next-generation sequencing of more than 200 additional patients with primary antibody deficiency, Schubert et al. (2018) identified a heterozygous nonsense mutation (c.1325G-T; E381X) in SEC61A1 in the index patient of family II, and Sanger resequencing identified 2 additional mutation carriers in the family. No variants leading to a premature stop codon in SEC61A1 were reported in the gnomAD database. SEC61A1 protein levels were reduced in naive B cells of 2 carriers, and no truncated protein was detected in a patient-derived EBV-transformed B-cell line, suggesting nonsense-mediated mRNA decay and haploinsufficiency.

### Pathogenesis

Schubert et al. (2018) found that, upon in vitro stimulation mimicking T cell-dependent and T cell-independent activation, B cells from mutation carriers in both families showed significantly decreased differentiation into plasmablasts and plasma cells, with decreased IgG and IgA production. EBV-transformed B-cell lines from patients had reduced SEC61A1 protein and mRNA levels, lacked the CD38-high CD138+ plasma cell subpopulation present in control lines, and secreted less IgG, IgA, and IgM per cell. The authors noted that SEC61A1 is a target gene of spliced XBP1 during plasma cell differentiation.

Reimold et al. (2001) found that the transcription factor XBP1 is required for the generation of plasma cells. Mouse lymphoid chimeras deficient in Xbp1 had normal numbers of activated B lymphocytes and formed normal germinal centers, but secreted very little immunoglobulin of any isotype and failed to control infection with polyoma virus because plasma cells were markedly absent. In contrast, Taubenheim et al. (2012) found efficient generation of antibody-secreting cells in the absence of Xbp1 in mice; Xbp1 deficiency resulted in a reduction but not ablation of antibody secretion and in failure to develop the cellular morphology characteristic of antibody-secreting cells.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 IMMUNODEFICIENCY, COMMON VARIABLE, 15**  
SEC61A1, VAL85ASP [c.254T>A; p.V85D; p.Val85Asp]

In affected members of a family (family I) with CVID15, Schubert et al. (2018) identified a heterozygous c.254T-A transversion in the SEC61A1 gene, resulting in a val85-to-asp (V85D) substitution at a highly conserved residue in the pore ring of the Sec61 channel. The mutation was found by linkage analysis and whole-exome sequencing and segregated perfectly with the disease by Sanger sequencing. In HeLa cells, the mutant impaired cotranslational protein translocation and disturbed cellular calcium homeostasis, and in multiple myeloma cell lines it triggered the terminal unfolded protein response.

**.0002 IMMUNODEFICIENCY, COMMON VARIABLE, 15**  
SEC61A1, GLU381TER [c.1325G>T; p.E381*]

In an 8-year-old boy (family II) with hypogammaglobulinemia since birth (CVID15), Schubert et al. (2018) identified a heterozygous c.1325G-T transversion in the SEC61A1 gene, resulting in a glu381-to-ter (E381X) substitution. The mutation was found by targeted next-generation sequencing and was also present in his grandmother, who had recurrent otitis media in childhood but normal immunoglobulin levels at age 66 years, and in his mother. SEC61A1 protein was reduced in naive B cells of carriers and no truncated protein was detected, consistent with haploinsufficiency.

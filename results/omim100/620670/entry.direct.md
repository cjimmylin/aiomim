### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that common variable immunodeficiency-15 (CVID15) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the SEC61A1 gene on chromosome 3.

### Description

Common variable immunodeficiency-15 (CVID15) is a primary antibody deficiency. It is characterized by hypogammaglobulinemia affecting IgM, IgG, and IgA, and by severe recurrent bacterial infections of the respiratory tract that usually begin in the first year of life. Peripheral B- and T-cell subpopulations are normal, but B cells have an intrinsic defect in differentiating into plasma cells. Expressivity is variable: within affected families the phenotype has ranged from transient hypogammaglobulinemia and specific antibody deficiency to CVID (Schubert et al., 2018).

### Clinical Features

Schubert et al. (2018) studied 10 individuals from a family (family I) who had antibody isotype deficiencies involving IgM, IgG, and IgA. They had severe recurrent bacterial infections, including tonsillitis, otitis, sinusitis, pneumonia, and gastrointestinal infections. Disease onset was mostly in the first year of life. Affected individuals did not respond to polysaccharide vaccination, and their responses to tetanus toxin vaccination varied. Since starting intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) replacement therapy, they had had a marked decrease in the number and severity of infections. B, T, and NK cells were present in normal ratios, and detailed T-cell phenotyping was normal, including numbers of regulatory T cells and recent thymic emigrants. Peripheral B-cell subpopulations were also normal, including naive, unswitched memory, switched memory, IgA+, IgG+, transitional, and CD21-low B cells. Only the very small population of peripheral CD27+CD38-high plasmablasts appeared reduced in family I, although the authors considered this value error-prone. Patients had no clinical symptoms other than infections. Serum levels of proteins secreted by the liver and pancreas were normal, as were laboratory values that are altered in autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease.

In a second, unrelated family (family II), Schubert et al. (2018) described an 8-year-old boy who had had hypogammaglobulinemia since birth. Because he was treated with IVIG, he had not had severe or recurrent infections. Repeated attempts to withdraw IVIG failed because his serum Ig levels stayed low. His grandmother, who also carried the mutation, reported more frequent upper respiratory infections in childhood, with recurrent bilateral bacterial otitis media. At age 66 years she had normal serum immunoglobulin levels. The boy's mother also carried the mutation but was not included in the study because she had a secondary immunodeficiency.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CVID15 in the families reported by Schubert et al. (2018) was consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. Penetrance was complete in family I, which carried a missense mutation, and incomplete in family II, which carried a nonsense mutation. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Schubert et al. (2018) performed a genome-wide linkage scan with microsatellite markers in a family with primary antibody deficiency. Only one region, on chromosome 3, gave LOD scores greater than 2. Fine mapping confirmed the linkage, with a peak single-marker and multipoint LOD score of 3.31, and placed the locus between markers D3S3697 and D3S3684.

### Molecular Genetics

Schubert et al. (2018) performed whole-exome sequencing on 4 affected and 2 unaffected members of family I, which had autosomal dominant primary antibody deficiency. Among the shared variants, only one fell within the linkage region on chromosome 3: a heterozygous c.254T-A transversion in the SEC61A1 gene, resulting in a val85-to-asp (V85D) substitution. Sanger sequencing showed that the mutation segregated perfectly with the disease in the family. V85 is a highly conserved residue and one of 6 bulky hydrophobic amino acids that form the pore ring of the Sec61 channel. In HeLa cells in which endogenous SEC61A1 was silenced and then replaced, the V85D mutant channel showed significantly reduced cotranslational translocation of preprolactin compared with wildtype. Overexpressing the mutant in HeLa cells reduced the ER calcium load, consistent with increased calcium leakage, and the authors concluded this was a dominant effect. Stable expression of the V85D mutant caused transduced cells of 3 multiple myeloma cell lines to disappear and induced the terminal unfolded protein response, including upregulation of ATF4 and CHOP. Other lymphoid and nonlymphoid cell lines were not affected. In thapsigargin-sensitized HEK293T cells, the mutant also had a dominant effect on XBP1s induction when coexpressed with wildtype SEC61A1. ⚠︎

Schubert et al. (2018) also performed targeted next-generation sequencing in more than 200 additional patients with primary antibody deficiency. They identified a heterozygous c.1325G-T transversion in the SEC61A1 gene, resulting in a glu381-to-ter (E381X) substitution, in the 8-year-old boy from family II. Sanger sequencing confirmed the mutation and found it in 2 more family members, his mother and grandmother. The gnomAD database contained no premature stop variants in SEC61A1. In naive B cells from the boy and his grandmother, SEC61A1 protein levels were reduced, whereas levels in T cells were comparable to control. No truncated protein was detected in an EBV-transformed B-cell line from the boy. The authors concluded that the mutation likely causes nonsense-mediated mRNA decay and SEC61A1 haploinsufficiency. ⚠︎

In both families, Schubert et al. (2018) found that purified B cells from mutation carriers had significantly reduced differentiation into plasmablasts and CD38-high CD138+ plasma cells after both T cell-dependent and T cell-independent stimulation in vitro. Secretion of IgG and IgA was also reduced. EBV-transformed B-cell lines from patients had reduced SEC61A1 mRNA and protein. They lacked the CD38-high CD138+ plasma cell subpopulation seen in control lines, and they secreted less IgG, IgA, and IgM per cell.

### Pathogenesis

Reimold et al. (2001) found that the transcription factor XBP1 is required to generate plasma cells. Mouse lymphoid chimeras deficient in XBP1 had normal numbers of activated B cells and formed normal germinal centers. However, they secreted very little immunoglobulin of any isotype and lacked plasma cells.

In contrast, Taubenheim et al. (2012) found that antibody-secreting cells were generated efficiently in the absence of Xbp1, both in vitro and in vivo. These cells were present at normal frequencies in resting and immunized mice. Loss of Xbp1 reduced, but did not abolish, antibody secretion, and the cells failed to develop the morphology typical of antibody-secreting cells.

Schubert et al. (2018) noted that SEC61A1 is a target gene of spliced XBP1 and is strongly induced during plasma cell differentiation. By proteomics, SEC61A protein was 2-fold more abundant in human plasmablasts than in naive and memory B cells. They proposed that plasma cells may need 2 functional SEC61A1 alleles to make enough SEC61A1 protein for high-level immunoglobulin production. They suggested that SEC61A1 haploinsufficiency causes hypogammaglobulinemia with variable expressivity and incomplete penetrance in family II. In family I, they proposed that the dominant-negative V85D mutation causes a functional haploinsufficiency combined with disturbed calcium homeostasis, resulting in fully penetrant disease.

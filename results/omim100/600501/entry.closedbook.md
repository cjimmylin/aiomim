### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that ABCD syndrome (ABCDS) is caused by homozygous mutation in the endothelin receptor type B gene (EDNRB; 131244) on chromosome 13q22.

### Description

ABCD syndrome is an autosomal recessive neurocristopathy. Its name is an acronym for its cardinal features: albinism, a black lock of hair, a cell migration disorder of the neurocytes of the gut (Hirschsprung disease, or intestinal aganglionosis), and sensorineural deafness. The disorder is now thought to belong to the Waardenburg-Shah (Waardenburg syndrome type 4) spectrum caused by EDNRB mutations. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Gross et al. (1995) described the syndrome in a child of Kurdish origin. The child had albinism of the skin and hair except for a black lock of hair, intestinal aganglionosis (Hirschsprung disease), and deafness. Gross et al. (1995) regarded these features as a newly recognized disorder of neural crest cell migration and named it with the acronym ABCD. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The family reported by Gross et al. (1995) suggested autosomal recessive inheritance. Verheij et al. (2002) confirmed this pattern when they found a homozygous EDNRB mutation in the original patient. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In the patient originally reported by Gross et al. (1995), Verheij et al. (2002) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in the EDNRB gene (arg201-to-ter; R201X). They concluded that ABCD syndrome is not a separate entity but part of the spectrum of Waardenburg syndrome type 4 (Shah-Waardenburg syndrome) caused by EDNRB mutation. ⚠︎

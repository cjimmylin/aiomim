### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that ABCD syndrome (ABCDS) is caused by homozygous mutation in the EDNRB gene (131244) on chromosome 13q22.3.

### Description

ABCD syndrome is an autosomal recessive neural crest syndrome. Its features are albinism, a black lock of hair, a cell migration disorder of the neurocytes of the gut (Hirschsprung disease), and deafness (Gross et al., 1995; Verheij et al., 2002). Gross et al. (1995) described the gut involvement as a complete lack of neural innervation.

### Nomenclature

The phenotype overlaps with Shah-Waardenburg syndrome, and Verheij et al. (2002) found a homozygous EDNRB mutation in an ABCD syndrome patient. On this basis they suggested that ABCD syndrome is not a separate entity but an expression of Shah-Waardenburg syndrome.

### Clinical Features

Gross et al. (1995) reported a macrosomic newborn girl, the 14th child of consanguineous Kurdish parents. She had albinism, a black lock at the right temporo-occipital region, and retinal depigmentation. Brainstem auditory-evoked potentials confirmed bilateral deafness. Biopsy showed aganglionosis of the large intestine and a total absence of neurocytes and nerve fibers in the small intestine, indicating a total lack of sympathetic and parasympathetic innervation. The infant died of intestinal dysfunction at 5 weeks. Four of her sibs had the same syndrome and died a few days after birth. The other 9 sibs were well and had an unremarkable phenotype.

### Inheritance

Gross et al. (1995) proposed autosomal recessive inheritance for ABCD syndrome because the parents were consanguineous and 5 of their 14 children were affected. Verheij et al. (2002) found a homozygous EDNRB mutation in an affected patient, which is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Verheij et al. (2002) screened DNA from the index patient of an ABCD syndrome family for mutations in the endothelin B receptor gene (EDNRB). They found a homozygous nonsense mutation in exon 3, arg201-to-ter (R201X).

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that ABCD syndrome (ABCDS) is caused by homozygous mutation in the EDNRB gene.

### Description

ABCD syndrome is an autosomal recessive neural crest disorder characterized by albinism, a black lock of hair, cell migration disorder of the neurocytes of the gut (Hirschsprung disease), and deafness (Gross et al., 1995; Verheij et al., 2002). Verheij et al. (2002) noted that the phenotype overlaps with Shah-Waardenburg syndrome and suggested that ABCD syndrome is not a separate entity but an expression of Shah-Waardenburg syndrome.

### Nomenclature

The acronym ABCD was introduced by Gross et al. (1995) for the combination of albinism, black lock, cell migration disorder of the neurocytes of the gut, and deafness.

### Clinical Features

Gross et al. (1995) reported a macrosomic newborn girl, the 14th child of consanguineous Kurdish parents, with albinism, a black lock at the right temporo-occipital region, and retinal depigmentation. Bilateral deafness was confirmed by brainstem auditory-evoked potentials. She also had a severe defect of intestinal innervation: biopsy showed aganglionosis of the large intestine and total absence of neurocytes and nerve fibers in the small intestine, indicating a total lack of sympathetic and parasympathetic innervation. The infant died of intestinal dysfunction at 5 weeks. Four sibs had the same syndrome and died a few days after birth; the other 9 sibs were well, with an unremarkable phenotype. Gross et al. (1995) considered the disorder to be a new neural crest syndrome.

### Inheritance

The occurrence of the disorder in 5 sibs born to consanguineous parents in the family reported by Gross et al. (1995) indicated autosomal recessive inheritance. Verheij et al. (2002) found homozygous mutation in the EDNRB gene in the index patient of this family. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Because the ABCD syndrome phenotype overlaps with Shah-Waardenburg syndrome (sensorineural deafness; hypopigmentation of skin, hair, and irides; and Hirschsprung disease), Verheij et al. (2002) screened DNA of the index patient of the family reported by Gross et al. (1995) for mutations in the endothelin B receptor gene (EDNRB), which is known to be involved in Shah-Waardenburg syndrome. They identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in exon 3 (R201X) of the EDNRB gene. Verheij et al. (2002) suggested that ABCD syndrome is not a separate entity but an expression of Shah-Waardenburg syndrome. ⚠︎

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 ABCD SYNDROME**  
EDNRB, ARG201TER [R201X]

In the index patient of the consanguineous Kurdish family with ABCD syndrome reported by Gross et al. (1995), Verheij et al. (2002) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in exon 3 of the EDNRB gene, resulting in an arg201-to-ter (R201X) substitution. The mutation was found by screening the EDNRB gene because of the phenotypic overlap with Shah-Waardenburg syndrome. ⚠︎

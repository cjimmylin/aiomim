### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that ABCD syndrome (ABCDS) is caused by homozygous mutation in the endothelin receptor type B gene (EDNRB; 131244) on chromosome 13q22.3.

### Description

ABCD syndrome is an autosomal recessive neural crest disorder (neurocristopathy) (Gross et al., 1995; Verheij et al., 2002). The name, given by Gross et al. (1995), is an acronym for albinism, black lock, cell migration disorder of the neurocytes of the gut, and deafness. Verheij et al. (2002) suggested that ABCD syndrome is not a separate entity but an expression of Shah-Waardenburg syndrome (Waardenburg syndrome type 4), which is caused by EDNRB mutations.

### Clinical Features

Gross et al. (1995) described a child of Kurdish origin who had albinism, a black lock of hair, intestinal aganglionosis (Hirschsprung disease), and deafness. Gross et al. (1995) regarded this combination of features as a new neural crest syndrome and named it with the acronym ABCD.

### Inheritance

The family reported by Gross et al. (1995) suggested autosomal recessive inheritance. Verheij et al. (2002), who described ABCD syndrome as autosomal recessive, found a homozygous EDNRB mutation in the index patient of an ABCD syndrome family.

### Molecular Genetics

In the index patient of a family with ABCD syndrome, Verheij et al. (2002) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in the EDNRB gene (arg201-to-ter; R201X). They concluded that ABCD syndrome is not a separate entity but an expression of Shah-Waardenburg syndrome (Waardenburg syndrome type 4), which is caused by EDNRB mutation. ⚠︎

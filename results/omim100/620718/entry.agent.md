### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that orofaciodigital syndrome XX (OFD20) is caused by biallelic (including compound heterozygous) mutation in the RAB34 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Orofaciodigital syndrome XX (OFD20) is a ciliopathy in which defects in development of the face and oral cavity occur together with digit anomalies. Bruel et al. (2023) reported that it was accompanied by cardiac, cerebral, skeletal, and anorectal defects. Batkovskyte et al. (2024) described an affected fetus with an atypical skeletal ciliopathy phenotype that included cleft lip and palate, short extremities, and both pre- and postaxial polydactyly. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Bruel et al. (2023) reported 4 individuals from 3 unrelated families with a novel form of oral-facial-digital syndrome, which they called OFDS-RAB34. Besides the defects of the face, oral cavity, and digits, affected individuals had cardiac, cerebral, skeletal, and anorectal defects.

Batkovskyte et al. (2024) reported a fetus with an atypical skeletal ciliopathy phenotype. The fetus had multiple malformations, among them posterior neck edema, micrognathia, small low-set ears, auricular hypoplasia, cleft lip and palate, short extremities, and an uncommon combination of preaxial and postaxial polydactyly. A sib of the fetus was unaffected.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OFD20 is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Bruel et al. (2023) found biallelic RAB34 variants in affected individuals. Batkovskyte et al. (2024) found compound heterozygous variants in an affected fetus: one variant was inherited from each parent, and the unaffected sib carried only the paternal variant. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Using exome sequencing in 4 individuals with OFD20 from 3 unrelated families, Bruel et al. (2023) identified biallelic missense mutations in the RAB34 gene. RAB34 encodes a Rab GTPase that mediates ciliary membrane formation and acts selectively in cell types that use the intracellular ciliogenesis pathway. The mutations clustered near the C terminus of RAB34. Functional studies showed that the mutant proteins had a strong loss of function. Some mutant proteins could still be recruited to the mother centriole, but cells expressing mutant RAB34 had a significant defect in cilium assembly.

In a fetus with an atypical skeletal ciliopathy, Batkovskyte et al. (2024) used genome sequencing to identify compound heterozygous variants in the RAB34 gene. The maternally inherited variant was a c.254T-C transition causing an ile85-to-thr (I85T) substitution, and the paternally inherited variant was a c.691C-T transition causing an arg231-to-ter (R231X) substitution. The unaffected sib carried only the paternal variant. The authors noted that Rab34-null mice show a ciliopathy phenotype with cleft palate and polydactyly, which they took as support for pathogenicity.

### Animal Model

Batkovskyte et al. (2024) cited earlier literature showing that Rab34 -/- mice have a ciliopathy phenotype that includes cleft palate and polydactyly.

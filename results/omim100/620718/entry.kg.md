### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that orofaciodigital syndrome XX (OFD20) is caused by biallelic mutation in the RAB34 gene (610917) on chromosome 17q11.2.

### Description

Orofaciodigital syndrome XX (OFD20) is an autosomal recessive form of oral-facial-digital syndrome caused by biallelic RAB34 mutations. Bruel et al. (2023) described the disorder as OFDS-RAB34. Besides oral-facial-digital features, it includes cardiac, cerebral, skeletal and anorectal defects. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Bruel et al. (2023) reported 4 affected individuals from 3 unrelated families with a new form of oral-facial-digital syndrome. Besides defects of the face, oral cavity and digits, the patients had cardiac, cerebral, skeletal and anorectal defects.

Batkovskyte et al. (2024) reported a fetus with an atypical skeletal ciliopathy phenotype and multiple malformations. These were posterior neck edema, micrognathia, low-set and small ears, auricular hypoplasia, cleft lip and palate, and short extremities. The fetus also had both preaxial and postaxial polydactyly, a combination that rarely occurs.

### Inheritance

Bruel et al. (2023) found biallelic RAB34 mutations in affected individuals from 3 unrelated families. Batkovskyte et al. (2024) found compound heterozygous RAB34 mutations in an affected fetus, with 1 mutation inherited from each parent. These findings indicate that OFD20 is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Bruel et al. (2023) used exome sequencing to study 4 individuals with oral-facial-digital syndrome from 3 unrelated families. In all of them, they identified biallelic missense mutations in the RAB34 gene. The mutations clustered near the C terminus of RAB34. In functional studies, the mutant RAB34 proteins showed strong loss of function. Some mutants could still be recruited to the mother centriole, but cells expressing mutant RAB34 had a significant defect in cilium assembly.

Batkovskyte et al. (2024) used genome sequencing to study a fetus with a skeletal ciliopathy phenotype. They identified compound heterozygous mutations in the RAB34 gene. One was a maternally inherited missense mutation, c.254T-C, causing an ile85-to-thr (I85T) substitution. The other was a paternally inherited nonsense mutation, c.691C-T, causing an arg231-to-ter (R231X) substitution. An unaffected sib carried only the paternal mutation. The authors noted that Rab34-null mice display a ciliopathy phenotype with cleft palate and polydactyly, which supported pathogenicity.

### Pathogenesis

Bruel et al. (2023) found that mutant RAB34 proteins showed strong loss of function. Cells expressing mutant RAB34 had a significant defect in cilium assembly, even though some mutants could still be recruited to the mother centriole. RAB34 acts selectively in cell types that use the intracellular ciliogenesis pathway. The authors concluded that RAB34 is the first small GTPase involved in OFDS.

### Animal Model

Batkovskyte et al. (2024) cited earlier evidence that Rab34-null mice display a ciliopathy phenotype with cleft palate and polydactyly. These features are consistent with those seen in their patient.

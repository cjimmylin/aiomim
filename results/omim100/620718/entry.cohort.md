### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that orofaciodigital syndrome XX (OFD20) is caused by biallelic mutation in the RAB34 gene (610917) on chromosome 17q11.2.

### Description

Orofaciodigital syndrome XX (OFD20) is a ciliopathy. It combines oral-facial-digital features with cardiac, cerebral, skeletal and anorectal defects (Bruel et al., 2023). A prenatally affected fetus had cleft lip and palate, micrognathia, ear anomalies, short extremities, and both preaxial and postaxial polydactyly (Batkovskyte et al., 2024).

### Clinical Features

Bruel et al. (2023) reported 4 individuals from 3 unrelated families. They had a novel form of oral-facial-digital syndrome, which the authors called OFDS-RAB34. The syndrome came with cardiac, cerebral, skeletal and anorectal defects.

Batkovskyte et al. (2024) reported an affected fetus with multiple malformations. These were posterior neck edema, micrognathia, low-set and small ears, auricular hypoplasia, cleft lip and palate, short extremities, and a combination of preaxial and postaxial polydactyly, which rarely occur together. An unaffected sib was also studied.

### Inheritance

Bruel et al. (2023) found biallelic RAB34 mutations in affected individuals from 3 unrelated families. Batkovskyte et al. (2024) found that compound heterozygous mutations in an affected fetus were inherited one from each parent. Together these findings show that OFD20 is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Bruel et al. (2023) used exome sequencing to study 4 individuals with OFD20 from 3 unrelated families. In all of them they identified biallelic missense mutations in the RAB34 gene. The variants clustered near the C terminus of the protein and caused a strong loss of function. Some variant proteins could still be recruited to the mother centriole. Even so, cells expressing mutant RAB34 had a significant defect in cilium assembly.

Batkovskyte et al. (2024) used genome sequencing to study a fetus with OFD20. They identified compound heterozygous mutations in the RAB34 gene: a maternally inherited c.254T-C transition causing an ile85-to-thr (I85T) substitution, and a paternally inherited c.691C-T transition causing an arg231-to-ter (R231X) substitution. The unaffected sib carried only the paternal variant. Rab34-null mice had previously been reported to have a ciliopathy phenotype that included cleft palate and polydactyly. The authors noted that these features matched the malformations in their patient, which supported the pathogenicity of the variants.

### Pathogenesis

Bruel et al. (2023) noted that RAB34 acts selectively in cell types that use the intracellular ciliogenesis pathway. They concluded that RAB34 is the first small GTPase involved in OFDS, and that impaired intracellular ciliogenesis causes its distinct clinical manifestations.

### Animal Model

Batkovskyte et al. (2024) noted that Rab34-null mice had been reported to have a ciliopathy phenotype that included cleft palate and polydactyly.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 OROFACIODIGITAL SYNDROME XX**  
RAB34, ILE85THR [c.254T>C; p.I85T]

Batkovskyte et al. (2024) studied a fetus with OFD20 who had posterior neck edema, micrognathia, ear anomalies, cleft lip and palate, short extremities, and preaxial and postaxial polydactyly. Genome sequencing found a c.254T-C transition in the RAB34 gene, which causes an ile85-to-thr (I85T) substitution. The fetus inherited this mutation from the mother and carried it in compound heterozygous state with a paternally inherited R231X mutation. The unaffected sib did not carry the maternal variant.

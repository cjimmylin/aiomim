### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that otofaciocervical syndrome-1 (OTFCS1) is caused by deletion or point mutation in the EYA1 gene (601653) on chromosome 8q13.3.

### Description

Otofaciocervical syndrome is a dysplasia (Estefanía et al., 2006). Pohl et al. (2013) listed its features as facial dysmorphism, external ear anomalies with preauricular pits, hearing impairment, branchial cysts or fistulas, anomalies of the vertebrae and the shoulder girdle, and mild intellectual disability. Neck and shoulder girdle abnormalities described by Dallapiccola and Mingarelli (1995) included a long neck, sloping shoulders and winged scapulae. Rickard et al. (2001) noted that the disorder is clinically similar to branchiootorenal (BOR) syndrome, which is also caused by mutation in EYA1.

### Clinical Features

Fára et al. (1967) reported a familial disorder that they termed oto-facio-cervical dysmorphia.

Dallapiccola and Mingarelli (1995) described a sporadic patient with otofaciocervical syndrome. The patient had a long neck, sloping shoulders and winged scapulae. Renal malformations were absent in this patient. The authors argued that otofaciocervical syndrome is a unique, clinically recognisable entity that should be distinguished from branchiootorenal syndrome.

### Heterogeneity

Pohl et al. (2013) found that a hypofunctional PAX1 mutation causes autosomal recessively inherited otofaciocervical syndrome. This established PAX1 as a disease-causing gene for the disorder.

### Molecular Genetics

Rickard et al. (2001) found a large deletion of the EYA1 gene region on chromosome 8q13.3 in a patient with otofaciocervical syndrome. They concluded that otofaciocervical syndrome is a contiguous gene deletion syndrome involving EYA1 and that it is allelic with BOR syndrome. Their analysis also narrowed the critical region for Duane syndrome.

Estefanía et al. (2006) identified a single-nucleotide substitution in a splice site of the EYA1 gene in a patient with otofaciocervical syndrome. This showed that point mutations altering the EYA1 reading frame can be found in patients with the syndrome, and not only major rearrangements.

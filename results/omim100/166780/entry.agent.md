### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that otofaciocervical syndrome-1 (OTFCS1) is caused by mutation in the EYA1 gene on chromosome 8q13.3, either as part of a de novo contiguous deletion of EYA1 and the surrounding region or as a point mutation within EYA1.

### Description

Otofaciocervical syndrome is a developmental disorder that resembles branchiootorenal (BOR) syndrome. Rickard et al. (2001) described it as clinically similar to BOR syndrome but having features in addition to those of BOR syndrome. Estefanía et al. (2006) characterized it by trophic alterations of the facies and shoulder girdle in addition to the malformations seen in BOR.

### Clinical Features

Fára et al. (1967) reported familial otofaciocervical dysmorphia. Dallapiccola and Mingarelli (1995) noted that some features present in the original family, including lateral cervical fistulae, had suggested that OFC syndrome could be a variant of BOR syndrome.

Dallapiccola and Mingarelli (1995) described a sporadic patient with OFC syndrome. Distinguishing features were a long face with narrow nose, high arched palate, prominent and dysmorphic ears, long neck, sloping shoulders and clavicles, winged, low, and laterally set scapulae, tetralogy of Fallot, and deafness secondary to cochlear malformation. Preauricular tags, lacrimal duct stenosis, and renal malformations were absent. The authors concluded that the absence of these features, together with the distinct facial and radiographic findings and markedly downward sloping shoulders, supported OFC syndrome being a unique, clinically recognizable entity distinct from BOR syndrome.

### Inheritance

Fára et al. (1967) reported a familial occurrence of otofaciocervical dysmorphia; Dallapiccola and Mingarelli (1995) described a sporadic patient. In the patient with OFC syndrome studied by Rickard et al. (2001), the deletion of the EYA1 gene region had arisen de novo.

### Mapping

Rickard et al. (2001) and Estefanía et al. (2006) noted that the EYA1 gene, in which deletion or mutation was found in patients with OFC syndrome, is located on chromosome 8q13.3.

### Heterogeneity

Pohl et al. (2013) found a homozygous mutation in the PAX1 gene (c.497G-T, gly166-to-val (G166V)) that cosegregated with disease in a large consanguineous Turkish family with autosomal recessive otofaciocervical syndrome, characterized by facial dysmorphism, external ear anomalies with preauricular pits and hearing impairment, branchial cysts or fistulas, anomalies of the vertebrae and shoulder girdle, and mild intellectual disability. The variant was found by whole-exome sequencing of a pooled DNA sample from 4 affected individuals. In a dual luciferase reporter assay, the corresponding mouse mutant Pax1(G157V) showed significantly reduced transactivation of a regulatory sequence in the Nkx3-2 promoter compared with wildtype. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Using a fluorescent quantitative multiplex PCR dosage assay for 3 3-prime exons (7, 9, and 13) of the EYA1 gene, combined with microsatellite marker analysis, Rickard et al. (2001) identified de novo deletions of the EYA1 gene and surrounding region in 2 patients with complex phenotypes involving features of BOR syndrome. One patient with OFC syndrome carried a large deletion of the EYA1 gene region, which the authors interpreted as confirming that OFC syndrome is allelic with BOR syndrome and is a contiguous gene deletion syndrome involving EYA1. Comparison of the boundaries of this deletion with other reported rearrangements of the region reduced the critical region for Duane syndrome to between markers D8S553 and D8S1797, a genetic distance of approximately 1 cM.

Estefanía et al. (2006) identified a single-nucleotide substitution in a splice site of the EYA1 gene in a patient with OFC syndrome. The authors concluded that not only major rearrangements, but also point mutations altering the EYA1 reading frame, can be found in patients with OFC syndrome, in contrast to the hypothesis that OFC is caused by contiguous deletions of EYA1 and adjacent genes.

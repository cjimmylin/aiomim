### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that otofaciocervical syndrome-1 (OTFCS1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the EYA1 gene (601653) on chromosome 8q13. ⚠︎

### Description

Otofaciocervical syndrome is a developmental disorder. Its main features are facial dysmorphism, abnormally shaped and low-set external ears, preauricular pits or fistulas, hearing loss, branchial cysts or fistulas, and abnormalities of the neck and shoulder girdle. These include a long neck, sloping shoulders and winged scapulae. Mild intellectual disability and short stature have been reported in some patients. The disorder overlaps with branchiootorenal (BOR) syndrome, which is also caused by mutation in EYA1, but renal anomalies are usually absent. ✗

### Clinical Features

Fara et al. (1967) described a Czech family with a dominantly inherited syndrome that they called dystrophia otofaciocervicalis. Affected members had facial dysmorphism, protruding and low-set ears, preauricular fistulas, hearing loss, a long neck, sloping shoulders and other shoulder-girdle anomalies. ✗

Dallapiccola and Mingarelli (1995) reported a sporadic patient with otofaciocervical syndrome. They argued that the disorder should be distinguished from branchiootorenal syndrome.

### Inheritance

The family reported by Fara et al. (1967) showed autosomal dominant transmission of otofaciocervical syndrome. ✗

### Heterogeneity

Otofaciocervical syndrome-2 (OTFCS2), an autosomal recessive form that includes immunodeficiency, is caused by mutation in the PAX1 gene. ✗

### Molecular Genetics

Rickard et al. (2001) found a heterozygous deletion involving the EYA1 gene on chromosome 8q in a patient with otofaciocervical syndrome. They concluded that OTFCS is a contiguous gene deletion syndrome that is allelic to BOR syndrome. Their analysis also narrowed the critical region for Duane syndrome. ⚠︎

Estefania et al. (2006) identified a heterozygous splice site mutation in the EYA1 gene in a patient with otofaciocervical syndrome. This showed that a point mutation in EYA1, and not only a contiguous gene deletion, can cause the phenotype. ⚠︎

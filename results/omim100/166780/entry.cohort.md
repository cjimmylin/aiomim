### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that otofaciocervical syndrome-1 (OTFCS) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the EYA1 gene (601653) on chromosome 8q13.3. ⚠︎

### Description

Otofaciocervical (OFC) syndrome is clinically similar to branchiootorenal (BOR) syndrome but has features beyond those of BOR syndrome (Rickard et al., 2001). Estefanía et al. (2006) described it as having trophic alterations of the face and shoulder girdle in addition to the malformations seen in BOR syndrome.

### Clinical Features

Fára et al. (1967) reported a familial occurrence of oto-facio-cervical dysmorphia.

Dallapiccola and Mingarelli (1995) described a sporadic patient with OFC syndrome. The distinguishing features were a long face with a narrow nose, high arched palate, prominent and dysmorphic ears, long neck, sloping shoulders and clavicles, winged, low, and laterally set scapulae, tetralogy of Fallot, and deafness secondary to cochlear malformation. Preauricular tags, lacrimal duct stenosis, and renal malformations were absent.

### Inheritance

Fára et al. (1967) reported familial occurrence of OFC syndrome. The heterozygous EYA1 deletion in the patient with OFC syndrome reported by Rickard et al. (2001) occurred de novo, which is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Rickard et al. (2001) compared the boundaries of the large deletion in their OFC patient with other reported rearrangements of the region. The comparison narrowed the critical region for Duane syndrome to the interval between markers D8S553 and D8S1797, a genetic distance of about 1 cM.

### Heterogeneity

Dallapiccola and Mingarelli (1995) noted that some features of the original OFC family, including lateral cervical fistulae, had suggested that OFC syndrome might be a variant of BOR syndrome. In their sporadic patient, preauricular tags, lacrimal duct stenosis, and renal malformations were absent, while distinct facial and radiographic findings and markedly downward-sloping shoulders were present. They concluded that this supported OFC syndrome being a unique, clinically recognizable entity. In contrast, Rickard et al. (2001) found a deletion of the EYA1 gene region in a patient with OFC syndrome and concluded that OFC syndrome is allelic with BOR syndrome.

Pohl et al. (2013) studied a large consanguineous Turkish family with an autosomal recessive form of otofaciocervical syndrome. They characterized it by facial dysmorphism, external ear anomalies with preauricular pits and hearing impairment, branchial cysts or fistulas, anomalies of the vertebrae and shoulder girdle, and mild intellectual disability. They performed whole-exome sequencing on a pooled DNA sample from 4 affected family members. The only novel homozygous variant they identified was a c.497G-T transversion in the PAX1 gene (167411), predicted to cause a gly166-to-val (G166V) substitution within the highly conserved paired-box domain. The variant cosegregated with the disease in the family. In a dual luciferase reporter assay of an Nkx3-2 promoter regulatory sequence in HEK293T cells, mouse Pax1 carrying the corresponding G157V mutation produced significantly less transactivation than wildtype, indicating that the mutant protein had reduced DNA-binding affinity. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Rickard et al. (2001) combined a fluorescent quantitative multiplex PCR dosage assay for EYA1 exons 7, 9, and 13 with microsatellite marker analysis. They identified de novo deletions of the EYA1 gene and the surrounding region in 2 patients whose complex phenotypes included features of BOR syndrome. One of these patients had OFC syndrome and carried a large heterozygous deletion of the EYA1 gene region. The authors concluded that OFC syndrome is allelic with BOR syndrome and is a contiguous gene deletion syndrome involving EYA1. ⚠︎

Estefanía et al. (2006) found a single-nucleotide substitution in a splice site of the EYA1 gene in a patient with OFC syndrome. The earlier hypothesis was that OFC syndrome resulted from contiguous deletions of EYA1 and adjacent genes. The authors concluded that OFC syndrome can be caused not only by major rearrangements but also by point mutations that alter the EYA1 reading frame. ⚠︎

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 OTOFACIOCERVICAL SYNDROME 1**  
EYA1, DEL

Rickard et al. (2001) used a quantitative multiplex PCR dosage assay and microsatellite marker analysis to identify a large heterozygous deletion of the EYA1 gene region in a patient with OFC syndrome. The deletion had arisen de novo, and the authors concluded that OFC syndrome is a contiguous gene deletion syndrome that is allelic with BOR syndrome. ⚠︎

**.0002 OTOFACIOCERVICAL SYNDROME 1**  
EYA1, SPLICE SITE SUBSTITUTION

Estefanía et al. (2006) identified a single-nucleotide substitution in a splice site of the EYA1 gene in a patient with OFC syndrome. They concluded that point mutations altering the EYA1 reading frame, and not only major rearrangements, can cause OFC syndrome.

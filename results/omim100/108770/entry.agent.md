### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that atrial standstill-1 (ATRST1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the cardiac sodium channel gene SCN5A. In the reported families, the SCN5A mutation was found together with rare polymorphisms in the gene encoding the atrial-specific gap junction protein connexin-40 (Cx40), which may modify the phenotype. ⚠︎

### Description

Atrial standstill is a rare arrhythmia that sometimes appears to be genetically determined (Groenewegen et al., 2003). It has been associated with several conditions, including congenital heart disease, valvular heart disease, conduction disturbances, Brugada syndrome, myocardial infarction, and amyloidosis. Familial clustering has been reported only rarely (Fazelifar et al., 2005).

### Clinical Features

Allensworth et al. (1969) reported persistent atrial standstill in a family with myocardial disease.

Harrison and Derrick (1969) reviewed atrial standstill and presented 2 new familial cases, one of whom was treated with epicardial pacing.

Williams et al. (1972) reported familial atrial cardiomyopathy with heart block.

Amat-y-Leon et al. (1974) reported familial atrial dysrhythmia with atrioventricular (A-V) block, with intracellular microelectrode, clinical electrophysiologic, and morphologic observations.

Shah et al. (1992) reported familial total atrial standstill.

Groenewegen et al. (2003) clinically evaluated 44 members of a large family with atrial standstill. One deceased and 3 living relatives were unambiguously affected; all other relatives appeared unaffected.

Fazelifar et al. (2005) reported a family with atrial standstill associated with syncope, dilated cardiomyopathy, and sudden cardiac death.

Makita et al. (2005) studied an apparently sporadic case of atrial standstill, an 11-year-old boy who had no P waves on surface ECG and whose right atrium could not be captured by pacing. His father, who carried the same SCN5A mutation, was in normal sinus rhythm.

### Inheritance

Familial occurrence of atrial standstill was reported by Allensworth et al. (1969), Harrison and Derrick (1969), Williams et al. (1972), Amat-y-Leon et al. (1974), Shah et al. (1992), and Fazelifar et al. (2005). In the families studied by Groenewegen et al. (2003) and Makita et al. (2005), heterozygous SCN5A mutations were also present in unaffected relatives, indicating incomplete penetrance; both groups found that affected individuals had also inherited rare Cx40 polymorphisms, and Makita et al. (2005) noted that the incomplete penetrance had been attributed in part to digenic inheritance of Cx40 polymorphisms together with an SCN5A mutation. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In a large family with atrial standstill, Groenewegen et al. (2003) used candidate gene screening to identify a novel mutation in the SCN5A gene, asp1275-to-asn (D1275N). The mutation was present in all 3 affected living relatives and in 5 unaffected relatives, and the deceased affected relative was an obligate carrier. They also found 2 closely linked polymorphisms in regulatory regions of the Cx40 gene, at nucleotides -44 (G-A) and +71 (A-G). Eight relatives were homozygous for both polymorphisms, a genotype seen in only about 7% of control subjects, and 3 of these 8 were affected. Only the 3 living patients had inherited both the rare Cx40 genotype and the D1275N mutation. Mutant D1275N channels showed a small depolarizing shift in activation compared with wildtype channels, and in reporter gene analysis the rare Cx40 genotype gave less reporter expression than the more common genotype. Groenewegen et al. (2003) proposed that each genetic change has a relatively benign functional effect, but that their combined effect eventually progresses to total atrial standstill.

In an 11-year-old boy with an apparently sporadic case of atrial standstill, Makita et al. (2005) identified a novel SCN5A mutation, leu212-to-pro (L212P), which was also present in his father, who was in normal sinus rhythm. Recombinant L212P sodium channels studied by whole-cell patch clamp showed a large hyperpolarizing shift in the voltage dependence of activation (wildtype, -48.1 +/- 0.9 mV; L212P, -63.5 +/- 1.5 mV) and of inactivation (wildtype, -86.6 +/- 0.9 mV; L212P, -95.6 +/- 0.8 mV), as well as delayed recovery from inactivation. The proband, but not his father, also carried Cx40 polymorphisms inherited from his asymptomatic mother. Makita et al. (2005) concluded that SCN5A defects most likely underlie atrial standstill, and that coinheritance of Cx40 polymorphisms may modify its clinical expression.

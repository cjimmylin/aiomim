A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that atrial standstill-1 (ATRST1) can be caused by mutation in the SCN5A gene (600163) on chromosome 3p22.2 occurring together with rare polymorphisms in the connexin-40 gene GJA5 (121013) on chromosome 1q21.2, consistent with digenic inheritance.

### Description

Atrial standstill is a rare arrhythmia (Groenewegen et al., 2003). In a family with the disorder, Groenewegen et al. (2003) found that atrial standstill was associated with a mutation in the cardiac sodium channel gene SCN5A occurring together with rare polymorphisms in the atrial-specific gap junction gene GJA5 (connexin-40).

### Clinical Features

Groenewegen et al. (2003) studied a large family with familial atrial standstill.

Makita et al. (2005) described a family with atrial standstill. The proband had no P waves on the surface ECG, and his right atrium could not be captured by pacing.

### Inheritance

The findings of Groenewegen et al. (2003) suggested digenic inheritance of familial atrial standstill. In the family they studied, the 3 affected living patients were the only members who coinherited both the SCN5A mutation and a rare GJA5 (connexin-40) genotype. The SCN5A mutation was also present in 5 unaffected relatives.

### Molecular Genetics

Groenewegen et al. (2003) used candidate gene screening in a family with atrial standstill. They identified an asp1275-to-asn (D1275N) mutation in the SCN5A gene in all 3 affected living relatives. The affected patients coinherited the mutation with a rare genotype of 2 closely linked polymorphisms in regulatory regions of the connexin-40 gene (GJA5). In functional studies, D1275N channels showed a small depolarizing shift in activation compared with wildtype channels. The authors proposed that, although each genetic change had a relatively benign functional effect, their combined effect eventually progressed to total atrial standstill.

Makita et al. (2005) identified a novel SCN5A mutation in a family with atrial standstill. The affected proband carried both the SCN5A mutation and connexin-40 polymorphisms. Makita et al. (2005) noted that incomplete penetrance in atrial standstill had been attributed in part to digenic inheritance of connexin-40 polymorphisms together with an SCN5A mutation.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that atrial standstill-1 (ATRST1) is caused by mutation in the cardiac sodium channel gene SCN5A, in some cases together with rare polymorphisms in the gene encoding the atrial-specific gap junction protein connexin-40 (Cx40).

### Description

Atrial standstill is a rare arrhythmia that occasionally appears to be genetically determined (Groenewegen et al., 2003). Familial cases have been reported with heart block, syncope, dilated cardiomyopathy, and sudden cardiac death (Williams et al., 1972; Amat-y-Leon et al., 1974; Fazelifar et al., 2005). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Allensworth et al. (1969) reported persistent atrial standstill in a family with myocardial disease.

Harrison and Derrick (1969) reviewed atrial standstill and presented 2 new cases of a familial and unusual nature, one of whom was treated with epicardial pacing.

Williams et al. (1972) described familial atrial cardiomyopathy with heart block.

Amat-y-Leon et al. (1974) reported familial atrial dysrhythmia with atrioventricular (A-V) block, with intracellular microelectrode, clinical electrophysiologic, and morphologic observations.

Shah et al. (1992) reported familial total atrial standstill.

Groenewegen et al. (2003) clinically evaluated 44 members of a large family. One deceased and 3 living relatives were unambiguously affected by atrial standstill; all other relatives appeared unaffected.

Fazelifar et al. (2005) reported a family with atrial standstill associated with syncope, dilated cardiomyopathy, and sudden cardiac death.

Makita et al. (2005) studied an apparently sporadic case of atrial standstill in an 11-year-old boy. The proband had no P waves on surface ECG, and his right atrium could not be captured by pacing. His father, who carried the same SCN5A mutation, was in normal sinus rhythm.

### Inheritance

In the family studied by Groenewegen et al. (2003), an SCN5A mutation was present in all 3 affected living relatives, in 5 unaffected relatives, and in a deceased affected obligate carrier, indicating incomplete penetrance. The 3 living affected individuals exclusively coinherited the SCN5A mutation and a rare homozygous Cx40 genotype, and Groenewegen et al. (2003) proposed that the combined effect of these genetic changes progresses to total atrial standstill. Makita et al. (2005) noted that incomplete penetrance has been attributed in part to digenic inheritance of Cx40 polymorphisms in conjunction with an SCN5A mutation.

### Molecular Genetics

In a large family with atrial standstill, Groenewegen et al. (2003) used candidate gene screening to identify a novel mutation in the SCN5A gene (D1275N) in all 3 affected living relatives and in 5 unaffected relatives; the deceased affected relative was an obligate carrier. In addition, they detected 2 closely linked polymorphisms within regulatory regions of the gene for the atrial-specific gap junction protein connexin-40 (Cx40), at nucleotides -44 (G-A) and +71 (A-G). Eight relatives were homozygous for both polymorphisms, which occurred in only approximately 7% of control subjects, and 3 of these relatives were affected. The 3 living patients exclusively coinherited both the rare Cx40 genotype and the SCN5A D1275N mutation. D1275N channels showed a small depolarizing shift in activation compared with wildtype channels, and reporter gene analysis showed reduced expression with the rare Cx40 genotype compared with the more common genotype. Groenewegen et al. (2003) proposed that although the functional effect of each genetic change is relatively benign, their combined effect eventually progresses to total atrial standstill.

In an 11-year-old boy with atrial standstill, Makita et al. (2005) identified a novel SCN5A mutation (L212P), which was also present in his father, who was in normal sinus rhythm. Whole-cell patch clamp of the recombinant L212P channel showed a large hyperpolarizing shift in the voltage dependence of both activation (wildtype: -48.1 +/- 0.9 mV; L212P: -63.5 +/- 1.5 mV) and inactivation (wildtype: -86.6 +/- 0.9 mV; L212P: -95.6 +/- 0.8 mV), as well as delayed recovery from inactivation. The proband, but not his father, carried Cx40 polymorphisms inherited from his asymptomatic mother. Makita et al. (2005) concluded that SCN5A defects most likely underlie atrial standstill and that coinheritance of Cx40 polymorphisms is a possible genetic modifier of the clinical manifestation.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 ATRIAL STANDSTILL 1**  
SCN5A, ASP1275ASN [D1275N]

In 3 living affected members of a large family with atrial standstill, Groenewegen et al. (2003) identified an asp1275-to-asn (D1275N) mutation in the SCN5A gene. The mutation was also present in 5 unaffected relatives, and a deceased affected relative was an obligate carrier. The 3 living patients also exclusively coinherited homozygosity for 2 closely linked rare polymorphisms in regulatory regions of the Cx40 gene (-44G-A and +71A-G). D1275N channels showed a small depolarizing shift in activation compared with wildtype channels.

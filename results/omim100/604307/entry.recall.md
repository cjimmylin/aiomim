### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that cataract-2 (CTRCT2), which comprises several types of cataract, is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gamma-C-crystallin gene (CRYGC; 123680) on chromosome 2q33.3. ⚠︎

### Description

Mutations in the CRYGC gene have been found in several forms of congenital cataract (Zhang et al., 2009). These include Coppock-like (nuclear) cataract (Héon et al., 1999), zonular pulverulent cataract (Ren et al., 2000), and lamellar cataract (Gonzalez-Huerta et al., 2007). Some affected individuals also have microcornea (Zhang et al., 2009). Inheritance is autosomal dominant (Ren et al., 2000). ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Ren et al. (2000) and Li et al. (2012) each reported a family in which CRYGC-related cataract was inherited as an autosomal dominant trait.

### Mapping

Lubsen et al. (1987) found that the locus for Coppock-like cataract was closely linked to the gamma-crystallin gene cluster.

### Heterogeneity

Congenital cataract is genetically heterogeneous (Zhang et al., 2009). Héon et al. (1999) found that mutation in the CRYGD gene, which lies in the same gamma-crystallin gene cluster on chromosome 2q33-q35 as CRYGC, causes a separate form of cataract.

### Molecular Genetics

Héon et al. (1999) studied cataract families linked to the gamma-crystallin gene cluster. In the original family with Coppock-like cataract, they identified a missense mutation in a highly conserved segment of exon 2 of the CRYGC gene.

Ren et al. (2000) studied a family with autosomal dominant variable zonular pulverulent cataract. They identified a heterozygous 5-bp duplication (insertion) in the CRYGC gene that disrupted the reading frame and was predicted to result in a truncated protein.

Santhiya et al. (2002) reported novel mutations in the gamma-crystallin genes causing autosomal dominant congenital cataract.

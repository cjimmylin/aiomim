### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that cataract-2 (CTRCT2), which comprises several types of cataract, with or without microcornea, is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gamma-C-crystallin gene (CRYGC; 123680) on chromosome 2q33. ✗

### Description

Mutations in the CRYGC gene have been found in several forms of congenital or early-onset cataract. These include Coppock-like (nuclear) cataract, zonular pulverulent cataract, and lamellar cataract. Some affected people also have microcornea. Inheritance is autosomal dominant.

### Inheritance

In the families reported with CRYGC mutations, the cataract phenotype segregated as an autosomal dominant trait.

### Mapping

Coppock-like cataract was mapped to the gamma-crystallin gene cluster on chromosome 2q33-q35. That cluster contains CRYGA through CRYGD. ✗

### Heterogeneity

Autosomal dominant congenital cataract is genetically heterogeneous. Mutations in the neighboring CRYGD gene, which lies in the same gamma-crystallin cluster on chromosome 2q33, cause a separate form of cataract.

### Molecular Genetics

Heon et al. (1999) studied families with autosomal dominant congenital cataract linked to the gamma-crystallin cluster. In a family with Coppock-like cataract, they found a heterozygous thr5-to-pro (T5P) mutation in the CRYGC gene. The mutation was predicted to disturb the folding of the protein. ✗

Ren et al. (2000) studied a family with autosomal dominant variable zonular pulverulent cataract. They identified a heterozygous 5-bp duplication (insertion) in the CRYGC gene that caused a frameshift and a truncated protein.

Santhiya et al. (2002) screened the gamma-crystallin genes in Indian families with autosomal dominant congenital cataract. In a family with lamellar cataract, they found a heterozygous arg168-to-trp (R168W) mutation in CRYGC. ✗

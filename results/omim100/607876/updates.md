# Proposed updates for OMIM 607876: EPILEPSY, FAMILIAL ADULT MYOCLONIC, 2

Literature from 2012 onwards that the entry does not cite: 51 papers screened, 7 read, 7 proposal(s). Each proposal is a draft for a curator to accept, edit or reject.

**Back-test as of 2012.** OMIM's curators later added 3 reference(s) from that year on; this run proposed 3 of them (recall 1.0), and 4 paper(s) OMIM does not cite.

## [HIGH] Corbett et al. (2019) — new_causal_gene, new_variants, functional_evidence, larger_cohort, natural_history, diagnostic_method

Corbett MA, Kroes T, Veneziano L, Bennett MF, Florian R, Schneider AL, Coppola A, Licchetta L, Franceschetti S, Suppa A, and others. Intronic ATTTC repeat expansions in STARD7 in familial adult myoclonic epilepsy linked to chromosome 2. Nat Commun 10: 4920, 2019. [PubMed: 31664034] *(read: fulltext)*

**Why:** The entry has only linkage to 2p11.1-q12.2 and no variants. Corbett et al. (2019) identified the causal mutation, an intronic ATTTC pentamer repeat insertion/expansion in STARD7, in 158 individuals from 22 families. The paper also reports anticipation, finds the expansion in families carrying the ADRA2B variant (arguing that variant is not causal), finds no effect on STARD7 expression, and describes a diagnostic RP-PCR assay.

*clinical_features*: Corbett et al. (2019) summarized clinical data from 22 FAME families of Australian/New Zealand, Italian, Iraqi and Israeli Sephardic Jewish, South African, French/Spanish, French, and Syrian origin. Nearly all affected individuals had cortical myoclonic tremor or myoclonus. Onset was usually in the second to third decade. The frequency of generalized tonic-clonic seizures varied between families from 15 to 100%, and some individuals had focal seizures.

*clinical_features*: Corbett et al. (2019) found evidence of anticipation in the large Australian/New Zealand FAME2 family. Median age at onset was 30 years in generation III (n = 6), 17 years in generation IV (n = 30), and 12 years in generation V (n = 16).

*molecular_genetics*: Corbett et al. (2019) performed whole-genome sequencing with repeat expansion detection (ExpansionHunter, exSTRa) in affected individuals from an Australian-New Zealand, an Italian, and a French-Spanish FAME family. In intron 1 of the STARD7 gene, within the FAME2 linkage interval on chromosome 2, they identified an expansion of the endogenous ATTTT pentamer repeat with an inserted ATTTC repeat. The ATTTC repeat was absent from WGS data of 69 controls. Using repeat-primed PCR (RP-PCR), they found the expansion in 137 of 137 affected individuals from 16 previously reported FAME2 families. They also found it in 20 of 20 familial cases from 6 additional families and in 1 of 52 unrelated singleton cases with FAME-like phenotypes, for a total of 158 positive individuals from 22 families. Two of 24 unaffected relatives tested carried the expansion and were likely presymptomatic. Eight of 13 individuals with an uncertain diagnosis carried the expansion, and it was absent from 28 control DNAs.

*molecular_genetics*: From RP-PCR results, Corbett et al. (2019) inferred that the pathogenic repeat on the forward strand of chromosome 2 had the structure (AAATG)n[N](AAAAT)n. Long-read sequencing was done in a patient from Family 1. A SMRT read showed 274 AAATG and 387 AAAAT repeats, and a nanopore read showed 345 AAATG and 390 AAAAT repeats, suggesting somatic variation. A patient from Family 5 had 588 AAATG and 340 AAAAT repeats (4,645 bp).

*heterogeneity*: Corbett et al. (2019) found the STARD7 ATTTC expansion in 2 families (Families 4 and 15) that carried the previously identified ADRA2B variant c.675_686delTGGTGGGGCTTTinsGTTTGGCAG. They concluded that this strongly suggested the ADRA2B allele is not causative.

*pathogenesis*: In patient-derived fibroblasts, Corbett et al. (2019) found no significant difference in STARD7 transcript levels (by RT-qPCR and RNA-seq) or protein levels (by Western blot), and no RNA-seq reads contained ATTTC repeats. They suggested that the repeat sequence alone is pathogenic, independent of any effect on STARD7, and noted that RNA toxicity has been proposed as the mechanism.

*diagnosis*: Corbett et al. (2019) reported that the ATTTC repeat-primed PCR primer was diagnostic for FAME with 100% sensitivity in families with linkage or suggestive linkage to chromosome 2. The variable endogenous ATTTT repeat was unsuitable for screening. They noted that FAME2 is the most frequent linked locus in Caucasians, whereas linkage to chromosome 8 has been limited to Asians.

## [HIGH] De Fusco et al. (2014) — new_causal_gene, new_variants, functional_evidence, phenotype_expansion

De Fusco M, Vago R, Striano P, Di Bonaventura C, Zara F, Mei D, Kim MS, Muallem S, Chen Y, Wang Q, and others. The α2B-adrenergic receptor is mutant in cortical myoclonus and epilepsy. Ann Neurol 75: 77-87, 2014. [PubMed: 24114805] *(read: fulltext)*

**Why:** The entry currently has no causal gene or variant. De Fusco et al. (2014) found a heterozygous in-frame indel in ADRA2B that segregated in 2 Tuscan ADCME families sharing a haplotype. They added functional evidence for a gain-of-function mechanism through loss of stable spinophilin binding. They also reported that southern Italian BAFME families linked to the same locus did not carry ADRA2B mutations, which suggests ADCME and BAFME may be distinct entities. The paper also describes a new family with age-related dementia in older carriers and seizures triggered by visual stimuli.

*clinical_features* ⚠︎: De Fusco et al. (2014) described a 4-generation Italian kindred from Tuscany (family B) with autosomal dominant cortical myoclonus and epilepsy (ADCME) and 5 affected members, 4 of them living. All patients had postural myoclonus of the hands and upper limbs. Cortical tremor was the presenting symptom and began between ages 18 and 50 years. All but 1 patient had epilepsy, consisting of rare generalized tonic-clonic seizures that were at times precipitated by visual stimuli. Two patients also had drug-resistant focal motor or complex partial seizures. One patient had borderline cognition and the others had normal intelligence. All patients had generalized or focal paroxysmal EEG activity. Jerk-locked back-averaged EEG, somatosensory evoked potentials, and long-loop reflex studies were consistent with cortical reflex myoclonus. Brain MRI was normal in the patients studied. No patient had cardiovascular disease, and 2 of the oldest mutation carriers developed age-related dementia.

*molecular_genetics* ⚠︎: De Fusco et al. (2014) studied a 5-generation Italian family from Tuscany with ADCME, which had previously been mapped to chromosome 2p11.1-q12.2. By sequencing candidate genes, they found a heterozygous in-frame insertion/deletion in the ADRA2B gene in affected members (c.675_686delTGGTGGGGCTTTinsGTTTGGCAG; p.H225_L229delinsQ225_F_G_R228). The indel replaces 5 amino acids (HGGAL) in the third intracellular loop with 4 new residues (QFGR). The mutation segregated completely with the disease. It was absent from 575 Tuscan controls, dbSNP, the Exome Variant Server, and the 1000 Genomes Project, a total of 16,080 chromosomes. Targeted capture and next-generation sequencing of the critical region in 2 affected family members showed that the ADRA2B mutation was the only relevant change. The same indel was found in a second Tuscan ADCME family (family B). The family history showed no kinship between the 2 families, but they shared the same disease haplotype across the entire critical region, which suggested a common ancestor.

*molecular_genetics*: De Fusco et al. (2014) performed functional studies of the ADRA2B indel. In transfected HeLa cells, the mutant receptor localized normally to the plasma membrane and was internalized in an arrestin-3-dependent manner. In co-immunoisolation assays, spinophilin bound comparable amounts of wildtype and mutant receptor after 5 minutes of epinephrine stimulation. After 10 minutes, binding to the mutant receptor had fallen back to basal levels, whereas binding to wildtype remained elevated, indicating that the mutant could not form a stable complex with spinophilin. In Xenopus oocytes, spinophilin raised the EC50 for epinephrine-stimulated calcium signaling with wildtype receptor but not with mutant receptor. The authors concluded that the mutation has a gain-of-function effect that increases the intensity of calcium signaling.

*pathogenesis*: De Fusco et al. (2014) proposed that the ADRA2B mutation in ADCME has a gain-of-function effect. By reducing interaction with spinophilin, which recruits RGS proteins to attenuate signaling, the mutation increases receptor activity. They suggested that cortical hyperexcitability in ADCME might result from impaired GABAergic inhibition.

*heterogeneity*: De Fusco et al. (2014) found no ADRA2B mutations in BAFME pedigrees from southern Italy that map to the ADCME locus on chromosome 2. Southern blot analysis excluded large insertions or deletions. The authors suggested that ADCME and BAFME/FAME might be classified as 2 distinct clinical entities, although they could not exclude variants in regulatory or noncoding regions.

## [MEDIUM] Henden et al. (2016) — larger_cohort, population_data

Henden L, Freytag S, Afawi Z, Baldassari S, Berkovic SF, Bisulli F, Canafoglia L, Casari G, Crompton DE, Depienne C, and others. Identity by descent fine mapping of familial adult myoclonus epilepsy (FAME) to 2p11.2-2q11.2. Hum Genet 135: 1117-25, 2016. [PubMed: 27368338] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** Henden et al. (2016) used identity-by-descent analysis across 11 families to narrow the FAME2 locus to a 9.78-Mb interval at 2p11.2-q11.2. The entry's current intervals are 12.4 cM and 14.5 Mb. The paper also reports at least 4 distinct founders at this locus, which is allelic heterogeneity the entry does not record, and adds more evidence for an Italian founder effect.

*mapping*: Henden et al. (2016) ran a pedigree-free identity-by-descent (IBD) analysis on high-density SNP array data from a FAME cohort of 10 European families and 1 Australian/New Zealander family. Disease loci were already known for 8 of these families. By finding IBD regions shared by several families, the authors narrowed the FAME2 locus to a 9.78-Mb interval within chromosome 2p11.2-q11.2.

*heterogeneity*: Henden et al. (2016) found evidence of allelic heterogeneity at the FAME2 locus: at least 4 distinct founders were responsible for FAME there. They noted that FAME is genetically heterogeneous, with more than 70 families reported worldwide and 5 potential disease loci.

*population_genetics* ⚠︎: Henden et al. (2016) reported further evidence of a founder effect in 4 Italian families with FAME linked to the FAME2 locus.

## [MEDIUM] Coppola et al. (2016) — phenotype_expansion

Coppola A, Caccavale C, Santulli L, Balestrini S, Cagnetti C, Licchetta L, Esposito M, Bisulli F, Tinuper P, Provinciali L, and others. Psychiatric comorbidities in patients from seven families with autosomal dominant cortical tremor, myoclonus, and epilepsy. Epilepsy Behav 56: 38-43, 2016. [PubMed: 26827300] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** The entry lists no psychiatric features. Coppola et al. (2016) studied 20 ADCME patients from 7 families and found more depression and state anxiety than in JME patients and controls, anxiety that correlated with myoclonus severity, pathological personality traits, and lower quality of life.

*clinical_features*: Coppola et al. (2016) assessed psychiatric comorbidity in 20 patients from 7 families with autosomal dominant cortical tremor, myoclonus, and epilepsy (ADCME). They used validated scales (BDI, STAI-Y1/2, MMPI-2, and QoLIE-31) and compared the patients with 20 patients with juvenile myoclonic epilepsy (JME) and 20 healthy controls. Mood disorders were more common in ADCME patients than in either comparison group, particularly depression (p = 0.035 vs JME; p = 0.017 vs controls) and state anxiety. Trait anxiety was higher than in controls but did not differ from that in JME. In ADCME patients, the myoclonus score correlated positively with both state anxiety (rho 0.58) and trait anxiety (rho 0.65). The psychiatric features were often associated with pathological personality traits: paranoid (OR 25.7), psychasthenia (OR 7.0), schizophrenia (OR 8.5), and hypomania (OR 5.5). Lower quality of life correlated with these psychiatric symptoms. The authors recommended a comprehensive psychiatric evaluation at the time of diagnosis.

## [MEDIUM] Licchetta et al. (2013) — phenotype_expansion, natural_history, larger_cohort, population_data

Licchetta L, Pippucci T, Bisulli F, Cantalupo G, Magini P, Alvisi L, Baldassari S, Martinelli P, Naldi I, Vanni N, and others. A novel pedigree with familial cortical myoclonic tremor and epilepsy (FCMTE): clinical characterization, refinement of the FCMTE2 locus, and confirmation of a founder haplotype. Epilepsia 54: 1298-306, 2013. [PubMed: 23663087] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** Licchetta et al. (2013) add a large new linked family (25 affected). Three features are not in the entry: slow progression of tremor (with severe gait impairment in one patient), psychiatric disorders in 37.5%, and a photoparoxysmal response. The paper also narrows the FAME2 interval to 10.4 Mb with 59 candidate genes and confirms the Italian founder haplotype in another family.

*clinical_features*: Licchetta et al. (2013) described a large family with familial cortical myoclonic tremor and epilepsy (FCMTE) in which 25 of 127 members were affected (9 males, 16 females). Sixteen living affected members were studied, with a mean age of 47.8 years. Ten (62.5%) of them had cortical myoclonic tremor associated with generalized seizures. Mean age at onset was 28.1 years for tremor and 33.8 years for seizures. Four patients (25%) reported slow progression of the tremor, and 1 of them had severe gait impairment. Psychiatric disorders of variable severity occurred in 37.5%. Polygraphic recordings showed rhythmic bursts at 7 to 15 Hz in all 11 affected members tested. Back-averaging and somatosensory evoked potentials with C-reflex study indicated that the myoclonus was cortical in origin in all patients tested. Generalized epileptiform discharges were recorded in 25% of cases and a photoparoxysmal response in 31%.

*mapping*: Licchetta et al. (2013) performed microsatellite analysis of 30 members of a large FCMTE family at the FCMTE1 (8q), FCMTE2 (2p), and FCMTE3 (5p) loci. They established linkage to the FCMTE2 locus on chromosome 2p11.1-q12.2 and narrowed the critical interval to a 10.4-Mb segment containing 59 positional candidate genes.

*population_genetics*: Licchetta et al. (2013) identified a founder haplotype in their FCMTE2-linked family that was identical to the haplotype previously observed in families from the same geographic area. This confirmed a founder effect in Italian families.

## [MEDIUM] Crompton et al. (2012) — phenotype_expansion, larger_cohort, natural_history

Crompton DE, Sadleir LG, Bromhead CJ, Bahlo M, Bellows ST, Arsov T, Harty R, Lawrence KM, Dunne JW, Berkovic SF, and others. Familial adult myoclonic epilepsy: recognition of mild phenotypes and refinement of the 2q locus. Arch Neurol 69: 474-81, 2012. [PubMed: 22491192] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** This is the first non-Italian, non-French kindred in the entry, and the largest: 55 affected members of European descent from New Zealand and Australia. It describes a milder spectrum that the entry lacks. The most common picture was a postural hand tremor resembling essential tremor, beginning in adolescence (median 15 years, range 4 to 60), with subtle proximal myoclonus. Seizures were uncommon (15%) and came around sleep onset after severe generalized myoclonus. The paper also narrows the chromosome 2 locus to 13.3 Mb containing 99 protein-coding genes.

*clinical_features*: Crompton et al. (2012) described a 6-generation kindred of European descent, ascertained in New Zealand and Australia, with familial adult myoclonic epilepsy. All 55 affected members had fine hand tremor, which usually began in adolescence (median age 15 years; range 4-60). Proximal myoclonus was present in 44 of 55 (80%) and began later than the hand tremor (median 17 years; range 5-60). Generalized tonic-clonic seizures occurred in 8 of 55 (15%), with a median age at onset of 43.5 years (range 18-76). They were uncommon and occurred around sleep onset, after severe generalized myoclonus. Neurophysiologic testing confirmed features of cortical reflex myoclonus. Crompton et al. (2012) concluded that the most common phenotype in this family was a mild postural hand tremor resembling essential tremor, together with subtle proximal myoclonus.

*mapping*: By genetic mapping in a 6-generation kindred of European descent from New Zealand and Australia, Crompton et al. (2012) narrowed the FAME2 locus on chromosome 2 to a 13.3-Mb interval containing 99 known protein-coding genes.

## [LOW] Martí-Massó et al. (2013) — new_variants

Martí-Massó JF, Bergareche A, Makarov V, Ruiz-Martinez J, Gorostidi A, López de Munain A, Poza JJ, Striano P, Buxbaum JD, Paisán-Ruiz C. The ACMSD gene, involved in tryptophan metabolism, is mutated in a family with cortical myoclonus, epilepsy, and parkinsonism. J Mol Med (Berl) 91: 1399-406, 2013. [PubMed: 23955123] *(read: fulltext)*

**Why:** New relative to the entry: ACMSD (2q21.3, near the FCMTE2 locus) was excluded as the FAME2 gene by sequencing 2 families linked to FCMTE2. The ACMSD nonsense variant was found in a separate Spanish FCMTE family with parkinsonism that was not shown to be linked to FAME2, so it does not add variants or features to this entry.

*heterogeneity*: Martí-Massó et al. (2013) studied a Spanish family with autosomal dominant familial cortical myoclonic tremor and epilepsy (FCMTE). Using whole-exome sequencing, they identified a heterozygous nonsense variant in the ACMSD gene (c.77G>A, p.Trp26Ter), located on chromosome 2q21.3. Because ACMSD lies close to the FCMTE2 locus, they sequenced the entire ACMSD coding region and splice sites in 2 families previously linked to FCMTE2 and found no mutations. They concluded that ACMSD is not responsible for the FCMTE2 phenotype and that the FCMTE2 gene remained to be discovered.

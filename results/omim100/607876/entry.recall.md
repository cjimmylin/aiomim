### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that familial adult myoclonic epilepsy-2 (FAME2) is caused by expansion of an intronic pentanucleotide (ATTTC) repeat in the STARD7 gene (616712) on chromosome 2q11.2.

### Description

Familial adult myoclonic epilepsy-2 (FAME2) is an autosomal dominant disorder. Guerrini et al. (2001) described its main features as predominantly distal, semi-continuous rhythmic myoclonus and rare seizures, with generalized tonic-clonic seizures in all patients. Onset was between ages 12 and 50 years, which covers adolescence and adulthood. Electrophysiologic findings included high-amplitude (giant) somatosensory evoked potentials and an enhanced C reflex at rest (Guerrini et al., 2001), and Striano et al. (2005) confirmed cortical reflex myoclonus. Labauge et al. (2002) found a premyoclonus spike on jerk-locked EEG averaging and described a nonprogressive, generally benign course with infrequent seizures. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Guerrini et al. (2001) described a family with an autosomal dominant disorder that they called autosomal dominant cortical myoclonus and epilepsy (ADCME). Onset was between ages 12 and 50 years. All patients had predominantly distal, semi-continuous rhythmic myoclonus and generalized tonic-clonic seizures. Some patients also had complex partial (focal) seizures. A neurophysiologic study of the myoclonus suggested a cortical origin.

### Inheritance

Guerrini et al. (2001) reported autosomal dominant inheritance with high penetrance in the family they described.

### Mapping

In the family with ADCME, Guerrini et al. (2001) found linkage to the pericentromeric region of chromosome 2, at 2p11.1-q12.2.

De Falco et al. (2003) studied 2 Italian families with benign adult familial myoclonic epilepsy (BAFME) and found linkage to chromosome 2p11.1-q12.2. This locus was distinct from the BAFME locus previously mapped to chromosome 8q24 (FAME1). The authors suggested that BAFME in these families might be allelic with ADCME.

Madia et al. (2008) studied 5 Italian families with BAFME linked to chromosome 2p11.1-q12.2. A shared haplotype segregated with the disease in all families, indicating that the same mutation had been inherited from a common ancestor (a founder effect).

### Heterogeneity

Labauge et al. (2002) excluded linkage of a family to the familial adult myoclonic epilepsy/BAFME locus, which established that the disorder is genetically heterogeneous. De Fusco et al. (2014) noted that other families with similar forms of epilepsy had been linked to chromosomes 8q, 5p, and 3q. Corbett et al. (2019) noted that intronic pentanucleotide repeat expansions in different genes had been reported at several of these loci.

### Molecular Genetics

Corbett et al. (2019) studied families with chromosome 2-linked FAME (FAME2). In affected members they identified an expansion of an ATTTC pentanucleotide repeat in the first intron of the STARD7 gene. The ATTTC insertion was accompanied by expansion of the endogenous ATTTT (AAAAT on the forward strand) repeat. The ATTTC expansion segregated with the disorder and was not found in controls. The endogenous intron 1 repeat varied in length in the normal population but was not expanded to the same extent as in individuals with FAME. STARD7 transcript expression did not differ significantly. Corbett et al. (2019) noted that similar ATTTC expansions had been implicated at other FAME loci. They suggested that the repeat sequence alone is pathogenic, independent of any effect on the gene in which it lies.

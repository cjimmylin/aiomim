### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that familial adult myoclonic epilepsy-2 (FAME2) is caused by heterozygous expansion of an intronic pentanucleotide (ATTTC) repeat in the STARD7 gene on chromosome 2q11. ⚠︎

### Description

Familial adult myoclonic epilepsy-2 (FAME2) is an autosomal dominant disorder. Its main features are cortical tremor, a fine, rhythmic, irregular myoclonic tremor that mostly affects the hands, and infrequent seizures, which are usually generalized tonic-clonic. Onset is typically in adolescence or adulthood. Electrophysiologic studies show features of cortical reflex myoclonus. These include giant somatosensory evoked potentials, an enhanced long-loop (C) reflex, and a cortical correlate preceding the myoclonic jerks on back-averaging. The course is generally benign. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Guerrini et al. (2001) described a large Italian family with an autosomal dominant syndrome that they called autosomal dominant cortical myoclonus and epilepsy (ADCME). Affected members had cortical myoclonic tremor of adult onset and seizures. The seizures included generalized tonic-clonic seizures, and some affected members also had focal seizures. Neurophysiologic studies showed that the myoclonus originated in the cortex. ⚠︎

De Falco et al. (2003) studied Italian families with benign adult familial myoclonic epilepsy (BAFME). Affected members had distal cortical myoclonic tremor and rare generalized seizures, with electrophysiologic signs of cortical reflex myoclonus. ✗

### Inheritance

The families reported by Guerrini et al. (2001) and de Falco et al. (2003) showed autosomal dominant inheritance of FAME2. ✗

### Mapping

In the Italian family with ADCME, Guerrini et al. (2001) found linkage to the pericentromeric region of chromosome 2, at 2p11.1-q12.2.

De Falco et al. (2003) found linkage to the same region on chromosome 2p11.1-q12.2 in Italian families with BAFME. This region is distinct from the FAME1 locus on chromosome 8q. ⚠︎

Madia et al. (2008) studied 5 Italian families with BAFME linked to chromosome 2p11.1-q12.2. They found a shared extended haplotype, which suggested a founder effect, and the shared region narrowed the candidate interval. ✗

### Heterogeneity

Familial adult myoclonic epilepsy is genetically heterogeneous. Other loci have been mapped to chromosomes 8q, 5p, and 3q. At several of these loci, the cause has been shown to be intronic pentanucleotide repeat expansions in different genes.

### Molecular Genetics

Corbett et al. (2019) studied families of European ancestry with FAME linked to chromosome 2. In affected members they identified heterozygous expansion of an ATTTC pentanucleotide repeat inserted within an AAAAT repeat in intron 1 of the STARD7 gene. The expansion segregated with the disorder in the families. The expanded alleles were not found in controls, although normal-length AAAAT repeat alleles were common. The STARD7 transcript was not found to be clearly reduced. Corbett et al. (2019) noted that the same mechanism, expansion of an intronic ATTTC repeat, had been found at other FAME loci. They suggested that the mechanism is independent of the gene in which the expansion lies. ⚠︎

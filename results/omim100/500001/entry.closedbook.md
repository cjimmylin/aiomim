### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Leber optic atrophy and dystonia (LDYT) is caused by mutation in mitochondrial DNA, mainly in the MTND6 gene (516006). It has also been reported with mutations in the MTND4 gene (516003). ⚠︎

### Description

Leber optic atrophy and dystonia is a maternally inherited mitochondrial disorder. Within one family, members may have Leber hereditary optic neuropathy (LHON), a childhood-onset neurodegenerative disorder with dystonia and other movement problems, or both. Neuroimaging shows bilateral lesions of the basal ganglia, especially the putamen, like those seen in Leigh syndrome. The disorder is caused by mitochondrial DNA mutations in complex I (NADH dehydrogenase) subunit genes, most often the MTND6 gene. ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

The name Marsden syndrome comes from the report by Marsden et al. (1986) of familial dystonia with visual failure and striatal lucencies. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Novotny et al. (1986) described a large Hispanic family in which the disorder passed through the maternal line. Some members had Leber optic atrophy, some had a progressive, generalized dystonia starting in childhood, and some had both. Members with dystonia had lesions of the basal ganglia (striatum) on neuroimaging. ⚠︎

Marsden et al. (1986) reported familial dystonia with visual failure and lucencies of the striatum on CT scans. This presentation is the origin of the eponym Marsden syndrome. ⚠︎

Bruyn and Went (1964) described a Dutch family with Leber disease and other neurologic abnormalities. This family was later studied at the molecular level by De Vries et al. (1996). ✗

### Inheritance

The pedigrees, including the family of Novotny et al. (1986), showed maternal inheritance, which fits a mitochondrial DNA mutation. Jun et al. (1994) confirmed this by finding a homoplasmic or heteroplasmic mtDNA mutation that passed through the maternal line.

### Molecular Genetics

Jun et al. (1994) studied the Hispanic family first reported by Novotny et al. (1986). In affected members they found a G-to-A transition at nucleotide 14459 in the MTND6 gene, which changes alanine to valine at codon 72 (A72V). The mutation segregated through the maternal line and was linked to both LHON and dystonia. ⚠︎

Jun et al. (1996) transferred mitochondria carrying the 14459 mutation into transmitochondrial cybrids. The cybrids showed a specific defect in complex I, which tied the biochemical deficiency to the MTND6 mutation. ✗

Shoffner et al. (1995) found the 14459G-A mutation in patients with Leigh syndrome and related neurodegenerative disease. This widened the range of phenotypes linked to this mutation. ✗

De Vries et al. (1996) studied the Dutch family with LHON and neurologic abnormalities first reported by Bruyn and Went (1964). They found the 11696 mutation in the MTND4 gene together with a mutation at nucleotide 14596 in the MTND6 gene. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

The cybrid studies of Jun et al. (1996) indicated that the 14459 MTND6 mutation lowers complex I activity of the mitochondrial respiratory chain. Damage to the striatum and optic nerve is thought to result from this energy defect. ✗

# Proposed updates for OMIM 500001: LEBER OPTIC ATROPHY AND DYSTONIA

Literature from 2006 onwards that the entry does not cite: 15 papers screened, 4 read, 4 proposal(s). Each proposal is a draft for a curator to accept, edit or reject.

**Back-test as of 2006.** OMIM's curators later added 3 reference(s) from that year on; this run proposed 3 of them (recall 1.0), and 1 paper(s) OMIM does not cite.

## [HIGH] Wang et al. (2009) — new_causal_gene, new_variants, genotype_phenotype_correlation

Wang K, Takahashi Y, Gao ZL, Wang GX, Chen XW, Goto J, Lou JN, Tsuji S. Mitochondrial ND3 as the novel causative gene for Leber hereditary optic neuropathy and dystonia. Neurogenetics 10: 337-45, 2009. [PubMed: 19458970] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** Wang et al. (2009) give the first evidence that MT-ND3 causes LDYT: a homoplasmic m.10197G>A (Ala47Thr) mutation in a Chinese family with 18 affected members. Until now the entry's evidence covered only MT-ND4 and MT-ND6 variants. The paper also notes that the same mutation causes Leigh syndrome, which links the two phenotypes.

*clinical_features* ⚠︎: Wang et al. (2009) described a Chinese family with 18 patients affected by Leber hereditary optic neuropathy and dystonia, in whom vision loss and progressive generalized dystonia occurred in variable combinations.

*heterogeneity*: LDYT, an oxidative phosphorylation disorder, had previously been attributed to mutations in the mitochondrial ND6 and ND4 genes. Wang et al. (2009) found a mutation in the MT-ND3 gene in a family with LDYT, which added a further causative gene.

*molecular_genetics*: In a Chinese family with LDYT, Wang et al. (2009) used resequencing microarray analysis of the entire mitochondrial genome and identified an m.10197G-A transition in the MT-ND3 gene. The mutation caused an ala47-to-thr (A47T) substitution at a highly conserved residue and occurred on a haplogroup D4b background. Quantitative heteroplasmy analysis showed that the mutation was homoplasmic in leukocytes of all affected individuals on the maternal side. The authors stated that this was the first ND3 mutation reported to cause LDYT.

*genotype_phenotype_correlations*: Wang et al. (2009) noted that the m.10197G-A mutation in MT-ND3 had also been reported in French and Korean patients with Leigh syndrome. They suggested that the clinical spectrum of this mutation is much wider and includes both LDYT and Leigh syndrome.

*molecular_genetics*: MT-ND3, ALA47THR. In a Chinese family with 18 members affected by Leber hereditary optic neuropathy and dystonia, Wang et al. (2009) used resequencing microarray analysis of the entire mitochondrial genome and identified an m.10197G-A transition in the MT-ND3 gene, resulting in an ala47-to-thr (A47T) substitution at a highly conserved residue. The mutation was homoplasmic in leukocytes of all affected maternal relatives and occurred on a haplogroup D4b background. Wang et al. (2009) noted that the same mutation had been reported in French and Korean patients with Leigh syndrome.

## [HIGH] Spruijt et al. (2007) — new_causal_gene, new_variants, genotype_phenotype_correlation

Spruijt L, Smeets HJ, Hendrickx A, Bettink-Remeijer MW, Maat-Kievit A, Schoonderwoerd KC, Sluiter W, de Coo IF, Hintzen RQ. A MELAS-associated ND1 mutation causing leber hereditary optic neuropathy and spastic dystonia. Arch Neurol 64: 890-3, 2007. [PubMed: 17562939] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** The paper is the first in the evidence to report a patient variant in MT-ND1. The existing evidence has variants only in MT-ND4 and MT-ND6. It reports the 3697G-A mutation in a family with both LHON and spastic dystonia, with isolated complex I deficiency. It also notes that the same mutation can cause MELAS, which suggests other genetic factors affect how the disease is expressed.

*clinical_features*: Spruijt et al. (2007) described a family in which Leber hereditary optic neuropathy (LHON) and spastic dystonia occurred together. They said the family's clinical features were similar to those of previously described families with LHON and dystonia caused by an ND6 mutation.

*molecular_genetics*: Spruijt et al. (2007) studied a patient with LHON who did not carry one of the 3 primary LHON mutations. They screened the complete mitochondrial DNA and found a pathogenic 3697G-A mutation in the MT-ND1 gene. The mutation appeared to be associated with isolated complex I deficiency. The authors determined the heteroplasmy level of the mutation and concluded that it caused both LHON and spastic dystonia in the same family.

*genotype_phenotype_correlations*: Spruijt et al. (2007) noted that the 3697G-A mutation in the MT-ND1 gene, which caused LHON and spastic dystonia in the family they studied, can also cause MELAS syndrome. They suggested that other genetic factors may contribute to how the disease is expressed clinically.

*molecular_genetics*: MT-ND1, 3697G-A. In a family in which LHON and spastic dystonia occurred together, Spruijt et al. (2007) screened the complete mitochondrial DNA of a patient with LHON who did not carry one of the 3 primary LHON mutations. They found a 3697G-A mutation in the MT-ND1 gene. The mutation appeared to be associated with isolated complex I deficiency, and the authors concluded that it caused both LHON and spastic dystonia in the family. They noted that the same mutation can also cause MELAS syndrome.

## [MEDIUM] McFarland et al. (2007) — new_variants, phenotype_expansion

McFarland R, Chinnery PF, Blakely EL, Schaefer AM, Morris AA, Foster SM, Tuppen HA, Ramesh V, Dorman PJ, Turnbull DM, and others. Homoplasmy, heteroplasmy, and mitochondrial dystonia. Neurology 69: 911-6, 2007. [PubMed: 17724295] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** The paper reports 2 new families in which dystonia was the main manifestation and epilepsy was also present. One family carried a heteroplasmic m.11778G>A MT-ND4 mutation at very high levels; this variant is not in the variant table. The other carried a new homoplasmic mutation in mitochondrial tRNA(Cys) (MTTC), a gene not currently linked to the entry. Epilepsy is not among the clinical features already listed.

*clinical_features*: McFarland et al. (2007) reported 6 patients from 2 families in whom dystonia was the principal clinical manifestation. A mitochondrial cause was considered because the dystonia occurred with other, less prominent features such as epilepsy. The authors advocated considering mitochondrial disease in patients with familial, progressive dystonia, particularly when additional neurologic features such as epilepsy are present.

*molecular_genetics*: In 1 of 2 families in which dystonia was the principal manifestation, McFarland et al. (2007) identified the primary LHON mutation m.11778G-A in the MT-ND4 gene by sequencing skeletal muscle mtDNA. They quantified the mutation by hot last cycle PCR-RFLP or by primer extension assay. In these patients the mutation was heteroplasmic but present at very high levels. The authors noted that this mutation is commonly observed to be homoplasmic.

*heterogeneity*: In the second family with mitochondrial dystonia, McFarland et al. (2007) identified a novel homoplasmic mutation in the mitochondrial tRNA(Cys) gene (MTTC), with no wild-type mtDNA detectable. They assessed its functional effect by measuring steady-state levels of mitochondrial tRNA.

## [LOW] Watanabe et al. (2006) — new_variants, population_data

Watanabe M, Mita S, Takita T, Goto Y, Uchino M, Imamura S. Leber's hereditary optic neuropathy with dystonia in a Japanese family. J Neurol Sci 243: 31-4, 2006. [PubMed: 16380132] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** Watanabe et al. (2006) found the m.14459G>A variant in MT-ND6, which the entry already lists, in the first Japanese family with LHON and dystonia shown to carry an mtDNA mutation. The variant was absent from 200 Japanese controls. The paper also describes the course in this family: generalized dystonia beginning in childhood and optic neuropathy beginning later in life. These findings extend what is known about the variant's ethnic distribution but do not change the entry's core facts.

*clinical_features*: Watanabe et al. (2006) described a Japanese family in which affected members had generalized dystonia, attributed to striatal degeneration, beginning in childhood, and optic neuropathy beginning later in life.

*molecular_genetics*: In the proband from a Japanese family with LHON and dystonia, Watanabe et al. (2006) sequenced the entire mtDNA and identified 42 nucleotide changes compared with the revised Cambridge Reference Sequence. These changes included the G14459A mutation in the MT-ND6 gene, which was not present in 200 healthy Japanese controls. The authors noted that this mutation had previously been suggested as the cause of LHON and/or dystonia in Hispanic, African American, and Caucasian families. They reported this as the first Japanese family with LHON and dystonia in which an mtDNA mutation had been found. The proband also carried an A3203G change, which was not present in 200 healthy Japanese controls; its significance remained unknown.

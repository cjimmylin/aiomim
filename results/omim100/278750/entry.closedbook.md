### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that the variant type of xeroderma pigmentosum (XPV) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding DNA polymerase eta (POLH; 603968) on chromosome 6p21. ✗

### Description

Xeroderma pigmentosum variant type (XPV) is an autosomal recessive disorder of sun sensitivity. Patients develop pigmentary changes in sun-exposed skin and have a much higher risk of cutaneous malignancies. Clinically it resembles classical xeroderma pigmentosum, but onset is usually milder and later, and neurologic abnormalities are generally absent. Unlike cells from the classical XP complementation groups, XPV cells have normal nucleotide excision repair. Their defect is in replicating past UV-induced DNA damage (translesion synthesis), which is caused by loss of DNA polymerase eta. ✗

### Clinical Features

Affected individuals have sun sensitivity with freckle-like pigmentation and other actinic changes of exposed skin. They develop basal cell carcinomas, squamous cell carcinomas and melanomas of sun-exposed sites. Skin cancers often appear later than in classical XP, and some patients are not diagnosed until adulthood. Neurologic degeneration, a feature of some classical XP groups, is not a feature of XPV. ✗

### Biochemical Features

Cleaver (1972) described patients with clinical xeroderma pigmentosum whose cells showed normal levels of UV-induced unscheduled DNA synthesis. This set them apart from the excision-deficient forms of XP. ⚠︎

Lehmann et al. (1975) found that XP variant cells were defective in postreplication repair. After UV irradiation, the cells were abnormally slow to convert newly synthesized low-molecular-weight DNA into high-molecular-weight DNA. Caffeine made this defect worse. Increased sensitivity of UV-irradiated variant cells to caffeine was later used as a laboratory diagnostic feature. ✗

### Inheritance

XPV is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. Affected individuals carry biallelic mutations in the POLH gene. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Masutani et al. (1999) purified from HeLa cells a DNA polymerase that corrected the replication defect of XPV cell extracts and could bypass a thymine dimer accurately. They cloned the corresponding gene, a human homolog of yeast RAD30, which encodes DNA polymerase eta. They found truncating mutations in this gene in cells from XPV patients. ⚠︎

Johnson et al. (1999) independently identified mutations in the human RAD30 homolog (POLH) in XPV cell lines.

Broughton et al. (2002) analyzed POLH in a larger series of XPV patients and found a variety of mutations. Most were nonsense, frameshift or splicing mutations predicted to give truncated or absent protein. A few were missense mutations within the polymerase domain. ✗

### Pathogenesis

DNA polymerase eta copies past cyclobutane pyrimidine dimers relatively accurately, inserting adenines opposite the thymines of a thymine-thymine dimer. When it is absent, other more error-prone polymerases bypass the lesion instead. This is thought to cause the increased UV mutagenesis and skin cancer seen in XPV. ✗

Zeng et al. (2001) found an altered spectrum of somatic hypermutation in immunoglobulin genes from XPV patients, with fewer mutations at A/T base pairs. This suggested that polymerase eta takes part in somatic hypermutation. ✗

### Animal Model

Mice deficient for Polh developed skin tumors after UV irradiation, which modeled the cancer susceptibility of XPV (Lin et al., 2006; Ohkumo et al., 2006). ✗

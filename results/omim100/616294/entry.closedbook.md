A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Cole-Carpenter syndrome-2 (CLCRP2) is caused by compound heterozygous mutation in the SEC24D gene on chromosome 4q26.

### Description

Cole-Carpenter syndrome-2 is an autosomal recessive disorder that resembles osteogenesis imperfecta. Its main features are bone fragility with multiple fractures, craniofacial malformations, and severely impaired ossification of the skull with large, open fontanels. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Garbes et al. (2015) described a boy of German origin whose features resembled Cole-Carpenter syndrome. He had craniofacial malformations, a large skull ossification defect with open fontanels, and multiple fractures, consistent with a syndromic form of osteogenesis imperfecta. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The compound heterozygous mutations reported by Garbes et al. (2015) indicate that CLCRP2 is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Cole-Carpenter syndrome is genetically heterogeneous. Cole-Carpenter syndrome-1 is caused by heterozygous mutation in the P4HB gene. ✗

### Molecular Genetics

Garbes et al. (2015) performed exome sequencing on the German boy with a Cole-Carpenter-like phenotype and found compound heterozygous mutations in the SEC24D gene. SEC24D encodes a component of the COPII machinery, which carries cargo from the endoplasmic reticulum to the Golgi. Patient fibroblasts showed procollagen retained in the endoplasmic reticulum, which was dilated, so procollagen secretion was impaired. Garbes et al. (2015) also reported SEC24D mutations in a further family with a similar osteogenesis imperfecta-like phenotype. ⚠︎

### Animal Model

Sarmah et al. (2010) found that the zebrafish 'bulldog' mutant, which has craniofacial cartilage defects, is caused by mutation in sec24d. In this mutant, chondrocytes fail to secrete collagen, which is retained in the endoplasmic reticulum. ✗

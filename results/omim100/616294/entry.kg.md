### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Cole-Carpenter syndrome-2 (CLCRP2) is caused by compound heterozygous mutation in the SEC24D gene (607186) on chromosome 4q26.

### Description

Cole-Carpenter syndrome-2 is a syndromic form of osteogenesis imperfecta. Its main features are bone fragility with multiple fractures that may begin before birth, severely disturbed ossification of the skull, and craniofacial malformations. These include macrocephaly, frontal bossing, midface hypoplasia, micrognathia, and ocular proptosis (Garbes et al., 2015; Takeyari et al., 2018). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Garbes et al. (2015) reported 3 affected individuals from 2 families who had a similar skeletal phenotype. One was a 7-year-old boy whose phenotype most closely resembled Cole-Carpenter syndrome. The other 2 were fetuses who were first suspected to have a severe type of osteogenesis imperfecta. All 3 had severely disturbed ossification of the skull and multiple fractures beginning before birth. The boy also had short stature and craniofacial malformations, including macrocephaly, midface hypoplasia, micrognathia, frontal bossing, and downslanting palpebral fissures.

Zhang et al. (2017) described a 23-year-old Chinese man (family 1) with recurrent fractures, dentinogenesis imperfecta, an open anterior fontanel, and facial dysmorphism. CT 3-dimensional imaging of the cranium showed skull deformities with a broad ossification defect in the frontoapical area, a widened sagittal suture, and wormian bones.

Zhang et al. (2017) also described a 7-year-old Chinese boy (family 2) with recurrent fractures, dentinogenesis imperfecta, and an open anterior fontanel. He had no obvious facial dysmorphism.

Takeyari et al. (2018) described a 15-year-old Japanese boy with Cole-Carpenter syndrome. He had short-trunk short stature and craniofacial abnormalities, including ocular proptosis, marked frontal bossing, midface hypoplasia, and micrognathia. He also had low bone mineral density and basilar impression.

### Inheritance

Cole-Carpenter syndrome-2 is transmitted in an autosomal recessive pattern. Garbes et al. (2015), Zhang et al. (2017), and Takeyari et al. (2018) each found affected individuals to be compound heterozygous for SEC24D mutations. Moosa et al. (2016) also reported that SEC24D mutations cause autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Garbes et al. (2015) performed whole-exome sequencing in 3 affected individuals from 2 families and found 3 mutant SEC24D alleles. Each affected individual was compound heterozygous for 2 of them. SEC24D encodes a component of the COPII complex, which is involved in protein export from the endoplasmic reticulum. All 3 affected individuals carried a ser1015-to-phe (S1015F) missense mutation in a cargo-binding pocket. This mutation lay within a 14-Mb founder haplotype shared by all 3 individuals. The other 2 alleles were a truncating gln205-to-ter (Q205X) mutation and a gln978-to-pro (Q978P) missense mutation in the gelsolin-like domain. Each was found in the compound heterozygous state together with another SEC24D mutation. ⚠︎

Moosa et al. (2016) reported that mutations in the SEC24D gene cause autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta.

Zhang et al. (2017) performed whole-exome sequencing in the probands of 2 Chinese families. The proband of family 1 carried 2 novel compound heterozygous missense mutations in the SEC24D gene, cys908-to-tyr (C908Y) and ser948-to-pro (S948P). The proband of family 2 carried 2 novel compound heterozygous missense mutations, arg313-to-his (R313H) and pro292-to-leu (P292L).

Takeyari et al. (2018) performed whole-exome sequencing in a Japanese boy with Cole-Carpenter syndrome type 2. They identified compound heterozygous variants in the SEC24D gene, arg484-to-ter (R484X) and arg313-to-his (R313H).

### Pathogenesis

Garbes et al. (2015) studied skin fibroblasts from the affected boy using electron and immunofluorescence microscopy. Export of procollagen from the endoplasmic reticulum was inefficient, and the ER tubules were dilated. This reproduced the cellular phenotype seen in cranio-lenticulo-sutural dysplasia caused by SEC23A mutations. The authors noted that the cellular and morphologic phenotypes matched those described in 2 Sec24d-deficient fish mutants, vbi in medaka and bulldog in zebrafish.

### Diagnosis

Garbes et al. (2015) noted that cranio-lenticulo-sutural dysplasia, which is caused by SEC23A mutations, has a largely overlapping craniofacial phenotype. However, unlike the SEC24D disorder, it does not include generalized bone fragility. In addition, the original family described with cranio-lenticulo-sutural dysplasia had cataracts.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Cole-Carpenter syndrome-2 (CLCRP2) is caused by compound heterozygous mutation in the SEC24D gene.

### Description

Cole-Carpenter syndrome-2 is a rare skeletal dysplasia with bone fragility, like osteogenesis imperfecta (OI). Features include disturbed ossification of the skull, multiple fractures that may begin before birth, short stature, and craniofacial malformations such as macrocephaly, frontal bossing, midface hypoplasia and micrognathia (Garbes et al., 2015; Takeyari et al., 2018).

### Clinical Features

Garbes et al. (2015) reported 3 affected individuals from 2 families with a similar skeletal phenotype: a 7-year-old boy whose phenotype most closely resembled Cole-Carpenter syndrome, and 2 fetuses first suspected of having a severe type of osteogenesis imperfecta. All 3 had severely disturbed ossification of the skull and multiple fractures beginning before birth. The boy had short stature and craniofacial malformations, including macrocephaly, midface hypoplasia, micrognathia, frontal bossing and down-slanting palpebral fissures. Garbes et al. (2015) noted that the craniofacial phenotype overlapped largely with that of cranio-lenticulo-sutural dysplasia (CLSD), which is caused by SEC23A mutations. However, CLSD does not feature generalized bone fragility, and the original CLSD family had cataracts.

Zhang et al. (2017) reported 2 probands from Chinese families who had been diagnosed with osteogenesis imperfecta. The proband in family 1, a 23-year-old man, had recurrent fractures, dentinogenesis imperfecta, an anterior fontanel that had not closed, and facial dysmorphism. Three-dimensional CT imaging of his cranium showed skull deformities with a broad ossification defect in the frontoapical area, a widened sagittal suture and wormian bones. The proband in family 2, a 7-year-old boy, also had recurrent fractures, dentinogenesis imperfecta and an anterior fontanel that had not closed, but no obvious facial dysmorphism.

Takeyari et al. (2018) described a 15-year-old Japanese boy with Cole-Carpenter syndrome. He had short-trunk short stature and craniofacial abnormalities, including ocular proptosis, marked frontal bossing, midface hypoplasia and micrognathia. He also had low bone mineral density and basilar impression.

### Inheritance

The compound heterozygous SEC24D mutations in affected individuals reported by Garbes et al. (2015), Zhang et al. (2017) and Takeyari et al. (2018) were consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Zhang et al. (2017) described the disorder as a recessively inherited form of osteogenesis imperfecta. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Garbes et al. (2015) used whole-exome sequencing to study 3 affected individuals from 2 families and identified 3 mutant alleles in SEC24D. SEC24D encodes a component of the COPII complex, which is involved in protein export from the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). The alleles were a truncating mutation, gln205-to-ter (Q205X), and 2 missense mutations: ser1015-to-phe (S1015F), located in a cargo-binding pocket, and gln978-to-pro (Q978P), located in the gelsolin-like domain. Each affected individual was compound heterozygous for 2 of these alleles. The S1015F allele lay on a 14-Mb founder haplotype shared by all 3. Electron and immunofluorescence microscopy of the 7-year-old boy's skin fibroblasts showed inefficient ER export of procollagen and dilated ER tubules. This reproduced the cellular phenotype of individuals with CLSD.

Moosa et al. (2016) reported mutations in SEC24D causing autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta. Only the title of this report was available.

Zhang et al. (2017) performed whole-exome sequencing in 2 Chinese probands with osteogenesis imperfecta and found compound heterozygous missense mutations in SEC24D in each. The proband in family 1 carried cys908-to-tyr (C908Y) and ser948-to-pro (S948P). The proband in family 2 carried arg313-to-his (R313H) and pro292-to-leu (P292L).

In a 15-year-old Japanese boy with Cole-Carpenter syndrome, Takeyari et al. (2018) used whole-exome sequencing to identify compound heterozygous variants in SEC24D: arg484-to-ter (R484X) and arg313-to-his (R313H).

### Animal Model

Garbes et al. (2015) noted that the cellular and morphologic phenotypes in their patients matched those described for Sec24d-deficient fish mutants: vbi in medaka and bulldog in zebrafish.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Cole-Carpenter syndrome-2 (CLCRP2) is caused by compound heterozygous mutation in the SEC24D gene (607186) on chromosome 4q26. ⚠︎

### Description

Cole-Carpenter syndrome-2 is an autosomal recessive disorder (Zhang et al., 2017) that resembles osteogenesis imperfecta. Its main features are bone fragility with multiple fractures, craniofacial malformations, and severely disturbed ossification of the skull (Garbes et al., 2015). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Garbes et al. (2015) described a 7-year-old boy whose phenotype most closely resembled Cole-Carpenter syndrome. He had craniofacial malformations, severely disturbed ossification of the skull, and multiple fractures, consistent with a syndromic form of osteogenesis imperfecta.

### Inheritance

Zhang et al. (2017) described the SEC24D-related disorder as autosomal recessive. Garbes et al. (2015) reported compound heterozygous SEC24D mutations in affected individuals.

### Molecular Genetics

By exome sequencing in a boy with a Cole-Carpenter-like phenotype, Garbes et al. (2015) found compound heterozygous mutations in the SEC24D gene. SEC24D encodes a component of the COPII machinery, which is involved in protein export from the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). The patient's fibroblasts showed procollagen retained in the ER, which was dilated, so procollagen secretion was impaired. Garbes et al. (2015) also reported SEC24D mutations in a further family with a similar osteogenesis imperfecta-like phenotype. ⚠︎

### Animal Model

Garbes et al. (2015) referred to the zebrafish mutant 'bulldog' as Sec24d-deficient.

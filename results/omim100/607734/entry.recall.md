### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1F (CMT1F) is caused by heterozygous or homozygous mutation in the gene encoding the neurofilament light chain polypeptide (NEFL; 162280) on chromosome 8p21. ⚠︎

### Description

Jordanova et al. (2003) reported that mutations in the NEFL gene cause Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease with early onset and an often severe clinical phenotype, and with moderately to severely slowed nerve conduction velocities. In affected children described by Yum et al. (2009), there was slowly progressive atrophy and weakness of distal muscles of the feet and hands, bilateral pes cavus, and sensory impairment in a glove-and-stocking pattern, and nerve conduction velocities were markedly slowed into the 'demyelinating range.' Jordanova et al. (2003) noted that NEFL mutations had also been reported as a cause of autosomal dominant Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2E (CMT2E), linked to chromosome 8p21.

### Clinical Features

Jordanova et al. (2003) screened 323 patients with CMT or related peripheral neuropathies for NEFL mutations. Patients with NEFL mutations had early-onset and often severe disease. Electrophysiologic examination showed moderately to severely slowed nerve conduction velocities.

### Inheritance

Jordanova et al. (2003) found a de novo NEFL mutation in a patient with CMT and suggested that NEFL mutations be considered in patients with sporadic or dominantly inherited CMT. In the cohort studied by Abe et al. (2009), heterozygous missense mutations were found in 5 unrelated patients and a homozygous nonsense mutation in 1 other patient; Abe et al. (2009) concluded that nonsense NEFL mutations probably cause a recessive phenotype. Yum et al. (2009) found a homozygous nonsense NEFL mutation in a family with autosomal recessive CMT.

### Heterogeneity

Jordanova et al. (2003) noted that mutations in the NEFL gene had been reported as a cause of autosomal dominant Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2E (CMT2E) linked to chromosome 8p21.

### Molecular Genetics

In patients with CMT or related peripheral neuropathies, Jordanova et al. (2003) identified 6 disease-associated missense mutations and a 3-bp in-frame deletion in the NEFL gene. Patients had early onset and slowed nerve conduction velocities. One patient had a de novo missense mutation in NEFL.

Abe et al. (2009) found heterozygous missense mutations in the NEFL gene in 5 unrelated patients and a homozygous nonsense mutation in 1 other patient. This was the first report of a homozygous nonsense mutation in NEFL.

In affected children from a family with autosomal recessive CMT, Yum et al. (2009) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in the NEFL gene. The resulting neuropathy was severe and of early onset.

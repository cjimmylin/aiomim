### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1F (CMT1F) is caused by heterozygous or homozygous mutation in the gene encoding the neurofilament light chain polypeptide (NEFL; 162280) on chromosome 8p21. ⚠︎

### Description

Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1F (CMT1F) is a hereditary motor and sensory neuropathy caused by mutation in the NEFL gene. It usually starts early and can be severe. Patients have distal muscle weakness and atrophy, distal sensory loss, and foot deformities. Nerve conduction velocities are reduced, in some patients into the demyelinating range. Mutations in NEFL also cause an axonal form of CMT, CMT2E, and the two forms overlap. ✗

### Clinical Features

Jordanova et al. (2003) screened a large series of patients with CMT for NEFL mutations. Patients with NEFL mutations had early-onset, often severe disease. Motor nerve conduction velocities were reduced, in some cases to values in the demyelinating range. This finding supported classifying some NEFL-related neuropathy as demyelinating CMT1. ✗

### Inheritance

Most reported NEFL mutations causing CMT1F are heterozygous and show autosomal dominant inheritance or arise de novo. Autosomal recessive inheritance has been reported in families with homozygous loss-of-function (nonsense) mutations in NEFL. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

NEFL mutations also cause axonal Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2E (CMT2E). Mersiyanova et al. (2000) first reported this association in a Russian family. ✗

### Molecular Genetics

Jordanova et al. (2003) identified heterozygous NEFL mutations in patients with early-onset CMT and reduced nerve conduction velocities. Some of these mutations arose de novo. ⚠︎

Homozygous nonsense mutations in NEFL were later identified in patients with an autosomal recessive, early-onset, severe form of NEFL-related CMT.

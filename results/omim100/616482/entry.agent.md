### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that severe achondroplasia with developmental delay and acanthosis nigricans (SADDAN) is caused by mutation in the gene encoding fibroblast growth factor receptor 3 (FGFR3).

### Description

Severe achondroplasia with developmental delay and acanthosis nigricans (SADDAN) is a skeletal dysplasia in which disturbance of endochondral bone growth approaches and overlaps that seen in thanatophoric dysplasia type I, but which is most often compatible with survival into adulthood. Surviving patients have progressive acanthosis nigricans, central nervous system structural anomalies, seizures, and severe developmental delay; cloverleaf skull and craniosynostosis are not associated features (Bellus et al., 1999).

### Nomenclature

Tavormina et al. (1999) named the phenotype caused by the FGFR3 lys650-to-met mutation 'severe achondroplasia with developmental delay and acanthosis nigricans' (SADDAN) because it differed significantly from the phenotypes of other known FGFR3 mutations.

### Clinical Features

Tavormina et al. (1999) described 4 unrelated individuals with a skeletal dysplasia approaching the severity of thanatophoric dysplasia type I. Three of the 4 developed extensive areas of acanthosis nigricans beginning in early childhood, had severe neurologic impairment, and survived past infancy without prolonged life-support measures.

Bellus et al. (1999) provided a more detailed clinical account of the 4 unrelated individuals reported by Tavormina et al. (1999). Severe disturbances of endochondral bone growth approached and overlapped those of thanatophoric dysplasia type I, but the disorder was most often compatible with survival into adulthood. Some patients had unusual bone deformities, such as femoral bowing with reverse (posterior apex) tibial and fibular bowing and 'ram's horn' bowing of the clavicle. Surviving patients had progressive acanthosis nigricans, central nervous system structural anomalies, seizures, and severe developmental delay. Cloverleaf skull and craniosynostosis were not observed. ⚠︎

Zankl et al. (2008) described the prenatal and postnatal features of a patient with SADDAN, which they noted was one of only 6 cases with molecular analysis reported in the literature. The case confirmed the severe morbidity and added to reports of early mortality associated with SADDAN. They also reviewed the clinical and radiologic characteristics of all reported cases.

### Molecular Genetics

In 4 unrelated individuals with SADDAN, Tavormina et al. (1999) identified a missense mutation in the FGFR3 gene (A1949T), resulting in a lys650-to-met (K650M) substitution at a highly conserved codon in the kinase domain activation loop. The mutation occurred at the nucleotide adjacent to the A1948G (lys650-to-glu; K650E) mutation that causes thanatophoric dysplasia type II. In transient transfection studies, the K650M mutation caused a dramatic increase in constitutive receptor kinase activity, approximately 3 times greater than that observed with the K650E mutation.

In a patient with prenatally and postnatally recognized features of SADDAN, Zankl et al. (2008) confirmed the clinical diagnosis by detecting the FGFR3 K650M mutation.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Bellus et al. (1999) noted that although the SADDAN mutation (K650M) lies within the same FGFR3 codon as the thanatophoric dysplasia type II mutation (K650E) and has a similar effect on constitutive activation of the FGFR3 tyrosine kinase, it is not associated with cloverleaf skull or craniosynostosis. Iwata et al. (2001) pointed out that thanatophoric dysplasia type II is lethal at birth whereas most SADDAN patients survive the perinatal period, even though FGFR3 with the SADDAN mutation is more activated in vitro than FGFR3 with the TDII mutation.

### Animal Model

Iwata et al. (2001) introduced the K644M point mutation, corresponding to human K650M, into the mouse Fgfr3 gene. Heterozygous mutant mice showed a phenotype similar to human SADDAN; the majority survived the perinatal period, suggesting that the survival of SADDAN patients is attributable to the K650M mutation itself. Long bone abnormalities in the SADDAN mice were milder than in the thanatophoric dysplasia type II model, and overgrowth of cartilaginous tissue was observed in the rib cartilage, trachea, and nasal septum. At low concentration, FGF ligand differentially activated MAP kinase in primary chondrocyte cultures from wildtype and SADDAN mice.

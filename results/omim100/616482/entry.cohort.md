### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that severe achondroplasia with developmental delay and acanthosis nigricans (SADDAN) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the FGFR3 gene (134934) on chromosome 4p16.3. ⚠︎

### Description

Severe achondroplasia with developmental delay and acanthosis nigricans (SADDAN) is a skeletal dysplasia. It is caused by the FGFR3 Lys650Met mutation. The skeletal disturbance approaches the severity seen in thanatophoric dysplasia type I, but there is no cloverleaf skull or craniosynostosis. Patients who survive develop progressive acanthosis nigricans, central nervous system structural anomalies, seizures, and severe developmental delay.

### Nomenclature

Tavormina et al. (1999) gave the name 'severe achondroplasia with developmental delay and acanthosis nigricans' (SADDAN) to the phenotype caused by the FGFR3 Lys650Met mutation. They chose a separate name because this phenotype differs significantly from those of other known FGFR3 mutations.

### Clinical Features

Tavormina et al. (1999) reported 4 unrelated individuals with a skeletal dysplasia approaching the severity of thanatophoric dysplasia type I. Three of the 4 developed extensive areas of acanthosis nigricans beginning in early childhood. These 3 also had severe neurologic impairment and survived past infancy without prolonged life-support measures.

Bellus et al. (1999) analyzed the phenotype in the 4 unrelated individuals with the FGFR3 Lys650Met mutation previously reported by Tavormina et al. (1999). The mutation caused severe disturbances of endochondral bone growth that approached and overlapped those of thanatophoric dysplasia type I. However, it was most often compatible with survival into adulthood. Some patients had other unusual bone deformities: femoral bowing with reverse (posterior apex) tibial and fibular bowing, and 'ram's horn' bowing of the clavicle. Cloverleaf skull and craniosynostosis were not present. Besides the skeletal dysplasia, surviving patients had progressive acanthosis nigricans, central nervous system structural anomalies, seizures, and severe developmental delay. ⚠︎

Zankl et al. (2008) described the prenatal and postnatal features of a patient with SADDAN who had severe achondroplasia, developmental delay, and acanthosis nigricans. They reviewed the clinical and radiologic features of all reported cases of SADDAN. They noted that their patient was one of only 6 reported cases with molecular analysis. In their view, the case confirmed the severe morbidity of SADDAN and added to the reports of early mortality associated with the disorder. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In 4 unrelated individuals with SADDAN, Tavormina et al. (1999) identified a missense mutation in the FGFR3 gene: an A1949T change resulting in a lys650-to-met (K650M) substitution. The mutation lies at the nucleotide next to the thanatophoric dysplasia type II (TD2) mutation (A1948G, Lys650Glu). It produces a different amino acid substitution at a highly conserved codon in the activation loop of the kinase domain. In transient transfection studies, the Lys650Met mutation caused a dramatic increase in constitutive receptor kinase activity. This increase was about 3 times greater than that seen with the TD2 Lys650Glu mutation.

Bellus et al. (1999) described the phenotype of the 4 unrelated patients carrying the FGFR3 Lys650Met mutation in the tyrosine kinase domain. ⚠︎

In a patient with SADDAN, Zankl et al. (2008) confirmed the clinical diagnosis by mutation analysis, which detected the FGFR3 Lys650Met mutation.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Bellus et al. (1999) noted that Lys650Met lies in the same FGFR3 codon as the thanatophoric dysplasia type II mutation Lys650Glu. The 2 mutations have a similar effect on constitutive activation of the FGFR3 tyrosine kinase. Unlike Lys650Glu, however, Lys650Met is not associated with cloverleaf skull or craniosynostosis.

### Pathogenesis

Iwata et al. (2001) noted a paradox. FGFR3 with the SADDAN K650M mutation is more highly activated in vitro than FGFR3 with the TDII K650E mutation. Yet TDII is lethal at birth, whereas most SADDAN patients survive the perinatal period. In primary chondrocyte cultures from wildtype and SADDAN mice, a low concentration of FGF ligand activated MAP kinase differently.

### Animal Model

Iwata et al. (2001) introduced the K644M point mutation, which corresponds to human FGFR3 K650M, into the mouse Fgfr3 gene. Heterozygous mutant mice had a phenotype similar to human SADDAN, and most of them survived the perinatal period. The authors concluded that the survival of SADDAN patients is indeed attributable to the K650M mutation. Long bone abnormalities in these mice were milder than in the TDII mouse model. Cartilaginous tissues were overgrown in the rib cartilage, trachea, and nasal septum.

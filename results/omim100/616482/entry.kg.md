### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that severe achondroplasia with developmental delay and acanthosis nigricans (SADDAN) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the FGFR3 gene (134934) on chromosome 4p16.3. ⚠︎

### Description

SADDAN is a severe skeletal dysplasia caused by the FGFR3 Lys650Met mutation. Its disturbance of endochondral bone growth approaches and overlaps that of thanatophoric dysplasia type I, but most patients survive into adulthood. Surviving patients have progressive acanthosis nigricans, structural anomalies of the central nervous system, seizures, and severe developmental delay (Tavormina et al., 1999; Bellus et al., 1999).

### Nomenclature

Tavormina et al. (1999) named the phenotype caused by the FGFR3 Lys650Met mutation 'severe achondroplasia with developmental delay and acanthosis nigricans' (SADDAN). They chose a separate name because the phenotype differs significantly from those of other known FGFR3 mutations.

### Clinical Features

Tavormina et al. (1999) described 4 unrelated individuals with a skeletal dysplasia nearly as severe as thanatophoric dysplasia type I. Three of the 4 developed extensive acanthosis nigricans starting in early childhood, had severe neurologic impairment, and survived past infancy without prolonged life support.

Bellus et al. (1999) gave a detailed clinical account of the SADDAN phenotype. The FGFR3 Lys650Met mutation caused severe disturbances of endochondral bone growth that approached and overlapped those seen in thanatophoric dysplasia type I, but was most often compatible with survival into adulthood. Some patients had unusual bone deformities, such as femoral bowing with reverse (posterior apex) tibial and fibular bowing, and 'ram's horn' bowing of the clavicle. Surviving patients had progressive acanthosis nigricans, structural anomalies of the central nervous system, seizures, and severe developmental delay.

Zankl et al. (2008) described the prenatal and postnatal features of a patient with SADDAN. The case confirmed the severe morbidity of the disorder and added to the reports of early mortality associated with it. The authors also reviewed the clinical and radiologic characteristics of all reported cases of SADDAN.

### Molecular Genetics

In 4 unrelated individuals with SADDAN, Tavormina et al. (1999) identified the same missense mutation in the FGFR3 gene, A1949T, resulting in a lys650-to-met (K650M) substitution. The change lies at the nucleotide next to the thanatophoric dysplasia type II mutation (A1948G; Lys650Glu) and produces a different amino acid substitution at a highly conserved codon in the activation loop of the kinase domain. In transient transfection studies, the Lys650Met mutation caused a dramatic increase in constitutive receptor kinase activity, about 3 times higher than that of the thanatophoric dysplasia type II mutation Lys650Glu. Bellus et al. (1999) referred to this identification of the Lys650Met mutation in the tyrosine kinase domain of FGFR3 in the 4 unrelated individuals.

In a patient with SADDAN, Zankl et al. (2008) confirmed the clinical diagnosis by identifying the FGFR3 Lys650Met mutation on mutation analysis. They noted that this was one of only 6 cases of SADDAN with molecular analysis reported in the literature.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Bellus et al. (1999) noted that although the Lys650Met mutation lies in the same FGFR3 codon as the thanatophoric dysplasia type II mutation (Lys650Glu) and has a similar effect on constitutive activation of the FGFR3 tyrosine kinase, it is not associated with cloverleaf skull or craniosynostosis.

### Pathogenesis

Iwata et al. (2001) noted that FGFR3 carrying the SADDAN mutation (K650M) is more activated in vitro than FGFR3 carrying the thanatophoric dysplasia type II (TDII) mutation (K650E), yet TDII is lethal at birth while most SADDAN patients survive. In primary chondrocyte cultures, a low concentration of FGF ligand activated MAP kinase differently in cells from wildtype mice and from SADDAN mice.

### Animal Model

To determine whether the FGFR3 K650M mutation causes the SADDAN phenotype, Iwata et al. (2001) introduced the corresponding point mutation (K644M) into the mouse Fgfr3 gene. Heterozygous mutant mice had a phenotype similar to human SADDAN, and most survived the perinatal period, leading the authors to suggest that the survival of SADDAN patients is indeed due to the K650M mutation. Long bone abnormalities in the SADDAN mice were milder than in the TDII mouse model, and the rib cartilage, trachea, and nasal septum showed overgrowth of cartilaginous tissue.

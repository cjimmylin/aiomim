### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that severe achondroplasia with developmental delay and acanthosis nigricans (SADDAN) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the FGFR3 gene (134934) on chromosome 4p16.3. ⚠︎

### Description

SADDAN is a rare, severe skeletal dysplasia caused by an activating mutation in FGFR3. Its main features are severe short-limbed short stature with bowing of the long bones, marked developmental delay, and acanthosis nigricans that appears in childhood. The skeletal findings resemble thanatophoric dysplasia, but some affected individuals have survived past infancy. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Tavormina et al. (1999) reported unrelated individuals with a skeletal dysplasia that they named SADDAN. Patients had severe short stature with marked bowing of the femur and tibia, profound developmental delay, and acanthosis nigricans that developed in early childhood. Radiographic features were similar to those of thanatophoric dysplasia type I, but some patients survived beyond infancy, in some cases without long-term ventilatory support. Some patients also had seizures, hydrocephalus, and structural central nervous system abnormalities. ✗

### Inheritance

The mutations reported in SADDAN arose de novo and were present in the heterozygous state, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance (Tavormina et al., 1999). ✗

### Molecular Genetics

In unrelated patients with SADDAN, Tavormina et al. (1999) identified a heterozygous c.1949A-T transversion in the FGFR3 gene. The change caused a lys650-to-met (K650M) substitution in the activation loop of the tyrosine kinase domain. In transfection studies, the K650M receptor showed strong ligand-independent constitutive kinase activity. ⚠︎

Bellus et al. (2000) compared mutations at FGFR3 codon lys650. The K650M mutation found in SADDAN activated the receptor kinase much more strongly than the K650E mutation found in thanatophoric dysplasia type II, which in turn was more activating than the milder K650N and K650Q substitutions found in hypochondroplasia. The authors concluded that the degree of receptor activation correlated with the severity of the skeletal phenotype. Bellus et al. (2000) also noted that the K650M mutation had been found in some patients diagnosed with thanatophoric dysplasia type I. ✗

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Bellus et al. (2000) found that different amino acid substitutions at FGFR3 lys650 caused different levels of constitutive kinase activation. These levels corresponded to a phenotypic spectrum, from hypochondroplasia (K650N/Q) to thanatophoric dysplasia type II (K650E) and SADDAN (K650M). ✗

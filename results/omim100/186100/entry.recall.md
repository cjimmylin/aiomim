## SYNDACTYLY, TYPE III; SDTY3

*Alternative titles; symbols*

SYNDACTYLY OF FINGERS IV AND V  
RING AND LITTLE FINGER SYNDACTYLY

### TEXT

This entry describes syndactyly type III (SDTY3). Type III syndactyly is also a characteristic feature of oculodentodigital dysplasia, which is caused by mutation in the gene encoding connexin-43 (GJA1; 121014) on chromosome 6q22.31 (Brueton et al., 1990; Paznekas et al., 2003). ⚠︎

### Description

Syndactyly type III is a limb malformation in which the ring and little fingers are joined (De Smet et al., 1994). Brachymesophalangia of the fifth finger is a frequent association (De Smet et al., 1994). McKiernan and McCann (1993) referred to familial syndactyly of the ring and little finger as type III syndactyly. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Johnston and Kirby (1955) reported syndactyly of the ring and little finger.

Brueton et al. (1990) regarded type III syndactyly as one of the characteristic findings of oculodentodigital dysplasia and asked whether oculodentodigital dysplasia and type III syndactyly are separate genetic entities or a disease spectrum.

Schrander-Stumpel et al. (1993) described a patient with complete cutaneous syndactyly of fingers 3 to 5 in the left hand and fingers 4 and 5 in the right hand.

McKiernan and McCann (1993) reported familial syndactyly of the ring and little finger (type III syndactyly).

De Smet et al. (1994) described 5 new cases of isolated syndactyly of the small and ring finger. Brachymesophalangia of the fifth finger was a frequent association.

Paznekas et al. (2003) noted that oculodentodigital dysplasia presents with craniofacial (ocular, nasal, and dental) and limb dysmorphisms, and that syndactyly type III can occur in some cases.

### Inheritance

De Smet et al. (1994) stated that autosomal dominant inheritance of syndactyly type III was obvious.

### Molecular Genetics

Paznekas et al. (2003) found that oculodentodigital dysplasia, in which type III syndactyly can occur, is caused by mutation in the GJA1 gene, which encodes connexin-43.

Richardson et al. (2004) reported that expression of Gja1 correlates with the phenotype observed in oculodentodigital syndrome/type III syndactyly.

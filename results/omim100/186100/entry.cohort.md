## SYNDACTYLY, TYPE III; SDTY3

*Alternative titles; symbols*

SYNDACTYLY OF FINGERS IV AND V  
RING AND LITTLE FINGER SYNDACTYLY

### TEXT

This entry describes type III syndactyly (SDTY3), syndactyly of the ring and little fingers, which has been reported as an isolated autosomal dominant trait and in families with features of oculodentodigital dysplasia.

### Description

Type III syndactyly is fusion of the ring and little fingers (McKiernan and McCann, 1993; De Smet et al., 1994). It can occur as an isolated trait with autosomal dominant inheritance, and brachymesophalangia of the fifth finger is a frequent association (De Smet et al., 1994). Type III syndactyly also occurs as part of oculodentodigital syndrome (Gladwin et al., 1997), and whether the two conditions are separate entities or form a clinical spectrum has been debated (Brueton et al., 1990; Schrander-Stumpel et al., 1993).

### Clinical Features

Opjordsmoen and Nyberg-Hansen (1980) reported a family from northern Norway in which 9 of 22 members across 3 generations had spastic paraplegia with neurogenic bladder disturbances (uninhibited, incomplete supranuclear bladder paresis) and syndactyly. The bladder disturbances were the main complaint and appeared early in the disease. The authors considered the family probably to represent an unusual form of hereditary spastic paraplegia with autosomal dominant inheritance.

Brueton et al. (1990) described a family with type III syndactyly and facies resembling the facial phenotype of oculodentodigital dysplasia, without any of the other characteristic findings of oculodentodigital dysplasia. They discussed whether the two conditions are separate genetic entities or part of a disease spectrum.

Schrander-Stumpel et al. (1993) reported a newborn male with complete cutaneous syndactyly of fingers 3-5 in the left hand and fingers 4-5 in the right hand. His mother and maternal grandmother were born with the same hand malformation. The mother had a thin nose and small teeth, and the grandmother had had very small teeth with enamel hypoplasia; eyes were normal in both. The authors concluded that the family showed the variable expression of oculodentodigital dysplasia and proposed that type III syndactyly and oculodentodigital dysplasia form a clinical spectrum. ⚠︎

McKiernan and McCann (1993) reported a pedigree containing 14 cases of familial syndactyly of the ring and little finger. Clinical study revealed variable phenotypic expression.

De Smet et al. (1994) described 5 new cases of isolated syndactyly of the ring and small finger. Brachymesophalangia of the fifth finger was a frequent association.

Gladwin et al. (1997) studied a large family with type III syndactyly and atypical facial features. They noted that type III syndactyly occurs as part of oculodentodigital syndrome and has also been reported as an isolated entity. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Autosomal dominant inheritance of type III syndactyly was observed across 3 generations in the family reported by Schrander-Stumpel et al. (1993). McKiernan and McCann (1993) stated that analysis of their pedigree with 14 affected individuals confirmed autosomal dominant inheritance, and De Smet et al. (1994) stated that autosomal dominant inheritance was obvious in their cases of isolated ring and small finger syndactyly. Opjordsmoen and Nyberg-Hansen (1980) considered the inheritance of spastic paraplegia, neurogenic bladder and syndactyly in their Norwegian family to be autosomal dominant.

### Mapping

Gladwin et al. (1997) analyzed a large family with type III syndactyly and atypical facial features. Their analysis suggested that isolated type III syndactyly maps to the same region of chromosome 6q (6q22-q24) as the oculodentodigital syndrome locus.

### Molecular Genetics

Paznekas et al. (2003) found mutations in the GJA1 gene, which encodes connexin-43, in all 17 families with autosomal dominant oculodentodigital dysplasia that they screened. They detected 16 different missense mutations and 1 codon duplication, and noted that type III syndactyly can occur in some cases of that syndrome.

### Pathogenesis

Richardson et al. (2004) reported that Gja1 expression correlated with the phenotype observed in oculodentodigital syndrome/type III syndactyly.

### Clinical Management

McKiernan and McCann (1993) treated 3 affected members of their pedigree surgically. The senior author had earlier treated a fourth surgically elsewhere.

De Smet et al. (1994) concluded that syndactyly of the ring and small finger requires surgery at a young age.

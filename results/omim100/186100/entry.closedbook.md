### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that syndactyly type III (SDTY3) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gap junction protein alpha-1 gene (GJA1; 121014), which encodes connexin-43, on chromosome 6q22. Type III syndactyly is also a characteristic feature of oculodentodigital dysplasia, which is caused by mutation in the same gene. ✗

### Description

Syndactyly type III is a limb malformation in which the ring and little fingers (fingers 4 and 5) are joined, usually completely and on both hands. The fusion is usually of soft tissue only, but the distal phalanges are sometimes fused as well. The little finger is often short, with a small or missing middle phalanx. The feet are usually not affected. In the Temtamy and McKusick classification of syndactylies, this pattern is listed as type III. ✗

### Clinical Features

The ring and little fingers are joined, usually on both hands. The little finger is typically short because its middle phalanx is small or absent. The toes are usually normal. The same hand defect occurs in oculodentodigital dysplasia, where it comes with eye, tooth and other limb anomalies. This has raised the question of whether isolated type III syndactyly is a separate condition or the mildest end of the oculodentodigital dysplasia spectrum. ✗

### Inheritance

Syndactyly type III is inherited as an autosomal dominant trait.

### Molecular Genetics

Heterozygous mutations in the GJA1 gene, which encodes connexin-43, have been found in families with type III syndactyly. Some of these families also had features of oculodentodigital dysplasia, which is caused by GJA1 mutation. Richardson et al. (2004) showed that, in the developing mouse, Gja1 is expressed in tissues that match the features of oculodentodigital syndrome and type III syndactyly. ✗

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Mitchell-Riley syndrome (MTCHRS) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the RFX6 gene (612659) on chromosome 6q22.

### Description

Mitchell-Riley syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder that presents in the neonatal period. Its main features are neonatal diabetes mellitus with a hypoplastic or annular pancreas, intestinal atresia (duodenal, jejunal, or both), and aplasia or hypoplasia of the gallbladder. Some patients have intestinal malrotation, biliary abnormalities, and chronic diarrhea or malabsorption.

### Clinical Features

Mitchell et al. (2004) described 5 infants with a new syndrome of neonatal diabetes, a hypoplastic or annular pancreas, duodenal or jejunal atresia, and aplasia or hypoplasia of the gallbladder. Some were born to consanguineous parents, which pointed to autosomal recessive inheritance. Intrauterine growth retardation was present, and several of the infants died in early life. ✗

### Inheritance

Mitchell-Riley syndrome is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. Mitchell et al. (2004) suggested this from the occurrence in consanguineous families. Smith et al. (2010) confirmed it when they found biallelic RFX6 mutations in affected individuals. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Smith et al. (2010) found homozygous or compound heterozygous mutations in the RFX6 gene in patients with Mitchell-Riley syndrome, including patients from the families reported by Mitchell et al. (2004). They identified the gene by homozygosity mapping in consanguineous families followed by candidate gene sequencing. RFX6 encodes a winged-helix transcription factor. Smith et al. (2010) showed that RFX6 is expressed in the developing gut endoderm and pancreatic endocrine lineage, and that it acts downstream of NGN3 in islet cell development. ⚠︎

Later reports described patients with biallelic RFX6 mutations and a milder or variable phenotype. Some lacked intestinal atresia, and in some the diabetes began after the neonatal period. ✗

### Animal Model

Smith et al. (2010) found that Rfx6-null mice developed hyperglycemia shortly after birth and died. Islet cells, including insulin-producing beta cells, failed to form, except for pancreatic polypeptide-producing cells. In zebrafish, Smith et al. (2010) found that rfx6 was needed for the differentiation of endocrine cells into hormone-producing islet cells. ✗

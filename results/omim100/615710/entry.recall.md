### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Mitchell-Riley syndrome (MTCHRS) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the RFX6 gene (612659) on chromosome 6q22.1.

### Description

Mitchell-Riley syndrome is a disorder that presents in the neonatal period and was suggested to be autosomal recessive by Mitchell et al. (2004). Its main features are neonatal diabetes mellitus with a hypoplastic or annular pancreas, intestinal atresia (duodenal, jejunal, or both), and aplasia or hypoplasia of the gallbladder (Mitchell et al., 2004). Some patients have intestinal malrotation (Galán-Gómez et al., 2007), biliary abnormalities (Martínez-Frías et al., 1992), and chronic diarrhea or malabsorption (Smith et al., 2010). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Mitchell et al. (2004) described 5 infants with a new syndrome of neonatal diabetes, a hypoplastic or annular pancreas, duodenal or jejunal atresia, and aplasia or hypoplasia of the gallbladder. One sib pair was born to consanguineous parents. Mitchell et al. (2004) concluded that the condition probably represented a new autosomal recessive syndrome. Four of the infants died in the first year of life.

### Inheritance

Mitchell et al. (2004) suggested that Mitchell-Riley syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder; in their report, one affected sib pair was born to consanguineous parents. Smith et al. (2010) confirmed autosomal recessive inheritance by finding biallelic RFX6 mutations in affected individuals, each inherited from carrier parents.

### Molecular Genetics

Smith et al. (2010) found homozygous or compound heterozygous mutations in the RFX6 gene in patients with Mitchell-Riley syndrome, and linked some of their probands to previous clinical case reports. They identified the gene by homozygosity mapping in consanguineous families followed by candidate gene sequencing. RFX6 encodes a winged-helix transcription factor. Smith et al. (2010) showed that RFX6 is expressed in the developing gut endoderm and in the pancreatic endocrine lineage, and that it acts downstream of NGN3 in islet cell development. ⚠︎

Sansbury et al. (2015) reported patients with biallelic RFX6 mutations and a milder phenotype, in whom the diagnosis of diabetes was not made in the neonatal period.

### Animal Model

Smith et al. (2010) found that Rfx6-null mice died shortly after birth. The mice failed to generate any of the normal islet cell types, including insulin-producing beta cells, except for pancreatic polypeptide-producing cells.

# Proposed updates for OMIM 615710: MITCHELL-RILEY SYNDROME

Literature from 2010 onwards that the entry does not cite: 27 papers screened, 8 read, 8 proposal(s). Each proposal is a draft for a curator to accept, edit or reject.

**Back-test as of 2010.** OMIM's curators later added 3 reference(s) from that year on; this run proposed 2 of them (recall 0.667), and 6 paper(s) OMIM does not cite.

## [HIGH] Sansbury et al. (2015) — phenotype_expansion, new_variants, genotype_phenotype_correlation, diagnostic_method, natural_history

Sansbury FH, Kirel B, Caswell R, Allen HL, Flanagan SE, Hattersley AT, Ellard S, Shaw-Smith CJ. Biallelic RFX6 mutations can cause childhood as well as neonatal onset diabetes mellitus. Eur J Hum Genet 23: 1744-8, 2015. [PubMed: 26264437] *(read: fulltext)*

**Why:** The entry currently records only neonatal-onset diabetes and has no RFX6 variants. Sansbury et al. (2015) describe 2 cousins with RFX6-confirmed Mitchell-Riley syndrome whose diabetes began in childhood (ages 3 and 6 years), and both were alive at 9 years. The paper also adds 2 new RFX6 nonsense mutations (R726X and R866X), a proposed genotype-phenotype correlation based on whether the mutant transcript escapes nonsense-mediated decay, carrier data that do not support a diabetes risk in heterozygotes, and a recommendation on genetic testing.

*clinical_features*: Sansbury et al. (2015) reported 2 Turkish double first cousins with Mitchell-Riley syndrome whose diabetes was diagnosed at ages 3 and 6 years, not in the neonatal period. The first, a girl born at 32 weeks' gestation, had duodenal atresia, a jejunal web, and a Meckel diverticulum. She had hyperglycemia at age 2 years and presented with diabetic ketoacidosis at age 3 years. Her insulin and C-peptide levels were very low and her autoantibodies were negative. She also had an absent gallbladder and gastrointestinal bleeding due to gastric heterotopia in the small bowel. Her cousin, a boy born at 34 weeks' gestation, had duodenal atresia and midgut malrotation. He had asymptomatic fasting hyperglycemia at age 5 years, was diagnosed with diabetes at age 6 years, and had chronic iron deficiency anemia. Neither child had liver disease, and both were alive at age 9 years.

*clinical_features*: Sansbury et al. (2015) reviewed 8 previously reported cases of Mitchell-Riley syndrome confirmed by biallelic RFX6 mutations. All 8 had duodenal atresia, 7 of 8 had gallbladder agenesis, and all had liver involvement, with intrahepatic cholestasis in 7. In all 8 patients, diabetes began within the first 2 weeks of life, and 5 died within the first year of life.

*inheritance*: In the family reported by Sansbury et al. (2015), several relatives heterozygous for an RFX6 mutation had adult-onset diabetes. However, C-peptide levels in heterozygotes did not differ between those with and without diabetes. HbA1c also did not differ between nondiabetic heterozygotes and relatives without the familial mutation. The authors concluded that the data did not support heterozygosity for either mutation as a risk factor for diabetes, although carriers of 5-prime mutations might still have an increased risk.

*molecular_genetics*: Sansbury et al. (2015) studied 2 Turkish double first cousins with Mitchell-Riley syndrome and childhood-onset diabetes. Using targeted next-generation sequencing of neonatal diabetes genes, with Sanger confirmation, they identified compound heterozygous nonsense mutations in the RFX6 gene in both cousins: R726X in exon 17 and R866X in exon 18. The fathers were heterozygous for R726X and the mothers carried R866X, and the mutations were traced back to the grandmothers. The authors predicted that R726X would be subject to nonsense-mediated decay. They predicted that R866X would escape it and would yield a partially truncated protein that keeps the entire DNA-binding domain. These were the first RFX6 mutations reported in exons 9 to 19.

*molecular_genetics*: RFX6, ARG726TER
In 2 Turkish double first cousins with Mitchell-Riley syndrome and childhood-onset diabetes, Sansbury et al. (2015) identified compound heterozygosity for a c.2176C-T transition in exon 17 of the RFX6 gene, resulting in an arg726-to-ter (R726X) substitution, and a nonsense mutation, R866X. The mutations were found by targeted next-generation sequencing and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. The fathers were heterozygous for R726X, and the mutation was traced back to the grandmothers. The authors predicted that the mutant transcript would be subject to nonsense-mediated decay.

*molecular_genetics*: RFX6, ARG866TER
In 2 Turkish double first cousins with Mitchell-Riley syndrome and childhood-onset diabetes, Sansbury et al. (2015) identified compound heterozygosity for a c.2596C-T transition in exon 18 of the RFX6 gene, resulting in an arg866-to-ter (R866X) substitution, and a nonsense mutation, R726X. The R866X mutation was inherited from the mothers. Because it lies within 50 nucleotides of the last exon-exon boundary, the authors predicted that it escapes nonsense-mediated decay. They predicted that it yields a partially truncated protein that keeps the entire DNA-binding domain and may retain some function.

*genotype_phenotype_correlations*: Sansbury et al. (2015) noted that the 8 previously reported molecularly confirmed cases of Mitchell-Riley syndrome had either splicing or frameshift mutations in exons 2 to 8 of RFX6 or missense mutations in or near the DNA-binding domain. They suggested a possible genotype-phenotype correlation: the 3-prime R866X allele may escape nonsense-mediated decay and preserve some RFX6 function, which could explain the later-onset diabetes and otherwise milder phenotype in their patients.

*diagnosis*: Sansbury et al. (2015) recommended considering genetic testing for RFX6 mutations in patients who present with intestinal atresias even when neonatal diabetes is absent, especially when there is evidence of autosomal recessive inheritance.

## [HIGH] Pearl et al. (2011) — functional_evidence, animal_model

Pearl EJ, Jarikji Z, Horb ME. Functional analysis of Rfx6 and mutant variants associated with neonatal diabetes. Dev Biol 351: 135-45, 2011. [PubMed: 21215266] *(read: fulltext)*

**Why:** The entry has no functional or animal-model evidence for RFX6 and an empty variant table. Pearl et al. (2011) gave the first in vivo functional proof: in Xenopus, three patient RFX6 mutations (R181Q, S217P and an exon 7 truncation) failed to rescue the rfx6 knockdown phenotype. Rfx6 knockdown abolished pancreatic and endocrine gene expression, but the gall bladder was unaffected.

*molecular_genetics*: Pearl et al. (2011) tested 3 of the 4 homozygous RFX6 mutations previously identified in patients with Mitchell-Riley syndrome in a Xenopus rescue assay. None of the mutant constructs could rescue the rfx6 morpholino knockdown phenotype, whereas wildtype rfx6 did. The first was an arg181-to-gln (R181Q) substitution in the DNA-binding domain (n = 16; 3 normal, 4 moderate, 9 severe). Based on the structure of the DNA-bound RFX1 DNA-binding domain, this residue makes direct contact with the X-box DNA, so R181Q introduces a charge reversal at a key DNA-binding residue. The second was a ser217-to-pro (S217P) substitution C-terminal to the DNA-binding domain, in a domain of unknown function (n = 16; 2 moderate, 14 severe). The authors noted that proline residues can introduce kinks into proteins and disrupt their structure. The third was a truncation after exon 7 that retained the DNA-binding domain but lacked the dimerization domain (n = 16; 1 normal, 1 moderate, 14 severe). Its failure to rescue was consistent with an inability to dimerize, since RFX transcription factors bind as homo- or heterodimers. The authors concluded that these mutations have an in vivo effect and that mutant RFX6 is responsible for at least some of the defects in Mitchell-Riley syndrome.

*animal_model*: Pearl et al. (2011) cloned the Xenopus laevis rfx6 homolog. Its protein shares 69% amino acid identity with human RFX6, and its RFX DNA-binding domain is identical to the human domain. Rfx6 was expressed in 2 phases: broadly throughout the anterior endoderm from stage NF15 to NF34, and then in a punctate pattern in endocrine cells of the gut and pancreas from NF40. Morpholino knockdown of rfx6 had no effect at gastrula and neurula stages. From NF25 it reduced expression of hnf6 and foxa2, and by NF35 it reduced expression of all anterior endodermal markers (hnf6, pdx1, ptf1a), with complete loss of insulin expression. In morphant tadpoles, pancreatic pdx1, ptf1a, elastase and insulin expression was almost completely abolished, and no glucagon, somatostatin or neuroD expression was detected in the gastrointestinal tract. A hormone-inducible wildtype rfx6 construct (100 pg rfx6-GR) partially rescued elastase expression (n = 36; 20 normal, 15 moderate, 1 severe) compared with morpholino alone (n = 47; 6 moderate, 41 severe). Rescue occurred only when dexamethasone was added at NF25; earlier induction produced a gain-of-function phenotype. Microarray analysis of morphant anterior foregut at NF30, NF40 and NF44 showed downregulation of pancreas, stomach, intestine, heart and lung genes and upregulation of kidney marker genes. These results were confirmed for agr2, frp5, surfactant C, nkx2.5 and kcnj1. The gall bladder was unaffected in morphants, with normal gall bladder expression of hnf6 (17 of 18) and sox17-alpha (18 of 20); hnf6 was reduced in the duodenum. Expression of the liver marker hex was unaffected, although the liver appeared smaller. This contrasts with the frequent gall bladder involvement in the human syndrome.

*pathogenesis*: Pearl et al. (2011) proposed from the biphasic rfx6 expression pattern and their functional studies in Xenopus that RFX6 has an early, broad role essential for development of most anterior endodermal organs and a later role restricted to endocrine cells. Rescue occurred only with induction at NF25, and pdx1 and hnf6 expression decreased with loss of function. On this basis the authors placed RFX6 upstream of pdx1 and ngn3, in contrast to mouse and zebrafish studies that placed it downstream of Ngn3.

## [HIGH] Smith et al. (2010) — new_causal_gene, new_variants, functional_evidence, animal_model, genotype_phenotype_correlation, phenotype_expansion

Smith SB, Qu HQ, Taleb N, Kishimoto NY, Scheel DW, Lu Y, Patch AM, Grabs R, Wang J, Lynn FC, and others. Rfx6 directs islet formation and insulin production in mice and humans. Nature 463: 775-80, 2010. [PubMed: 20148032] *(read: fulltext)*

**Why:** The entry currently records no causal gene or variants. Smith et al. (2010) identified biallelic RFX6 mutations in 5 of 6 probands, found by homozygosity mapping and sequencing, and supported them with EMSA and RNA studies, a knockout mouse, and a genotype-phenotype correlation. The paper also adds intractable diarrhea as a feature, proposes the name Mitchell-Riley syndrome, and argues that the disorder is distinct from Martínez-Frías syndrome.

*description*: Mitchell-Riley syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder of neonatal diabetes with small bowel obstruction due to intestinal atresia, a hypoplastic gallbladder, a somewhat reduced pancreas size and intractable diarrhea. Smith et al. (2010) showed that it is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the RFX6 gene.

*nomenclature*: Smith et al. (2010) noted that OMIM listed their patients under Martínez-Frías syndrome. They considered the cases distinct because the original Martínez-Frías patients had esophageal atresia and hypospadias without diabetes. In addition, no RFX6 mutation was found in the parents of 1 Martínez-Frías case. They proposed the name Mitchell-Riley syndrome, after the 2 clinicians who first described the condition.

*clinical_features*: Smith et al. (2010) described patients with neonatal diabetes and small bowel obstruction due to bowel atresia. Pancreas size was somewhat reduced, but exocrine pancreatic enzymes were not deficient. The syndrome also included a hypoplastic gallbladder and intractable diarrhea that did not respond to pancreatic enzyme replacement. Autopsy in 2 cases showed a normal-appearing exocrine pancreas with clusters of chromogranin A-positive cells but no cells staining for insulin, glucagon, or somatostatin.

*mapping*: Smith et al. (2010) performed high-resolution homozygosity mapping in 2 probands whose parents were first and second cousins, respectively. They found 10 homozygosity-by-descent (HBD) segments larger than 500 kb in proband 1 and 25 in proband 2. Only 3 HBD regions, totaling 24 Mb, were shared: 2p15-16, 6q21-22, and 6q23. Of the 194 RefSeq genes in these regions, only RFX6 had pancreas-enriched expression; it lay in the largest segment, at 6q21-22.

*molecular_genetics*: Smith et al. (2010) used array capture and deep sequencing of the exons within the 3 shared HBD regions in proband 2, the child of consanguineous parents. Of 30 novel variants, the only nonsynonymous change was a homozygous ser217-to-pro (S217P) substitution in the RFX6 gene, which direct sequencing confirmed. The variant lies between the DNA-binding and dimerization domains. In EMSA it only modestly reduced DNA binding, and it did not affect dimer formation. The patient was alive at age 4.5 years.

*molecular_genetics*: Smith et al. (2010) sequenced the 19 exons and splice junctions of RFX6 directly. They found missense, splicing, or frameshift mutations in 5 of 6 available probands with the syndrome, and all mutations were inherited from carrier parents. Proband 1, the child of first cousins, was homozygous for a splice donor mutation in intron 2 (IVS2+2T-C). RT-PCR of RNA from the patient's autopsy pancreas detected no properly spliced RFX6 transcript. No RNA was detected from exons 1 and 2, which the authors attributed probably to nonsense-mediated decay. Proband 4 was homozygous for an out-of-frame deletion in exon 7. Proband 5 was homozygous for an arg181-to-gln (R181Q) mutation that affects a highly conserved arginine in the DNA-binding domain; in EMSA this mutation completely abolished DNA binding. Proband 3 was compound heterozygous for 2 splice mutations: loss of the donor site in intron 6 (IVS6+2T-G) and disruption of the acceptor site in intron 1 (IVS1-12A-G).

*genotype_phenotype_correlations*: Smith et al. (2010) reported a genotype-phenotype correlation. Three probands died in the first few months of life; they were homozygous for a splice donor loss, a frameshift deletion, or the R181Q mutation, which abolished DNA binding. Two patients survived longer. One was homozygous for S217P, which only modestly reduced DNA binding, and was alive at 4.5 years. The other was a compound heterozygote for 2 splice-site mutations and was alive at 9 years and intermittently off insulin.

*heterogeneity*: Smith et al. (2010) found no RFX6 mutation in 1 proband (proband 6). Proband DNA was unavailable. Neither parent carried a point mutation in RFX6 or NEUROG3, and long-range PCR showed no RFX6 deletion. The authors could not rule out a de novo mutation but considered the case most likely a phenocopy. They also found no RFX6 mutation in a case of Martínez-Frías syndrome.

*animal_model*: Smith et al. (2010) generated Rfx6-null mice by replacing the first 5 exons, which include the DNA-binding domain, with an eGFP-cre cassette. Homozygous mice were born at the expected Mendelian ratio. However, they fed poorly, had gross bowel distension from small bowel obstruction, and died within 2 days of birth; some had a reduced pancreas size. At embryonic day 17.5, mutant pancreata contained many chromogranin A-positive endocrine cells, but none expressed insulin, glucagon, somatostatin, or ghrelin. Pancreatic polypeptide-expressing cells were increased.

*pathogenesis*: Smith et al. (2010) found that Rfx6 is expressed broadly in the definitive endoderm and later becomes restricted to the gut and pancreatic bud. It acts downstream of neurogenin-3 and upstream of many islet transcription factors, and it is required for the differentiation of 4 of the 5 islet cell types. Mutations in both RFX6 and NEUROG3 cause diarrhea and diabetes. The authors attributed the small bowel atresia and biliary abnormalities, which are seen only with RFX6 mutation, to Rfx6 expression in the early gut endoderm, where neurogenin-3 is not expressed.

## [MEDIUM] Zegre Amorim et al. (2015) — new_variants, phenotype_expansion

Zegre Amorim M, Houghton JA, Carmo S, Salva I, Pita A, Pereira-da-Silva L. Mitchell-Riley Syndrome: A Novel Mutation in RFX6 Gene. Case Rep Genet 2015: 937201, 2015. [PubMed: 26770845] *(read: fulltext)*

**Why:** The paper adds an RFX6 mutation, homozygous R181W, found in a consanguineous family in which it segregated; the entry's variant table currently has no variants. It also adds features the entry does not list: transient neonatal cholestasis with biliary duct dilation, possible ectopic perijejunal pancreatic tissue, and chronic diarrhea and malabsorption that did not respond to exocrine pancreatic supplementation.

*clinical_features*: Zegre Amorim et al. (2015) reported a girl born to first-cousin Gipsy parents. Prenatal ultrasound showed intrauterine growth restriction and a 'double bubble.' She was born at 35 weeks with weight, length, and head circumference below the 3rd centile. She developed hyperglycemia on day 2 and needed continuous insulin. On day 3, insulin and C-peptide levels were very low, and GAD, islet cell, and IA-2 autoantibodies were negative. Laparotomy on day 4 confirmed type II duodenal atresia with annular pancreas, malrotation, and gallbladder agenesis. From day 6 she had cholestasis (conjugated bilirubin 3.07 mg/dL, GGT 458 IU/L), which resolved without treatment by 2 months of age. Ultrasound showed biliary duct dilation and a disproportionately large pancreatic head; the pancreatic tail could not be identified. At surgery, perijejunal submucosal tissue that looked like ectopic pancreatic tissue was seen. The authors described this as a novel feature, but the tissue was not biopsied. She had chronic diarrhea and malabsorption that did not respond to exocrine pancreatic supplementation. At 7 months she had failure to thrive and still depended on insulin and partial parenteral nutrition. Her mother had previously had 3 first-trimester spontaneous abortions and a child with isolated congenital anorectal malformation.

*inheritance* ⚠︎: In the family reported by Zegre Amorim et al. (2015), the affected proband was homozygous for an RFX6 mutation, and her first-cousin parents were both heterozygous carriers. This is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

*molecular_genetics*: Zegre Amorim et al. (2015) studied a girl with Mitchell-Riley syndrome born to consanguineous Gipsy parents. Using Sanger sequencing, they identified a homozygous c.541C-T transition in exon 4 of the RFX6 gene, resulting in an arg181-to-trp (R181W) substitution. Both parents were heterozygous for the mutation. The arg181 residue lies in the DNA-binding domain and is highly conserved across species. The authors noted that a different mutation at the same residue, R181Q, had been reported previously.

*molecular_genetics*: RFX6, ARG181TRP
In a girl with Mitchell-Riley syndrome born to first-cousin Gipsy parents, Zegre Amorim et al. (2015) identified a homozygous c.541C-T transition in exon 4 of the RFX6 gene, resulting in an arg181-to-trp (R181W) substitution at a highly conserved residue in the DNA-binding domain. The mutation was found by Sanger sequencing, and both parents were heterozygous for it.

## [MEDIUM] Sansbury et al. (2015) — phenotype_expansion, natural_history

Sansbury FH, Kirel B, Caswell R, Lango Allen H, Flanagan SE, Hattersley AT, Ellard S, Shaw-Smith CJ. Biallelic RFX6 mutations can cause childhood as well as neonatal onset diabetes mellitus. Eur J Hum Genet 23: 1750, 2015. [PubMed: 26559129] *(read: title)*

**Why:** Sansbury et al. (2015) show that biallelic RFX6 mutations can cause diabetes starting in childhood as well as in the newborn period. The entry records only neonatal-onset diabetes, so this widens the age-of-onset range. Only the title is available, so the finding has no specific variants or patient details.

*molecular_genetics* ⚠︎: Sansbury et al. (2015) reported that biallelic mutations in the RFX6 gene can cause diabetes mellitus with onset in childhood as well as diabetes with onset in the neonatal period, extending the age-of-onset range of RFX6-related diabetes.

## [MEDIUM] Concepcion et al. (2014) — new_variants, phenotype_expansion, genotype_phenotype_correlation

Concepcion JP, Reh CS, Daniels M, Liu X, Paz VP, Ye H, Highland HM, Hanis CL, Greeley SA. Neonatal diabetes, gallbladder agenesis, duodenal atresia, and intestinal malrotation caused by a novel homozygous mutation in RFX6. Pediatr Diabetes 15: 67-72, 2014. [PubMed: 23914949] *(read: fulltext)*

**Why:** This paper adds an RFX6 variant, c.779A-C (K260T), found homozygous in a Vietnamese boy whose parents were both carriers; the entry's variant table is empty. It also adds features the entry doesn't list: severe chronic diarrhea that pancreatic enzymes didn't help, cholestatic jaundice leading to liver failure, and recurrent infections. Finally, it gives a genotype-phenotype observation across 8 patients with RFX6 mutations.

*clinical_features*: Concepcion et al. (2014) described a male infant born at 34 weeks' gestation to nonconsanguineous Vietnamese parents. Before birth he had intrauterine growth restriction and duodenal atresia. He was small for gestational age (birth weight 1375 g). Within 24 hours of birth he had severe hyperglycemia (446 mg/dL), which required an insulin infusion. C-peptide, amylase, lipase, and stool pancreatic elastase were all low. Laparotomy on day 5 confirmed duodenal atresia, intestinal malrotation, and jejunal perforation, and ultrasound suggested an annular pancreas. His later course included cholestatic jaundice, recurrent infections, and feeding intolerance. He also had severe chronic diarrhea that required intermittent parenteral nutrition and did not improve with pancreatic enzymes. Laparotomy at 3 months showed gallbladder agenesis. A hepatobiliary scan did not visualize the gallbladder or the common bile duct, and liver biopsy showed bile ductular proliferation. He developed liver failure and died of sepsis at 5 months of age while awaiting liver transplantation.

*molecular_genetics*: Concepcion et al. (2014) studied a Vietnamese boy with Mitchell-Riley syndrome. Whole-exome sequencing found a homozygous missense mutation in the RFX6 gene (c.779A-C, K260T). Sanger sequencing confirmed the mutation, and both parents were heterozygous carriers. The variant was not in dbSNP or the 1000 Genomes database. It affects a highly conserved lysine and was predicted to be damaging. It also changes the consensus 5-prime splice donor site of intron 7, and was predicted to disrupt splicing. The parents were nonconsanguineous, but runs of homozygosity suggested they might be distantly related.

*molecular_genetics*: RFX6, LYS260THR
Concepcion et al. (2014) found a homozygous c.779A-C transversion in the RFX6 gene in a Vietnamese boy with Mitchell-Riley syndrome. The change results in a lys260-to-thr (K260T) substitution at a highly conserved residue. It also alters the consensus 5-prime splice donor site of intron 7 and was predicted to disrupt splicing. The mutation was found by whole-exome sequencing and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. Both parents were heterozygous carriers. The variant was not in dbSNP or the 1000 Genomes database.

*genotype_phenotype_correlations*: Concepcion et al. (2014) reviewed 8 patients with RFX6 mutations. The 2 longest survivors were a compound heterozygote with 2 splice site mutations and a patient homozygous for a missense mutation that reduced, but did not abolish, DNA binding. These 2 patients also had the highest birth weights. By contrast, the authors' patient, who was homozygous for the K260T mutation, had disease as severe as patients with homozygous splice site or frameshift mutations or with mutations that abolished DNA binding. The authors noted that chronic diarrhea had been reported in most patients with RFX6 mutations.

## [MEDIUM] Chandra et al. (2014) — new_variants, functional_evidence

Chandra V, Albagli-Curiel O, Hastoy B, Piccand J, Randriamampita C, Vaillant E, Cavé H, Busiah K, Froguel P, Vaxillaire M, and others. RFX6 regulates insulin secretion by modulating Ca2+ homeostasis in human β cells. Cell Rep 9: 2206-18, 2014. [PubMed: 25497100] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** The entry lists no RFX6 variants. This paper reports a previously unreported homozygous RFX6 missense mutation (V506G) linked to neonatal diabetes, with functional evidence that the mutant protein cannot activate the insulin promoter. It also shows in human beta cells that RFX6 controls insulin secretion through calcium-channel expression.

*molecular_genetics*: Chandra et al. (2014) identified a previously unreported homozygous missense mutation in the RFX6 gene, val506-to-gly (V506G), associated with neonatal diabetes. Functional studies showed that the mutant protein could not activate the insulin promoter or increase calcium-channel expression.

*pathogenesis*: Chandra et al. (2014) found RFX6 in adult human pancreatic endocrine cells. In the human beta-cell line EndoC-betaH2, RFX6 regulated insulin gene transcription, insulin content, and insulin secretion. Knocking down RFX6 lowered expression of calcium-channel genes and reduced L-type calcium-channel activity, which suppressed depolarization-evoked insulin exocytosis. The authors concluded that RFX6 regulates insulin secretion by modulating calcium homeostasis in human beta cells.

## [MEDIUM] Spiegel et al. (2011) — new_variants, natural_history

Spiegel R, Dobbie A, Hartman C, de Vries L, Ellard S, Shalev SA. Clinical characterization of a newly described neonatal diabetes syndrome caused by RFX6 mutations. Am J Med Genet A 155A: 2821-5, 2011. [PubMed: 21965172] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** The entry's evidence includes no patient variants. Spiegel et al. (2011) report a new patient with a novel homozygous splice-site mutation in RFX6. They also review all RFX6-mutation patients and suggest that, after a severe course early after birth, those who survive may have a relatively favorable prognosis. The entry does not yet record this outcome information.

*molecular_genetics*: Spiegel et al. (2011) reported a new patient with Mitchell-Riley syndrome, which includes neonatal diabetes, intestinal atresia, and hepatobiliary abnormalities. The patient carried a novel homozygous splice site mutation in the RFX6 gene. The authors also gave an update on the clinical outcome of a previously reported patient.

*clinical_features*: Spiegel et al. (2011) reviewed the clinical and molecular features of all patients with RFX6 mutations, 6 of whom had been reported before their study. They suggested that, despite a fulminant course early after birth, patients who survive may have a relatively favorable prognosis.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that neurodevelopmental disorder with hypotonia, poor growth, dysmorphic facies, and agammaglobulinemia (NEDHGFA) is caused by homozygous mutation in the SEL1L gene (602329) on chromosome 14q31.

### Description

Neurodevelopmental disorder with hypotonia, poor growth, dysmorphic facies, and agammaglobulinemia (NEDHGFA) combines an ERAD-associated neurodevelopmental disorder with onset in infancy with infantile-onset agammaglobulinemia and absence of mature B cells. The result is frequent infections and early death (Weis et al., 2024).

### Nomenclature

Weis et al. (2024) named the disorder 'ENDI-agammaglobulinemia (ENDI-A) syndrome.' ENDI stands for ERAD-associated neurodevelopmental disorder with onset in infancy.

### Clinical Features

Weis et al. (2024) reported 5 sibs from 2 consanguineous sibships of a large Roma family from an isolated region of southern Slovakia. From birth, all had feeding problems, with repeated vomiting after eating, failure to gain weight, pallor, and gradual cachexia. They shared similar facial dysmorphisms (triangular face, big ears). All had severe axial hypotonia, global developmental delay, short stature, microcephaly, intellectual disability, and absent speech. Three patients could not sit up or hold up their heads. Only patient 4 could sit without support, which she achieved at age 4.5 years.

The patients described by Weis et al. (2024) had severe respiratory infections almost every month, including otitis media, sinusitis, bronchitis, and pneumonia. These were often accompanied by respiratory insufficiency due to food aspiration. Patients 3 and 4 were diagnosed with hearing impairment at 1 year of age, which the authors considered likely secondary to otitis media. From 12 months of age, patients 3, 4, and 5 had no detectable mature CD19+ memory B cells and no circulating immunoglobulins. Their absolute CD4+ and CD8+ T-cell counts were largely normal, but the CD4:CD8 ratio was reduced.

Weis et al. (2024) also reported individual findings. A duodenal biopsy from patient 3 showed subtotal villous atrophy (Marsh IIIb enteropathy) with no CD20+ B cells. Patient 3 also had a ventricular septal defect and epilepsy, and MRI showed normal myelination. Patient 1 developed marasmus, acute renal failure, and pulmonary hypertension. Patient 4 had leukoencephalopathy on MRI, ptosis, partial papillary atrophy, and sideropenic anemia. At 7.7 years, she developed COVID-19 with respiratory failure. The course was prolonged, and no antigen-specific memory T-cell response was detected. Patient 5 had leukoencephalopathy and later discrete cortical atrophy, as well as ptosis, micropenis, central hypothyroidism, a hypoplastic thymus, hepatopathy, and dystrophic nails. All patients died young of infection, sepsis, or respiratory failure: patient 1 at 2.9 years, patient 2 at 2 months, and patient 5 at 5.1 years. ⚠︎

### Other Features

Weis et al. (2024) also described a female sib (IV-4) who carried 1 C141Y allele. She was born with alobar holoprosencephaly, cleft lip and palate, 'frog' eyes, low-set ears, microcephaly, pterygium colli, polydactyly/syndactyly, anal malformation, contractures, and an atrioventricular septal defect with patent ductus arteriosus. She died at 9 days of age of cardiorespiratory failure and had no immune problems.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of NEDHGFA in the family reported by Weis et al. (2024) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. The affected sibs were born to consanguineous parents and were homozygous for the SEL1L mutation, and the mutation was not found in homozygous state in the parents or unaffected sibs.

### Molecular Genetics

Weis et al. (2024) studied 5 affected sibs from 2 consanguineous sibships of a Slovakian Roma family with NEDHGFA. Whole-exome sequencing of 2 patients and their parents, assuming recessive inheritance, identified a homozygous c.422G-A transition in exon 4 of the SEL1L gene (NM_005065.6), resulting in a cys141-to-tyr (C141Y) substitution. The mutation was confirmed by Sanger sequencing. It was present in all 5 patients but not in the parents or unaffected sibs. The mutation affects a conserved cysteine in the luminal fibronectin type II (FNII) domain that forms a disulfide bond with cys168. Exome sequencing also identified a FAR2 variant (R490W), and Sanger sequencing confirmed that it segregated with symptoms in patient 2. It was excluded because loss of FAR2 function is not linked to neurologic disorder or agammaglobulinemia. Chromosome analysis, SNP array, and next-generation sequencing found no other genomic imbalance or variant that would explain the phenotype, and aCGH found no large deletions or duplications. In patient fibroblasts, SEL1L protein was largely undetectable and HRD1 protein was reduced by more than 90%, although mRNA levels were normal, and ERAD substrates accumulated. The authors classified the variant as pathogenic under ACMG criteria. They acknowledged, however, that causality could not be firmly established without a knockin mouse. ⚠︎

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Weis et al. (2024) contrasted their patients with ENDI patients who carry hypomorphic SEL1L (G585D, M528R) or HRD1 (P398L) variants and survived into their teens and twenties. Patients with the more severe C141Y variant had B-cell immunodeficiency and died early. The authors attributed this difference to differing degrees of ERAD dysfunction. In knockin HEK293T cells, C141Y produced the lowest SEL1L and HRD1 levels and the highest substrate accumulation of the variants compared, and the authors proposed an inverse correlation between ERAD function and disease severity.

### Pathogenesis

In fibroblasts from patients with the C141Y mutation, Weis et al. (2024) found that SEL1L protein was largely undetectable and HRD1 protein was reduced by more than 90%, while mRNA levels were normal. Both proteins were unstable, and the ERAD substrates IRE1-alpha and CD147 accumulated. Levels of the lectins OS9 and ERLEC1 were decreased. There was no overt unfolded protein response, but ER chaperones were elevated. Duodenal biopsies from the patients also showed reduced SEL1L and HRD1.

Weis et al. (2024) compared knockin HEK293T cells carrying C141Y with cells carrying the hypomorphic ENDI variants SEL1L M528R, SEL1L G585D, and HRD1 P398L. C141Y cells showed the most severe ERAD dysfunction. Effects were milder than in SEL1L-null cells, suggesting that C141Y is not a complete loss of function. C141Y did not impair formation of the ERAD complex. Disrupting the other FNII disulfide bond (C127Y) had similar effects, whereas deleting the whole FNII domain left ERAD function largely normal. SEL1L levels were rescued by proteasome inhibition and by deletion of HRD1, but not by deletion of RNF5, and the accumulated mutant SEL1L formed high-molecular-weight aggregates. The authors concluded that the unpaired cysteine causes HRD1-mediated, proteasome-dependent self-destruction of the SEL1L-HRD1 complex. ⚠︎

Ji et al. (2016) found that Sel1l and Hrd1 expression was induced around the pre-B cell stage, and they identified the pre-B cell receptor (pre-BCR) complex as an endogenous substrate of Sel1L-Hrd1 ERAD. In pro-/pre-B cells of B cell-specific Sel1l-deficient mice, protein levels of the pre-BCR components (Igmu, lambda5, VpreB) were dramatically increased and stabilized, while transcript levels were unchanged. Loss of Sel1L or Hrd1 in 70z/3 pre-B cells increased the half-life of Igmu from about 60 minutes to more than 120 minutes and reduced Igmu ubiquitination. Degradation required BiP. The mature BCR was not an ERAD substrate, and plasma cell differentiation and total IgG were unaffected by Sel1L deficiency in mature B cells. Sel1L-Hrd1 ERAD deficiency also caused increased ER exit and elevated surface pre-BCR, with nearly 3-fold more endoH-resistant Igmu, together with increased Syk phosphorylation and increased proliferation of pro-/pre-B cells. Ji et al. (2016) concluded that Sel1L-Hrd1 ERAD is required to attenuate pre-BCR signaling by promoting its turnover. ⚠︎

### Clinical Management

Weis et al. (2024) treated patients 3, 4, and 5 with IgG replacement every 4 weeks. Acute infections improved substantially, but chronic respiratory symptoms continued to recur. All 3 patients eventually deteriorated suddenly with sepsis and multiorgan failure.

### Animal Model

Ji et al. (2016) generated B cell-specific Sel1l-deficient mice by crossing Sel1l flox/flox mice with CD19-Cre mice. The mutant mice were born in a normal Mendelian ratio and appeared healthy, with no obvious growth defects. However, their spleen weights were significantly reduced. The percentage of peripheral B cells in spleen, blood, and lymph nodes was reduced by 60 to 75%, and absolute numbers of splenic B cells were more than 75% lower than in controls. CD4+ and CD8+ T cells and myeloid cells were unaffected.

The mutant mice of Ji et al. (2016) had a severe developmental block at the transition from large to small pre-B cells. Pre-B I and large pre-B cells were twice as numerous as in controls, small pre-B cells were reduced by 50%, and apoptotic rates were comparable to controls. The block did not depend on Chop: Chop deficiency did not correct the low spleen weight, the paucity of peripheral B cells, or the developmental block. Ji et al. (2016) also noted that global Sel1l-deficient mice are embryonic lethal and that acute Sel1l deletion in adult mice results in pancreatic atrophy and premature lethality.

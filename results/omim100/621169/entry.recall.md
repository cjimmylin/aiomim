### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that monilethrix-2 (MNLIX2) is caused by mutation in the gene encoding the type II hair cortex keratin hHb1 (KRT81; 602153) on chromosome 12q13.13.

### Description

Monilethrix is an inherited hair shaft disorder. Winter et al. (1997) described the hair as beaded or moniliform because the hair shaft periodically thins, and noted that the hair is highly prone to excess weathering and fracturing. Winter et al. (1998) noted follicular keratosis in 1 patient and found that the disease phenotype varied particularly widely among members of a family carrying an hHb1 mutation.

### Inheritance

Winter et al. (1998) stated that monilethrix caused by mutations in hair keratins has autosomal dominant transmission. They noted that family members with an hHb1 (KRT81) mutation showed a particularly variable disease phenotype, indicating variable expressivity.

### Mapping

Winter et al. (1997) noted that several cases of monilethrix had been linked to the type II keratin gene cluster on chromosome 12q13. hHb1 (KRT81) is a type II hair keratin.

### Heterogeneity

Autosomal dominant monilethrix is genetically heterogeneous. Winter et al. (1997) found that it can be caused by mutations in either of 2 type II hair keratin genes: hHb6 (KRT86) or hHb1 (KRT81).

### Molecular Genetics

Winter et al. (1997) studied a family with monilethrix. In the affected members they identified a mutation in the type II hair cortex keratin hHb1 (KRT81) that substituted lysine for glutamic acid at residue 403 (Glu403Lys). This glutamic acid residue corresponds to the hHb6 Glu410 site in the helix termination motif, which is affected in hHb6 (KRT86)-associated monilethrix. The finding showed that monilethrix can be caused by mutations in hHb1 as well as in hHb6. Winter et al. (1997) suggested that this site is a mutational hotspot in these closely related type II hair keratins.

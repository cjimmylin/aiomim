### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that monilethrix-2 (MNLIX2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding the type II hair cortex keratin hHb1 (KRT81; 602153) on chromosome 12q13. ⚠︎

### Description

Monilethrix is an inherited hair shaft disorder. The hair is beaded: elliptical nodes alternate with narrow, constricted internodes, and the hair breaks easily at the internodes. The result is short, sparse, brittle hair, mainly on the scalp, often with keratotic follicular papules (keratosis pilaris), especially on the occiput and nape. The disorder usually appears in early childhood after the lanugo hair is shed. Severity varies, even within a family. ✗

### Inheritance

Monilethrix caused by KRT81 mutation is inherited as an autosomal dominant trait with variable expressivity.

### Mapping

Linkage studies in families with autosomal dominant monilethrix mapped the disorder to the type II keratin gene cluster on chromosome 12q13, which contains KRT81. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Autosomal dominant monilethrix is genetically heterogeneous. It can be caused by mutations in the type II hair cortex keratin genes KRT86 (hHb6), KRT81 (hHb1) and KRT83 (hHb3), which are clustered on chromosome 12q13. A clinically similar autosomal recessive monilethrix-like hypotrichosis is caused by mutation in the desmoglein-4 gene (DSG4). ✗

### Molecular Genetics

Winter et al. (1997) found a heterozygous mutation in the helix termination motif of the hair cortex keratin hHb1 (KRT81) in a patient with monilethrix. This showed that monilethrix can be caused by mutations in hHb1 as well as in hHb6 (KRT86). The mutation changed a conserved glutamic acid residue in the highly conserved helix termination motif of the rod domain, the same region that is affected in hHb6-associated monilethrix. ⚠︎

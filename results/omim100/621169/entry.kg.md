### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that monilethrix-2 (MNLIX2) is caused by mutation in the gene encoding the type II hair cortex keratin hHb1 (KRT81).

### Description

Monilethrix is a rare dominant hair disorder. The hair is beaded (moniliform) because the hair shaft thins at regular intervals, and it is highly prone to excess weathering and breakage (Winter et al., 1997). Expression can vary widely within a family (Winter et al., 1998).

### Clinical Features

Winter et al. (1997) described monilethrix as a rare dominant hair disorder. The hair is beaded (moniliform) because the hair shaft thins at regular intervals, and it is highly prone to excess weathering and breakage.

Winter et al. (1998) described a French family with monilethrix that showed a particularly variable phenotype. One infant had pronounced dystrophic alopecia, follicular keratosis, and clear-cut moniliform hair. A second infant had no hair loss, and the moniliform hair could be detected only by electron microscopy. An adult family member had no symptoms detectable clinically or by electron microscopy but had clear historical proof of the disease.

### Inheritance

Winter et al. (1997, 1998) described monilethrix as a dominant disorder and reported KRT81 (hHb1) mutations in affected members of families with the disorder.

### Heterogeneity

Monilethrix is also caused by mutation in the closely related type II hair keratin hHb6. Winter et al. (1997) found the common hHb6 Glu410Lys mutation in affected members of a monilethrix family and in 1 isolated patient. These were unrelated to a family in which they found a mutation in hHb1.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of a family with monilethrix, Winter et al. (1997) identified a mutation in the type II hair keratin hHb1 that changed glutamic acid-403 to lysine (E403K). This residue lies in the helix termination motif and corresponds to Glu410 of hHb6. In affected members of a second, unrelated family and in 1 isolated patient, they found the common hHb6 Glu410Lys mutation. The authors suggested that this site is a mutational hotspot in these closely related type II hair keratins.

In a French family with a variable monilethrix phenotype, Winter et al. (1998) identified a glu402-to-lys (E402K) mutation in the type II hair keratin hHb1 (KRT81). The mutation affected another highly conserved glutamic acid residue (Glu106 of the 2B subdomain) in the EIATYRRLLEGEE helix termination motif. The authors stated that, before their report, pathogenic mutations in hHb6 and hHb1 had been limited to Glu413.

### Pathogenesis

Winter et al. (1997) noted that hHb1 and hHb6 are largely expressed together in the cortical trichocytes of the hair shaft. They concluded that monilethrix is a disease of the hair cortex.

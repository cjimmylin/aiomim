### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive axonal Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2EE (CMT2EE) is caused by homozygous mutation in the MPV17 gene (137960) on chromosome 2p23.3. ⚠︎

### Description

CMT2EE is an autosomal recessive axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy with onset in childhood, adolescence, or adulthood. Patients have distal muscle weakness and atrophy, gait difficulties, distal sensory loss that mainly affects vibration and position sense, and absent reflexes. Most reported patients had no liver or brain involvement. One patient also had leukoencephalopathy, mild liver involvement, and mitochondrial abnormalities in muscle (Blakely et al., 2012; Choi et al., 2015; Baumann et al., 2019). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Blakely et al. (2012) reported a 21-year-old Pakistani man, born to nonconsanguineous parents, who developed neurologic symptoms at age 14 years. Walking difficulties began gradually and progressed over several years to bilateral foot drop requiring orthoses. He had numbness in the distal feet and hands, an unsteady gait, hypotonia, severe distal muscle weakness, hip flexion weakness, distal muscle wasting of the hands and lower limbs, and clawed toes; he did not have pes cavus. Tendon reflexes were absent. Pinprick and light touch were decreased distal to the knees, vibration sense was impaired to the ankles, and joint position sense was absent at the toes. Cognition was not impaired. Electrophysiologic studies showed an axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy, and sural nerve biopsy showed a severe chronic axonal neuropathy. He had mild hepatomegaly, elevated gamma-GT, hepatic steatosis, and early bridging liver fibrosis. CSF protein and lactate were elevated. Brain MRI showed leukoencephalopathy with T2 hyperintensity of the cerebral and cerebellar white matter. Skeletal muscle biopsy showed ragged-red fibers, about 20% COX-deficient fibers, and abnormal mitochondria with paracrystalline inclusions. Muscle had multiple mtDNA deletions but no mtDNA depletion. Blakely et al. (2012) noted that the phenotype resembled the classic form of Navajo neurohepatopathy but was unusual because of the muscle involvement, with ragged-red fibers and mosaic COX deficiency.

Choi et al. (2015) reported 2 patients from unrelated Korean families with axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy and no liver or brain involvement. The first patient was a 34-year-old woman (family FC26), the first child of healthy nonconsanguineous parents. At age 9 years she first noticed weakness of the distal lower limbs. She had distal muscle atrophy that mainly affected the lower limbs, atrophy of the intrinsic hand muscles, flexion deformities of the interphalangeal joints, ankle joint deformity, and scoliosis, but she could walk with orthopedic assistance. Vibration and position senses were more severely disturbed than pain and touch senses, and knee and ankle jerks were absent. Serum lactate was elevated. She had no hepatomegaly, cognitive impairment, or ophthalmoplegia. Sural nerve biopsy showed loss of large and medium myelinated fibers. The second patient was a 22-year-old man (family FC355), the second child of healthy nonconsanguineous parents. He first had gait and balance problems at age 13 years. He had distal muscle weakness and atrophy, bilateral flat feet, atrophy of the intrinsic hand, foot, and calf muscles, impaired vibration and position sense, and areflexia. Serum lactate was slightly elevated. Muscle biopsy showed ragged-red fibers and subsarcolemmal accumulation of enlarged mitochondria. In both patients, sensory nerve action potentials were absent from early in the disease and peroneal and tibial CMAPs were absent. Lower leg MRI showed selective fatty infiltration of the soleus muscle, similar to that seen in CMT2A, which was consistent with length-dependent axonal degeneration.

Baumann et al. (2019) described 5 patients from 2 unrelated families with juvenile- or adult-onset sensorimotor axonal neuropathy and no hepatocerebral involvement.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CMT2EE is autosomal recessive. Homozygous MPV17 mutations were found in affected individuals by Blakely et al. (2012), Choi et al. (2015), and Baumann et al. (2019). In both families reported by Choi et al. (2015), the parents were healthy and nonconsanguineous, and the mutant allele was putatively inherited from each parent.

### Molecular Genetics

In a 21-year-old Pakistani man with CMT2EE, Blakely et al. (2012) identified a homozygous missense mutation in the MPV17 gene (c.2898C-T, as reported), resulting in a pro98-to-leu (P98L) substitution. This variant had previously been reported as a recessive mutation associated with mtDNA depletion in liver. Parental samples were not available. The patient's muscle had multiple mtDNA deletions with a normal mtDNA copy number, a finding not previously noted in patients with MPV17 mutations.

Choi et al. (2015) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a homozygous c.122G-A transition in the MPV17 gene, resulting in an arg41-to-gln (R41Q) substitution, in 2 patients from unrelated Korean families with CMT2EE. The mutation was not found in 300 controls or in in-house exome data, and it was present at very low frequency in the ESP and ExAC databases. Knockdown of MPV17 in NSC34 mouse motor neuronal cells significantly reduced cell proliferation and viability against reactive oxygen species and affected OXPHOS levels. Overexpression of the R41Q mutant significantly inhibited cell proliferation. The authors concluded that this pointed to both loss of function and a detrimental effect of the mutant protein.

In affected members of 2 unrelated families with CMT2EE, Baumann et al. (2019) identified homozygous mutations in the MPV17 gene. Patients in the first family carried the previously reported R41Q mutation (c.122G-A). Patients in the second family carried a novel c.376-9T-G near-splice variant, which was shown to cause an in-frame deletion of 11 amino acids.

### Pathogenesis

Blakely et al. (2012) found that single laser-captured COX-deficient muscle fibers from their patient had high levels of clonally expanded mtDNA deletions involving MTND4, with no abnormality of mtDNA copy number. They concluded that MPV17 is involved in both mtDNA replication and repair, and that it is associated with both quantitative and qualitative mtDNA abnormalities.

### Diagnosis

Baumann et al. (2019) concluded that MPV17 mutations should be considered in patients with pure, nonsyndromic axonal neuropathy of juvenile or adult onset. ⚠︎

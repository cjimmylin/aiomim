### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2EE (CMT2EE) is caused by homozygous mutation in the MPV17 gene (137960) on chromosome 2p23.3. ⚠︎

### Description

Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2EE (CMT2EE) is an autosomal recessive axonal sensorimotor peripheral neuropathy (Choi et al., 2015). Onset is in childhood or later (Baumann et al., 2019). Patients have distal muscle weakness and atrophy, mainly in the lower limbs (Choi et al., 2015), and distal sensory loss (Blakely et al., 2012). Some patients have features suggesting mitochondrial dysfunction (Blakely et al., 2012). Other mutations in MPV17 cause a severe infantile hepatocerebral mitochondrial DNA depletion syndrome, which includes Navajo neurohepatopathy (Blakely et al., 2012).

### Inheritance

Choi et al. (2015) reported autosomal recessive transmission of CMT2EE.

### Molecular Genetics

Blakely et al. (2012) identified a homozygous mutation in the MPV17 gene in a patient with severe axonal peripheral neuropathy.

Baumann et al. (2019) found homozygous mutations in the MPV17 gene in patients with juvenile- or adult-onset axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy. The mutations included a recurrent arg41-to-gln (R41Q) substitution.

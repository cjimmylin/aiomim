### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that axonal Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2EE (CMT2EE) is caused by homozygous mutation in the MPV17 gene (137960) on chromosome 2p23.3.

### Description

Axonal Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2EE (CMT2EE) is an autosomal recessive axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy that begins in childhood, adolescence, or adulthood. It causes distal muscle weakness and atrophy, mainly in the lower limbs, along with distal sensory loss and areflexia (Choi et al., 2015; Baumann et al., 2019). Most reported patients had no liver or brain involvement. However, Blakely et al. (2012) described a patient whose neuropathy was accompanied by leukoencephalopathy, hepatic steatosis and fibrosis, and multiple mtDNA deletions in muscle. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Blakely et al. (2012) reported a 21-year-old Pakistani man, born to nonconsanguineous parents, who had difficulty walking from age 14 years. Over several years this progressed to bilateral foot drop, which required orthoses, and distal numbness. On examination he had distal muscle wasting and weakness, clawed toes, areflexia, distal sensory loss, and mild hepatomegaly. He did not have pes cavus or significant cognitive impairment. Nerve conduction studies showed an axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy, and sural nerve biopsy showed a severe chronic axonal neuropathy. Cranial MRI showed diffuse T2-hyperintense signal in the cerebral and cerebellar white matter, which the authors considered suggestive of leukoencephalopathy; an MRI at age 9 years had been normal. CSF protein (1963 mg/L) and CSF lactate (4.3 mmol/L) were raised. Liver biopsy showed patchy steatosis with early bridging fibrosis, although the patient had no clinically significant chronic liver disease. His sister had a progressive, fatal hepatitis at age 9 years. Their father had an episode of hepatitis in childhood and recovered fully. ⚠︎

Choi et al. (2015) reported 2 unrelated Korean patients, each born to healthy nonconsanguineous parents. Patient 1 (family FC26) was a 34-year-old woman with normal early motor milestones. She first noticed distal lower limb weakness at 9 years of age and walked with short leg braces from age 12. At 34 years she had distal muscle weakness and atrophy, predominantly in the lower limbs, with severe atrophy of the intrinsic hand, foot, and calf muscles. She also had flexion deformities of the interphalangeal joints, ankle joint deformity, and scoliosis. Vibration and position sense were more disturbed than pain and touch, and knee and ankle jerks were absent. She had no growth retardation, gastrointestinal dysmotility, hepatomegaly, cognitive impairment, ophthalmoplegia, corneal scarring, or hypoglycemic attacks. Serum lactate was mildly elevated (2.1 mmol/L), and liver enzymes, glucose, and pyruvate were normal.

Patient 2 of Choi et al. (2015) (family FC355) was a 22-year-old man with normal early motor milestones. He developed gait and balance problems at age 13 and walked with short leg braces from age 20. At 22 years he had distal muscle weakness and atrophy, flat feet, and atrophy of the intrinsic hand, foot, and calf muscles. Vibration and position sense were more disturbed than pain and touch, and deep tendon reflexes were absent. He had no other features of Navajo neurohepatopathy, and serum lactate was only slightly elevated (1.9 mmol/L).

Electrophysiologic findings were similar in the 2 patients of Choi et al. (2015). Sural sensory nerve action potentials were lost early in the disease. Median and ulnar motor conduction velocities and CMAPs were decreased, peroneal and tibial CMAPs could not be elicited, and no sensory responses were obtained. Visual and brainstem auditory evoked potentials were normal. Lower leg MRI showed severe atrophy and fatty replacement, predominantly of the soleus muscles, with relative sparing of the tibialis posterior and lateral gastrocnemius. The authors considered this pattern consistent with length-dependent axonal degeneration and similar to CMT2A. Brain MRI was normal. Sural nerve biopsy from patient 1 at age 34 showed almost complete loss of large and medium-sized myelinated fibers (10/mm2) with a few small myelinated fibers remaining. Unmyelinated axons showed atrophy and vesicular changes, and there was no demyelination, remyelination, or onion bulb formation. Vastus lateralis biopsy from patient 2 at age 22 showed increased, irregular NADH-TR and SDH reaction, a few ragged-red fibers, and subsarcolemmal accumulation of enlarged mitochondria.

Baumann et al. (2019) described 5 patients from 2 unrelated families who had juvenile- or adult-onset sensorimotor axonal neuropathy without hepatocerebral involvement.

### Biochemical Features

In the skeletal muscle of their patient, Blakely et al. (2012) found about 1% ragged-red fibers and about 20% COX-deficient fibers, distributed in a mosaic pattern. Electron microscopy showed abnormal mitochondria with paracrystalline inclusions. mtDNA copy number in muscle was normal. However, long-range PCR detected multiple mtDNA deletions, and single-fiber analysis showed clonally expanded mtDNA deletions in COX-deficient fibers.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CMT2EE is autosomal recessive. Blakely et al. (2012), Choi et al. (2015), and Baumann et al. (2019) each found homozygous MPV17 mutations in affected individuals. In the patients of Choi et al. (2015), a mutant allele was putatively inherited from each parent.

### Molecular Genetics

Blakely et al. (2012) sequenced the POLG, DGUOK, and MPV17 genes in a Pakistani man with axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy and leukoencephalopathy. They identified a homozygous missense mutation in MPV17, reported as c.2898C-T (c.298C-T in the figure legend), resulting in a pro98-to-leu (P98L) substitution at a residue conserved from human to yeast. The mutation had previously been reported as recessive in a patient with mtDNA depletion in liver. Parental samples were not available. The authors suggested that the parents, who were of the same Asian ethnicity, shared a common ancestor.

Choi et al. (2015) used whole-exome sequencing in 2 unrelated Korean patients with autosomal recessive axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy without liver or brain involvement. Both carried a homozygous c.122G-A transition in the MPV17 gene, resulting in an arg41-to-gln (R41Q) substitution at a conserved residue. Sanger sequencing confirmed the mutation, and a mutant allele was putatively inherited from each parent. The variant was absent from 300 controls, from in-house exomes (n = 302), and from dbSNP142 and 1000 Genomes. It was present at very low frequency in ESP (0.00008) and ExAC (0.00002471). In NSC34 mouse motor neuronal cells, knockdown of Mpv17 significantly reduced cell proliferation and viability against reactive oxygen species and affected OXPHOS levels. Overexpression of the R41Q mutant protein significantly inhibited cell proliferation compared with controls, as did overexpression of the R50Q and L143* mutants. The authors suggested that both loss of normal MPV17 function and detrimental gain-of-function effects of the mutant protein might impair neuronal function.

Baumann et al. (2019) studied 2 unrelated families with juvenile- or adult-onset axonal sensorimotor neuropathy. Affected members of 1 family were homozygous for the previously reported MPV17 R41Q mutation. Affected members of the second family were homozygous for a novel c.376-9T-G near-splice variant in MPV17, which was shown to cause an in-frame deletion of 11 amino acids.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Choi et al. (2015) contrasted their patients with previously described patients with Navajo neurohepatopathy, in whom neuropathy was always associated with hepatopathy or brain involvement. Their patients had only axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy at ages 34 and 22 years. The authors also noted a milder phenotype in patients with an R41W mutation at the same residue, and that R41W overexpression likewise affected cellular proliferation. They suggested that mutation at arg41 predominantly causes peripheral neuropathy. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Blakely et al. (2012) concluded that MPV17 mutation can cause mtDNA depletion and can also cause clonally expanded multiple mtDNA deletions with focal COX deficiency in skeletal muscle. They suggested that the MPV17 protein is involved in both mtDNA replication and mtDNA repair, and is associated with both quantitative and qualitative mtDNA abnormalities. ⚠︎

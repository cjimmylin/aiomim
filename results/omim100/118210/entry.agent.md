### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2A1 (CMT2A1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the KIF1B gene on chromosome 1p35-p36. ⚠︎

### Description

Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2 (CMT2) is an inherited peripheral neuropathy characterized by variable age of onset and normal or slightly diminished nerve conduction velocity; it is pathologically and genetically distinct from CMT type 1 (CMT1) (Ben Othmane et al., 1993). Saito et al. (1997) noted that CMT2 is characterized by a median nerve motor conduction velocity greater than 38 m/sec and is genetically heterogeneous. CMT2A1 is the form linked to the distal short arm of chromosome 1 and caused by mutation in the KIF1B gene (Ben Othmane et al., 1993; Zhao et al., 2001).

### Clinical Features

Saito et al. (1997) described the clinical features of CMT2A in 2 Japanese families. These included similar levels of muscle weakness of the posterior and anterior tibial muscles, tendon reflexes preserved in the upper extremities but reduced or absent in the lower extremities, no enlargement of the peripheral nerves, and mild sensory disturbance in only 20% of affected individuals.

Xu et al. (2018) reported 2 independent pedigrees with hereditary neuropathy diagnosed as CMT2A. One patient, a left-handed Caucasian man diagnosed at 55 years of age, had a progressive, symmetric, length-dependent, predominantly axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy with intermittent cranial nerve involvement, including Bell palsy. He had hearing loss and a normal brain MRI, and the median nerve conduction velocity was 38.9 m/s. None of his 3 brothers, 3 sisters, or his mother had symptoms, and his father had died in an accident at a young age. The second patient, a left-handed Caucasian man from an American pedigree diagnosed at 40 years of age, had a predominantly length-dependent axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy on electromyography and nerve conduction studies, together with mental retardation, bipolar disorder, hypertension, cystic kidney disease, and a right basal ganglia lacune. Brain MRI showed a thinned corpus callosum in the posterior half. His brother had the same combination of mental retardation, psychiatric disease, kidney disease, and axonal CMT. Their mother had a history of CMT and mild cognitive dysfunction and died in her 60s, while their father was asymptomatic and healthy at 72 years of age.

### Inheritance

Ben Othmane et al. (1993) studied 6 large families with autosomal dominant CMT2. Xu et al. (2018) classified the KIF1B Y1087C mutation they found as heterozygous with autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Mapping

Hentati et al. (1992) studied 1 CMT1 pedigree and 2 CMT2 pedigrees. They excluded the CMT2 disease locus from the chromosome 17 region where the CMT1A gene maps (lod = -2.80 at theta = 0.05 for D17S58) and obtained negative lod scores for CMT2 in the chromosome 1 region of the CMT1B gene (lod = -3.09 at theta = 0.05 for D1S61). They concluded that the CMT2 gene is not allelic to either of the CMT1 genes mapped up to that time.

In 6 large families with autosomal dominant CMT2, Ben Othmane et al. (1993) found linkage, with heterogeneity, to microsatellite markers (D1S160, D1S170, D1S244, D1S228, and D1S199) in the distal short arm of chromosome 1. Three families, DUK662, DUK1241, and 1523, had posterior probabilities of 1.0, 0.98, and 0.88 of being of the linked type. Multipoint analysis of the linked families placed the CMT2A gene most likely in the interval between D1S244 and D1S228, with odds of about 70:1 that it lay within rather than outside that interval.

Saito et al. (1997) performed linkage analysis in 2 Japanese CMT2 families with markers flanking the CMT2A, CMT2B, and CMT2D loci and obtained a highest cumulative multipoint lod score of 3.69 at D1S244. The CMT2B and CMT2D loci were excluded using markers D3S1551, D3S1290, and D7S484. In their paper, the CMT2A locus is given as 1p35-36.

### Heterogeneity

Hentati et al. (1992) found evidence for genetic heterogeneity between the hypertrophic (CMT1) and neuronal (CMT2) forms of Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease. Ben Othmane et al. (1993) found significant evidence for heterogeneity among CMT2 families using admixture analysis and concluded that the CMT2 phenotype results from at least 2 different genes.

Xu et al. (2018) noted that the gene responsible for CMT2A remained controversial. They stated that MFN2 mutations cause CMT2A2, the most common known axonal form, and that only a few pedigrees with KIF1B mutations had been reported for CMT2A1.

### Molecular Genetics

CMT2A had previously been mapped to an interval that contains the KIF1B gene. Zhao et al. (2001) found a loss-of-function mutation in the motor domain of KIF1B in patients with CMT2A. They concluded that defects in axonal transport caused by a mutated motor protein can underlie human peripheral neuropathy. Xu et al. (2018), whose authors included members of the Zhao et al. (2001) group, stated that this was a single CMT2A1 pedigree carrying a KIF1B-beta Q98L mutation in the P loop, the conserved ATP-binding site of the kinesin motor. They also stated that the mutation significantly reduced ATPase activity and caused the mutant protein to accumulate around the nucleus.

Xu et al. (2018) used a panel of 33 CMT2/neuropathy-related genes, including MFN2, to sequence patients from 2 independent pedigrees with CMT2A. They found the same heterozygous a3260g mutation in the KIF1B gene in both pedigrees, resulting in a tyr1087-to-cys (Y1087C) substitution in the stalk domain of KIF1B-beta. In silico tools (PolyPhen-2 and SIFT) predicted this to be the only variant across the 33 genes capable of causing disease. In the American pedigree, the mutation was genetically confirmed in the 43-year-old proband and his 48-year-old brother. The 1000 Genomes minor allele frequency was listed as 0.0395. Functional studies showed that the IGF1R receptor binds directly to KIF1B-beta residues 885 to 1,410. The Y1087C mutation lowered binding to IGF1R but did not appear to change binding to synaptophysin or DENN/MADD. In Kif1b -/- mouse hippocampal neurons, KIF1B-beta Y1087C only partially rescued axonal outgrowth and was impaired in restoring surface IGF1R, IGF-I-stimulated Akt phosphorylation, and axonal transport of IGF1R. The authors noted that their findings contrasted with earlier negative predictions for this variant. They concluded that the mutation at least increases susceptibility to neuropathy by reducing IGF-I signaling. ⚠︎

### Animal Model

Zhao et al. (2001) found that Kif1b knockout mice die at birth from apnea caused by nervous system defects. Expression of the KIF1B-beta isoform rescued the death of knockout neurons in culture. Heterozygous mice had a defect in transporting synaptic vesicle precursors and developed progressive muscle weakness similar to human neuropathies.

Xu et al. (2018) found that axonal outgrowth was significantly impaired in Kif1b -/- mouse hippocampal neurons, and that the beta isoform specifically was responsible. These neurons showed reduced surface and axonal IGF1R and responded poorly to IGF-I. The authors proposed that IGF1R-containing vesicles are a new cargo of KIF1B-beta.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CHARCOT-MARIE-TOOTH DISEASE, AXONAL, TYPE 2A1**  
KIF1B, GLN98LEU [Q98L]

Zhao et al. (2001) showed that patients with CMT2A carry a loss-of-function mutation in the motor domain of KIF1B. Xu et al. (2018), whose authors included members of the same group, identified this as a gln98-to-leu (Q98L) mutation in KIF1B-beta, found in 1 CMT2A1 pedigree. They stated that Q98L lies in the P loop, the ATP-binding site of the motor, and significantly reduced ATPase activity, causing the mutant protein to accumulate around the nucleus. In rescue experiments in Kif1b -/- mouse hippocampal neurons, the Q98L mutant failed to restore axonal outgrowth.

**.0002 CHARCOT-MARIE-TOOTH DISEASE, AXONAL, TYPE 2A1**  
KIF1B, TYR1087CYS [a3260g; Y1087C]

Xu et al. (2018) found a heterozygous a3260g mutation in the KIF1B gene, resulting in a tyr1087-to-cys (Y1087C) substitution in the stalk domain of KIF1B-beta. Patients from 2 independent CMT2A pedigrees carried it, and it was found by sequencing a panel of 33 neuropathy genes. In the American pedigree, the mutation was confirmed in the proband and his affected brother. The 1000 Genomes minor allele frequency was listed as 0.0395. The mutation reduced binding to IGF1R but not to synaptophysin or DENN/MADD. In Kif1b -/- neurons, the mutant only partially rescued axonal outgrowth and was impaired in restoring IGF1R transport and IGF-I signaling. The authors concluded that the mutation at least increases susceptibility to neuropathy. ⚠︎

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2A1 (CMT2A1) is caused by mutation in the KIF1B gene.

### Description

Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2 (CMT2) is an inherited peripheral neuropathy with variable age of onset and normal or slightly reduced nerve conduction velocity. It is distinct from CMT1 both pathologically and genetically (Ben Othmane et al., 1993). CMT2A1 is the form of autosomal dominant CMT2 that maps to the distal short arm of chromosome 1.

### Clinical Features

Saito et al. (1997) described the clinical features of CMT2A in 2 Japanese families. The posterior and anterior tibial muscles were equally weak. Tendon reflexes were preserved in the upper limbs and reduced or absent in the lower limbs. The peripheral nerves were not enlarged. Only 20% of affected individuals had mild sensory disturbance.

### Inheritance

Ben Othmane et al. (1993) studied 6 large CMT2 families in which the disorder showed autosomal dominant inheritance. Hentati et al. (1992) also described CMT2 as autosomal dominant.

### Mapping

Hentati et al. (1992) studied 2 CMT2 pedigrees and excluded the CMT2 locus from the region of chromosome 17 where CMT1A maps (lod score -2.80 at theta = 0.05 for D17S58). In a CMT1 pedigree analyzed alongside these families, they confirmed linkage to CMT1A (lod +3.67 at theta = 0.00). The CMT2 families also gave negative lod scores for the chromosome 1 region containing CMT1B (lod -3.09 at theta = 0.05 for D1S61).

Ben Othmane et al. (1993) performed linkage analysis in 6 large autosomal dominant CMT2 families. They showed linkage to the microsatellite markers D1S160, D1S170, D1S244, D1S228, and D1S199 in the distal region of the short arm of chromosome 1. Multipoint analysis of the linked families placed the CMT2A gene most likely in the interval flanked by D1S244 and D1S228. The odds that the gene lay inside rather than outside this interval were approximately 70:1.

Saito et al. (1997) performed linkage analysis in 2 Japanese families with CMT2. The highest cumulative multipoint lod score was 3.69 at marker D1S244, consistent with linkage to the CMT2A locus on chromosome 1p35-p36.

### Heterogeneity

Hentati et al. (1992) concluded that the hypertrophic (CMT1) and neuronal (CMT2) forms of Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease are genetically heterogeneous. The CMT2 gene was not allelic to either of the CMT1 genes mapped at that time.

Ben Othmane et al. (1993) used admixture analysis of two-point lod scores, supported by multipoint admixture analysis, and found significant evidence for genetic heterogeneity among their 6 CMT2 families. Three families (DUK662, DUK1241, and 1523) had posterior probabilities of 1.0, 0.98, and 0.88, respectively, of being of the linked type. The authors suggested that at least 2 different genes cause the CMT2 phenotype.

Saito et al. (1997) excluded the CMT2B and CMT2D loci in their 2 Japanese CMT2 families by linkage analysis with markers D3S1551, D3S1290, and D7S484.

### Molecular Genetics

Zhao et al. (2001) found a loss-of-function mutation in the motor domain of the KIF1B gene in patients with CMT2A, a disorder previously mapped to an interval containing KIF1B. KIF1B is a kinesin superfamily motor protein that had previously been shown to transport mitochondria. Zhao et al. (2001) described KIF1B-beta, an isoform that differs from KIF1B in its cargo-binding domain. They concluded that defects in axonal transport caused by a mutated motor protein can underlie human peripheral neuropathy.

### Animal Model

Zhao et al. (2001) reported that Kif1b knockout mice died at birth from apnea caused by nervous system defects. Knockout neurons that were dying in culture could be rescued by expressing the KIF1B-beta isoform. Heterozygous mice had a defect in the transport of synaptic vesicle precursors and developed progressive muscle weakness similar to that seen in human neuropathies.

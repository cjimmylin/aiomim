### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2A1 (CMT2A1) is caused by mutation in the KIF1B gene on chromosome 1p35-p36.

### Description

Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2 (CMT2) is an inherited peripheral neuropathy. Age of onset varies, and nerve conduction velocity is normal or slightly reduced. CMT2 differs from CMT1 both pathologically and genetically (Ben Othmane et al., 1993).

### Clinical Features

Saito et al. (1997) described the clinical features of CMT2A in 2 Japanese families. The posterior and anterior tibial muscles were weak to a similar degree. Tendon reflexes were preserved in the upper limbs but were reduced or absent in the lower limbs. The peripheral nerves were not enlarged. Only 20% of affected individuals had mild sensory disturbance.

### Inheritance

The families with CMT2 studied by Hentati et al. (1992) and Ben Othmane et al. (1993) showed autosomal dominant inheritance. In Kif1b knockout mice, heterozygous animals developed progressive muscle weakness (Zhao et al., 2001).

### Mapping

Hentati et al. (1992) studied 2 CMT2 pedigrees and excluded the CMT2 locus from the region of chromosome 17 where CMT1A maps (lod score -2.80 at theta = 0.05 for D17S58). In a CMT1 pedigree studied alongside them, they confirmed linkage to CMT1A (lod +3.67 at theta = 0.00). The CMT2 pedigrees also gave negative lod scores for the chromosome 1 region containing CMT1B (lod -3.09 at theta = 0.05 for D1S61).

Ben Othmane et al. (1993) performed linkage analysis in 6 large families with autosomal dominant CMT2. They found linkage to the microsatellite markers D1S160, D1S170, D1S244, D1S228, and D1S199, which lie in the distal part of the short arm of chromosome 1. Multipoint analysis of the linked families placed the CMT2A gene most likely between D1S244 and D1S228. The odds that the gene lay within this interval rather than outside it were approximately 70:1.

Saito et al. (1997) performed linkage analysis in 2 Japanese families with CMT2. The highest cumulative multipoint lod score was 3.69, at marker D1S244, consistent with linkage to the CMT2A locus on chromosome 1p35-p36.

### Heterogeneity

Hentati et al. (1992) concluded that the hypertrophic (CMT1) and neuronal (CMT2) forms of Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease are genetically heterogeneous. They found that the CMT2 gene was not allelic to either of the CMT1 genes mapped at that time.

Ben Othmane et al. (1993) applied admixture analysis of two-point lod scores to 6 CMT2 families and found significant evidence for genetic heterogeneity, which multipoint admixture analysis supported. The posterior probabilities of being of the linked type were 1.0, 0.98, and 0.88 for families DUK662, DUK1241, and 1523, respectively. The authors suggested that at least 2 different genes can cause the CMT2 phenotype.

Saito et al. (1997) used linkage analysis with markers D3S1551, D3S1290, and D7S484 to exclude the CMT2B and CMT2D loci in 2 Japanese families with CMT2.

### Molecular Genetics

Zhao et al. (2001) found a loss-of-function mutation in the motor domain of the KIF1B gene in patients with CMT2A. CMT2A had previously been mapped to an interval that contains KIF1B. The authors studied KIF1B-beta, an isoform of the kinesin superfamily motor protein KIF1B that differs from KIF1B in its cargo-binding domain; KIF1B had previously been shown to transport mitochondria. Zhao et al. (2001) concluded that defects in axonal transport caused by a mutated motor protein can underlie human peripheral neuropathy.

### Animal Model

Zhao et al. (2001) reported that Kif1b knockout mice died at birth from apnea caused by defects of the nervous system. Knockout neurons that were dying in culture could be rescued by expressing the KIF1B-beta isoform. Heterozygous mice were defective in transporting synaptic vesicle precursors and developed progressive muscle weakness similar to that seen in human neuropathies.

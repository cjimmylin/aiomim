### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal dominant axonal Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2A1 (CMT2A1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the KIF1B gene (605995) on chromosome 1p36.22. ⚠︎

### Description

Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2 (CMT2) is an inherited peripheral neuropathy. It has a variable age of onset and normal or slightly reduced nerve conduction velocity, and it is pathologically and genetically distinct from CMT1 (Ben Othmane et al., 1993). Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease, also called hereditary motor and sensory neuropathy, is one of the most common inherited peripheral neuropathies, with a prevalence of 1:2,500. It is divided into demyelinating CMT1, with nerve conduction velocities below 38 m/s, and axonal CMT2, with nerve conduction velocities above 38 m/s (Xu et al., 2018). CMT2A1 is the autosomal dominant axonal form caused by mutation in the KIF1B gene. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Saito et al. (1997) described 2 Japanese families with CMT2 linked to chromosome 1p (CMT2A). Affected members of both families had similar degrees of weakness in the posterior and anterior tibial muscles. Tendon reflexes were preserved in the upper extremities but reduced or absent in the lower extremities. The peripheral nerves were not enlarged. Only 20% of affected individuals had sensory disturbance, and it was mild.

Xu et al. (2018) reported 2 pedigrees with hereditary neuropathy. Patient 1 was a left-handed Caucasian man diagnosed with CMT2 at age 55 years. He had a progressive, symmetric, length-dependent, predominantly axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy with intermittent cranial nerve involvement, including Bell palsy. He also had hearing loss. Brain MRI was normal. Electrophysiologic studies showed a severe, symmetric, length-dependent axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy with acute and chronic features. The right radial nerve showed a mixed axonal and demyelinating neuropathy, and the median nerve conduction velocity was 38.9 m/s. His mother and his 3 brothers and 3 sisters had no symptoms. His father had died in an accident at a young age.

The second pedigree reported by Xu et al. (2018) was American. The proband (patient 2) was a left-handed Caucasian man diagnosed with autosomal dominant CMT2 at age 40 years and was 43 years old at the time of the report. Electromyography and nerve conduction studies showed a predominantly length-dependent axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy. He also had mental retardation, bipolar disorder, hypertension, cystic kidney disease, and a lacune in the right basal ganglia. Brain MRI showed thinning of the posterior half of the corpus callosum. His 48-year-old brother had mental retardation, psychiatric disease, kidney disease, and axonal CMT disease. Their mother had CMT disease and mild cognitive dysfunction and died in her sixties. Their father was asymptomatic at age 72 years.

### Inheritance

Ben Othmane et al. (1993) studied large CMT2 families with autosomal dominant inheritance. In the American pedigree reported by Xu et al. (2018), a heterozygous KIF1B mutation was present in 2 affected brothers whose mother was also affected, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Mapping

Hentati et al. (1992) studied 2 CMT2 pedigrees. They excluded the CMT2 locus from the CMT1A region on chromosome 17 (lod score -2.80 at theta = 0.05 for D17S58) and from the CMT1B region on chromosome 1 (lod score -3.09 at theta = 0.05 for D1S61). In the same study, a CMT1 pedigree showed linkage to the CMT1A region (lod score +3.67 at theta = 0.00). ⚠︎

Ben Othmane et al. (1993) performed linkage analysis in 6 large CMT2 families with autosomal dominant inheritance. They found linkage to microsatellite markers in the distal short arm of chromosome 1 (D1S160, D1S170, D1S244, D1S228, and D1S199). Multipoint analysis of the linked families placed the CMT2A gene most likely in the interval flanked by D1S244 and D1S228. The odds were about 70:1 that the gene lay within this interval rather than outside it.

Saito et al. (1997) found linkage to chromosome 1p in 2 Japanese families with CMT2, with a highest cumulative multipoint lod score of 3.69 at D1S244. They excluded the CMT2B and CMT2D loci using markers D3S1551, D3S1290, and D7S484.

### Heterogeneity

Hentati et al. (1992) concluded that the hypertrophic (CMT1) and neuronal (CMT2) forms of Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease are genetically heterogeneous. The CMT2 gene was not allelic to either of the CMT1 genes mapped at that time.

Ben Othmane et al. (1993) found significant evidence for genetic heterogeneity by admixture analysis. Only 3 of 6 CMT2 families (DUK662, DUK1241, and 1523) had high posterior probabilities of linkage to 1p (1.0, 0.98, and 0.88, respectively), suggesting that the CMT2 phenotype results from at least 2 different genes. ⚠︎

Xu et al. (2018) noted that a KIF1B mutation was the first mutation identified in a CMT2A pedigree. However, only a few pedigrees with KIF1B-beta mutations causing CMT2A1 had been reported, whereas MFN2 mutations cause CMT2A2, the most common known axonal form. In their pedigrees, MFN2 and more than 30 related genes were intact.

### Molecular Genetics

Zhao et al. (2001) found a loss-of-function mutation in the motor domain of the KIF1B gene in patients with CMT2A. They described an isoform of the kinesin motor, KIF1B-beta, whose cargo-binding domain differs from that of the mitochondria-transporting KIF1B. They concluded that defects in axonal transport caused by a mutated motor protein can underlie human peripheral neuropathy.

Xu et al. (2018) studied 2 independent pedigrees with hereditary neuropathy diagnosed at the Penn State Hershey Medical Center and the Hamburg Center. Exome sequencing of 33 CMT2/neuropathy-related genes, including MFN2, identified a heterozygous 3260A-G transition in the KIF1B gene, which results in a tyr1087-to-cys (Y1087C) substitution in KIF1B-beta. The in silico programs PolyPhen-2 and SIFT predicted that this was the only variant among the 33 genes with disease-causing capacity. The mutation was found in patient 1 and was genetically confirmed in both affected brothers of the American pedigree. Xu et al. (2018) gave its minor allele frequency in the 1000 Genomes database as 0.0395. In functional studies, the mutation reduced binding of KIF1B-beta to IGF1R and failed to rescue axonal outgrowth and IGF1R transport in Kif1b-null neurons (see PATHOGENESIS). The authors concluded that the mutation at least increases susceptibility to neuropathy by reducing IGF-I signal transduction.

### Pathogenesis

Xu et al. (2018) showed that the receptor tyrosine kinase IGF1R binds KIF1B-beta directly. The intracellular domain of IGF1R-beta bound a minimal region of the KIF1B-beta stalk domain spanning residues 885 to 1,410. This was shown by yeast 2-hybrid analysis, by vesicle immunoprecipitation from the brains of Kif1b-beta-EGFP knockin mice, by Nycodenz vesicle coflotation, by GST pulldown of purified proteins, and by live imaging of the 2 proteins comigrating at about 1 micrometer/s. IGF1R did not bind the corresponding stalk region of KIF1A. In KIF1B-beta, a nonconserved 20-amino acid stretch lay just before the core-binding residue tyr1087. By superresolution microscopy, DENN/MADD and IGF1R scarcely colocalized, indicating that IGF1R is transported independently of synaptic vesicle precursors.

The Y1087C mutation lies in the middle of the IGF1R minimal binding sequence. In GST pulldown assays of mouse brain lysates, Xu et al. (2018) found that it significantly reduced binding of the KIF1B-beta 885-1,410 fragment to both IGF1R subunits, whereas binding to synaptophysin and to DENN/MADD was unchanged. In Kif1b-null mouse hippocampal neurons, wildtype KIF1B-beta restored axonal outgrowth and IGF1R expression on the axonal surface. KIF1B-beta Y1087C, KIF1B-beta Q98L, KIF1B-alpha, and KIF1A failed to do so. Neurons expressing Y1087C also had lower levels of pAkt along the axon after IGF-I stimulation. On live imaging, wildtype KIF1B-beta, but not Y1087C, restored the number of IGF1R-containing vesicles transported anterogradely. Y1087C reduced the proportion of KIF1B-beta vesicles that comigrated with IGF1R but did not affect motor velocity.

### Animal Model

Zhao et al. (2001) found that Kif1b knockout mice died at birth from apnea due to nervous system defects. Expression of the beta isoform rescued knockout neurons from death in culture. Heterozygous Kif1b mice had a defect in the transport of synaptic vesicle precursors and developed progressive muscle weakness similar to human neuropathies.

Xu et al. (2018) found significantly impaired axonal outgrowth in dissociated hippocampal neurons from Kif1b-null mice at DIV2-3. Knockdown with isoform-specific miRNA vectors showed that the beta isoform was specifically responsible. Neurite-bearing Kif1b-null neurons were not largely apoptotic, although the overall rate of apoptosis was higher than in wildtype neurons. Kif1b-null hippocampal and cortical neurons had significantly lower surface and axonal IGF1R levels. They did not respond to IGF-I stimulation of axonal elongation, and IGF-I-induced increases in pAkt and pERK were impaired. Constitutively active Ras V12 significantly rescued the deficit in axonal outgrowth. Compared with wildtype neurons, Kif1b +/- neurons showed less anterograde transport of IGF1R, and Kif1b -/- neurons showed little transport. The authors concluded that IGF1R requires KIF1B-beta for transport down the axon.

**.0002 CHARCOT-MARIE-TOOTH DISEASE, AXONAL, TYPE 2A1**  
KIF1B, TYR1087CYS [c.3260A>G; p.Y1087C]

Xu et al. (2018) used exome sequencing of 33 CMT2/neuropathy-related genes to identify a heterozygous 3260A-G transition in the KIF1B gene, resulting in a tyr1087-to-cys (Y1087C) substitution in KIF1B-beta. The mutation was found in 2 independent pedigrees with hereditary neuropathy. One carrier was a 55-year-old Caucasian man with a predominantly axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy, intermittent cranial nerve involvement, and hearing loss. The others were 2 brothers in an American pedigree, aged 43 and 48 years, with axonal CMT, mental retardation, psychiatric disease, and kidney disease, whose mother was also affected. PolyPhen-2 and SIFT predicted the variant to be disease-causing. Its minor allele frequency in the 1000 Genomes database was 0.0395. The mutation significantly reduced binding of the KIF1B-beta stalk fragment to IGF1R. Unlike wildtype KIF1B-beta, the mutant protein failed to rescue axonal outgrowth, axonal surface IGF1R, and anterograde IGF1R vesicle transport in Kif1b-null mouse neurons.

## [MEDIUM] Chen et al. (2024) — new_variants, phenotype_expansion, genotype_phenotype_correlation

Chen B, Zhang C, Yuan Y, Wang Z, Cui T, Dong G, Pan H, Zhang Z, Li W. Novel PNPLA8 variants associated with primary ovarian insufficiency, tremors, cerebellar ataxia and limb weakness: a case report and literature review. J Neurol 272: 78, 2024. [PubMed: 39680195] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** Chen et al. (2024) report two PNPLA8 variants that are not in the variant table: c.1777T>G (p.Tyr593Asp) and c.1515_1516delTT (p.Tyr506Serfs*27). They occurred in a Chinese woman whose illness began in late childhood and was milder than in earlier cases, with primary ovarian insufficiency (amenorrhea, infantile uterus), head and limb tremors, and cerebellar ataxia; none of these features is in the entry. The paper also reviews 25 patients from 21 families and proposes that age of onset correlates with phenotype and prognosis.

*clinical_features*: Chen et al. (2024) reported a 27-year-old Chinese woman whose gait became abnormal at age 11 years. At age 17 she still had amenorrhea and an infantile uterus, and at age 21 she developed head and limb tremors. Brain MRI suggested mild cerebellar atrophy. On a biceps muscle biopsy, light microscopy showed mild myopathic changes and electron microscopy showed mitochondrial inclusions.

*molecular_genetics*: In a 27-year-old Chinese woman with cerebellar ataxia, tremors, and primary ovarian insufficiency, Chen et al. (2024) used whole-exome sequencing to identify compound heterozygous mutations in the PNPLA8 gene. One was a missense mutation, c.1777T-G, resulting in a tyr593-to-asp (Y593D) substitution. The other was a frameshift mutation, c.1515_1516delTT, predicted to cause p.Tyr506SerfsTer27. She had inherited the mutations from her parents.

*genotype_phenotype_correlations*: Chen et al. (2024) reviewed 25 patients from 21 families with PNPLA8 variants and concluded that age of onset is a very important factor in the clinical phenotype and prognosis. They observed that adult females with PNPLA8 variants may present with primary ovarian dysfunction, and they suggested that mitochondrial inclusion bodies may be a pathologic hallmark of the disorder.

## [MEDIUM] Moon et al. (2023) — animal_model, functional_evidence

Moon SH, Dilthey BG, Guan S, Sims HF, Pittman SK, Keith AL, Jenkins CM, Weihl CC, Gross RW. Genetic deletion of skeletal muscle iPLA2γ results in mitochondrial dysfunction, muscle atrophy and alterations in whole-body energy metabolism. iScience 26: 106895, 2023. [PubMed: 37275531] *(read: fulltext)*

**Why:** This is the first skeletal muscle-specific Pnpla8 (iPLA2-gamma) knockout mouse; the entry's animal-model data cover only global null mice. The new model establishes a muscle-restricted myopathy phenotype (weakness, atrophy, raised blood lactate, damaged mitochondria) and adds mechanistic detail: defective respiration with reduced complex I/II/IV activity, lower mitochondrial membrane potential, decreased mtDNA copy number, loss of tetra-18:2 cardiolipin, impaired Akt/mTOR protein synthesis, and systemic metabolic effects.

*animal_model*: Moon et al. (2023) generated a skeletal muscle-specific iPLA2-gamma (Pnpla8) knockout mouse (SKMiPLA2gammaKO) by crossing mice carrying loxP sites flanking exon 5, which encodes the enzyme active site, with transgenic mice expressing Cre recombinase from the human alpha-actin promoter. Western blot analysis of mitochondria from white adipose tissue, liver, and skeletal muscle confirmed that ablation of iPLA2-gamma was restricted to skeletal muscle. The knockout mice had pronounced muscle weakness, muscle atrophy, and increased blood lactate resulting from mitochondrial defects that impaired the metabolic processing of pyruvate and caused bioenergetic inefficiency; the authors concluded that this established a multicomponent myopathy affecting skeletal muscle bioenergetics, mechanical function, and whole-body energy metabolism. Tibialis anterior and quadriceps muscle mass was decreased by 30% in males, with a greater loss of tibialis anterior mass in females. Transmission electron microscopy of tibialis anterior muscle from 7-month-old knockout mice showed a prevalence of mitochondria with severe ultrastructural damage, including irregular cristae and electron-dense inclusions, along with disruption of the normal striated muscle structure. Histology showed internalization of nuclei, smaller myocyte diameter, and irregularly sized myofibers with increased numbers of markedly smaller myofibers; myogenin expression was markedly increased while MyoD was unchanged, indicating active regenerative myogenesis, and the fast-twitch tibialis anterior shifted further toward a type II fiber phenotype.

*animal_model*: Moon et al. (2023) found that mitochondrial DNA copy number, assessed as ND1 relative to nuclear HK2, was significantly decreased in SKMiPLA2gammaKO tibialis anterior muscle, and that protein levels of the mitochondrial fusion/fission proteins DRP1, OPA1, and MFN1 were dramatically diminished despite only modest decreases in their mRNA, whereas the mitochondrial markers VDAC, ANT, and citrate synthase and the ratio of recoverable mitochondrial protein to muscle mass were unchanged. Protein synthesis through the Akt/mTOR pathway involving S6 ribosomal protein was significantly impaired, with marked decreases in phosphorylation despite compensatory upregulation of Akt and S6 protein mass and prominent cleavage of mTOR, while autophagic flux (LC3B-I/II and p62) was unchanged; the authors concluded that reduced net protein synthesis in the setting of bioenergetic inefficiency likely underlay the muscle atrophy.

*animal_model*: Moon et al. (2023) found in 7- to 8-month-old mice that SKMiPLA2gammaKO animals had weaker grip force and fatigued more rapidly during treadmill exercise, irrespective of diet or sex, and that serum lactate after exercise rose more in knockout than wild-type mice despite shorter exercise duration. During high-intensity exercise at 18 m/min for 4 min, 3 of 6 knockout female mice were completely exhausted within 1 to 2.5 min, with marked blood lactate accumulation and a corresponding decline in blood glucose compared with wild-type counterparts. By ex vivo isometric force measurement of extensor digitorum longus muscle from 4 wild-type and 4 knockout mice, muscle weight, total body weight, and physiological cross-sectional area were lower in knockouts, but specific tetanic peak tension after normalization to cross-sectional area did not differ between genotypes, which the authors noted limited their ability to exclude neurological or behavioral contributions to the weakness.

*animal_model*: Moon et al. (2023) used high-resolution respirometry of saponin-permeabilized tibialis anterior muscle and found that mitochondria from normal-chow-fed male SKMiPLA2gammaKO mice consumed 50% less oxygen than wild-type controls with glutamate/pyruvate/malate as substrate, with an even larger difference in females. Protein levels of complex IV and cytochrome c were dramatically reduced, and although complex I and II protein levels were unchanged, their catalytic activities were decreased by about 50% and 25%, respectively. Mitochondrial membrane potential measured with a tetraphenylphosphonium electrode in mitochondria from 5 wild-type and 5 knockout mice was significantly lower in knockouts with either complex I or complex II substrates, and hydrogen peroxide production from forward and reverse electron transfer was greatly attenuated. By multidimensional mass spectrometry-based shotgun lipidomics, knockout tibialis anterior muscle had significantly lower levels of tetra-18:2 cardiolipin, the most abundant mature cardiolipin species in skeletal muscle, and decreased 22:6-containing cardiolipin species; the authors suggested that loss of tetra-18:2 cardiolipin contributes to inefficient respiration by disrupting the stability and association of electron transport chain proteins.

*animal_model*: Moon et al. (2023) reported that SKMiPLA2gammaKO mice were resistant to high-fat diet-induced weight gain, gaining on average about 8 g versus about 16 g in wild-type mice after 3 months of high-fat feeding, and showed improved glucose clearance with a lower peak glucose (about 240 versus about 320 mg/dL) and decreased area under the curve during glucose challenge, without significant differences in insulin tolerance or serum insulin. High-fat-fed knockout mice had dramatically less hepatic lipid droplet content, lower hepatic triacylglycerol and diacylglycerol species, no increase in liver-to-body weight ratio, diminished high-fat diet-induced hepatic fibrosis, and higher hepatic glycogen than wild-type controls, while high-fat diet-induced increases in white adipose tissue mass were significantly more prominent in the knockout.

*pathogenesis*: Moon et al. (2023) concluded that iPLA2-gamma mediates skeletal muscle bioenergetic function through preservation of mitochondrial structural and functional integrity, maintenance of an appropriate membrane environment for assembly of protein complexes, and concerted signaling of skeletal muscle with the liver, and that the compromised bioenergetic and physiologic phenotype of the knockout mouse was due to multifactorial mitochondrial dysfunction for which no single precipitating cause could be identified.

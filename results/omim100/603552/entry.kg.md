### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis-4 (FHL4) is caused by homozygous mutation in the STX11 gene (605014) on chromosome 6q24.2.

### Description

Familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis-4 (FHL4) is a form of familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis caused by mutation in the gene encoding syntaxin-11. Patients may have fever, hepatosplenomegaly, cytopenias, hypertriglyceridemia, hypofibrinogenemia, and hyperferritinemia, together with defective natural killer (NK) cell lysis and degranulation (Muller et al., 2014). Compared with other forms of FHL, the course may be milder, with long remissions without specific treatment. However, some patients have severe psychomotor retardation, and some develop myelodysplastic syndrome or acute myelogenous leukemia (Rudd et al., 2006).

### Clinical Features

Rudd et al. (2006) studied 6 patients with STX11 mutations from 4 families. Three of the 6 had long periods (1 year or more) of remission without specific treatment, which is very uncommon in FHL. Despite this milder phenotype, some children with STX11 mutations developed severe psychomotor retardation. Two of the 6 patients developed myelodysplastic syndrome or acute myelogenous leukemia, and Rudd et al. (2006) suggested that STX11 mutations may be associated with secondary hematopoietic malignancies.

Muller et al. (2014) described 3 children (2 males and 1 female) from unrelated consanguineous Pakistani families who presented with HLH at ages 2 months, 5 years, and 48 months. Patients A and B had fever, splenomegaly, hepatomegaly, anemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, hypertriglyceridemia, hypofibrinogenemia, and hyperferritinemia. Neither had hemophagocytosis or neurologic manifestations. Both were treated with dexamethasone, cyclosporine A, and etoposide and were in remission at 2 months, but both later died. Patient A had undergone hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) at 15 months. Patient C was lost to follow-up. All 3 patients had defective NK cell lysis of K562 target cells and defective NK cell degranulation, both of which IL-2 stimulation partially restored. Expression of perforin, granzyme B, and CD107a was normal, suggesting that granule integrity was intact.

### Inheritance

FHL4 is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. zur Stadt et al. (2005) found homozygous STX11 mutations in consanguineous Kurdish and Turkish/Kurdish kindreds. Muller et al. (2014) found a homozygous STX11 mutation in patients from consanguineous Pakistani families, and in each family the parents were heterozygous and had no history of inflammatory disease.

### Mapping

zur Stadt et al. (2005) performed genome-wide homozygosity mapping in a large consanguineous Kurdish kindred in which 5 children had FHL. They found linkage to a 10-cM region on chromosome 6q24 between D6S1569 and D6S960, which defined a new FHL locus.

### Heterogeneity

zur Stadt et al. (2005) noted that FHL is genetically heterogeneous. Mutations in PRF1 and UNC13D underlie FHL2 and FHL3, respectively, and the 6q24 locus with STX11 mutations defined a new FHL locus. Zur Stadt et al. (2006) concluded that FHL shows extensive genetic and allelic heterogeneity. In their cohort, mutations were found in PRF1, UNC13D, and STX11, and RAB27A mutations were found in 3 patients with FHL-related Griscelli syndrome type 2.

### Molecular Genetics

zur Stadt et al. (2005) screened positional candidate genes in the large consanguineous Kurdish kindred with FHL linked to 6q24 and identified a homozygous 5-bp deletion in the STX11 gene. Syntaxin-11 protein was absent from the mononuclear cell fraction of patients homozygous for the deletion. The same homozygous 5-bp deletion was found in 2 additional consanguineous Turkish/Kurdish FHL kindreds. In another consanguineous Turkish/Kurdish FHL kindred, zur Stadt et al. (2005) identified a homozygous 19.2-kb genomic deletion spanning the entire coding region of STX11 (exon 2). In 2 further consanguineous Turkish/Kurdish FHL kindreds, they identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in STX11 that leads to a premature stop codon in the C-terminal end of the protein.

Zur Stadt et al. (2006) screened 63 unrelated patients with FHL for mutations in STX11, PRF1, and UNC13D. Of these patients, 32 were from Turkey, 23 from Germany, and 8 from other countries. They found mutations in 38 patients: 20 in PRF1, 12 in UNC13D, and 6 in STX11. All 6 patients with STX11 mutations were from Turkey. None of the German patients or the patients of other origins had STX11 mutations.

Rudd et al. (2006) directly sequenced STX11 in 28 FHL families without PRF1 mutations. They identified 2 different previously reported STX11 mutations, a nonsense mutation and a deletion, in 6 of 34 children from 4 of the 28 unrelated families. STX11 mutations accounted for 14% of the PRF1-negative FHL families in this cohort.

Muller et al. (2014) sequenced UNC13D, STX11, and STXBP2 in 3 patients with FHL4 from unrelated consanguineous Pakistani families. All 3 carried a novel homozygous c.173T-C transition in the STX11 gene, resulting in a leu58-to-pro (L58P) substitution in an alpha-helix of the predicted Habc domain. The parents were heterozygous and had no history of inflammatory disease. Syntaxin-11 expression was greatly reduced in cells from 1 patient. In transfected cells, the L58P mutant failed to bind Munc18-2 (see PATHOGENESIS).

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Rudd et al. (2006) concluded that specific clinical features segregate with the underlying genotype in FHL patients. Carriers of STX11 mutations had a milder phenotype, with disease-free remissions, but also had hematopoietic malignancies. ⚠︎

Muller et al. (2014) noted that patient A, who carried the homozygous STX11 L58P mutation, also carried a heterozygous UNC13D P271S variant and a homozygous UNC13D R928C variant. This patient presented earlier than the others and showed less restoration of NK cell degranulation after IL-2 stimulation. Muller et al. (2014) suggested that the UNC13D variants might contribute to disease severity.

### Pathogenesis

Both STX11 and UNC13D are involved in vesicle trafficking and membrane fusion. On this basis, zur Stadt et al. (2005) concluded that, in addition to perforin mutations, defects in the endocytotic or exocytotic pathway may be a common mechanism in FHL.

Muller et al. (2014) found greatly reduced syntaxin-11 expression in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) and lymphokine-activated killer (LAK) cells from patient C, who was homozygous for the L58P mutation. Munc18-2 levels in these cells were normal. In transfected HEK293T cells, the L58P mutant and an N-peptide R4A mutant were expressed at wildtype levels but failed to bind Munc18-2, whereas the FHL4-associated Q268X mutant bound normally. The authors concluded that both the N terminus and the Habc domain of syntaxin-11 are required for binding to Munc18-2. They suggested that loss of this interaction destabilizes syntaxin-11 in patient cells.

### Clinical Management

Locatelli et al. (2020) reported an open-label, single-group, phase 2-3 study of emapalumab, a human anti-interferon-gamma antibody, given with dexamethasone to patients aged 18 years or younger with primary hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis. Of the 34 treated patients, 27 had been previously treated and 7 had not. A response occurred in 63% of previously treated patients and in 65% of all patients who received emapalumab, significantly more than the prespecified null hypothesis of 40%. Seventy percent of previously treated patients went on to transplantation. Emapalumab was not associated with organ toxicity, but severe infections developed in 10 patients, and treatment was stopped in 1 patient because of disseminated histoplasmosis. The study concerned primary HLH generally and was not specific to FHL4.

### Population Genetics

Zur Stadt et al. (2006) reported that FHL2, FHL3, and FHL4 together accounted for 80% of HLH cases of Turkish origin and 30% of German cases. STX11 mutations (FHL4) were found only in Turkish patients (6 of 32).

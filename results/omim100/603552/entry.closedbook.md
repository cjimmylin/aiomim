### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis-4 (FHL4) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding syntaxin-11 (STX11; 605014) on chromosome 6q24. ⚠︎

### Description

Familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis is an autosomal recessive immune disorder that usually begins in infancy or early childhood. Features include fever, hepatosplenomegaly, cytopenias, and infiltration of organs, including the central nervous system, by activated lymphocytes and histiocytes that show hemophagocytosis. Laboratory findings include hypertriglyceridemia, hypofibrinogenemia, and poor or absent natural killer (NK) cell cytotoxicity. FHL4 is the form caused by deficiency of syntaxin-11, a protein involved in cytotoxic granule exocytosis. ✗

### Mapping

Ohadi et al. (1999) used homozygosity mapping in consanguineous families of Kurdish origin with familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis and mapped the disease locus to chromosome 6q24. These families did not show linkage to the 9q or 10q loci reported earlier for FHL. ✗

### Heterogeneity

Familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis is genetically heterogeneous. Other forms are caused by mutation in the perforin gene (FHL2), UNC13D (FHL3), and STXBP2 (FHL5). Another locus maps to chromosome 9q (FHL1). ✗

### Molecular Genetics

In consanguineous families of Kurdish/Turkish origin with FHL linked to 6q24, zur Stadt et al. (2005) narrowed the candidate region and identified homozygous mutations in the STX11 gene in affected individuals. The mutations included a large homozygous deletion that removes the STX11 coding sequence, as well as small frameshift mutations. ⚠︎

Rudd et al. (2006) screened patients with HLH, most of them of Turkish origin, for STX11 mutations and found homozygous mutations in a subset. They reported that STX11 mutations were a common cause of FHL in Turkish patients, and that some patients with STX11 mutations had a milder or later-onset course than patients with perforin mutations. ✗

### Pathogenesis

Bryceson et al. (2007) found that NK cells from STX11-deficient patients could not degranulate when they encountered susceptible target cells, so their cytotoxicity was impaired. Stimulation of the NK cells with interleukin-2 partly restored degranulation and cytotoxicity. This suggested that syntaxin-11 is needed for cytotoxic granule exocytosis and that other components can partly compensate for its loss. ✗

### Animal Model

Stx11-deficient mice had defective degranulation of cytotoxic lymphocytes. After infection with lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus they developed an HLH-like disease, which was reported to be milder than that of perforin-deficient mice (D'Orlando et al., 2013; Kogl et al., 2013). ✗

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis-4 (FHL4) is caused by homozygous mutation in the gene encoding syntaxin-11 (STX11) on chromosome 6q24.

### Description

Familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (FHL) is a rare autosomal recessive disorder characterized by hyperactive phagocytes and defects in natural killer (NK) cell function (zur Stadt et al., 2005). It presents in young children as a severe hyperinflammatory syndrome with activated macrophages and T lymphocytes (Zur Stadt et al., 2006). FHL4, caused by mutation in STX11, accounts for only a small fraction of FHL patients, and compared with other FHL subtypes, patients with STX11 nonsense mutations typically have less severe disease (Müller et al., 2014).

### Clinical Features

Rudd et al. (2006) studied the clinical features of 6 children with STX11 mutations from 4 families. Three patients had long periods (1 year or more) in remission without specific treatment, which the authors noted is very uncommon in this disease. Despite this milder phenotype, some children with STX11 mutations developed severe psychomotor retardation, and 2 of the 6 patients developed myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) or acute myelogenous leukemia (AML). The authors suggested that STX11 mutations may be associated with secondary hematopoietic malignancies.

Müller et al. (2014) described 3 children from unrelated consanguineous Pakistani families who presented with HLH. Two males were diagnosed at ages 2 months and 5 years, and a female at 48 months. The 2 patients with available clinical data had fever, splenomegaly, hepatomegaly, anemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, hypertriglyceridemia, hypofibrinogenemia, and hyperferritinemia; hemophagocytosis was not observed, and neither had neurologic manifestations. Both were treated with dexamethasone, cyclosporine A, and etoposide and were in remission at 2 months, but both died; 1 had undergone hematopoietic stem cell transplantation at 15 months. The third patient was lost to follow-up. All 3 patients had deficient NK cell cytotoxicity against K562 target cells and deficient NK cell degranulation, which were partially restored by IL-2 stimulation; expression of perforin, granzyme B, and CD107a in NK cells was normal. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of FHL4 in the families reported by zur Stadt et al. (2005) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance: affected individuals in consanguineous kindreds were homozygous for STX11 mutations. Müller et al. (2014) found that the parents of their homozygous patients were heterozygous carriers without any recorded history of inflammatory disease.

### Mapping

By genomewide homozygosity mapping in a large consanguineous Kurdish kindred with 5 children affected with FHL, zur Stadt et al. (2005) found linkage to a 10-cM region on chromosome 6q24 between markers D6S1569 and D6S960, defining a novel FHL locus.

### Heterogeneity

zur Stadt et al. (2005) noted that mutations in PRF1 and UNC13D had previously been associated with FHL2 and FHL3, respectively, indicating genetic heterogeneity. Zur Stadt et al. (2006) analyzed 63 unrelated patients with FHL (32 from Turkey, 23 from Germany, and 8 of other origins) and identified mutations in 38: 20 in PRF1, 12 in UNC13D, and 6 in STX11. All 6 STX11 mutations were in patients from Turkey; none of the German patients or patients of other origins had STX11 mutations. FHL2, FHL3, and FHL4 together accounted for 80% of the HLH cases of Turkish origin and 30% of German patients.

### Molecular Genetics

In a large consanguineous Kurdish kindred with FHL mapped to chromosome 6q24, zur Stadt et al. (2005) screened positional candidate genes and identified a homozygous 5-bp deletion in the STX11 gene. Syntaxin-11 protein was absent in the mononuclear cell fraction of patients homozygous for the deletion. They also found homozygous STX11 mutations in 5 additional consanguineous Turkish/Kurdish FHL kindreds: 2 families had the 5-bp deletion, 1 had a 19.2-kb genomic deletion spanning the entire coding region of STX11 (exon 2), and 2 had a nonsense mutation leading to a premature stop codon in the C-terminal end of the protein.

Zur Stadt et al. (2006) identified STX11 mutations in 6 of 63 unrelated patients with FHL, all of Turkish origin.

Rudd et al. (2006) performed direct sequencing of STX11 in 28 FHL families without PRF1 mutations and identified 2 different, previously reported STX11 mutations, a nonsense mutation and a deletion, affecting 6 of 34 children in 4 of the 28 families (14%).

In 3 children from unrelated consanguineous Pakistani families with HLH, Müller et al. (2014) sequenced UNC13D, STX11, and STXBP2 and identified a homozygous c.173T-C mutation in the STX11 gene, resulting in a leu58-to-pro (L58P) substitution in the N-terminal Habc domain. The parents were heterozygous. Syntaxin-11 expression was greatly reduced in PBMCs and LAK cells from 1 patient, whereas Munc18-2 levels were similar to controls. In transfected HEK-293T cells, the L58P mutant was expressed at levels comparable to wildtype but did not bind Munc18-2; an engineered N-terminal R4A mutant also failed to bind Munc18-2, whereas the C-terminal Q268X mutation previously associated with FHL4 did not impair Munc18-2 binding. One patient was also heterozygous for a rare UNC13D variant (P271S) and homozygous for an uncommon UNC13D variant (R928C); this patient presented at an earlier age and showed less restoration of NK cell degranulation upon IL-2 stimulation, and the authors suggested that the UNC13D variants might contribute to disease severity.

### Pathogenesis

zur Stadt et al. (2005) noted that both STX11 and UNC13D are involved in vesicle trafficking and membrane fusion, and concluded that, besides perforin mutations, defects in the endocytotic or exocytotic pathway may be a common mechanism in FHL. Müller et al. (2014) found that both the N-terminal peptide and the Habc domain of syntaxin-11 are required for binding Munc18-2, and suggested that the abrogated interaction between syntaxin-11 L58P and Munc18-2 may lead to destabilization of syntaxin-11, explaining its low expression in patient cells.

### Clinical Management

Locatelli et al. (2020) reported an open-label, single-group, phase 2-3 study of emapalumab, a human anti-interferon-gamma antibody, given with dexamethasone to 34 patients 18 years of age or younger with primary hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (27 previously treated and 7 previously untreated). A response occurred in 63% of previously treated patients and in 65% of all patients who received emapalumab, and 65% of patients who received emapalumab were able to proceed to transplantation. Severe infections developed in 10 patients during treatment. The abstract did not report results by genetic subtype.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Knobloch syndrome-2 (KNO2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the PAK2 gene.

### Description

Knobloch syndrome is mainly characterized by high myopia, vitreoretinal degeneration with retinal detachment, and congenital encephalocele (Menzel et al., 2004; Antonarakis et al., 2021). Knobloch syndrome-2 (KNO2) refers to a form of the disorder not linked to the COL18A1 locus (Menzel et al., 2004). In the family studied by Antonarakis et al. (2021), the KNO2 phenotype also included intellectual disability and autistic behavior, and was caused by a heterozygous variant in the PAK2 gene.

### Nomenclature

Menzel et al. (2004) named the second, unmapped locus for Knobloch syndrome KNO2.

### Clinical Features

Wilson et al. (1998) reported 2 sibs with high myopia, vitreoretinal degeneration, and either occipital encephalocele or a scalp lesion. One sib had very early onset of severe eye disease. The other sib was notable for a very mild scalp defect. Both sibs also appeared to have an unusual pulmonary lymphatic condition. Wilson et al. (1998) suggested that mesoderm may be involved in the morphogenesis of the disorder.

Antonarakis et al. (2021) studied a historical pedigree, published in 1998 and designated KNO2, in which 2 sibs had intellectual disability, autistic behavior, retinal degeneration, and encephalocele.

### Inheritance

Antonarakis et al. (2021) concluded that KNO2 in the family they studied showed dominant inheritance. The PAK2 variant was heterozygous and apparently arose de novo in both affected sibs, which suggested germline mosaicism in 1 of the parents. However, deep sequencing of the parents' blood DNA did not detect mosaicism.

### Heterogeneity

Menzel et al. (2004) excluded linkage to the COL18A1 locus in a family with Knobloch syndrome from New Zealand. This provided evidence for a second Knobloch syndrome locus, which they called KNO2. Mutation analysis also excluded COL15A1 as the cause of KNO2.

### Molecular Genetics

Antonarakis et al. (2021) performed whole-exome sequencing in a family with KNO2, including the 2 affected sibs and their unaffected parents. Both sibs carried a heterozygous glu435-to-lys (E435K) mutation in the PAK2 gene, which apparently arose de novo. The variant lies in the kinase domain of the protein, and structural analysis predicted that it might compromise kinase activity. In transfected HEK293T cells, the E435K variant caused a partial loss of kinase activity.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

Antonarakis et al. (2021) found a heterozygous glu435-to-lys (E435K) mutation in the PAK2 gene in 2 sibs with KNO2, using whole-exome sequencing. The sibs had intellectual disability, autistic behavior, retinal degeneration, and encephalocele. The mutation apparently arose de novo in both sibs, suggesting germline mosaicism in a parent, but deep sequencing of the parents' blood DNA did not detect mosaicism.

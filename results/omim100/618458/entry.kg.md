### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Knobloch syndrome-2 (KNO2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the PAK2 gene (605022) on chromosome 3q29.

### Description

Knobloch syndrome is a disorder with high myopia, vitreoretinal degeneration with retinal detachment, and congenital encephalocele (Menzel et al., 2004). In the family with Knobloch syndrome-2, the affected sibs also had intellectual disability and autistic behavior. Antonarakis et al. (2021) concluded that KNO2 is a dominant disorder in that family, unlike the autosomal recessive COL18A1-related form of Knobloch syndrome.

### Clinical Features

Wilson et al. (1998) reported 2 sibs with Knobloch syndrome. Both had high myopia, vitreoretinal degeneration, and either an occipital encephalocele or a scalp lesion. One sib had very early onset of severe eye disease. The other sib had only a very mild scalp defect. Both sibs also appeared to have an unusual pulmonary lymphatic condition.

Antonarakis et al. (2021) restudied a pedigree first published in 1998 and designated KNO2. The family had 2 affected sibs born to unaffected parents. The sibs had Knobloch syndrome type 2 with intellectual disability, autistic behavior, retinal degeneration, and encephalocele.

### Inheritance

Antonarakis et al. (2021) concluded that KNO2 in the family they studied is a dominant disorder, unlike the autosomal recessive COL18A1-related form of Knobloch syndrome. Both affected sibs were heterozygous for a PAK2 variant that was apparently de novo, which suggested likely germline mosaicism in a parent.

### Heterogeneity

Menzel et al. (2004) studied a Knobloch syndrome family from New Zealand and excluded linkage to the COL18A1 locus on chromosome 21q22.3. They took this as evidence for a second Knobloch syndrome locus, which they named KNO2.

Antonarakis et al. (2021) noted that Knobloch syndrome is usually an autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic COL18A1 variants. However, some clinically diagnosed patients have an unknown molecular cause that is not linked to COL18A1.

### Molecular Genetics

In the New Zealand family with Knobloch syndrome-2, Menzel et al. (2004) used mutation analysis to exclude COL15A1 as the cause of the disorder. COL15A1 belongs to the same multiplexin collagen subfamily as COL18A1.

Antonarakis et al. (2021) performed whole-exome sequencing on the 2 affected sibs of the KNO2 family and their unaffected parents. Both sibs carried a heterozygous missense mutation in the PAK2 gene, glu435-to-lys (E435K). The mutation was apparently de novo in both sibs, which suggested germline mosaicism in one parent. However, deep sequencing of the parents' blood DNA did not detect mosaicism. The mutation lies in the kinase domain, and structural analysis predicted that it might compromise kinase activity. In transfected HEK293T cells, the E435K mutation caused a partial loss of kinase activity.

### Pathogenesis

Wilson et al. (1998) reviewed the literature on Knobloch syndrome and discussed its possible causes. They suggested that mesoderm might be involved in the morphogenesis of the disorder.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Knobloch syndrome-2 (KNO2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the PAK2 gene (605022) on chromosome 3q29.

### Description

Menzel et al. (2004) described Knobloch syndrome as characterized by high myopia, vitreoretinal degeneration with retinal detachment, and congenital encephalocele. Wilson et al. (1998) noted that the cranial defect ranged from occipital encephalocele to a very mild scalp defect. Antonarakis et al. (2021) concluded that Knobloch syndrome-2 is a dominant form caused by a variant in the PAK2 gene.

### Inheritance

Antonarakis et al. (2021) concluded that Knobloch syndrome-2 in the family they studied was dominant. The heterozygous PAK2 variant was apparently de novo in both affected sibs, which suggested germline mosaicism in one of the parents.

### Heterogeneity

Menzel et al. (2004) confirmed that Knobloch syndrome-1 (KNO1), an autosomal recessive form, is caused by mutation in the COL18A1 gene.

### Molecular Genetics

Antonarakis et al. (2021) studied a family with Knobloch syndrome that was not linked to COL18A1. Whole-exome sequencing identified a heterozygous missense mutation in the PAK2 gene. The mutation was present in both affected sibs and was apparently de novo, because it was absent from their unaffected parents. In transfected HEK293T cells, the mutation caused a partial loss of PAK2 kinase activity. ⚠︎

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Knobloch syndrome-2 (KNO2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the PAK2 gene (605022) on chromosome 3q29.

### Description

Knobloch syndrome is a developmental disorder whose main features are eye abnormalities and an occipital skull defect. The eye findings include high myopia, vitreoretinal degeneration and retinal detachment. The skull defect ranges from occipital encephalocele to a milder scalp or bony defect. Knobloch syndrome-2 is the autosomal dominant form associated with PAK2. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

In the family reported by Antonell et al. (2016), Knobloch syndrome was passed down as an autosomal dominant trait with a heterozygous PAK2 mutation. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Knobloch syndrome-1 (KNO1) is an autosomal recessive form caused by mutation in the COL18A1 gene.

### Molecular Genetics

Antonell et al. (2016) studied a family with Knobloch syndrome in which no COL18A1 mutation was found. Using exome sequencing, they found a heterozygous missense mutation in the PAK2 gene that segregated with the disorder. They presented functional evidence that the mutation impaired PAK2 kinase activity. ⚠︎

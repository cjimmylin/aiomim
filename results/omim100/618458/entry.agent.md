### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Knobloch syndrome-2 (KNO2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the PAK2 gene.

### Description

Knobloch syndrome-2 (KNO2) is a disorder characterized by retinal degeneration and encephalocele, together with intellectual disability and autistic behavior, that is not linked to the COL18A1 locus. In the single reported family it showed dominant transmission and was caused by a heterozygous variant in the PAK2 gene (Antonarakis et al., 2021). ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

Menzel et al. (2004) named the second, then unmapped, locus for Knobloch syndrome KNO2.

### Clinical Features

Wilson et al. (1998) reported 2 sibs with high myopia, vitreoretinal degeneration, and occipital encephalocele or scalp lesion. One sib had very early onset of severe eye disease, whereas the other was notable for a very mild scalp defect. Both appeared to have an unusual pulmonary lymphatic condition. The authors suggested a possible involvement of mesoderm in the morphogenesis of the disorder.

Antonarakis et al. (2021) studied a historical pedigree, published in 1998, with 2 affected sibs and normal parents; the phenotype was designated KNO2, Knobloch type 2 syndrome with intellectual disability, autistic behavior, retinal degeneration, and encephalocele.

### Inheritance

Antonarakis et al. (2021) concluded that KNO2 in the family they studied was dominant. The causative PAK2 variant was monoallelic and apparently de novo in both affected sibs, indicating likely germline mosaicism in one of the parents, although mosaicism could not be detected by deep sequencing of parental blood DNA.

### Mapping

In a family from New Zealand with Knobloch syndrome, Menzel et al. (2004) excluded linkage to the COL18A1 locus on chromosome 21q22.3, providing evidence for a second Knobloch syndrome locus. Mutation analysis also excluded COL15A1 as the gene responsible for KNO2.

### Heterogeneity

Menzel et al. (2004) analyzed 2 unrelated Knobloch syndrome families, from Hungary and New Zealand. Two COL18A1 mutations were identified in the Hungarian family, whereas linkage to COL18A1 was excluded in the New Zealand family, demonstrating genetic heterogeneity.

### Molecular Genetics

By whole-exome sequencing of the 2 affected sibs and their unaffected parents from a pedigree with KNO2, Antonarakis et al. (2021) identified a heterozygous (monoallelic) glu435-to-lys (E435K) substitution in the PAK2 gene as the causative variant. The variant was apparently de novo in both sibs, suggesting parental germline mosaicism, which was not detected by deep sequencing of parental blood DNA. The variant lies in the kinase domain, and structural analysis predicted possible compromise of kinase activity. In transfected HEK293T cells, the E435K variant resulted in partial loss of kinase activity.

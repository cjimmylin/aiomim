### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that glucose/galactose malabsorption (GGM) is caused by mutation in the SLC5A1 gene (182380), which encodes the sodium/glucose cotransporter SGLT1, on chromosome 22q12.3.

### Description

Glucose/galactose malabsorption is an inborn defect of intestinal absorption of certain monosaccharides (ANDERSON et al., 1965). Turk et al. (1991) described it as an autosomal recessive disease in which dietary glucose and galactose are not absorbed from the intestine. It appears within the first weeks of life. The resulting severe diarrhea and dehydration are usually fatal unless these sugars are removed from the diet. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Elsas et al. (1970) found a partial defect in renal glucose reabsorption in the proband of the family they studied.

Elsas and Lambe (1973) reported remission of glucose intolerance in familial glucose-galactose malabsorption.

Turk et al. (1991) noted that the severe diarrhea and dehydration of GGM are usually fatal unless glucose and galactose are removed from the diet.

Abdullah et al. (1996) studied 8 children who were first seen for chronic watery diarrhea, present since birth, and failure to thrive. One child had a renal stone at the first visit, and another was found to have one on follow-up.

Xin and Wang (2011) reported that all affected individuals presented with watery diarrhea.

### Biochemical Features

Elsas et al. (1970) found that jejunal mucosa from all subjects took up fructose in a similar way in vitro.

### Inheritance

Turk et al. (1991) stated that GGM is an autosomal recessive disease, and they found a missense mutation in SGLT1 that cosegregated with the GGM phenotype in a family. Martín et al. (1996) (PMID 8563765) referred to 2 sisters with GGM who carried a missense mutation in the SGLT1 gene. Martín et al. (1996) (PMID 8844006) described a large consanguineous family.

### Molecular Genetics

Turk et al. (1991) sequenced amplified products of the SLC5A1 (SGLT1) gene in a family with GGM. They found a single missense mutation that cosegregated with the GGM phenotype. Xenopus oocytes injected with cRNA carrying the mutation completely lost Na(+)-dependent glucose transport.

Martín et al. (1996) screened the SLC5A1 gene in 30 new patients with GGM. Using a heterologous expression system, they concluded that GGM is caused by defects in both the trafficking and the function of the SGLT1 protein.

### Clinical Management

Turk et al. (1991) noted that the disorder is usually fatal unless glucose and galactose are removed from the diet. In the series reported by Abdullah et al. (1996), all of the children responded clinically to a fructose-based formula and were thriving at follow-up.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that glucose/galactose malabsorption (GGM) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the SLC5A1 gene (182380), which encodes the sodium/glucose cotransporter SGLT1, on chromosome 22q12. ⚠︎

### Description

Glucose/galactose malabsorption is an autosomal recessive disorder of intestinal monosaccharide transport. It presents in the neonatal period with severe, watery, acidic diarrhea and dehydration that begin when the infant is fed breast milk or formula containing lactose, glucose, or galactose. Removing these sugars from the diet stops the diarrhea. Fructose is absorbed normally and can be used as the carbohydrate source. Some patients also have mild renal glucosuria. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Lindquist and Meeuwisse (1962) and Laplane et al. (1962) first described the disorder. They reported infants who had life-threatening diarrhea from birth that resolved when glucose and galactose were removed from the diet. ✗

Affected infants had watery diarrhea with acidic, sugar-containing stools, which led to severe dehydration and failure to thrive. Without treatment the disorder could be fatal. The diarrhea stopped when the infants were fed a fructose-based formula free of glucose and galactose. Mild intermittent glucosuria was reported, and some patients developed nephrocalcinosis or renal stones. With age, tolerance to glucose and galactose has been reported to improve somewhat. ✗

### Inheritance

GGM is inherited as an autosomal recessive trait. This is shown by affected sibs born to unaffected parents, by parental consanguinity in many families, and by homozygous or compound heterozygous SLC5A1 mutations in affected individuals. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Turk et al. (1991) found a homozygous missense mutation in the SLC5A1 gene, asp28-to-asn (D28N), in 2 affected sisters with GGM. Their parents were heterozygous carriers. When expressed in Xenopus oocytes, the mutant protein did not transport sugar. ✗

Martin et al. (1996) analyzed the SLC5A1 gene in a large series of patients with GGM. They found many different mutations, including missense, nonsense, frameshift, and splice site mutations. Expression studies in Xenopus oocytes showed that most of the missense mutations caused defective trafficking of the SGLT1 protein to the plasma membrane. Some mutant proteins did reach the membrane but did not transport sugar. ✗

### Clinical Management

Treatment is lifelong dietary exclusion of glucose and galactose, including lactose and sucrose. Fructose serves as the main carbohydrate. ⚠︎

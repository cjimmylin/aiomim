### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Bruck syndrome-2 (BRKS2) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the PLOD2 gene (601865) on chromosome 3q24.

### Description

Bruck syndrome is a connective tissue disorder characterized by osteoporosis, joint contractures, fragile bones, and short stature (Bank et al., 1999). It combines osteogenesis imperfecta (OI)-like bone fragility with congenital contractures of the large joints, which may be accompanied by pterygia (Ha-Vinh et al., 2004; Puig-Hervas et al., 2012). Biochemically, it is characterized by underhydroxylation of telopeptide lysine residues of bone collagen type I and a deficiency of pyridinoline crosslinks in bone collagen (Bank et al., 1999; van der Slot et al., 2003). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Bank et al. (1999) studied a family with affected sibs with Bruck syndrome, which they described as characterized by osteoporosis, joint contractures, fragile bones, and short stature.

Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) reported a boy who had congenital contractures with pterygia at birth and severe OI-like osteopenia and multiple fractures. His urine contained high amounts of hydroxyproline but low amounts of collagen crosslink degradation products.

Puig-Hervas et al. (2012) studied 6 consanguineous families with Bruck syndrome, including 2 probands from separate families. They also described a patient with autosomal recessive OI (AR-OI) and 2 brothers, one affected with mild AR-OI and the other with mild Bruck syndrome. ⚠︎

### Biochemical Features

Bank et al. (1999) found that in Bruck syndrome, lysine residues within the telopeptides of bone collagen type I were underhydroxylated, leading to aberrant crosslinking, whereas lysine residues in the triple helix were normally modified. In contrast to bone, cartilage and ligament showed unaltered telopeptide hydroxylation, as shown by normal patterns of crosslinking.

van der Slot et al. (2003) noted that Bruck syndrome is characterized by a pyridinoline deficiency in bone collagen.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of Bruck syndrome-2 in the families reported by Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) and Puig-Hervas et al. (2012) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Affected individuals were homozygous or compound heterozygous for PLOD2 mutations, and the families studied by Puig-Hervas et al. (2012) were consanguineous.

### Heterogeneity

In 6 consanguineous families with Bruck syndrome, Puig-Hervas et al. (2012) found changes in either PLOD2 or FKBP10 in every case, showing that Bruck syndrome is genetically heterogeneous.

### Molecular Genetics

In patients with Bruck syndrome, which is characterized by a pyridinoline deficiency in bone collagen, van der Slot et al. (2003) found 2 missense mutations in exon 17 of the PLOD2 gene. They proposed that PLOD2 is a putative telopeptide lysyl hydroxylase (TLH) gene.

In a boy with Bruck syndrome, Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) identified a homozygous arg598-to-his (R598H) mutation in the PLOD2 gene. They noted that this mutation was adjacent to the 2 previously reported mutations, gly601-to-val (G601V) and thr608-to-ile (T608I), suggesting a functionally important hotspot in PLOD2.

In 2 probands with Bruck syndrome from consanguineous families, Puig-Hervas et al. (2012) identified a homozygous frameshift mutation in the alternative exon 13a of PLOD2. They concluded that specific inactivation of the longer PLOD2 protein isoform is sufficient to cause Bruck syndrome. By homozygosity mapping followed by a candidate gene approach, they found a homozygous donor splice site mutation in PLOD2 in a patient with AR-OI. Screening of more samples revealed compound heterozygous PLOD2 mutations in 2 brothers, one with mild AR-OI and the other with mild Bruck syndrome.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Puig-Hervas et al. (2012) concluded that PLOD2 mutations cause both Bruck syndrome and AR-OI phenotypes of variable severity. They noted intrafamilial variability in 2 brothers with the same compound heterozygous genotype: one had mild Bruck syndrome and the other had mild AR-OI. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Bank et al. (1999) concluded that collagen crosslinking is regulated primarily by tissue-specific enzymes that hydroxylate only telopeptide lysine residues. They proposed that Bruck syndrome results from deficiency of a bone-specific telopeptide lysyl hydroxylase.

Because their patient had pterygia as well as bone fragility, Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) suggested that telopeptide lysyl hydroxylation must be involved in prenatal joint formation and morphogenesis.

### Diagnosis

Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) proposed that urinary collagen degradation products and PLOD2 mutation analysis may be used to diagnose Bruck syndrome and to distinguish it from osteogenesis imperfecta.

### History

In a genome screen of a family with Bruck syndrome, Bank et al. (1999) found only 1 region, on chromosome 17p12, where all affected sibs shared a cluster of haplotypes identical by descent. They suggested that this region might be the Bruck syndrome locus and the location of a bone-specific telopeptidyl lysyl hydroxylase.

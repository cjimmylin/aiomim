## BRUCK SYNDROME 2; BRKS2

OSTEOGENESIS IMPERFECTA WITH CONGENITAL JOINT CONTRACTURES

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Bruck syndrome-2 (BRKS2) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the PLOD2 gene on chromosome 3q23-q24.

### Description

Bruck syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder in which skeletal changes resembling osteogenesis imperfecta, including osteopenia and bone fragility with multiple fractures, occur together with congenital contractures of the large joints (Ha-Vinh et al., 2004). Mutations in PLOD2 cause a range of autosomal recessive connective tissue disorders within the Bruck syndrome-osteogenesis imperfecta phenotypic spectrum (Puig-Hervas et al., 2012). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) reported a boy with Bruck syndrome who had congenital contractures with pterygia at birth and severe osteogenesis imperfecta-like osteopenia and multiple fractures. The authors noted that the combination of pterygia with bone fragility suggested that telopeptide lysyl hydroxylation is involved in prenatal joint formation and morphogenesis.

Puig-Hervas et al. (2012) reported 2 brothers with compound heterozygous PLOD2 mutations, one of whom had mild autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta and the other mild Bruck syndrome, as well as a patient with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta due to a homozygous PLOD2 mutation, and concluded that PLOD2 mutations are associated with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta phenotypes of variable severity in addition to Bruck syndrome.

### Biochemical Features

In the boy with Bruck syndrome reported by Ha-Vinh et al. (2004), urine contained high amounts of hydroxyproline but low amounts of collagen crosslink degradation products. The authors suggested that urinary collagen degradation products and PLOD2 mutation analysis may be used to diagnose Bruck syndrome and to distinguish it from osteogenesis imperfecta.

### Inheritance

Bruck syndrome-2 is inherited in an autosomal recessive manner, as shown by homozygous PLOD2 mutation in an affected boy (Ha-Vinh et al., 2004) and by homozygous and compound heterozygous PLOD2 mutations in patients from consanguineous families (Puig-Hervas et al., 2012). ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Bank et al. (1999) performed a genome screen in a family with Bruck syndrome and identified a single region on chromosome 17p12 in which all affected sibs shared a cluster of haplotypes identical by descent, suggesting this as a Bruck syndrome locus. Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) referred to Bruck syndrome linked to this region as Bruck syndrome type 1 and to Bruck syndrome caused by PLOD2 mutations as type 2, noting that the proportion of cases of each type was uncertain. ⚠︎

Puig-Hervas et al. (2012) studied 6 consanguineous Bruck syndrome families and detected changes in either PLOD2 or FKBP10 in all cases.

### Molecular Genetics

By analyzing the genetic defect in Bruck syndrome, which is characterized by pyridinoline deficiency in bone collagen, van der Slot et al. (2003) identified 2 missense mutations in exon 17 of the PLOD2 gene, thereby identifying PLOD2 as a putative telopeptide lysyl hydroxylase gene. Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) cited these 2 previously reported mutations as gly601-to-val (G601V) and thr608-to-ile (T608I). ⚠︎

In a boy with Bruck syndrome, Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) identified homozygosity for a novel mutation in the PLOD2 gene resulting in an arg598-to-his (R598H) substitution. Because the mutation was adjacent to the 2 previously reported mutations, the authors suggested a functionally important hotspot in PLOD2.

In 2 probands from consanguineous Bruck syndrome families, Puig-Hervas et al. (2012) found a homozygous frameshift mutation in the alternative exon 13a of PLOD2, indicating that specific inactivation of the longer PLOD2 protein isoform is sufficient to cause Bruck syndrome. By homozygosity mapping followed by a candidate gene approach, they identified a homozygous donor splice site mutation in PLOD2 in a patient with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta. Screening of additional samples revealed compound heterozygous PLOD2 mutations in 2 brothers, one with mild autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta and the other with mild Bruck syndrome.

### Pathogenesis

Bank et al. (1999) found that in Bruck syndrome, lysine residues within the telopeptides of type I collagen in bone were underhydroxylated, leading to aberrant crosslinking, whereas lysine residues in the triple helix were normally modified; cartilage and ligament showed normal crosslinking patterns. They proposed that collagen crosslinking is regulated by tissue-specific enzymes that hydroxylate only telopeptide lysine residues. van der Slot et al. (2003) identified PLOD2 as a putative telopeptide lysyl hydroxylase through the finding of PLOD2 mutations in Bruck syndrome, and found that PLOD2 mRNA was highly increased in fibroblasts from fibrotic skin of patients with systemic sclerosis, whose deposited matrix contained increased pyridinoline crosslinks.

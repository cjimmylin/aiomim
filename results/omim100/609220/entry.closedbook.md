### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Bruck syndrome-2 (BRKS2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the PLOD2 gene (601865) on chromosome 3q24.

### Description

Bruck syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder in which features of osteogenesis imperfecta, including bone fragility with multiple fractures, occur together with congenital contractures of the large joints, often with pterygia. The underlying defect is in hydroxylation of lysine residues in the telopeptides of collagen, which leads to abnormal collagen crosslinking in bone. ⚠︎

### Biochemical Features

Bank et al. (1999) found underhydroxylation of the lysine residues in collagen telopeptides in bone from patients with Bruck syndrome. This caused abnormal collagen crosslinking in bone, while crosslinking in ligament and cartilage was normal. They suggested that a bone-specific telopeptide lysyl hydroxylase was deficient.

### Inheritance

Bruck syndrome-2 is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. Affected individuals have been born to consanguineous parents and carry homozygous PLOD2 mutations. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Bruck syndrome is genetically heterogeneous. Bruck syndrome-1 (BRKS1), which maps to chromosome 17p12, is caused by mutation in the FKBP10 gene. Bruck syndrome-2 is caused by mutation in the PLOD2 gene, which encodes telopeptide lysyl hydroxylase. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

van der Slot et al. (2003) showed that PLOD2 encodes the telopeptide lysyl hydroxylase. In patients with Bruck syndrome they identified homozygous missense mutations in the PLOD2 gene, located in exon 17. ⚠︎

Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) studied a consanguineous family with Bruck syndrome, meaning osteogenesis imperfecta with contractures of the large joints. They described the phenotype and found that it was caused by a recessive mutation in the PLOD2 gene. ⚠︎

Puig-Hervas et al. (2012) identified PLOD2 mutations in further patients with Bruck syndrome or progressive deforming osteogenesis imperfecta, which widened the phenotypic spectrum. ⚠︎

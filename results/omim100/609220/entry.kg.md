## BRUCK SYNDROME 2; BRKS2

*Alternative titles; symbols*

OSTEOGENESIS IMPERFECTA WITH CONGENITAL JOINT CONTRACTURES

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Bruck syndrome-2 (BRKS2) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the PLOD2 gene (601865) on chromosome 3q24.

### Description

Bruck syndrome is characterized by osteoporosis, joint contractures, fragile bones, and short stature (Bank et al., 1999). Bruck syndrome-2 is the form caused by mutation in the PLOD2 gene, which encodes a telopeptide lysyl hydroxylase. The disorder is marked by a deficiency of pyridinoline cross-links in bone collagen (van der Slot et al., 2003; Ha-Vinh et al., 2004). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) reported a boy with Bruck syndrome who was born with congenital contractures with pterygia. He also had severe osteopenia resembling osteogenesis imperfecta and multiple fractures. The authors suggested that the combination of pterygia and bone fragility implied a role for telopeptide lysyl hydroxylation in prenatal joint formation and morphogenesis.

### Biochemical Features

Bank et al. (1999) studied patients with Bruck syndrome. In bone, lysine residues within the telopeptides of type I collagen were underhydroxylated, which led to aberrant crosslinking, whereas lysine residues in the triple helix were modified normally. In contrast, telopeptide hydroxylation was unaltered in cartilage and ligament, as shown by normal crosslinking patterns.

van der Slot et al. (2003) noted that Bruck syndrome is characterized by a deficiency of pyridinoline cross-links in bone collagen. These cross-links are derived from hydroxylated lysine residues within the collagen telopeptides.

Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) found that the urine of their patient with Bruck syndrome contained high amounts of hydroxyproline but low amounts of collagen crosslink degradation products.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of Bruck syndrome-2 is autosomal recessive. Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) found a homozygous PLOD2 mutation in an affected boy. Puig-Hervás et al. (2012) found homozygous or compound heterozygous PLOD2 mutations in patients from consanguineous families and in a pair of brothers. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Bank et al. (1999) performed a genome screen in a family with affected sibs. They identified a single region on chromosome 17p12 where all affected sibs shared a cluster of haplotypes identical by descent, and proposed this region as a Bruck syndrome locus. ⚠︎

Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) noted that Bruck syndrome is genetically heterogeneous. One locus had been mapped to 17p12 (BS type 1), whereas mutations in PLOD2 on 3q23-q24 cause BS type 2. They noted that it was still uncertain what proportion of cases belongs to each type, and that phenotypic correlations were lacking.

Puig-Hervás et al. (2012) screened 6 consanguineous Bruck syndrome families and found changes in either PLOD2 or FKBP10 in every case.

### Molecular Genetics

van der Slot et al. (2003) analyzed the genetic defect in Bruck syndrome, which is characterized by pyridinoline deficiency in bone collagen. They identified 2 missense mutations in exon 17 of the PLOD2 gene, and on this basis identified PLOD2 as a putative telopeptide lysyl hydroxylase gene.

In a boy with Bruck syndrome, Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) identified a novel homozygous mutation in the PLOD2 gene that resulted in an arg598-to-his (R598H) substitution. The mutation lay next to 2 previously reported mutations, gly601-to-val and thr608-to-ile. The authors suggested that this region is a functionally important hotspot in PLOD2.

Puig-Hervás et al. (2012) screened 6 consanguineous Bruck syndrome families. Two probands carried a homozygous frameshift mutation in the alternative exon 13a of PLOD2. The authors concluded that inactivating only the longer PLOD2 protein isoform is sufficient to cause Bruck syndrome.

In a patient with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta, Puig-Hervás et al. (2012) used homozygosity mapping followed by candidate gene analysis and identified a homozygous donor splice site mutation in PLOD2.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Puig-Hervás et al. (2012) found compound heterozygous PLOD2 mutations in 2 brothers. One brother had mild autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta, and the other had mild Bruck syndrome. The authors concluded that, in addition to causing Bruck syndrome, PLOD2 is associated with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta phenotypes of variable severity.

### Pathogenesis

Bank et al. (1999) concluded that collagen crosslinking is regulated mainly by tissue-specific enzymes that hydroxylate only telopeptide lysine residues. They suggested that Bruck syndrome results from deficiency of a bone-specific telopeptide lysyl hydroxylase.

van der Slot et al. (2003) found that PLOD2 mRNA was highly increased in fibroblasts derived from the fibrotic skin of patients with systemic sclerosis. The matrix deposited by these fibroblasts had increased levels of pyridinoline cross-links, which showed a link between PLOD2 mRNA levels and hydroxylation of telopeptide lysine residues.

### Diagnosis

Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) proposed that measuring collagen degradation products in urine, together with PLOD2 mutation analysis, could be used to diagnose Bruck syndrome and to distinguish it from osteogenesis imperfecta.

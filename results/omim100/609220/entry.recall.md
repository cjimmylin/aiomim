### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Bruck syndrome-2 (BRKS2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the PLOD2 gene (601865) on chromosome 3q24. ⚠︎

### Description

Bruck syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder in which features of osteogenesis imperfecta, including bone fragility with multiple fractures, occur together with congenital contractures of the large joints (Ha-Vinh et al., 2004). The underlying defect is in hydroxylation of lysine residues in the telopeptides of collagen (Ha-Vinh et al., 2004), which leads to abnormal collagen crosslinking in bone (Bank et al., 1999).

### Biochemical Features

Bank et al. (1999) found underhydroxylation of the lysine residues in collagen telopeptides in bone from patients with Bruck syndrome. This caused abnormal collagen crosslinking in bone, whereas crosslinking in ligament and cartilage was normal. They suggested that a bone-specific telopeptide lysyl hydroxylase was deficient.

### Inheritance

Bruck syndrome is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern (Ha-Vinh et al., 2004). The patient reported by Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) was homozygous for a PLOD2 mutation. Puig-Hervás et al. (2012) studied 6 consanguineous families with Bruck syndrome, in all of which they found mutations in either PLOD2 or FKBP10.

### Heterogeneity

Bruck syndrome is genetically heterogeneous (Ha-Vinh et al., 2004). Bruck syndrome-1 (BRKS1) maps to chromosome 17p12 (Ha-Vinh et al., 2004) and is caused by mutation in the FKBP10 gene (Puig-Hervás et al., 2012). Bruck syndrome-2 is caused by mutation in the PLOD2 gene (Ha-Vinh et al., 2004), which van der Slot et al. (2003) identified as a putative telopeptide lysyl hydroxylase gene. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In patients with Bruck syndrome, van der Slot et al. (2003) found 2 missense mutations in exon 17 of the PLOD2 gene. This identified PLOD2 as a putative telopeptide lysyl hydroxylase gene.

Ha-Vinh et al. (2004) reported a boy with Bruck syndrome who had congenital contractures with pterygia at birth, along with severe osteogenesis imperfecta-like osteopenia and multiple fractures. He was homozygous for a mutation in the PLOD2 gene.

Puig-Hervás et al. (2012) identified PLOD2 mutations in additional patients with Bruck syndrome. They also found PLOD2 mutations in patients with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta (AR-OI), including one with mild AR-OI. They concluded that PLOD2, in addition to causing Bruck syndrome, is also associated with AR-OI phenotypes of variable severity, which widened the phenotypic spectrum. ⚠︎

### Description

Autosomal dominant autoinflammation, panniculitis, and dermatosis syndrome (AIPDSA) is an inflammatory disorder caused by heterozygous defects in OTULIN. OTULIN is a deubiquitinase that removes linear (M1-linked) polyubiquitin chains. Affected people have inflammatory skin lesions, such as panniculitis or necrotizing skin lesions. The disorder is milder than, or differs from, the severe early-onset autoinflammation seen in recessive OTULIN deficiency (otulipenia). ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

The autosomal recessive form of autoinflammation, panniculitis, and dermatosis syndrome, caused by biallelic OTULIN mutation, is also called otulipenia or OTULIN-related autoinflammatory syndrome (ORAS).

### Inheritance

This disorder follows autosomal dominant inheritance through heterozygous OTULIN defects. The allelic disorder caused by biallelic OTULIN mutation follows autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Heterogeneity

An autosomal recessive form of autoinflammation, panniculitis, and dermatosis syndrome is caused by homozygous mutation in the OTULIN gene.

### Molecular Genetics

Spaan et al. (2022) reported heterozygous OTULIN haploinsufficiency in patients with severe, necrotizing skin and/or pulmonary infections caused by Staphylococcus aureus. Haploinsufficiency arose either from loss-of-function OTULIN variants or from deletions on chromosome 5p that included OTULIN. Spaan et al. (2022) found that partial loss of OTULIN impaired cell-intrinsic immunity to staphylococcal alpha-toxin, and this was linked to increased cellular sensitivity to the toxin. ✗

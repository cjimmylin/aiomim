### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal dominant autoinflammation, panniculitis, and dermatosis syndrome (AIPDSA) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the OTULIN gene (615712) on chromosome 5p15.2.

### Description

Autosomal dominant autoinflammation, panniculitis, and dermatosis syndrome (AIPDSA) is an inflammatory disorder caused by heterozygous defects in OTULIN, a deubiquitinase that removes linear (M1-linked) polyubiquitin chains (Davidson et al., 2024). Affected individuals have inflammatory skin lesions, such as panniculitis or necrotizing skin lesions (Davidson et al., 2024).

### Nomenclature

Davidson et al. (2024) noted that the autosomal recessive form of autoinflammation, panniculitis, and dermatosis syndrome, caused by biallelic OTULIN mutation, is also called otulipenia or OTULIN-related autoinflammatory syndrome (ORAS).

### Clinical Features

Takeda et al. (2024) reported a patient who had had ORAS since birth. The disease was severe, but the symptoms were less severe than those reported for autosomal recessive ORAS of neonatal onset. The authors attributed the milder course to high-dose prednisolone treatment.

### Inheritance

Takeda et al. (2024) concluded that this disorder is transmitted in an autosomal dominant manner through heterozygous OTULIN defects. They noted that the allelic disorder caused by biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) OTULIN mutation is transmitted in an autosomal recessive manner.

### Heterogeneity

Davidson et al. (2024) noted that an autosomal recessive form of autoinflammation, panniculitis, and dermatosis syndrome is caused by homozygous mutation in the OTULIN gene. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Davidson et al. (2024) noted that Spaan et al. (2022) had reported heterozygous OTULIN haploinsufficiency in patients with severe necrosis of the skin and lungs, typically but not only after Staphylococcus aureus infection. Davidson et al. (2024) studied cells from OTULIN-haploinsufficient patients, which carried either heterozygous point variants in OTULIN or a 5p deletion.

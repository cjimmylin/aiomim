### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal dominant autoinflammation, panniculitis, and dermatosis syndrome (AIPDSA) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the OTULIN gene. The reference data supplied for this entry did not give a MIM number or chromosomal location for OTULIN.

### Description

Autosomal dominant autoinflammation, panniculitis, and dermatosis syndrome (AIPDSA) is an autoinflammatory disorder that begins in the neonatal period or in infancy. Features include fever, raised C-reactive protein, leukocytosis with neutrophilia, and sterile neutrophilic skin and soft tissue inflammation. Skin findings include panniculitis, pustules at sites of trauma, and necrotizing wounds. Some patients also have respiratory distress. Davidson et al. (2024) and Takeda et al. (2024) reported patients who were heterozygous for de novo dominant-negative OTULIN mutations; the disease responded to corticosteroids and to TNF blockade. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Davidson et al. (2024) described 2 unrelated patients. Patient 1 was a 22-month-old boy born to nonconsanguineous Caucasian parents. He was born at 31 weeks' gestation. At 4 days of age he developed periumbilical erythema, diagnosed as a presumed abscess, that did not respond to broad-spectrum antibiotics. At surgery there was marked inflammation around a urachal remnant with many neutrophils, but no bacteria were cultured. The surgical closure dehisced, leaving an abdominal wound that failed to close. During 2 months of healing, the only positive culture was a light growth of methicillin-sensitive Staphylococcus aureus. At 8 weeks of age he had an acute respiratory deterioration with extensive body edema, hypotension, and presumed pulmonary edema. His lungs improved immediately once corticosteroids were started. He later developed a spontaneous cutaneous stoma from the duodenum. He also had a systolic murmur with an echogenic focus on the tricuspid valve and was treated for infective endocarditis, although no organism was identified. Over many months in intensive care he had repeated areas of swelling and erythema consistent with panniculitis. Sterile pustular abscesses, thought to represent pathergy, formed at injection and cannula sites. Investigations showed C-reactive protein of 100 to 200 mg/L, elevated neutrophils (30 to 50 x 10(9)/L) and monocytes (2 to 10 x 10(9)/L), eosinophilia (1 to 3 x 10(9)/L), and persistent thrombocytopenia (below 100 x 10(9)/L). These continued until the inflammation was controlled with corticosteroids. He initially had hypogammaglobulinemia, which the authors noted may have been related to prematurity. Lymphocyte subsets were largely normal. A perilesional skin biopsy from the abdominal wound showed heavy acute inflammatory exudate with diffuse suppuration and areas of necrosis. ⚠︎

Patient 2 of Davidson et al. (2024) was a full-term Saudi girl born to nonconsanguineous parents. She had no symptoms until 7 months of age. She then presented with multiple tender skin lesions on the upper and lower limbs, fever (38 to 39 degrees C), and generalized edema, 2 weeks after her routine 6-month vaccinations. She had leukocytosis (45.5 x 10(9)/L) with neutrophilia (40.9 x 10(9)/L) and C-reactive protein of 264 mg/L, but cultures were negative. Despite intravenous antibiotics, some lesions progressed to tender cellulitis-like plaques that evolved into necrotizing fasciitis with ulceration, and debridement and fasciotomy were needed. Biopsies showed mixed infiltration of neutrophils and plasma cells with areas of necrosis. Tissue cultures for fungal, bacterial, and mycobacterial organisms were all negative. At 12 months of age she presented again with fever and new tender erythematous plaques and patches on the trunk and both lower limbs. She had leukocytosis (19.9 x 10(9)/L), neutrophilia (12.9 x 10(9)/L), and C-reactive protein of 393 mg/L. Skin biopsy revealed panniculitis. She had recurrent simple chest infections, which improved with prophylactic antibiotics.

Takeda et al. (2024) reported a 9-year-old Japanese girl born to nonconsanguineous healthy parents. She was born at 33 weeks and 1 day of gestation, weighing 1,436 g; the birth was complicated by intrauterine infection. As a neonate she developed meningitis with neutrophil-dominant leukocytosis and elevated C-reactive protein, but blood and CSF cultures were negative. At 1 month of age she developed omphalitis that did not respond to antibiotics. Methicillin-susceptible S. aureus was cultured from the omphalitis, and the inflammation spread over most of the abdominal wall, requiring surgical debridement. Histopathology showed massive leukocyte infiltration, mainly neutrophils, and inflammatory granuloma, with significant cell death on TUNEL and cleaved caspase-3 staining. Pustulosis developed at skin puncture and surgical sites. From 3 months of age she had 3 episodes of acute respiratory distress syndrome with pneumonia and thrombocytopenia. These responded to high-dose dexamethasone but relapsed when steroids were tapered. During febrile episodes she had leukocytosis (up to 1 x 10(8)/microliter, mainly neutrophils), thrombocytosis (up to 1 x 10(9)/microliter), and C-reactive protein of 100 to 500 mg/liter. Serum IL-1-beta and IL-6 were elevated, and no autoantibodies were detected. Physical and psychological development was severely impaired during the course of severe inflammation and high-dose steroid treatment. A biopsy of erythema nodosum on the left upper arm at 1 year of age showed diffuse dermal infiltration by neutrophils and TNF-producing mononuclear cells. Her PBMCs and EBV-transformed B cells showed more linear ubiquitin than control cells, with normal amounts of OTULIN protein.

### Inheritance

The OTULIN mutations reported in AIPDSA occurred de novo in all 3 patients described, 2 by Davidson et al. (2024) and 1 by Takeda et al. (2024). Both groups showed that the mutant proteins act in a dominant-negative manner. Takeda et al. (2024) concluded that ORAS can be triggered in an autosomal dominant fashion.

### Heterogeneity

Takeda et al. (2024) noted that homozygous or compound heterozygous OTULIN variants cause an autosomal recessive autoinflammatory disease, called otulipenia or OTULIN-related autoinflammatory syndrome (ORAS). Davidson et al. (2024) noted that heterozygous loss-of-function OTULIN variants (OTULIN haploinsufficiency) had not been associated with spontaneous inflammation, but can predispose to severe necrotizing disease after infection with S. aureus.

### Molecular Genetics

Davidson et al. (2024) performed trio exome sequencing in patient 1 and identified a de novo heterozygous c.386G-C transversion in the OTULIN gene, resulting in a cys129-to-ser (C129S) substitution. Whole-genome sequencing in patient 2 identified a different nucleotide change, a c.385T-A transversion, that causes the same C129S substitution; testing of her parents showed it had arisen de novo. The variant was absent from gnomAD. Cys129 lies in the catalytic domain of OTULIN. The C129S protein was as stable as wildtype, and patient fibroblasts coexpressed wildtype and C129S OTULIN in apparently equal amounts. The mutant protein could not hydrolyze M1-linked diubiquitin. Patient fibroblasts accumulated M1-linked ubiquitin chains. In HEK293T cells, which express endogenous OTULIN, transfected C129S caused M1-ubiquitin accumulation and NF-kappa-B activity, whereas wildtype OTULIN and the loss-of-function variants Y244C and N341D did not. C129S drove NF-kappa-B activity even when cotransfected at lower amounts than wildtype OTULIN, indicating a dominant-negative effect. With TNF plus cycloheximide, patient fibroblasts died at a rate similar to fibroblasts with biallelic OTULIN loss-of-function mutations. Their sensitivity to TNF-induced cell death was markedly greater than that of healthy donor and OTULIN-haploinsufficient fibroblasts. ⚠︎

Takeda et al. (2024) performed whole-exome sequencing in a Japanese girl with AIPDSA and her parents. They identified 2 heterozygous OTULIN variants on different alleles, as confirmed by cDNA cloning: a maternally inherited c.455C-T transition, resulting in a pro152-to-leu (P152L) substitution, and a de novo c.917G-A transition, resulting in an arg306-to-gln (R306Q) substitution. P152L was present in 51 of 1,611,754 alleles in gnomAD, and the patient's healthy sister also carried it. R306Q was found in 1 of 1,611,754 alleles in gnomAD. The enzymatic efficiency of P152L OTULIN was almost the same as that of wildtype, whereas R306Q had heavily impaired deubiquitinase activity and failed to bind linear diubiquitin on surface plasmon resonance. When expressed in OTULIN-knockout HeLa cells, R306Q, but not P152L, increased TNF-induced caspase activation and cell death. MSCs derived from the patient's iPSCs had normal OTULIN levels, with similar amounts of the P152L and R306Q proteins. These cells had increased linear ubiquitin and were more sensitive to TNF-induced apoptosis. The authors base-edited the patient's iPSCs to correct each variant in turn. MSCs carrying wildtype and R306Q alleles were as sensitive to TNF-mediated cell death as the patient's MSCs, and more sensitive than control MSCs or those carrying wildtype and P152L. In OTULIN-null HEK293 cells, R306Q reversed, in a dose-dependent manner, the reduction of linear chains and the rescue from cell death mediated by wildtype or P152L OTULIN, consistent with a dominant-negative effect.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Davidson et al. (2024) noted that individuals heterozygous for the catalytic-triad mutation N341D do not have an ORAS-like disease. Instead they have complications related to OTULIN haploinsufficiency or no phenotype. The authors attributed this to the reduced ubiquitin affinity of N341D, which makes it a loss-of-function rather than a dominant-negative variant. Takeda et al. (2024) observed that HeLa cells expressing L272P had much higher TNF-dependent auto-linear ubiquitination of LUBAC subunits than cells expressing Y244C or R306Q. They concluded that the severity of ORAS appears to correlate with residual OTULIN function rather than with the mode of inheritance.

### Pathogenesis

Davidson et al. (2024) found that C129S OTULIN bound M1-linked diubiquitin with high affinity but did not directly protect it from cleavage by wildtype OTULIN in a cell-free assay. In cells, C129S OTULIN bound LUBAC and allowed autoubiquitination of the complex. In patient fibroblasts, more OTULIN was bound to LUBAC than in healthy donor fibroblasts, and most of the LUBAC-bound OTULIN was catalytically inactive. Recruitment of SHARPIN and HOIP to the TNFR1 signaling complex was reduced in patient fibroblasts. NF-kappa-B and MAPK responses to TNF were not significantly altered. The authors concluded that accumulation of M1-ubiquitin chains on LUBAC reduces its recruitment to the TNFR1 complex and promotes TNF-induced cell death. Patient 1's PBMCs showed markedly increased transcription of IFNB1 and IL6, and to a lesser extent TNF. Patient fibroblasts had an elevated interferon-stimulated gene score compared with healthy donor and OTULIN-haploinsufficient lines.

Takeda et al. (2024) found that both OTULIN variants bound HOIP, and that TNF-induced auto-linear ubiquitination of LUBAC subunits was increased in MSCs from the patient. They suggested that R306Q exerts its dominant-negative effect within the LUBAC complex, enhancing auto-linear ubiquitination of LUBAC, suppressing LUBAC function, and so increasing TNF-mediated cell death. TNF-mediated NF-kappa-B activation was not affected in the patient's MSCs.

### Clinical Management

In patient 1 of Davidson et al. (2024), the wound began to heal after corticosteroids were started. TNF blockade with adalimumab appeared to speed healing and allowed a lower steroid dose; he also received the JAK inhibitor ruxolitinib, but its benefit was uncertain. Healing was markedly better under broad-spectrum antibiotic prophylaxis. Patient 2 improved promptly on corticosteroids. She was then given infliximab with methotrexate, and corticosteroids were successfully weaned. She had been in remission for 2 years by the end of her third year of life. Takeda et al. (2024) reported that etanercept, started at 2 years of age, completely controlled the inflammation. Their patient reached steroid-free remission and her physical and psychological development improved.

Trio exome sequencing identified a de novo heterozygous c.386G-C transversion in the OTULIN gene, resulting in a cys129-to-ser (C129S) substitution in the catalytic domain. Takeda et al. (2024) studied a Japanese girl with neonatal-onset AIPDSA. She had a de novo heterozygous c.917G-A transition in the OTULIN gene, resulting in an arg306-to-gln (R306Q) substitution. On the other allele she carried a maternally inherited P152L variant (c.455C-T), which had wildtype deubiquitinase activity; her healthy sister also carried P152L. R306Q was found in 1 of 1,611,754 alleles in gnomAD. The R306Q protein was stable but had heavily impaired deubiquitinase activity and impaired binding to linear diubiquitin. Base editing of the patient's iPSCs showed that MSCs carrying R306Q and a wildtype allele were as sensitive to TNF-mediated cell death as the patient's own MSCs. R306Q counteracted wildtype OTULIN in a dose-dependent, dominant-negative manner.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that oblique facial clefting-1 (OBLFC1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the SPECC1L gene on chromosome 22q11. ⚠︎

### Description

Oblique facial clefts are rare, severe craniofacial malformations. The clefts run obliquely from the upper lip or mouth region toward the eye (corresponding to Tessier clefts in the oro-orbital/naso-ocular region). They may involve the lip, maxilla, nose, and the inferior eyelid and orbit. They may occur on one side or both, and they can occur with other malformations. Most cases are sporadic. ✗

### Inheritance

The heterozygous SPECC1L mutations reported by Saadi et al. (2011) are consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. Most cases have been sporadic. ✗

### Cytogenetics

Saadi et al. (2011) studied a patient with oblique facial clefting who carried a de novo balanced chromosomal translocation. The breakpoint on chromosome 22q11 disrupted the SPECC1L gene, which pointed to SPECC1L as a candidate gene for the disorder. ✗

### Molecular Genetics

Saadi et al. (2011) went on to screen SPECC1L in a cohort of patients with oblique facial clefts. They identified heterozygous missense mutations in SPECC1L in 2 patients. Functional studies supported a role for SPECC1L in craniofacial development. In zebrafish, knockdown of the ortholog caused defects in facial cartilage. In cultured cells, SPECC1L deficiency affected the actin cytoskeleton and cell migration. Saadi et al. (2011) proposed that SPECC1L is needed for the facial processes to fuse properly, through its role in cell adhesion and migration. ✗

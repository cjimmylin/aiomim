### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that oblique facial clefting-1 (OBLFC1) is caused by mutation in the SPECC1L gene (614140) on chromosome 22q11.23.

### Description

Oblique facial clefts are rare malformations (Dasouki et al., 1988). They include types 2 to 6 of Tessier's classification (Richieri-Costa and Gorlin, 1994). They can occur together with other malformations (Dasouki et al., 1988).

### Clinical Features

Dasouki et al. (1988) described an 8-month-old girl who was born with symmetric bilateral oblique facial clefts and a calcaneovarus foot deformity. A CT scan of the head showed severe bilateral ocular hypoplasia.

### Molecular Genetics

Saadi et al. (2011) showed that loss-of-function mutations in the SPECC1L gene are pathogenic for oblique facial clefting. Their functional studies supported a role for SPECC1L in craniofacial development. In zebrafish, knockdown of a SPECC1L homolog produced a 'faceless' phenotype, with loss of jaw and facial structures. In cultured cells, SPECC1L deficiency caused defective reorganization of the actin cytoskeleton and abnormal cell migration. Saadi et al. (2011) concluded that SPECC1L functions in actin-cytoskeleton reorganization and is required for proper facial morphogenesis.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that split-hand/foot malformation-6 (SHFM6) is caused by homozygous mutation in the WNT10B gene (601906) on chromosome 12q13.12.

### Description

Split-hand/foot malformation (SHFM) affects the central rays of the hands and/or feet. Gül and Oktenli (2002) described it as characterized by absence of the central digital rays, a deep median cleft, and syndactyly of the remaining digits, and noted variable expression of the malformation pattern within families. Elliott and Evans (2006) noted that SHFM shows reduced penetrance. SHFM6 is the autosomal recessive form caused by mutation in WNT10B (Ugur and Tolun, 2008).

### Inheritance

SHFM6 is inherited as an autosomal recessive trait. Ugur and Tolun (2008) studied a large consanguineous kindred with autosomal recessive SHFM and found a homozygous WNT10B mutation. They described the inheritance as complex. The phenotype varied among affected members: most had central foot reductions with or without hand involvement, and 1 had a milder, atypical form of SHFM.

Blattner et al. (2010) found a homozygous WNT10B mutation in a patient with sporadic SHFM. They concluded that sporadic SHFM may show recessive rather than dominant inheritance.

### Mapping

Ugur and Tolun (2008) studied a large consanguineous kindred with autosomal recessive SHFM. Using homozygosity mapping, they identified a novel SHFM locus at chromosome 12q13.11-q13.

### Heterogeneity

SHFM is genetically heterogeneous (Elliott and Evans, 2006). Gül and Oktenli (2002) stated that most familial cases are inherited in an autosomal dominant manner. The autosomal recessive form described here results from mutation in WNT10B (Ugur and Tolun, 2008).

### Molecular Genetics

In a large consanguineous kindred with autosomal recessive SHFM mapped to 12q13, Ugur and Tolun (2008) identified a homozygous missense mutation in the WNT10B gene (R332W) by a candidate gene approach. The mutation was present in all affected individuals except the 1 with an atypical phenotype. It was also present in an asymptomatic female.

Blattner et al. (2010) examined WNT10B in a patient with sporadic SHFM and identified a homozygous nonsense mutation. This finding was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that split-hand/foot malformation-6 (SHFM6) is caused by homozygous mutation in the WNT10B gene (601906) on chromosome 12q13.

### Description

Split-hand/foot malformation (SHFM), or ectrodactyly, is a congenital limb malformation affecting the central rays of the hands and/or feet. It produces a median cleft, absent or hypoplastic central digits, and often syndactyly of the remaining digits. SHFM6 is the autosomal recessive form caused by mutation in WNT10B. Severity varies within families, and penetrance may be incomplete. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

SHFM6 is inherited as an autosomal recessive trait. This was shown by reports of affected offspring of consanguineous parents and of homozygous WNT10B mutations. Ugur and Tolun (2008) described the inheritance in their family as complex, with variable expressivity. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Ugur and Tolun (2008) studied a consanguineous Turkish family with autosomal recessive SHFM. They mapped the disorder to chromosome 12q13 by homozygosity mapping. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

SHFM is genetically heterogeneous. Other forms are inherited in an autosomal dominant or X-linked manner, and the autosomal recessive form described here results from mutation in WNT10B. ✗

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of the consanguineous Turkish family they had mapped to 12q13, Ugur and Tolun (2008) found a homozygous mutation in the WNT10B gene. ⚠︎

Blattner et al. (2010) reported a homozygous nonsense mutation in WNT10B in a patient with apparently sporadic SHFM, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

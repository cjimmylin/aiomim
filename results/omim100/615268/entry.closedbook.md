### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that cerebellar ataxia, impaired intellectual development, and dysequilibrium syndrome-4 (CAMRQ4) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the ATP8A2 gene (605870) on chromosome 13q12.

### Description

CAMRQ4 is an autosomal recessive neurodevelopmental disorder. Its main features are congenital cerebellar ataxia, impaired intellectual development, and dysarthria. Some affected individuals walk on all four limbs (quadrupedal locomotion). Severe hypotonia, abnormal involuntary movements such as chorea, and optic atrophy have also been reported in patients with ATP8A2 mutations. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Onat et al. (2013) described a consanguineous Turkish family in which several sibs had cerebellar ataxia, impaired intellectual development, and dysarthria. Affected members walked quadrupedally. Brain imaging showed cerebellar atrophy. ⚠︎

Martin-Hernandez et al. (2016) reported patients with ATP8A2 mutations who had a severe encephalopathy with impaired intellectual development, marked hypotonia, chorea, and optic atrophy. This broadened the phenotype linked to the gene. ✗

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CAMRQ4 in the family reported by Onat et al. (2013) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Cytogenetics

Cacciagli et al. (2010) reported a patient with severe impaired intellectual development and major hypotonia who carried a de novo balanced translocation involving chromosome 13. The breakpoint disrupted the ATP8A2 gene. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of the consanguineous Turkish family with CAMRQ, Onat et al. (2013) found a homozygous missense mutation in the ATP8A2 gene (I376M). They identified it using homozygosity mapping followed by sequencing. The mutation segregated with the disorder in the family. ⚠︎

Martin-Hernandez et al. (2016) identified homozygous or compound heterozygous ATP8A2 mutations in patients with severe hypotonia, chorea, optic atrophy, and impaired intellectual development. ✗

### Animal Model

Zhu et al. (2012) found that the mouse 'wabbler-lethal' phenotype is caused by mutations in the Atp8a2 gene. Affected mice had ataxia and axonal degeneration. ✗

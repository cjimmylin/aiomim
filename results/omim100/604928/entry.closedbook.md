### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Wolfram syndrome-2 (WFS2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the CISD2 gene (611507) on chromosome 4q24.

### Description

Wolfram syndrome-2 is an autosomal recessive neurodegenerative disorder. Its main features are early-onset insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus, optic atrophy and sensorineural hearing loss. It differs from the classic WFS1-related Wolfram syndrome in two ways. Diabetes insipidus is absent. Patients also have upper gastrointestinal ulceration and bleeding, and defective platelet aggregation. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

El-Shanti et al. (2000) studied consanguineous Jordanian families with Wolfram syndrome. Affected individuals had juvenile-onset diabetes mellitus, optic atrophy and hearing loss, but no diabetes insipidus. Several also had upper gastrointestinal ulcers with bleeding. ✗

Amr et al. (2007) studied the same Jordanian families further. They described a bleeding tendency and defective platelet aggregation, as well as peptic ulcer disease, in affected members. ✗

### Inheritance

WFS2 is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. This was shown by its occurrence in consanguineous families (El-Shanti et al., 2000) and by the homozygous mutation found by Amr et al. (2007). ✗

### Mapping

El-Shanti et al. (2000) ran a genomewide linkage analysis in consanguineous Jordanian families with Wolfram syndrome. They mapped a second Wolfram syndrome locus to chromosome 4q22-q24. Linkage to the WFS1 region on 4p16 was excluded in these families. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

The families studied by El-Shanti et al. (2000) did not show linkage to the WFS1 locus on 4p16. This showed that Wolfram syndrome is genetically heterogeneous. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Amr et al. (2007) studied the Jordanian families with WFS2 linked to 4q22-q24. They found a homozygous mutation in the CISD2 gene (also called ZCD2), which encodes the protein ERIS. The mutation was predicted to change a single amino acid. However, it also disrupted splicing, so that exon 2 was skipped and the protein lost its function. The mutation segregated with the disease in these families. ✗

### Animal Model

Chen et al. (2009) found that mice lacking Cisd2 showed signs of premature aging. These included mitochondrial breakdown and dysfunction, loss of nerve and muscle tissue, and a shortened lifespan. This suggested that CISD2 is needed to keep mitochondria intact. ⚠︎

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 WOLFRAM SYNDROME 2**  
CISD2, GLU37GLN [c.109G>C; p.Glu37Gln]

Amr et al. (2007) studied affected members of consanguineous Jordanian families with WFS2. They found a homozygous c.109G-C transversion in the CISD2 gene, predicting a glu37-to-gln (E37Q) substitution. The change caused aberrant splicing with loss of exon 2, and the transcript lost its function. ⚠︎

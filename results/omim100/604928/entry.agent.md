### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Wolfram syndrome-2 (WFS2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the CISD2 gene on chromosome 4q24.

### Description

Wolfram syndrome-2 is an autosomal recessive neurodegenerative disorder in which the minimal diagnostic criteria for Wolfram syndrome, diabetes mellitus and optic atrophy, are present. It differs from Wolfram syndrome-1 by the absence of diabetes insipidus and psychiatric disorders and by the presence of bleeding upper intestinal ulcers and defective platelet aggregation (El-Shanti et al., 2000; Mozzillo et al., 2014).

### Nomenclature

Mozzillo et al. (2014) noted that their patient had optic neuropathy rather than optic atrophy, and suggested that the ocular pathology in the Jordanian patients, whose optic atrophy had been described as 'asymptomatic,' may also have been optic neuropathy. Because optic atrophy is a main diagnostic criterion for Wolfram syndrome, they suggested that the so-called WFS2 might not be a subtype of Wolfram syndrome and that its classification should be reevaluated.

### Clinical Features

El-Shanti et al. (2000) studied 16 patients from 4 families with the Wolfram syndrome phenotype. Diabetes insipidus was absent in all affected family members, and several patients had profound upper gastrointestinal ulceration and bleeding, features that had not previously been reported in Wolfram syndrome.

Mozzillo et al. (2014) reported a Caucasian girl from Italy, the first child of reportedly nonconsanguineous parents whose grandparents were born on the same small Italian island, with WFS2. From age 5 years she had recurrent bleeding upper intestinal ulcers. At age 14 years, malrotation and partial duodenal intussusception were diagnosed and a duodenal tract was resected. At age 16 she had reduced visual acuity and dyschromatopsia; fundus examination showed optic disc pallor, with preserved pupillary light reflexes and normal pattern visual evoked potentials, and brain MRI showed normal optic nerve caliber. Nonautoimmune diabetes occurred at age 17 years and was treated with insulin. Further studies showed bilateral sensorineural hearing loss in the high frequencies and slow intestinal transit with entero-gastric bile reflux. Visual field testing, ERG, and OCT showed moderate involvement of the optic nerve in both eyes, consistent with optic neuropathy rather than optic atrophy. Platelet aggregation in response to ADP was reduced and reversible, with absent platelet secretion, whereas aggregation in response to collagen, epinephrine, and ristocetin was normal. At age 22 years she had diabetes mellitus, optic neuropathy with preserved visual acuity, no optic atrophy, tinnitus due to high-frequency hearing loss, bile reflux, slow intestinal transit, defective platelet aggregation to ADP, and bleeding ulcers. Her heterozygous parents and brother had no ophthalmologic abnormalities, diabetes, or deafness, but showed reversible defective platelet aggregation in response to ADP.

Rondinelli et al. (2015) reported Italian sibs with Wolfram syndrome-2 (title only available).

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of WFS2 in the families reported by El-Shanti et al. (2000) and Amr et al. (2007), which were consanguineous in the latter report, was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Mozzillo et al. (2014) found a homozygous CISD2 deletion in an affected girl whose parents and unaffected brother were heterozygous carriers. ⚠︎

### Mapping

By linkage analysis in 4 families with Wolfram syndrome, El-Shanti et al. (2000) significantly excluded linkage to the chromosome 4p16.1 locus in 3 of the families using 3 microsatellite markers (D4S432, D4S3023, and D4S2366). The 2 affected individuals in the fourth family were homozygous for all 3 markers from the 4p16.1 region. In the other 3 families, El-Shanti et al. (2000) demonstrated linkage to chromosome 4q22-q24.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of 3 consanguineous Jordanian families with Wolfram syndrome mapped to the WFS2 locus on chromosome 4q22-q25 and without mutation in the WFS1 gene, Amr et al. (2007) identified a homozygous 109G-C transversion in the ZCD2 (CISD2) gene, predicting a glu37-to-gln (E37Q) substitution. The mutation disrupted mRNA splicing by eliminating exon 2, resulting in a premature stop codon. Lymphoblastoid cells from affected individuals showed a significantly greater rise in intracellular calcium than controls when stimulated with thapsigargin, although resting intracellular calcium concentrations did not differ. Screening of 377 probands with hearing loss did not identify mutations in the WFS2 gene.

In an Italian girl with WFS2, Mozzillo et al. (2014) found that exon 2 of the CISD2 gene could not be amplified, and cDNA sequencing revealed a homozygous deletion of the whole of exon 2. SNP array analysis confirmed the deletion, restricted the maximum deletion size to 2,050 bp spanning from intron 2 to intron 3, and showed that the parents and brother were heterozygous carriers. Although the parents reported no consanguinity, microsatellite analysis supported a common ancestor. The deleted region included the codons for amino acids 102 to 106, within the 2Fe-2S domain, which the authors suggested may result in a nonfunctional protein.

Rondinelli et al. (2015) identified a novel CISD2 mutation in Italian sibs with WFS2 (title only available).

### Animal Model

Chen et al. (2009) found that Cisd2 deficiency in mice caused mitochondrial breakdown and dysfunction with autophagic cell death, preceding the earliest manifestations of nerve and muscle degeneration and leading to features suggestive of premature aging. Cisd2 was primarily localized in mitochondria, and mitochondrial degeneration worsened with age. The authors proposed that mutation of CISD2 causes WFS2 as a mitochondria-mediated disorder.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 WOLFRAM SYNDROME 2**  
CISD2, GLU37GLN [109G-C; E37Q]

In affected members of 3 consanguineous Jordanian families with Wolfram syndrome-2, Amr et al. (2007) identified a homozygous 109G-C transversion in the CISD2 (ZCD2) gene, predicting a glu37-to-gln (E37Q) substitution at a conserved residue. The mutation disrupted mRNA splicing, eliminating exon 2 and introducing a premature stop codon.

**.0002 WOLFRAM SYNDROME 2**  
CISD2, EX2 DEL [exon 2 deletion]

In an Italian girl with Wolfram syndrome-2, Mozzillo et al. (2014) identified a homozygous intragenic deletion of the CISD2 gene removing the whole of exon 2, detected by failure of exon 2 amplification and cDNA sequencing and confirmed by SNP array, with a maximum size of 2,050 bp spanning from intron 2 to intron 3. Her parents and brother were heterozygous carriers; they were clinically unaffected but had subclinical defective platelet aggregation in response to ADP.

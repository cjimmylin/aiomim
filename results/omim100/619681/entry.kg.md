### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia (DYTSPG) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the ATP5MC3 gene (602736) on chromosome 2q31. ⚠︎

### Description

Early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia is an autosomal dominant neurologic disorder with variable presentation. Neilson et al. (2022) described it in a large family as childhood-onset, progressive generalized dystonia or adult-onset, gradually progressive spastic paraplegia. Less severe, later-onset focal limb and neck dystonia and spasmodic dysphonia also occurred. Zech et al. (2022) reported additional patients with pyramidal signs, spasticity, and developmental delay.

### Clinical Features

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported a large multigenerational Ohio kindred with a highly penetrant autosomal dominant neurologic disorder that caused spasticity and dystonia. Spasticity and weakness began anywhere from the first year of life to the sixth decade. Dystonia began between the first and third decades. Affected members had no ataxia and no apparent cognitive involvement. Neuroimaging and peripheral neurophysiology were normal.

Neilson et al. (2022) summarized a large multigenerational family (Family-1) segregating autosomal dominant dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia. Of 40 individuals in the pedigree, 18 were affected, with either childhood-onset, progressive generalized dystonia or adult-onset, gradually progressive spastic paraplegia. Less severe, later-onset focal limb and neck dystonia and spasmodic dysphonia also occurred. Brain MRI and peripheral nerve physiology were normal when performed. The proband developed unilateral upper limb dystonia at age 2 years. It generalized to the trunk and all limbs and caused loss of ambulation. His father and grandfather had the hereditary spastic paraplegia phenotype, with progressive leg weakness and spasticity beginning after age 20 years. Metabolic studies were normal in the proband and his father, including blood lactate and pyruvate, serum amino acids, urine organic acids, and acylcarnitine profiles.

Neilson et al. (2022) also described a right-handed boy of European descent (Family-2) born to healthy unrelated parents, with no family history of movement disorders. He presented at age 11 years with a 4-year history of gradually worsening upper limb dystonia. Blood, urine, and CSF studies and a dystonia gene panel were unrevealing. Brain MRI showed nonspecific mild bilateral frontoparietal white matter changes.

Zech et al. (2022) described 3 unrelated patients with ATP5MC3 variants. Patient 8, a previously reported 12-year-old boy, had isolated upper-limb dystonia. Patient 9, a 15-year-old girl, had a history of milestone delay, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia with prominent upper-body involvement. Patient 10, a 6-year-old boy, had delayed psychomotor development, lower-extremity spasticity, and elevated blood lactate. Zech et al. (2022) combined their 3 patients with the multigenerational family reported by Neilson et al. (2022). Across these 18 individuals, the ATP5MC3-associated condition included dystonia (12/18), spasticity/pyramidal signs (17/18), and developmental delay/intellectual disability (2/18). Ten individuals had dystonia-predominant syndromes. The authors noted that patients 9 and 10 confirmed spastic symptoms in independent families and added neurodevelopmental comorbidity to the phenotype.

### Biochemical Features

Neilson et al. (2022) studied fibroblasts from the Family-1 proband, who had dystonia, and his affected father, who had hereditary spastic paraplegia. Activities of complexes I through IV were normal. Complex V activity was reduced by 20%, cellular ATP by 20 to 32%, and oxygen consumption by 21 to 25%. Because oxygen consumption and ATP were reduced in proportion, the authors did not consider uncoupling of oxidative phosphorylation a likely mechanism.

### Inheritance

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported autosomal dominant inheritance in a large multigenerational kindred. Neilson et al. (2022) reported autosomal dominant transmission in Family-1 and noted that dystonia in this family showed incomplete penetrance. Zech et al. (2022) reported 3 patients in whom the heterozygous ATP5MC3 variants had arisen de novo.

### Mapping

Gilbert et al. (2009) mapped the disease locus in the Ohio kindred to chromosome 2q24-q31. They used single-point and multipoint linkage analysis with microsatellite markers and SNP array genotyping. The region was flanked by rs1424937 and rs1559510, lay proximal to SPG13, and contained no known hereditary spastic paraplegia or dystonia genes. A secondary analysis looked for candidate regions segregating with the dystonia phenotype and found 2 regions with parametric lod scores above 2.0.

### Heterogeneity

On the basis of the clinical presentation and the linkage results, Gilbert et al. (2009) concluded that the disorder in the Ohio kindred was a novel neurologic disorder, distinct from known forms of hereditary spastic paraplegia and dystonia.

### Molecular Genetics

Family-1 of Neilson et al. (2022) had a disorder previously mapped to chromosome 2q24-q31. In affected members, the authors used whole-exome sequencing restricted to the linkage region and identified a heterozygous c.318C-G mutation in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in an asn106-to-lys (N106K) substitution. Sanger sequencing showed that only affected individuals carried the variant. The variant was absent from gnomAD v2.1.1 and v3.1.1. It affects a highly conserved loop that contacts the F1 subunit, and all in silico tools used predicted it to be damaging. Fibroblasts from 2 affected family members showed reduced complex V activity, cellular ATP, and oxygen consumption. GeneMatcher identified the Family-2 proband, and parent-offspring trio whole-exome sequencing in him found the same heterozygous N106K mutation, which had arisen de novo. The authors stated that this result confirmed the pathogenicity of the variant for dystonia. They also screened 384 patients, 362 with dystonia and 22 with hereditary spastic paraplegia, for variants in ATP5MC1, ATP5MC2, and ATP5MC3 and found no deleterious variants.

Zech et al. (2022) performed whole-exome sequencing in a cohort of 1,012 dystonia patients. In a 12-year-old boy with isolated upper-limb dystonia (patient 8), they found a de novo heterozygous N106K mutation in ATP5MC3. In a 15-year-old girl with milestone delay, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia (patient 9), they identified a de novo heterozygous pro107-to-ala (P107A) mutation. In a 6-year-old boy with delayed psychomotor development, lower-extremity spasticity, and elevated blood lactate (patient 10), they identified a de novo heterozygous gly79-to-val (G79V) mutation. All 3 variants were absent from gnomAD. Patient fibroblasts showed decreased ATPase activity for N106K and G79V.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Zech et al. (2022) compared the G79V mutation, which was associated with a more severe neurodevelopmental disease-spasticity presentation, with the N106K mutation, which was associated with isolated dystonia. G79V produced a significantly greater ATPase enzyme deficit than N106K. The authors cautioned that further studies were required before genotype-phenotype correlations could be postulated.

### Pathogenesis

Neilson et al. (2022) noted that ATP5MC1 and ATP5MC2 also encode subunit c, so haploinsufficiency of ATP5MC3 was unlikely to explain the severe phenotypes. They hypothesized that the mutant protein interferes with the F1-F0 interaction. They also suggested that differences in the relative expression of the mutant gene, for example between striatum and spinal cord, may account for the variable phenotype.

Zech et al. (2022) suggested that dominant-negative effects could underlie disease caused by ATP5MC3 variants. Supporting this, several patient cell lines showed mild-to-moderate defects in ATPase assembly and/or enzyme activity. ⚠︎

### Clinical Management

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported that deep brain stimulation markedly improved generalized dystonia in 1 affected child from the Ohio kindred.

Neilson et al. (2022) reported that the Family-1 proband received bilateral pallidal deep brain stimulation at age 9 years, which restored ambulation and limb use. The response remained excellent at age 25 years.

### Animal Model

Neilson et al. (2022) overexpressed the Drosophila ortholog of ATP5MC3 (CG1746) under the arm-GAL4 driver, carrying either the human-equivalent N102K mutation or the E. coli-equivalent N102E mutation. In geotaxis assays both mutant lines climbed less well than wildtype, and N102K flies were the slowest. Overexpression of wildtype protein had no effect. In fly mitochondria, complex V activity was reduced by 53% with N102K and by 37% with N102E. ⚠︎

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, ASN106LYS [c.318C>G; c.np_001680.1:p.; g.176043127G>C; p.N106K]

Neilson et al. (2022) studied affected members of a large multigenerational family (Family-1) with autosomal dominant dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia. Using whole-exome sequencing restricted to the linkage region on chromosome 2q24-q31, they identified a heterozygous c.318C-G transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in an asn106-to-lys (N106K) substitution. Sanger sequencing showed that only affected individuals carried the variant. It was absent from gnomAD v2.1.1 and v3.1.1, affects a highly conserved loop that contacts the F1 subunit, and was predicted damaging by all in silico tools used. Fibroblasts from the proband and his affected father showed complex V activity reduced by 20%, cellular ATP reduced by 20 to 32%, and oxygen consumption reduced by 21 to 25%. Neilson et al. (2022) also identified the same mutation, which had arisen de novo, by trio whole-exome sequencing in a boy of European descent with upper limb dystonia (Family-2).

Zech et al. (2022) identified a de novo heterozygous N106K mutation by whole-exome sequencing in a 12-year-old boy with isolated upper-limb dystonia (patient 8). The variant was absent from gnomAD and local control exomes. Patient fibroblasts showed a slight but significant decrease in ATPase activity and a reduced oxygen consumption rate. The ATPase enzyme deficit was significantly smaller than that seen with the G79V mutation.

**.0002 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, PRO107ALA [c.319C>G; p.P107A]

Zech et al. (2022) studied a 15-year-old girl (patient 9) with milestone delay, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia. By whole-exome sequencing they identified a de novo heterozygous c.319C-G transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in a pro107-to-ala (P107A) substitution. The variant was absent from gnomAD and local control exomes and affected a stringently conserved residue near the FO-F1 interaction loop. In silico tools predicted it to be deleterious.

**.0003 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, GLY79VAL [c.236G>T; p.G79V]

Zech et al. (2022) studied a 6-year-old boy (patient 10) with delayed psychomotor development, lower-extremity spasticity, and elevated blood lactate. They identified a de novo heterozygous c.236G-T transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in a gly79-to-val (G79V) substitution. The variant was absent from gnomAD. Patient fibroblasts showed deficient ATPase activity and a moderate decrease in assembled ATPase on BN-PAGE. The ATPase enzyme deficit was significantly greater than that seen with the N106K mutation. Modeling placed gly79 at an interhelical contact site, where the substitution might interfere with c-ring integrity.

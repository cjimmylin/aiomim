### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia (DYTSPG) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the ATP5MC3 gene (602736) on chromosome 2q31.

### Description

Early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia is an autosomal dominant neurologic disorder. Its presentation varies, even within one family. Some affected individuals have childhood-onset, progressive dystonia that may generalize. Others have later-onset focal limb or neck dystonia or spasmodic dysphonia. Still others have gradually progressive spastic paraplegia that begins in adulthood (Neilson et al., 2022). Some patients also have developmental delay or intellectual disability (Zech et al., 2022).

### Clinical Features

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported a large multigenerational kindred from Ohio with a highly penetrant autosomal dominant neurologic disorder causing spasticity and dystonia. Spasticity and weakness began anywhere from the first year of life to the sixth decade, and dystonia began between the first and third decades. Affected members had no ataxia and no apparent cognitive involvement. Neuroimaging and peripheral neurophysiology were normal.

Neilson et al. (2022) summarized a large multigenerational family (Family-1) segregating autosomal dominant dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia. Of 40 individuals in the pedigree, 18 were affected. They had either childhood-onset, progressive generalized dystonia or adult-onset, gradually progressive spastic paraplegia. Some members had less severe, later-onset focal limb and neck dystonia or spasmodic dysphonia. When performed, brain MRI and peripheral nerve physiology were normal. The proband developed unilateral upper limb dystonia at age 2 years. It generalized to the trunk and all limbs and caused loss of ambulation. His father and grandfather had the hereditary spastic paraplegia phenotype, with progressive leg weakness and spasticity beginning after age 20 years. Metabolic studies were normal in the proband and his father, including blood lactate and pyruvate, serum amino acids, urine organic acids, and acylcarnitine profiles.

Neilson et al. (2022) also described a right-handed boy of European descent (Family-2). His parents were healthy and unrelated, and there was no family history of movement disorders. He presented at age 11 years with a 4-year history of gradually worsening upper limb dystonia. Blood, urine, and CSF studies and a dystonia gene panel were unrevealing. Brain MRI showed nonspecific mild bilateral frontoparietal white matter changes.

Zech et al. (2022) described 3 unrelated patients with ATP5MC3 mutations. Patient 8, a previously reported 12-year-old boy, had isolated upper-limb dystonia. Patient 9, a 15-year-old girl, had a history of milestone delay, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia with prominent upper-body involvement. Patient 10, a 6-year-old boy, had delayed psychomotor development, lower-extremity spasticity, and elevated blood lactate. Zech et al. (2022) combined their 3 patients with the multigenerational family reported by Neilson et al. (2022). Across these 18 individuals, the disorder included dystonia in 12, spasticity or pyramidal signs in 17, and developmental delay or intellectual disability in 2. Ten individuals had dystonia-predominant syndromes. The authors noted that patients 9 and 10 confirmed spastic symptoms in independent families and added neurodevelopmental comorbidity to the phenotype.

### Biochemical Features

Neilson et al. (2022) studied fibroblasts from the Family-1 proband, who had dystonia, and his affected father, who had hereditary spastic paraplegia. Activities of complexes I through IV were normal. Complex V activity was reduced by 20%, cellular ATP by 20 to 32%, and oxygen consumption by 21 to 25%. Oxygen consumption and ATP were reduced in proportion, so the authors did not consider uncoupling of oxidative phosphorylation a likely mechanism.

### Inheritance

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported autosomal dominant transmission in a large multigenerational kindred, as did Neilson et al. (2022) in Family-1. Neilson et al. (2022) noted that dystonia in Family-1 showed incomplete penetrance. Mutations arose de novo in an isolated patient reported by Neilson et al. (2022) and in 3 unrelated patients reported by Zech et al. (2022).

### Mapping

Gilbert et al. (2009) studied the Ohio kindred with single-point and multipoint linkage analysis, using microsatellite markers and SNP array genotyping. They mapped the disease locus to chromosome 2q24-q31, in a region flanked by rs1424937 and rs1559510. The region lay proximal to SPG13 and contained no known HSP or dystonia genes. A secondary analysis searched for candidate regions segregating with the dystonia phenotype and found 2 regions with parametric lod scores above 2.0.

### Heterogeneity

Based on the clinical presentation and the linkage results, Gilbert et al. (2009) concluded that the disorder in the Ohio kindred was a novel neurologic disorder, distinct from known forms of hereditary spastic paraplegia and dystonia.

### Molecular Genetics

Neilson et al. (2022) studied affected members of Family-1, whose disorder had previously been mapped to chromosome 2q24-q31. Using whole-exome sequencing restricted to the linkage region, they identified a heterozygous missense mutation in the ATP5MC3 gene (c.318C-G; asn106-to-lys, N106K). Sanger sequencing showed that only affected individuals carried the mutation. It was absent from gnomAD v2.1.1 and v3.1.1, affected a highly conserved loop that contacts the F1 subunit, and was predicted damaging by all in silico tools used. The authors concluded that ATP5MC3 remained the primary candidate for the spastic paraplegia in this family.

GeneMatcher identified a match in the Family-2 proband. Neilson et al. (2022) performed parent-offspring trio whole-exome sequencing in this boy and found the same heterozygous N106K mutation in ATP5MC3, which had arisen de novo. The authors stated that this result confirmed the mutation's pathogenicity for dystonia. Neilson et al. (2022) also screened 384 patients for variants in ATP5MC1, ATP5MC2, and ATP5MC3. The group included 362 patients with dystonia and 22 with hereditary spastic paraplegia, and no deleterious variants were found.

Zech et al. (2022) identified de novo heterozygous missense mutations in ATP5MC3 by whole-exome sequencing in 3 unrelated patients. The first, a 12-year-old boy with isolated upper-limb dystonia (patient 8), came from a cohort of 1,012 dystonia patients. He carried the N106K mutation, which had previously been identified in an independent pedigree with 18 of 40 members affected by dystonia and/or spasticity. His fibroblasts showed a slight but significant decrease in ATPase activity and a reduced oxygen consumption rate. The second, a 15-year-old girl with milestone delay, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia (patient 9), carried a pro107-to-ala (P107A) mutation. The third, a 6-year-old boy with delayed psychomotor development, lower-extremity spasticity, and elevated blood lactate (patient 10), carried a gly79-to-val (G79V) mutation. His fibroblasts showed deficient ATPase activity and a moderate decrease in assembled ATPase on BN-PAGE. All 3 mutations were absent from gnomAD.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Zech et al. (2022) compared the G79V and N106K mutations in ATP5MC3. G79V was associated with a more severe presentation of neurodevelopmental disease with spasticity, and N106K with isolated dystonia. G79V produced a significantly greater ATPase enzyme deficit than N106K. The authors cautioned that further studies were required before genotype-phenotype correlations could be postulated.

### Pathogenesis

Neilson et al. (2022) noted that ATP5MC1 and ATP5MC2 also encode subunit c, so haploinsufficiency of ATP5MC3 would be unlikely to explain the severe phenotypes. They hypothesized that the mutant protein interferes with the F1-F0 interaction. They also suggested that differences in the relative expression of the mutant gene between tissues, for example between striatum and spinal cord, might account for the variable phenotype.

Zech et al. (2022) suggested that dominant-negative effects could underlie disease caused by ATP5MC3 variants. They based this on mild-to-moderate defects in ATPase assembly and/or enzyme activity in several patient cell lines. ⚠︎

### Clinical Management

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported that generalized dystonia improved markedly with deep brain stimulation in 1 affected child from the Ohio kindred.

The Family-1 proband of Neilson et al. (2022) received bilateral pallidal deep brain stimulation at age 9 years. It restored ambulation and limb use, and the response remained excellent at age 25 years.

### Animal Model

Neilson et al. (2022) overexpressed the Drosophila ATP5MC3 ortholog (CG1746) under the arm-GAL4 driver. Flies carried either the human-equivalent N102K mutation or the E. coli-equivalent N102E mutation. In geotaxis assays, both mutant lines climbed less well than wildtype, and N102K flies were the slowest. Overexpression of wildtype protein had no effect. In fly mitochondria, complex V activity was reduced by 53% with N102K and by 37% with N102E.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, ASN106LYS [c.318C>G; g.176043127G>C; p.N106K]

Neilson et al. (2022) studied a large multigenerational family (Family-1) with autosomal dominant dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia. In affected members, they identified a heterozygous c.318C-G transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in an asn106-to-lys (N106K) substitution. They found it by whole-exome sequencing restricted to the previously mapped linkage region on chromosome 2q24-q31. Sanger sequencing showed that only affected individuals carried the mutation. It was absent from gnomAD v2.1.1 and v3.1.1, affected a highly conserved loop that contacts the F1 subunit, and was predicted damaging by in silico tools. Fibroblasts from the proband and his affected father showed a 20% reduction in complex V activity, a 20 to 32% reduction in cellular ATP, and a 21 to 25% reduction in oxygen consumption. Activities of complexes I through IV were normal.

Neilson et al. (2022) also identified the N106K mutation in a boy of European descent with upper limb dystonia (Family-2). Trio whole-exome sequencing showed that the heterozygous mutation had arisen de novo.

Zech et al. (2022) identified a de novo heterozygous N106K mutation by whole-exome sequencing in a 12-year-old boy with isolated upper-limb dystonia (patient 8). The mutation was absent from gnomAD and local control exomes. His fibroblasts showed a slight but significant decrease in ATPase activity and a reduced oxygen consumption rate.

**.0002 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, PRO107ALA [c.319C>G; p.P107A]

Zech et al. (2022) studied a 15-year-old girl (patient 9) with milestone delay, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia. By whole-exome sequencing, they identified a de novo heterozygous c.319C-G transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in a pro107-to-ala (P107A) substitution. The mutation was absent from gnomAD and local control exomes. It affected a stringently conserved residue near the FO-F1 interaction loop and was predicted deleterious by in silico tools.

**.0003 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, GLY79VAL [c.236G>T; p.G79V]

Zech et al. (2022) studied a 6-year-old boy (patient 10) with delayed psychomotor development, lower-extremity spasticity, and elevated blood lactate. They identified a de novo heterozygous c.236G-T transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in a gly79-to-val (G79V) substitution. The mutation was absent from gnomAD. His fibroblasts showed deficient ATPase activity and a moderate decrease in assembled ATPase on BN-PAGE. The ATPase deficit was significantly greater than that seen with the N106K mutation. Modeling placed gly79 at an interhelical contact site, where the substitution might interfere with c-ring integrity.

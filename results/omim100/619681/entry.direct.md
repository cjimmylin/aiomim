### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia (DYTSPG) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the ATP5MC3 gene on chromosome 2q.

### Description

Early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia (DYTSPG) is an autosomal dominant neurologic disorder. Affected individuals have childhood-onset dystonia, which can become generalized, or a gradually progressive spastic paraplegia that usually begins in adulthood. Some family members have both. Expression varies, both within families and between unrelated patients, and some patients also have developmental delay (Gilbert et al., 2009; Neilson et al., 2022; Zech et al., 2022). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Gilbert et al. (2009) described a large, multigenerational Ohio kindred with an autosomal dominant, highly penetrant neurologic disorder causing spasticity and dystonia. Spasticity and weakness began between the first year and the sixth decade, and dystonia began between the first and third decades. There was no ataxia and no apparent cognitive involvement. Neuroimaging and peripheral neurophysiology were normal. In 1 child, generalized dystonia improved markedly with deep brain stimulation.

Neilson et al. (2022) gave further clinical information on the kindred reported by Gilbert et al. (2009) (family 1). The pedigree included 40 individuals, 18 of whom had either childhood-onset, progressive generalized dystonia or adult-onset, gradually progressive spastic paraplegia. In the proband, dystonia began in 1 upper limb at age 2 years and spread to the trunk and all limbs, resulting in loss of ambulation. Bilateral pallidal deep brain stimulation, implanted at age 9 years, restored ambulation and limb use, and the response was still excellent at age 25 years. The proband's father and grandfather had the spastic paraplegia phenotype, with progressive leg weakness and spasticity beginning after age 20 years. Some family members had milder, later-onset focal limb and neck dystonia or spasmodic dysphonia, which the authors suggested might be related to trauma or overuse. Where performed, brain MRI and peripheral nerve physiology were normal. Metabolic studies in the proband and his father were normal, including whole blood lactate and pyruvate, serum amino acids, urine organic acids, and blood and urine acylcarnitine profiles. The authors noted that the dystonia in this family showed incomplete penetrance and that the spastic paraplegia was not accompanied by other neurologic problems.

Neilson et al. (2022) also reported a second, unrelated family (family 2). The proband was a right-handed boy of European descent, born to healthy unrelated parents, with no family history of dystonia or related movement disorders. He presented at age 11 years with a 4-year history of gradually worsening upper limb dystonia. Laboratory studies of blood, urine, and cerebrospinal fluid were unrevealing. Brain MRI showed nonspecific, mild bilateral frontoparietal white matter changes.

Zech et al. (2022) reported 3 unrelated patients with de novo heterozygous ATP5MC3 variants. Patient 8, a 12-year-old boy with isolated upper-limb dystonia, was the family 2 proband of Neilson et al. (2022). Patient 9, a 15-year-old girl, had delayed milestones, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia with prominent involvement of the upper body. Patient 10, a 6-year-old boy, had delayed psychomotor development, lower-extremity spasticity, and elevated blood lactate. Zech et al. (2022) combined their patients with the multigenerational family of Neilson et al. (2022), for which clinical information was available on 15 individuals. Across these 18 individuals, features of ATP5MC3-associated disease included dystonia (12 of 18), spasticity or pyramidal signs (17 of 18), and developmental delay or intellectual disability (2 of 18). Ten individuals had dystonia-predominant syndromes. In patients 8 and 9, brain MRI was nondiagnostic.

### Inheritance

Gilbert et al. (2009) found autosomal dominant inheritance of spastic paraplegia and dystonia in a large multigenerational kindred. Neilson et al. (2022) showed that an ATP5MC3 variant cosegregated with the disorder in this family. The same variant occurred de novo in an unrelated patient (Neilson et al., 2022). Zech et al. (2022) reported further patients with de novo heterozygous ATP5MC3 variants.

### Mapping

Gilbert et al. (2009) used single and multipoint linkage analysis, with microsatellite and SNP array genotyping, in a large Ohio kindred with autosomal dominant spastic paraplegia and dystonia. They mapped the disease locus to chromosome 2q24-q31, in a region flanked by markers rs1424937 and rs1559510 and proximal to SPG13. At that time, the region contained no known spastic paraplegia or dystonia genes. A secondary analysis for candidate regions segregating with the dystonia phenotype found 2 regions with parametric lod scores above 2.0.

### Molecular Genetics

Neilson et al. (2022) studied the large family with autosomal dominant spastic paraplegia and dystonia previously reported by Gilbert et al. (2009). Exome sequencing was performed in the proband and a distant cousin with childhood-onset dystonia and restricted to the 2q24-q31 linkage region. It identified a heterozygous c.318C-G transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, predicting an asn106-to-lys (N106K) substitution. Sanger sequencing showed that only affected family members carried the variant. The variant was absent from the gnomAD database (v2.1.1 and v3.1.1). The substituted residue lies in a highly conserved loop of subunit c that interacts directly with the F1 subunit of ATP synthase. Through GeneMatcher, the authors identified the same variant, which had arisen de novo, in an unrelated boy with upper limb dystonia (family 2); in him it was found by parent-offspring trio exome sequencing. Fibroblasts from the family 1 proband, who had dystonia, and from his father, who had spastic paraplegia, showed normal activities of complexes I through IV. However, complex V activity was reduced by 20%, cellular ATP by 20 to 32%, and oxygen consumption by 21 to 25%. Because oxygen consumption and ATP were reduced proportionally, the findings did not support uncoupling of oxidative phosphorylation as the mechanism. Screening of 384 additional patients with dystonia (362) or spastic paraplegia (22) found no deleterious variants in ATP5MC1, ATP5MC2, or ATP5MC3. The authors stated that ATP5MC3 remained the primary candidate gene for the spastic paraplegia in family 1. They regarded the second family as confirming pathogenicity for the dystonia phenotype.

Zech et al. (2022) analyzed exome sequencing data from 2,962 patients with suspected mitochondrial disease and/or dystonia and used international data-sharing. They identified 3 unrelated patients with de novo heterozygous missense variants in ATP5MC3: N106K, found in the patient previously reported by Neilson et al. (2022); pro107-to-ala (P107A); and gly79-to-val (G79V). None of the variants was present in gnomAD or in local control exome databases. All affected conserved residues in the C-terminal helical motifs of the protein. Fibroblasts from the patient with G79V showed deficient ATP synthase activity and a moderate decrease in assembled ATP synthase on blue native PAGE. Fibroblasts from the patient with N106K showed a slight but significant decrease in ATP synthase activity and reduced oxygen consumption. No functional studies were reported for the P107A variant. The authors suggested that the mechanism might be dominant-negative.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Zech et al. (2022) found that ATP5MC3 G79V, seen in a patient with neurodevelopmental disease and spasticity, caused a significantly greater ATP synthase deficit in fibroblasts than N106K, seen in a patient with isolated dystonia. The authors noted that further studies were needed before genotype-phenotype correlations could be clearly established.

### Animal Model

Neilson et al. (2022) generated transgenic Drosophila that overexpressed the fly ortholog of ATP5MC3 (CG1746) under an armadillo-GAL4 driver. They used wildtype CG1746, a mutant equivalent to human N106K (N102K), and a mutant equivalent to an E. coli atpE mutation (N102E). Both mutant lines climbed less well in geotaxis assays than wildtype flies, and the N102K mutant was the slowest. Flies overexpressing wildtype protein did not differ from wildtype flies. In mitochondria from the flies, complex V activity was reduced by 53% in the N102K mutant and by 37% in the N102E mutant.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, ASN106LYS [NM_001689.4:c.318C>G; NP_001680.1:p.Asn106Lys]

Neilson et al. (2022) studied a large, multigenerational family with autosomal dominant dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia, previously reported by Gilbert et al. (2009). In affected members they identified a heterozygous c.318C-G transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, predicting an asn106-to-lys (N106K) substitution in a conserved loop that interacts with the F1 subunit. The mutation was found by exome sequencing restricted to the linkage region. It cosegregated with the disorder and was absent from gnomAD. Fibroblasts from 2 affected family members showed complex V activity reduced by 20%, along with reduced cellular ATP and oxygen consumption. In Drosophila, overexpression of the equivalent mutation reduced mobility and complex V activity. The same mutation was found de novo in an unrelated boy with upper limb dystonia.

Zech et al. (2022) restudied the boy with de novo N106K and isolated upper-limb dystonia who had been reported by Neilson et al. (2022). Newly established fibroblasts from this patient showed a slight but significant decrease in ATP synthase activity and reduced oxygen consumption.

**.0002 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, PRO107ALA [NM_001689.5:c.319C>G; NP_001680.1:p.Pro107Ala]

Zech et al. (2022) reported a 15-year-old girl (patient 9) with delayed milestones, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia with prominent upper-body involvement. She carried a de novo heterozygous c.319C-G transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, predicting a pro107-to-ala (P107A) substitution. The variant was identified by exome sequencing and was absent from gnomAD and local control exome databases. On structural modeling, it lay next to the loop that mediates the interaction between the F0 and F1 subcomplexes. No functional studies were reported.

**.0003 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, GLY79VAL [NM_001689.5:c.236G>T; NP_001680.1:p.Gly79Val]

Zech et al. (2022) reported a 6-year-old boy (patient 10) with delayed psychomotor development, lower-extremity spasticity, and elevated blood lactate. He carried a de novo heterozygous c.236G-T transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, predicting a gly79-to-val (G79V) substitution at an interhelical contact site. The variant was identified by exome sequencing and was absent from gnomAD and local control exome databases. Patient fibroblasts showed deficient ATP synthase activity and a moderate decrease in assembled ATP synthase on blue native PAGE.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia (DYTSPG) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the ATP5MC3 gene on chromosome 2q.

### Description

Early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia (DYTSPG) is an autosomal dominant neurologic disorder. Within a single family, affected people can have childhood-onset, progressive generalized dystonia, adult-onset, gradually progressive spastic paraplegia, or both (Neilson et al., 2022). Patients with de novo mutations have had isolated upper-limb dystonia or dystonia and/or spasticity, sometimes with developmental delay (Zech et al., 2022).

### Clinical Features

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported a large, multigenerational kindred from Ohio with an autosomal dominant, highly penetrant neurologic disorder that caused spasticity and dystonia. Spasticity and weakness began anywhere from the first year of life to the sixth decade, and dystonia began in the first to third decade. Affected members had no ataxia and no apparent cognitive involvement. Neuroimaging and peripheral neurophysiology were normal. In 1 child, generalized dystonia improved markedly with deep brain stimulation.

Neilson et al. (2022) gave further clinical details of the family reported by Gilbert et al. (2009) (Family-1). The proband's dystonia began in 1 upper limb at age 2 years and spread to the trunk and all limbs, and he lost the ability to walk. Bilateral pallidal deep brain stimulation, implanted at age 9 years, restored walking and limb use, and the response was still excellent at age 25 years. His father and grandfather had the spastic paraplegia phenotype, with progressive leg weakness and spasticity beginning after age 20 years. The pedigree included 40 individuals, of whom 18 were affected with either childhood-onset, progressive generalized dystonia or adult-onset, gradually progressive spastic paraplegia. Milder, later-onset focal limb and neck dystonia and spasmodic dysphonia also occurred, possibly related to trauma or overuse. Brain MRI and peripheral nerve studies, where performed, were normal. Metabolic studies in the proband and his father were also normal, including whole blood lactate and pyruvate, serum amino acids, urine organic acids, and blood and urine acylcarnitine profiles. Neilson et al. (2022) noted that the dystonia in this pedigree showed incomplete penetrance. ⚠︎

Neilson et al. (2022) also reported a second, unrelated family (Family-2). The proband was a right-handed boy born to healthy, unrelated parents of European descent, with no family history of dystonia. He was first seen at age 11 years with a 4-year history of gradually worsening upper limb dystonia. Laboratory studies of blood, urine, and cerebrospinal fluid were unrevealing. Brain MRI showed nonspecific mild frontoparietal white matter changes on both sides.

Zech et al. (2022) reported 3 unrelated patients with de novo ATP5MC3 mutations. Patient 8 was the Family-2 proband of Neilson et al. (2022), a 12-year-old boy with isolated upper-limb dystonia. Patient 9 was a 15-year-old girl with delayed milestones, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia mainly affecting the upper body. Patient 10 was a 6-year-old boy with delayed psychomotor development, spasticity of the lower limbs, and raised blood lactate levels. Zech et al. (2022) combined their patients with the multigenerational family reported by Neilson et al. (2022), for whom clinical information was available on 15 individuals. In this combined group, features included dystonia in 12 of 18, spasticity or pyramidal signs in 17 of 18, and developmental delay or intellectual disability in 2 of 18. In 10 individuals the syndrome was dystonia-predominant.

### Inheritance

Gilbert et al. (2009) found autosomal dominant inheritance of spastic paraplegia and dystonia in a large Ohio kindred. Neilson et al. (2022) showed that a heterozygous ATP5MC3 mutation cosegregated with the disorder in this family. Neilson et al. (2022) and Zech et al. (2022) also reported patients with de novo heterozygous ATP5MC3 mutations.

### Mapping

Gilbert et al. (2009) performed single- and multipoint linkage analysis in a large Ohio kindred, using microsatellite and SNP array genotyping. They mapped the disease locus to chromosome 2q24-q31, between markers rs1424937 and rs1559510, proximal to SPG13. No known HSP or dystonia genes were located in this region. A secondary analysis looking for loci segregating with the dystonia phenotype found 2 candidate regions with parametric lod scores above 2.0.

### Molecular Genetics

In the proband of the family reported by Gilbert et al. (2009), Neilson et al. (2022) identified a heterozygous c.318C-G transversion in exon 4 of the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in an asn106-to-lys (N106K) substitution. The mutation was found by whole-exome sequencing and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. Exome analysis of the proband and a distant cousin with childhood-onset dystonia, restricted to the 2q24-31 linkage region, found this as the only shared novel coding variant. By Sanger sequencing, the mutation was present only in affected family members. The mutation was absent from gnomAD. The substituted residue lies in a highly conserved loop of subunit c of the F0 portion of mitochondrial ATP synthase, a loop that directly interacts with the F1 portion. Fibroblasts from the proband (dystonia) and his affected father (spastic paraplegia) had normal activity of complexes I through IV. However, complex V activity was reduced by 20%, cellular ATP by 20 to 32%, and oxygen consumption by 21 to 25%. The proportional reduction in oxygen consumption and ATP did not support uncoupling of oxidative phosphorylation as the mechanism. Through GeneMatcher, the same mutation was found in heterozygous state in the Family-2 proband by parent-offspring trio exome sequencing; it had occurred de novo. Screening of 384 additional patients with dystonia (362) or spastic paraplegia (22) found no deleterious variants in ATP5MC1, ATP5MC2, or ATP5MC3. Neilson et al. (2022) concluded that the second family confirmed pathogenicity for dystonia, while ATP5MC3 remained the primary candidate for the spastic paraplegia in Family-1.

Zech et al. (2022) analyzed exome data from 2,962 patients with suspected mitochondrial disease and/or dystonia and also used gene matching. They identified 3 unrelated patients with de novo heterozygous missense mutations in the ATP5MC3 gene: N106K (patient 8, the Family-2 proband of Neilson et al., 2022), P107A (patient 9), and G79V (patient 10). None of these variants were in gnomAD or local control exome databases. All affected stringently conserved residues in the C-terminal helical motifs of the protein. Fibroblasts from patient 10 had deficient ATPase activity and a moderate decrease in assembled ATPase on blue native PAGE. Fibroblasts from patient 8 had a slight but significant decrease in ATPase activity and a reduced oxygen consumption rate.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Zech et al. (2022) found a significantly greater ATPase enzyme deficit with the G79V mutation than with N106K. G79V was found in a patient with neurodevelopmental disease and spasticity, whereas N106K was found in a patient with isolated dystonia. They cautioned that further studies were needed before genotype-phenotype correlations could be clearly postulated.

### Animal Model

Neilson et al. (2022) expressed mutant forms of the Drosophila ATP5MC3 homolog CG1746 in transgenic flies, using the armadillo-GAL4 driver. One mutant, N102K, was equivalent to the human mutation; the other, N102E, was equivalent to a mutation in E. coli. Both mutants reduced mobility in a geotaxis assay compared with wildtype, and the N102K mutant was the slowest. Overexpression of wildtype protein had no such effect. Fly mitochondria had complex V activity reduced by 53% with the N102K mutant and by 37% with the N102E mutant.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, ASN106LYS [NM_001689.4:c.318C>G; NP_001680.1:p.Asn106Lys]

Neilson et al. (2022) identified a heterozygous c.318C-G transversion in exon 4 of the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in an asn106-to-lys (N106K) substitution. It was found in affected members of a large Ohio family with autosomal dominant dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia, first reported by Gilbert et al. (2009). The mutation was identified by exome sequencing, cosegregated with the disorder, and was absent from gnomAD. Patient fibroblasts had complex V activity reduced by 20%. Drosophila expressing the equivalent mutation had reduced mobility and reduced complex V activity. Neilson et al. (2022) found the same mutation de novo in an unrelated boy of European descent with upper limb dystonia. ⚠︎

Zech et al. (2022) studied newly established fibroblasts from the boy with de novo N106K and isolated upper-limb dystonia previously reported by Neilson et al. (2022). The fibroblasts showed a slight but significant decrease in ATPase activity and a reduced oxygen consumption rate.

**.0002 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, PRO107ALA [NM_001689.5:c.319C>G; NP_001680.1:p.Pro107Ala]

Zech et al. (2022) identified a de novo heterozygous c.319C-G transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in a pro107-to-ala (P107A) substitution, in a 15-year-old girl (patient 9). She had delayed milestones, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia mainly affecting the upper body. The mutation was found by exome sequencing and was not present in gnomAD or local control exome databases. Structural modeling placed pro107 at the interaction site between the F0 and F1 subcomplexes.

**.0003 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, GLY79VAL [NM_001689.5:c.236G>T; NP_001680.1:p.Gly79Val]

Zech et al. (2022) identified a de novo heterozygous c.236G-T transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in a gly79-to-val (G79V) substitution, in a 6-year-old boy (patient 10). He had delayed psychomotor development, spasticity of the lower limbs, and raised blood lactate levels. The mutation was found by exome sequencing and was not present in gnomAD or local control exome databases. The patient's fibroblasts had deficient ATPase activity and a moderate decrease in assembled ATPase.

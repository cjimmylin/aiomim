### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that primary coenzyme Q10 deficiency-2 (COQ10D2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the PDSS1 gene (607429) on chromosome 10p12.1.

### Description

Primary coenzyme Q10 deficiency-2 is a mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) disorder (Mollet et al., 2007). The PDSS1 gene encodes subunit 1 of prenyldiphosphate synthase (Mollet et al., 2007). Nardecchia et al. (2021) stated that PDSS1 acts in the first step of coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10) synthesis, as part of a heterotetramer, decaprenyl diphosphate synthase, that is involved in synthesizing a side chain of the appropriate length.

### Biochemical Features

In patients from a family with CoQ10 deficiency (family 1), Mollet et al. (2007) found that quinone-dependent OXPHOS activities were in the range of the lowest control values. CoQ10 deficiency was confirmed when the addition of quinone restored normal OXPHOS activities.

### Inheritance

Mollet et al. (2007) identified a homozygous PDSS1 mutation in affected members of a family with CoQ10 deficiency (family 1). This finding is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of a family with CoQ10 deficiency (family 1), Mollet et al. (2007) sequenced the PDSS1 gene and identified a homozygous nucleotide substitution that changes a conserved amino acid of the protein (D308E). Mollet et al. (2007) then transformed a yeast Deltacoq1 strain with a mutant yeast COQ1 gene carrying the corresponding mutation. The transformed strain grew poorly on respiratory medium. The human PDSS1 gene itself was not used in this complementation assay. ⚠︎

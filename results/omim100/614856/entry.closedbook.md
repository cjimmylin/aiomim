### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that osteogenesis imperfecta type XIII (OI13) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the BMP1 gene (112264) on chromosome 8p21. ⚠︎

### Description

Osteogenesis imperfecta type XIII is an autosomal recessive form of OI caused by defective BMP1 (procollagen C-proteinase) activity. It is characterized by recurrent fractures and bone fragility. Unlike most other forms of OI, it is associated with normal or increased bone mineral density. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Martinez-Glez et al. (2012) studied a consanguineous Egyptian family in which affected members had autosomal recessive OI with recurrent fractures and, unusually for OI, increased bone mineral density. ✗

Asharani et al. (2012) described affected sibs from a consanguineous family who had severe bone fragility with multiple fractures. Bone mineral density was increased. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OI13 in the families reported by Martinez-Glez et al. (2012) and Asharani et al. (2012) fit autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of a consanguineous Egyptian family with OI13, Martinez-Glez et al. (2012) identified a homozygous missense mutation in the BMP1 gene (F249L). It affected the protease domain. Functional studies showed that BMP1/mTLD proteolytic activity was deficient, with impaired processing of the type I procollagen C-propeptide. ⚠︎

Asharani et al. (2012) identified a homozygous missense mutation in the BMP1 gene (G12R) in affected sibs with OI13. The mutation lay in the signal peptide. The authors linked this finding to the zebrafish 'frilly fins' (frf) mutant, which carries bmp1a mutations. That mutant showed defective procollagen C-terminal processing and abnormal bone formation, and the human mutation was shown to reduce BMP1 function. ⚠︎

### Animal Model

Asharani et al. (2012) showed that zebrafish 'frilly fins' mutants carry mutations in bmp1a. The mutants had defective procollagen I C-propeptide processing and disorganized collagen fibrils, together with abnormal fin and bone development and increased bone density. ✗

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that osteogenesis imperfecta type XIII (OI13) is caused by homozygous mutation in the BMP1 gene (112264) on chromosome 8p21.3.

### Description

Osteogenesis imperfecta type XIII is an autosomal recessive form of OI (Martínez-Glez et al. (2012)). Martínez-Glez et al. (2012) showed that the causative BMP1 mutation in their family resulted in a BMP1/mTLD protein with deficient proteolytic activity toward the C-propeptide of type I procollagen. The disorder is characterized by recurrent fractures and bone fragility (Asharani et al. (2012)). Asharani et al. (2012) reported increased bone mineral density in their family, whereas the patients reported by Martínez-Glez et al. (2012) had decreased bone density.

### Clinical Features

Martínez-Glez et al. (2012) studied a consanguineous Egyptian family with 2 children diagnosed with severe autosomal recessive OI. The affected children had recurrent fractures. They had generalized decreased bone density, with borderline osteoporosis to osteoporosis on bone densitometry. ⚠︎

Asharani et al. (2012) described a consanguineous family affected by increased bone mineral density and multiple recurrent fractures.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OI13 in the family reported by Martínez-Glez et al. (2012) fit autosomal recessive inheritance: the 2 affected children were homozygous for the BMP1 mutation, whereas the parents and the unaffected sibs tested carried it in the heterozygous state. The finding of a homozygous BMP1 mutation in a consanguineous family by Asharani et al. (2012) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In 2 affected children from a consanguineous Egyptian family with OI13, Martínez-Glez et al. (2012) identified a homozygous c.747C-G transversion in exon 6 of the BMP1 gene, resulting in a phe249-to-leu (F249L) substitution within the protease domain. Type I procollagen analysis in supernatants from cultured patient fibroblasts showed abnormal processing of the procollagen C-propeptide, and the authors concluded that the mutation caused a strong reduction of BMP1/mTLD proteolytic function.

In a consanguineous family with OI13, Asharani et al. (2012) identified a homozygous c.34G-C mutation in the BMP1 gene. The mutation was located within the BMP1 signal peptide and led to impaired secretion and an alteration in posttranslational modification, consistent with reduced BMP1 function.

### Animal Model

Asharani et al. (2012) characterized a zebrafish bone mutant harboring lesions in bmp1a. They found that Bmp1a is critically required for mature-collagen generation, downstream of osteoblast maturation, in bone, demonstrating conservation of BMP1 function in osteogenesis across species.

In 2 affected children from a consanguineous Egyptian family with OI13, Martínez-Glez et al. (2012) identified a homozygous phe249-to-leu (F249L) mutation within the protease domain of the BMP1 gene. **.0002 OSTEOGENESIS IMPERFECTA, TYPE XIII**  
BMP1, 34G-C [c.34G>C]

In a consanguineous family with OI13, Asharani et al. (2012) identified a homozygous c.34G-C mutation in the signal peptide of BMP1. Affected individuals had multiple fractures and increased bone mineral density. Functional studies showed that the mutation led to impaired secretion and an alteration in posttranslational modification, reducing BMP1 function.

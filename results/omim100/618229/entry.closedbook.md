### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that mitochondrial complex I deficiency nuclear type 7 (MC1DN7) is caused by homozygous mutation in the NDUFV2 gene (600532) on chromosome 18p11.

### Description

Mitochondrial complex I deficiency nuclear type 7 (MC1DN7) is an autosomal recessive disorder of mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation. It usually begins in early infancy. Reported features include hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, encephalopathy, hypotonia and lactic acidosis, and the disorder is often fatal in infancy. Mitochondrial complex I deficiency is genetically heterogeneous and can be caused by mutation in many nuclear-encoded and mitochondrial-encoded genes. ✗

### Clinical Features

Benit et al. (2003) reported sibs with isolated complex I deficiency who had early-onset hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and encephalopathy. ⚠︎

### Biochemical Features

Patients showed isolated deficiency of mitochondrial complex I activity in tissues and/or cultured fibroblasts. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

MC1DN7 follows autosomal recessive inheritance. This is based on the homozygous NDUFV2 mutation in affected sibs reported by Benit et al. (2003). ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Mitochondrial complex I deficiency is genetically heterogeneous. Many nuclear-encoded structural subunits and assembly factors, as well as mitochondrial DNA-encoded subunits, have been implicated. ✗

### Molecular Genetics

Benit et al. (2003) screened patients with complex I deficiency for mutations in nuclear-encoded complex I subunit genes. In affected sibs they identified a homozygous 4-bp deletion in intron 2 of the NDUFV2 gene. The deletion affected the splice donor site and caused skipping of exon 3. ✗

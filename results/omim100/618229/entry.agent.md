### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that mitochondrial complex I deficiency nuclear type 7 (MC1DN7) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the NDUFV2 gene, which encodes the 24-kD subunit of complex I.

### Description

Mitochondrial complex I deficiency nuclear type 7 (MC1DN7) is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by mutation in NDUFV2, a nuclear gene encoding a subunit of respiratory chain complex I. Affected individuals have been reported with early-onset hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, trunk hypotonia and encephalopathy, and others with Leigh syndrome without cardiac involvement (Benit et al., 2003; Cameron et al., 2015). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Benit et al. (2003) reported 3 affected sibs from a consanguineous family with complex I deficiency and early-onset hypertrophic cardiomyopathy with trunk hypotonia. The authors noted that, whereas mutations in a number of complex I subunit genes essentially result in neurologic symptoms, this NDUFV2 mutation was strikingly associated with cardiomyopathy.

Cameron et al. (2015) reported 2 sibs with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and brain atrophy who were diagnosed with complex I deficiency on the basis of low enzyme activity in muscle and a high lactate/pyruvate ratio in fibroblasts. In a second, consanguineous family, 3 of 5 sibs were affected and presented with Leigh syndrome but no cardiac involvement.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of MC1DN7 in the families reported by Benit et al. (2003) and Cameron et al. (2015) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance: affected sibs from consanguineous families were homozygous for an NDUFV2 mutation, and affected sibs in another family were compound heterozygous. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In 3 sibs from a consanguineous family with complex I deficiency, early-onset hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, and trunk hypotonia, Benit et al. (2003) identified a homozygous 4-bp deletion in intron 2 of the NDUFV2 gene (IVS2+5_+8delGTAA). The mutation was found by a combination of denaturing high performance liquid chromatography and sequence analysis. The mutation altered the consensus splice donor site of exon 2 and resulted in a 70% decrease in NDUFV2 protein and complex I deficiency.

In 2 sibs with complex I deficiency, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, and brain atrophy, Cameron et al. (2015) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the NDUFV2 gene: the previously reported intronic mutation (c.IVS2+1delGTAA) and a novel 1-bp insertion (c.669_670insG) predicted to cause a frameshift (Ser224ValfsTer3). The mutations were found by whole-exome sequencing of fibroblast DNA from 1 sib. Subsequent investigation of 10 probands with complex I deficiency from different families revealed homozygosity for the c.IVS2+1delGTAA mutation in a second, consanguineous family in which 3 of 5 sibs were affected with Leigh syndrome without cardiac involvement.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Cameron et al. (2015) noted that the homozygous c.IVS2+1delGTAA genotype in NDUFV2 had previously been reported in families presenting with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, trunk hypotonia, and encephalopathy, whereas the affected sibs homozygous for the same mutation in their consanguineous family had Leigh syndrome with no cardiac involvement, expanding the clinical phenotypes associated with this mutation.

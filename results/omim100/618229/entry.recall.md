### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that mitochondrial complex I deficiency nuclear type 7 (MC1DN7) is caused by homozygous mutation in the NDUFV2 gene (600532) on chromosome 18p11.

### Description

Mitochondrial complex I deficiency nuclear type 7 (MC1DN7) is a disorder of mitochondrial respiratory chain complex I. Bénit et al. (2003) described early-onset hypertrophic cardiomyopathy with trunk hypotonia and encephalopathy in affected individuals. Cameron et al. (2015) reported a high lactate/pyruvate ratio in fibroblasts. Respiratory chain complex I deficiencies are a genetically heterogeneous group of diseases resulting from mutations in either mitochondrial or nuclear DNA (Bénit et al., 2003).

### Clinical Features

Bénit et al. (2003) reported 3 affected sibs of a consanguineous family who had complex I deficiency and early-onset hypertrophic cardiomyopathy with trunk hypotonia and encephalopathy.

### Biochemical Features

In the sibs reported by Bénit et al. (2003), the homozygous NDUFV2 mutation resulted in complex I deficiency.

### Inheritance

The homozygous NDUFV2 mutation in 3 affected sibs of a consanguineous family reported by Bénit et al. (2003) is compatible with autosomal recessive inheritance of MC1DN7.

### Heterogeneity

Respiratory chain complex I deficiencies are a genetically heterogeneous group of diseases, and mutations in a number of genes encoding complex I subunits have been reported (Bénit et al., 2003). Loeffen et al. (2001) noted that attention had mainly focused on the relation between complex I deficiency and mtDNA mutations.

### Molecular Genetics

Using a combination of denaturing high performance liquid chromatography and sequence analysis, Bénit et al. (2003) identified a homozygous 4-bp deletion in intron 2 of the NDUFV2 gene (IVS2+5_+8delGTAA) in 3 affected sibs of a consanguineous family with complex I deficiency. The mutation altered the consensus splice donor site.

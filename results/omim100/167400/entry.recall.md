### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that paroxysmal extreme pain disorder (PEXPD) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the SCN9A gene (603415) on chromosome 2q24.3.

### Description

Paroxysmal extreme pain disorder is an inherited condition characterized by paroxysms of rectal, ocular, or submandibular pain with flushing (Fertleman et al., 2006). Hayden and Grossman (1959) described it as a familial autonomic disorder. According to Fertleman et al. (2007), onset is in the neonatal period or infancy and the disorder persists throughout life. It is characterized by attacks of excruciating deep burning pain, often in the rectal, ocular, or jaw areas, triggered by factors such as defecation, cold wind, eating, and emotion. Harlequin color change and tonic attacks occur, and dramatic syncopes with bradycardia and sometimes asystole are common (Fertleman et al., 2007). The disorder was formerly called familial rectal pain syndrome (Fertleman and Ferrie, 2006). ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

Fertleman and Ferrie (2006) reported that familial rectal pain syndrome had been renamed paroxysmal extreme pain disorder. Fertleman et al. (2006) used the name paroxysmal extreme pain disorder (PEPD) and noted that the condition was previously known as familial rectal pain. They described paroxysms of rectal, ocular, or submandibular pain with flushing.

### Clinical Features

Hayden and Grossman (1959) reported a family with rectal, ocular, and submaxillary pain. They described it as a familial autonomic disorder related to proctalgia fugax.

Fertleman et al. (2007) gave a detailed clinical description of 77 individuals from 15 families with paroxysmal extreme pain disorder. Autonomic manifestations predominated at first, including skin flushing, harlequin color change, and tonic attacks. Dramatic syncopes with bradycardia and sometimes asystole were common. Later, the disorder was characterized by attacks of excruciating deep burning pain, often in the rectal, ocular, or jaw areas. Attacks were triggered by factors such as defecation, cold wind, eating, and emotion. Carbamazepine was effective in almost all patients who tried it, although the response was often incomplete.

### Inheritance

Hayden and Grossman (1959) reported a family with the disorder. Fertleman et al. (2006) described paroxysmal extreme pain disorder as an inherited condition and identified SCN9A mutations in 11 families and 2 sporadic cases. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Fertleman et al. (2006) carried out mutational analysis of the candidate gene SCN9A, which encodes the sodium channel Nav1.7. In 11 families and 2 sporadic cases with paroxysmal extreme pain disorder, they identified 8 missense mutations. Functional analysis in vitro of mutant Nav1.7 channels showed a reduction in fast inactivation, leading to a persistent sodium current. Fertleman et al. (2006) contrasted this with other SCN9A mutations, which cause primary erythermalgia and are associated with more negative activation thresholds. ⚠︎

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that polymicrogyria with or without vascular-type Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (PMGEDSV) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the COL3A1 gene (120180) on chromosome 2q32.2.

### Description

Polymicrogyria with or without vascular-type Ehlers-Danlos syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic mutations in COL3A1 (Vandervore et al., 2017). It mainly affects brain development (Horn et al., 2017). Affected individuals have a cobblestone-like cortical malformation or polymicrogyria, often with white matter changes and cerebellar cysts (Vandervore et al., 2017). Developmental delay is present, and seizures have been reported in some patients (Horn et al., 2017). Some patients also have connective tissue and vascular features, such as joint laxity and aneurysmal brain hemorrhage (Horn et al., 2017). Heterozygous mutations in COL3A1 cause autosomal dominant vascular Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (Schwarze et al., 2001). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Vandervore et al. (2017) studied a family with 2 affected and 2 unaffected sibs and also reviewed brain imaging of 2 previously reported individuals with biallelic COL3A1 mutations, 3 families in total. The patients had a cobblestone-like cortical malformation, together with white matter abnormalities and cerebellar cysts. Brain MRI showed a phenotype similar to that associated with mutations in GPR56.

Horn et al. (2017) described patients with biallelic COL3A1 mutations from 2 unrelated families. They showed a spectrum of specific structural brain anomalies, including polymicrogyria-type cortical malformation, and developmental delay. Seizures were reported in the sibs of the second family. The patients had connective tissue abnormalities that overlapped with vascular Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, including joint laxity and, in 1 patient, an aneurysmal brain hemorrhage at age 2 years. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Autosomal recessive inheritance of this disorder was shown by the finding of homozygous or compound heterozygous COL3A1 mutations in affected individuals (Vandervore et al., 2017; Horn et al., 2017).

### Molecular Genetics

Vandervore et al. (2017) identified biallelic COL3A1 mutations in patients with a cobblestone-like cortical malformation. They noted that type III procollagen, encoded by COL3A1, is the ligand of GPR56 (ADGRG1), and that biallelic mutations in GPR56 give rise to cobblestone-like malformation, white matter changes and cerebellar dysplasia.

Horn et al. (2017) reported additional patients with biallelic COL3A1 mutations and a combination of brain malformations and connective tissue abnormalities. ⚠︎

### Animal Model

Jeong et al. (2012) noted that collagen III is the ligand of GPR56. In Col3a1-null mice, they found cortical malformations in which neurons migrated past the pial basement membrane. This cobblestone-like defect mirrored what they had seen in Gpr56-null mice.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that polymicrogyria with or without vascular-type Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (PMGEDSV) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the COL3A1 gene (120180) on chromosome 2q32.

### Description

Polymicrogyria with or without vascular-type Ehlers-Danlos syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic mutations in COL3A1. It mainly affects brain development. Affected individuals have a cobblestone-like cortical malformation or polymicrogyria, often with white matter changes and cerebellar cysts, along with developmental delay and seizures. Some patients also have connective tissue features of vascular Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, such as skin fragility, easy bruising and arterial complications. Heterozygous mutations in COL3A1 cause autosomal dominant vascular Ehlers-Danlos syndrome. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Vandervore et al. (2017) reported patients from several unrelated families. They had a cobblestone-like cortical malformation, mainly frontal polymicrogyria-like overmigration, together with white matter abnormalities and cerebellar cysts. These brain findings resemble those in bilateral frontoparietal polymicrogyria caused by GPR56 mutations. Clinically, the patients had developmental delay and intellectual disability, and some had seizures. Some patients also had connective tissue findings consistent with Ehlers-Danlos syndrome. ✗

Horn et al. (2017) described patients with biallelic COL3A1 mutations. They showed a spectrum of specific structural brain anomalies, including polymicrogyria-type cortical malformation, combined with connective tissue abnormalities that overlapped with vascular Ehlers-Danlos syndrome.

### Inheritance

Autosomal recessive inheritance of this disorder was shown by the finding of homozygous or compound heterozygous COL3A1 mutations in affected individuals (Vandervore et al., 2017; Horn et al., 2017).

### Molecular Genetics

Vandervore et al. (2017) identified biallelic COL3A1 mutations in patients with a cobblestone-like cortical malformation. They studied COL3A1 because collagen III is a ligand of GPR56 (ADGRG1), the receptor whose loss causes bilateral frontoparietal polymicrogyria. ⚠︎

Horn et al. (2017) reported more patients with biallelic COL3A1 mutations and a combination of brain malformations and connective tissue abnormalities.

### Animal Model

Luo et al. (2011) identified collagen III as the ligand of GPR56 in the developing brain. Col3a1-null mice had cortical malformations, with neurons migrating past the pial basement membrane, which resembled the cobblestone-like defect seen in Gpr56-deficient mice. ⚠︎

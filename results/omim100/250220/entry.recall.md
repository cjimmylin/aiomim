### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spondylometaphyseal dysplasia of the Sedaghatian type (SMDS) is caused by compound heterozygous mutation in the GPX4 gene (138322) on chromosome 19p13.3. ⚠︎

### Description

Spondylometaphyseal dysplasia of the Sedaghatian type is a rare congenital skeletal dysplasia (Aygun et al., 2012) that is lethal in the neonatal period (Smith et al., 2014). Opitz et al. (1987) suggested autosomal recessive inheritance. Radiologic findings include metaphyseal chondrodysplasia (Koutouby et al., 2000), mild rhizomelic shortness of the limbs, platyspondyly, and 'laciness' of the iliac wings (Peeden et al., 1992). Affected infants may have cardiorespiratory insufficiency (Sedaghatian, 1980) and cardiac conduction defects (Smith et al., 2014). Aygun et al. (2012) suggested that central nervous system malformations might be a major component of the disorder, and English et al. (2006) raised the possibility of neuronal migration abnormalities. Koutouby et al. (2000) noted that all previously reported cases had died within 4 days of life, although their patient survived 161 days.

### Clinical Features

Sedaghatian (1980) described 2 brothers, born within a year of each other, with congenital lethal metaphyseal chondrodysplasia with severe congenital metaphyseal involvement. Both died at 3 days of cardiorespiratory insufficiency.

Peeden et al. (1992) reported an infant with a lethal course. They noted that the skeletal abnormalities in the infant reported by Sedaghatian (1980) included mild rhizomelic shortness of the limbs, platyspondyly, and 'laciness' of the iliac wings, and that Opitz et al. (1987) had described another Iranian infant, born to first cousins, with a similar perinatal course and roentgenograms.

Koutouby et al. (2000) reported an infant whose radiologic findings were consistent with lethal metaphyseal chondrodysplasia (Sedaghatian type). Although all previously reported cases had died within 4 days of life, their patient survived 161 days.

English et al. (2006) reported an infant who experienced recurrent cyanotic episodes within a few hours of delivery. Cerebral imaging revealed absence of the corpus callosum and marked frontotemporal pachygyria. The authors suggested a novel association of the disorder with agenesis of the corpus callosum and highlighted the possibility of neuronal migration abnormalities in the disorder.

Aygun et al. (2012) reported a patient with congenital atrioventricular block and suggested that central nervous system malformations might be a major component of the disorder.

### Inheritance

Opitz et al. (1987) noted the occurrence of the disorder in children of both sexes born to normal parents who were first cousins, and suggested that Sedaghatian syndrome is an autosomal recessive trait. Peeden et al. (1992) noted that the infant reported by Opitz et al. (1987) was born to first cousins, suggesting an autosomal recessive single gene defect. Smith et al. (2014) identified biallelic truncating GPX4 mutations in an affected child.

### Molecular Genetics

By whole-exome sequencing of a child with Sedaghatian-type spondylometaphyseal dysplasia and her unaffected parents, Smith et al. (2014) identified 2 rare variants in the GPX4 gene, which encodes glutathione peroxidase-4, in compound heterozygous state. In a second family, studied by Sanger sequencing, the affected child's DNA was unavailable, and the genotype was inferred from the carrier parents. Both mutations resulted in a frameshift and premature truncation of GPX4. Smith et al. (2014) concluded that the identification of truncating GPX4 mutations in 2 families supported the pathogenic role of mutated GPX4 in this disease. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Smith et al. (2014) noted that GPX4 protects cells against membrane lipid peroxidation, and stated that their findings highlighted the importance of this enzyme in the development of the cardiac, nervous, and skeletal systems.

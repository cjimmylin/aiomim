A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spondylometaphyseal dysplasia of the Sedaghatian type (SMDS) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the GPX4 gene (138322) on chromosome 19p13. ⚠︎

### Description

Spondylometaphyseal dysplasia of the Sedaghatian type is a rare, usually neonatally lethal, autosomal recessive skeletal dysplasia. Its main features are severe irregular metaphyseal chondrodysplasia with mild limb shortening, platyspondyly, and abnormal iliac crests. Affected infants often have cardiorespiratory problems, including cardiac conduction defects or arrhythmias, and central nervous system malformations such as abnormal neuronal migration with a simplified gyral pattern. Most die within days to weeks of birth. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Sedaghatian (1980) described a lethal congenital metaphyseal chondrodysplasia in sibs. The disorder was later named after him. ✗

Later reports described more affected infants with the same radiographic picture: severe irregular metaphyseal changes in the long bones, mild shortening of the limbs, flattened vertebral bodies, and irregular, lacy iliac crests. Many of these infants had cardiac arrhythmias or heart block, respiratory insufficiency, and brain malformations, including cortical dysplasia with a simplified gyral pattern and abnormalities of the corpus callosum. Most died in the neonatal period. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The disorder affected sibs born to unaffected parents, sometimes in consanguineous families, which is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. The finding of biallelic GPX4 mutations confirmed this pattern. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Smith et al. (2014) used exome sequencing in families with Sedaghatian-type spondylometaphyseal dysplasia and found biallelic loss-of-function mutations in the GPX4 gene, which encodes glutathione peroxidase-4. The mutations were predicted to truncate the protein. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

GPX4 is a selenoprotein that reduces lipid hydroperoxides in membranes and protects cells from lipid peroxidation, including ferroptotic cell death. Loss of GPX4 activity is thought to underlie the skeletal, cardiac, and neurologic features of the disorder. ✗

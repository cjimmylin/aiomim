### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spondylometaphyseal dysplasia of the Sedaghatian type (SMDS) is caused by compound heterozygous or homozygous mutation in the GPX4 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Sedaghatian-type spondylometaphyseal dysplasia is a neonatal lethal form of spondylometaphyseal dysplasia. It is characterized by severe metaphyseal chondrodysplasia with mild limb shortening, platyspondyly, cardiac conduction defects, and central nervous system abnormalities (summary by Smith et al., 2014).

### Clinical Features

Sedaghatian (1980) reported 2 brothers, born within a year of each other, who had a similar skeletal disorder with severe congenital metaphyseal involvement, mild rhizomelic shortness of the upper limbs, and mild platyspondyly. Both died at 3 days of age of cardiorespiratory insufficiency. Only 1 of the brothers had laboratory tests before death, which showed low calcium, high phosphorus, and high alkaline phosphatase levels, attributed to a renal defect. At autopsy, this infant had pulmonary, renal, and adrenal hemorrhage, as well as subendocardial myocarditis and myocardial necrosis. The parents were normal and had recently had an affected baby girl.

Opitz et al. (1987) reported another Iranian infant with the Sedaghatian syndrome who died on day 4 and had histologic evidence of severe epimetaphyseal dysplasia. They noted that 1 of the patients of Sedaghatian (1980) had also had microphthalmia, asymmetric ears, depressed nasal bridge, broad nose, short neck, prominent sternum, and short lower limbs, and they suggested that the syndrome may be more widely pleiotropic than the radiographs indicated.

Peeden et al. (1992) reported another infant with a lethal course. In their review, they noted that the infant of Sedaghatian (1980) had subacute myocarditis, renal cortical necrosis, and adrenal and pulmonary hemorrhage at autopsy, and skeletal abnormalities including mild rhizomelic limb shortness, platyspondyly, and 'laciness' of the iliac wings. They also noted that the Iranian infant reported by Opitz et al. (1987) had a similar perinatal course and similar radiographs.

Elcioglu and Hall (1998) described the radiologic findings in 2 unrelated patients with Sedaghatian-type spondylometaphyseal dysplasia. From these patients and a review of the literature, they identified additional anomalies, including disproportionately long fibulae, dysharmonic maturation, and turricephaly.

Koutouby et al. (2000) reported an infant born to nonconsanguineous Yemeni parents who had radiologic findings consistent with Sedaghatian-type lethal metaphyseal chondrodysplasia. All earlier reported patients had died within 4 days of life, but this patient survived 161 days. The patient underwent extensive investigation, including serum calcium, magnesium, zinc, ammonia, phosphate, alkaline phosphatase, and parathormone levels, liver and renal function tests, TORCH studies, metabolic screening, skeletal survey, chromosome studies, muscle enzymes, EEG, and CT and MRI of the brain.

English et al. (2006) reported a girl, born to nonconsanguineous Caucasian parents, with metaphyseal dysplasia and a neuronal migration abnormality, consistent with Sedaghatian spondylometaphyseal dysplasia. She was hypotonic from birth and had recurrent cyanotic episodes within a few hours of delivery. Brain imaging showed absence of the corpus callosum and marked frontotemporal pachygyria. She developed seizures on day 14 of life and died at 17 days of age. English et al. (2006) suggested that neuronal migration abnormalities may occur in this disorder and that agenesis of the corpus callosum is a novel association.

Aygun et al. (2012) reported a patient with Sedaghatian-type spondylometaphyseal dysplasia who had metaphyseal dysplasia, congenital atrioventricular block, simplified gyral pattern, hypogenesis of the corpus callosum, and severe cerebellar hypoplasia. They suggested that central nervous system malformations may be a major component of the disorder.

### Inheritance

Sedaghatian (1980) suggested autosomal recessive inheritance because the affected brothers had normal parents who had also recently had an affected daughter. Opitz et al. (1987) noted the occurrence of affected children of both sexes born to normal parents who were first cousins. Because the disorder then appeared to be limited to Iranians, they suggested that it is an autosomal recessive trait with a high gene frequency in Iranians.

The finding by Smith et al. (2014) of compound heterozygous GPX4 mutations in an affected child, and of the same heterozygous truncating GPX4 mutation in both unaffected parents of a second affected child, was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Koutouby et al. (2000) sent genomic DNA from their patient and the parents for analysis, but no abnormal gene was found.

As part of the FORGE Canada Consortium, Smith et al. (2014) studied 2 unrelated families with Sedaghatian-type spondylometaphyseal dysplasia. Whole-exome sequencing of an affected girl and her unaffected parents identified 2 rare variants in the GPX4 gene: a c.587+5G-A change inherited from the mother and a de novo deletion (c.588-8_588-4del). Both variants were predicted to affect splicing. In vitro studies confirmed that the c.587+5G-A mutation caused part of exon 4 to be spliced out and that the deletion caused skipping of exon 5. In both cases this led to a frameshift and premature truncation of GPX4. In a second family, DNA from the affected child was not available, but Sanger sequencing showed that both unaffected parents were heterozygous for the same nonsense mutation in exon 3 of GPX4 (c.381C-A, tyr127-to-ter, Y127X).

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 SPONDYLOMETAPHYSEAL DYSPLASIA, SEDAGHATIAN TYPE**  
GPX4, 587+5G-A [NM_001039848.1:c.587+5G>A]

In a girl with Sedaghatian-type spondylometaphyseal dysplasia, Smith et al. (2014) used whole-exome sequencing to identify compound heterozygous mutations in the GPX4 gene. One was a c.587+5G-A splice site mutation inherited from the mother, and the other was a de novo deletion (c.588-8_588-4del). In vitro studies showed that the c.587+5G-A mutation caused part of exon 4 to be spliced out, resulting in a frameshift and premature truncation of GPX4.

**.0002 SPONDYLOMETAPHYSEAL DYSPLASIA, SEDAGHATIAN TYPE**  
GPX4, 5-BP DEL, 588-8_588-4 [NM_001039848.1:c.588-8_588-4del]

In a girl with Sedaghatian-type spondylometaphyseal dysplasia, Smith et al. (2014) found compound heterozygosity for a de novo deletion in the GPX4 gene (c.588-8_588-4del) and a maternally inherited splice site mutation (c.587+5G-A). In vitro studies showed that the deletion caused skipping of exon 5, resulting in a frameshift and premature truncation of GPX4.

**.0003 SPONDYLOMETAPHYSEAL DYSPLASIA, SEDAGHATIAN TYPE**  
GPX4, TYR127TER [NM_001039848.1:c.381C>A; p.Tyr127Ter]

In a family with a child with Sedaghatian-type spondylometaphyseal dysplasia, Smith et al. (2014) used Sanger sequencing to find that both unaffected parents were heterozygous for the same c.381C-A transversion in exon 3 of the GPX4 gene, resulting in a tyr127-to-ter (Y127X) substitution. DNA from the affected child was not available.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spondylometaphyseal dysplasia of the Sedaghatian type (SMDS) is caused by compound heterozygous mutation in the GPX4 gene. In a second family, both unaffected parents of an affected child carried the same heterozygous truncating GPX4 mutation, but DNA from the child was not available. ⚠︎

### Description

Sedaghatian-type spondylometaphyseal dysplasia is a neonatally lethal skeletal dysplasia. Smith et al. (2014) described its features as severe metaphyseal chondrodysplasia with mild limb shortening, platyspondyly, cardiac conduction defects, and central nervous system abnormalities.

### Clinical Features

Sedaghatian (1980) reported 2 brothers, born within a year of each other, with a skeletal disorder of severe congenital metaphyseal involvement, mild rhizomelic shortness of the upper limbs, and mild platyspondyly. Both died at 3 days of cardiorespiratory insufficiency. Only 1 had antemortem laboratory tests, which showed low calcium, high phosphorus, and high alkaline phosphatase levels, attributed to a renal defect. At autopsy, this infant had pulmonary, renal, and adrenal hemorrhage, as well as subendocardial myocarditis and myocardial necrosis. The parents were normal and had recently had an affected baby girl.

Opitz et al. (1987) reported a second Iranian family, in which an infant with the disorder died on day 4 and had histologic evidence of severe epimetaphyseal dysplasia. Opitz et al. (1987) noted that one of the patients of Sedaghatian (1980) also had 'microphthalmia, asymmetry of ears, depressed nasal bridge, broad nose, short neck, prominent sternum, and short lower extremities,' and suggested that the syndrome may be more complexly pleiotropic than the radiographs indicated.

Peeden et al. (1992) reported another infant with the disorder who had a lethal course. In summarizing the earlier report, they noted that the patient of Sedaghatian (1980) had, at autopsy, subacute myocarditis, cortical necrosis of the kidneys, and adrenal and pulmonary hemorrhage, and that the skeletal abnormalities included mild rhizomelic limb shortness, platyspondyly, and 'laciness' of the iliac wings.

Elcioglu and Hall (1998) presented the radiologic findings in 2 unrelated cases of Sedaghatian-type spondylometaphyseal dysplasia and, on review of the literature, identified additional anomalies including disproportionately long fibulae, dysharmonious maturation, and turricephaly.

Koutouby et al. (2000) reported an infant born to nonconsanguineous Yemeni parents with radiologic findings consistent with the Sedaghatian type of lethal metaphyseal chondrodysplasia. Whereas all previously reported cases had died within 4 days of life, this patient survived 161 days. Investigations included serum calcium, magnesium, zinc, ammonia, phosphate, alkaline phosphatase, and parathormone levels, liver and renal function tests, TORCH, metabolic screening, chromosome studies, muscle enzymes, EEG, and CT and MRI of the brain. Genomic DNA analysis of the patient and parents did not detect an abnormal gene.

English et al. (2006) reported a female infant, born to nonconsanguineous Caucasian parents, with metaphyseal dysplasia and a neuronal migration abnormality consistent with Sedaghatian spondylometaphyseal dysplasia. She was hypotonic from birth and had recurrent cyanotic episodes within a few hours of delivery. Brain imaging showed absence of the corpus callosum and marked frontotemporal pachygyria. She developed seizures on day 14 and died at 17 days of age.

Aygun et al. (2012) reported a patient with Sedaghatian-type spondylometaphyseal dysplasia who had metaphyseal dysplasia, congenital atrioventricular block, a simplified gyral pattern, hypogenesis of the corpus callosum, and severe cerebellar hypoplasia. They suggested that central nervous system malformations may be a major component of the disorder.

### Inheritance

Sedaghatian (1980) presumed autosomal recessive inheritance because the parents of the affected brothers were normal and had recently had an affected daughter. Opitz et al. (1987) noted the occurrence in children of both sexes born to normal parents, parental first-cousin consanguinity, and the apparent confinement of the disorder to Iranians, suggesting autosomal recessive inheritance with a high gene frequency in Iranians. The finding by Smith et al. (2014) of 2 GPX4 mutations in an affected child, 1 inherited from the mother and 1 de novo, and of the same heterozygous mutation in both unaffected parents of another affected child, is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

As part of the FORGE Canada Consortium, Smith et al. (2014) studied 2 unrelated families with Sedaghatian-type spondylometaphyseal dysplasia. Whole-exome sequencing of an affected girl and her unaffected parents identified compound heterozygous variants in the GPX4 gene: c.587+5G-A, inherited from the mother, and c.588-8_588-4del, which arose de novo. Both were predicted to affect splicing; in vitro studies confirmed that the first mutation spliced out part of exon 4 and the second caused skipping of exon 5, each resulting in a frameshift and premature truncation of GPX4. In a second family, DNA from the affected child was not available, but Sanger sequencing showed that both unaffected parents carried the same heterozygous nonsense mutation in exon 3 of GPX4, c.381C-A, resulting in a tyr127-to-ter (Y127X) substitution. ⚠︎

Earlier, Koutouby et al. (2000) had sent genomic DNA from an affected Yemeni infant and the parents for analysis, but no abnormal gene was detected.

### Population Genetics

Opitz et al. (1987) noted the apparent confinement of the disorder to Iranians at the time of their report and suggested a high gene frequency in that population. Subsequent reports described affected infants born to nonconsanguineous Yemeni parents (Koutouby et al., 2000) and nonconsanguineous Caucasian parents (English et al., 2006).

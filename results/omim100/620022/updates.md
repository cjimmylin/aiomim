# Proposed updates for OMIM 620022: STICKLER SYNDROME, TYPE VI

Literature from 2021 onwards that the entry does not cite: 32 papers screened, 4 read, 4 proposal(s). Each proposal is a draft for a curator to accept, edit or reject.

**Back-test as of 2021.** OMIM's curators later added 2 reference(s) from that year on; this run proposed 2 of them (recall 1.0), and 2 paper(s) OMIM does not cite.

## [MEDIUM] Rad et al. (2022) — new_variants, phenotype_expansion, genotype_phenotype_correlation, larger_cohort

Rad A, Najafi M, Suri F, Abedini S, Loum S, Karimiani EG, Daftarian N, Murphy D, Doosti M, Moghaddasi A, and others. Identification of three novel homozygous variants in COL9A3 causing autosomal recessive Stickler syndrome. Orphanet J Rare Dis 17: 97, 2022. [PubMed: 35241111] *(read: fulltext)*

**Why:** Rad et al. (2022) report 3 new homozygous COL9A3 loss-of-function variants (c.107_116del, c.1204C-T/R402X, c.355delC), none of which is in the variant table, in 5 patients from 3 consanguineous Iranian families. The paper also widens the phenotype with vitreoretinal degeneration, recurrent rhegmatogenous retinal detachment, cataract, spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia and pes planus. This conflicts with the entry's earlier note that vitreoretinal degeneration had not been seen with COL9A3 mutations.

*clinical_features*: Rad et al. (2022) described a 28-year-old Iranian woman (family 1), the only affected child of first-cousin parents. She had high myopia in both eyes and vitreoretinal degeneration with an empty vitreous, multiple lattice degenerations and retinal pigmentary changes. She also had severe progressive sensorineural hearing loss. Skull x-ray showed that the frontal sinus was absent on one side. Her joints and bones were normal on examination, but she complained of knee pain. She did not have the typical Stickler craniofacial features, including midface hypoplasia, cleft palate, micrognathia, depressed nasal bridge and anteverted nares. Her height was 157 cm (-0.8 SD).

*clinical_features*: Rad et al. (2022) also described 2 affected brothers, aged 65 and 57 years, from an Iranian family (family 2) with first-cousin parents. Both had undergone multiple vitreoretinal surgeries for recurrent rhegmatogenous retinal detachments caused by advanced vitreoretinal degeneration. The older brother was blind, with no light perception in either eye. Both brothers had severe to profound progressive sensorineural hearing loss, high myopia, cataract and a herniated cervical disc. The older brother also had muscular atrophy.

*clinical_features*: In a third Iranian family with first-cousin parents (family 3), Rad et al. (2022) described 2 affected sibs, a boy aged 11.8 years and his sister aged 3.1 years. Both had myopia and congenital moderate to severe progressive sensorineural hearing impairment, and x-rays showed spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia. Both sibs had pes planus, a depressed nasal bridge and anteverted nares. Midface hypoplasia and downslanting palpebral fissures were more pronounced in the girl. The boy had knee pain, genu valgum, irregular femoral epiphyses, a flat acetabular roof, flattened femoral heads and short metacarpals. None of the patients in the 3 families had intellectual disability.

*inheritance*: Rad et al. (2022) found homozygous loss-of-function variants in COL9A3 in 3 unrelated consanguineous Iranian families, which is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance of Stickler syndrome type VI.

*molecular_genetics*: In the proband of an Iranian family with autosomal recessive Stickler syndrome (family 1), Rad et al. (2022) used exome sequencing to identify a homozygous 10-bp deletion in exon 2 of the COL9A3 gene (c.107_116del, NM_001853.3). The deletion was predicted to cause a frameshift (Pro36ArgfsTer49). Segregation was checked by Sanger sequencing in available family members. The variant was present in gnomAD at an allele frequency of 0.00001390 (2 carriers) and was not reported in the Iranome, GME or 1000 Genomes databases.

*molecular_genetics*: In 2 affected brothers from a second Iranian family (family 2), Rad et al. (2022) used exome sequencing of the proband to identify a homozygous c.1204C-T transition in exon 23 of the COL9A3 gene, resulting in an arg402-to-ter (R402X) substitution. The variant had not been reported in public databases.

*molecular_genetics*: In 2 affected sibs from a third Iranian family (family 3), Rad et al. (2022) used exome sequencing of the proband to identify a homozygous 1-bp deletion in exon 7 of the COL9A3 gene (c.355delC). The deletion was predicted to cause a frameshift (Leu119SerfsTer9, given as fs*10 in a table in the paper). The variant had not been reported in public databases.

*genotype_phenotype_correlations* ✗: Rad et al. (2022) reviewed their 3 families together with 4 previously reported families carrying biallelic COL9A3 loss-of-function variants. They found that moderate to profound progressive sensorineural hearing loss and moderate to high myopia with vitreoretinal degeneration were consistent features. Retinal detachment and cataract occurred occasionally, and skeletal involvement was more variable. The older patients in family 2 had milder skeletal findings than the young sibs in family 3. Unlike Nixon et al. (2019), Rad et al. (2022) did not observe mild phenotypes in heterozygous carriers. Their finding of vitreoretinal degeneration in COL9A3-related disease contrasts with the observation by Hanson-Kahn et al. (2018) that such degeneration had not been reported with COL9A3 mutations.

## [MEDIUM] Uehara et al. (2022) — new_variants, phenotype_expansion, genotype_phenotype_correlation

Uehara N, Fujita T, Yamashita D, Yokoi J, Katsunuma S, Kakigi A, Nishio SY, Nibu KI, Usami SI. Genetic background in late-onset sensorineural hearing loss patients. J Hum Genet 67: 223-230, 2022. [PubMed: 34824372] *(read: fulltext)*

**Why:** The paper reports a new homozygous COL9A3 variant, c.1587dupT (p.Gly530TrpfsTer71), which is not in the variant table. It also describes a milder, later-onset form of the disorder: hearing loss was diagnosed in the patient's twenties and was first called nonsyndromic, and she had cataract and retinal detachment, neither of which the entry lists.

*clinical_features*: Uehara et al. (2022) described a 58-year-old Japanese woman (case 7), born to first-cousin parents, whose hearing loss was diagnosed in her twenties. The hearing loss was severe in the left ear and moderate in the right, with a flat audiometric pattern. Her older sister also had hearing loss. The patient did not have arthritis but had a history of cataract and retinal detachment. She was first diagnosed with nonsyndromic hearing loss. The cataract and retinal detachment were found only when she was re-evaluated after the genetic diagnosis. The authors classified her as having a very mild type of Stickler syndrome.

*molecular_genetics*: In a Japanese woman with mild Stickler syndrome whose parents were first cousins, Uehara et al. (2022) identified a homozygous frameshift mutation in the COL9A3 gene (c.1587dupT, p.Gly530TrpfsTer71). It was found by massively parallel sequencing of a panel of known deafness genes and confirmed by direct sequencing. The variant was novel and was present at less than 1% in public databases and in in-house Japanese controls. Under ACMG criteria (PVS1, PM2), the authors classified it as likely pathogenic.

*genotype_phenotype_correlations*: Uehara et al. (2022) suggested that COL9A3 variants may cause a broad range of phenotypes, from mild nonsyndromic hearing loss to more severe syndromic forms. They noted that variability between and within families makes clinical diagnosis difficult.

## [MEDIUM] Markova et al. (2021) — new_variants, phenotype_expansion, genotype_phenotype_correlation

Markova T, Sparber P, Borovikov A, Nagornova T, Dadali E. Clinical and genetic characterization of autosomal recessive stickler syndrome caused by novel compound heterozygous mutations in the COL9A3 gene. Mol Genet Genomic Med 9: e1620, 2021. [PubMed: 33570243] *(read: fulltext)*

**Why:** Markova et al. (2021) report the first compound heterozygous COL9A3 case: two nonsense variants, c.268C>T (p.Arg90Ter) and c.1729C>T (p.Arg577Ter), neither of which is in the variant table, in a child of nonconsanguineous parents. They also widen the phenotype. Features not recorded in the entry include early-onset arthropathy, vitreoretinal findings (vitreous floaters, a preretinal membrane and peripheral pigment rearrangement), hip dysplasia, kyphosis, sacral spina bifida occulta and coxa valga.

*clinical_features* ✗: Markova et al. (2021) described a boy, born to healthy nonconsanguineous parents, with autosomal recessive Stickler syndrome. At 1 month he had hip dysplasia and bilaterally absent ossification centers. Congenital high bilateral myopia was noted at 3 months. At 11 months he was diagnosed with bilateral sensorineural hearing loss grade 3-4. Ophthalmic findings included peripheral pigment rearrangement, multiple vitreous floaters, a preretinal membrane, and congenital progressive high myopia; the ERG was normal. Radiographs at 1 and 3 years showed mild spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia with platyspondyly, kyphosis, sacral spina bifida occulta, flattened irregular femoral heads, coxa valga, and flattened distal femoral epiphyses. He was examined at age 2 years 5 months. From age 3 years he had valgus knee deformity, waddling gait, fatigability, and pain in the lower limb joints, which the authors took as indicating early-onset arthropathy. Height was normal. He had a flattened nasal bridge, small nose, mild midface hypoplasia, and high palate, without cleft palate. The authors concluded that he had a more severe phenotype than previously reported COL9A3 patients, with early-onset arthropathy and vitreoretinal degeneration.

*molecular_genetics*: In a boy with autosomal recessive Stickler syndrome, Markova et al. (2021) used targeted sequencing of 166 skeletal disorder genes and identified compound heterozygous nonsense mutations in the COL9A3 gene. One was a c.268C-T transition in exon 5, resulting in an arg90-to-ter (R90X) substitution; the other was a c.1729C-T transition in exon 30, resulting in an arg577-to-ter (R577X) substitution. Sanger sequencing of the parents showed that the variants were in trans, and the healthy sibs did not carry pathogenic variants. The authors described this as the first COL9A3-related recessive Stickler case caused by compound heterozygous rather than homozygous loss-of-function variants.

*genotype_phenotype_correlations* ⚠︎: Markova et al. (2021) compared their patient with 6 previously reported patients from 3 families with homozygous COL9A3 variants. All of the reported variants caused loss of function. High myopia and sensorineural hearing loss were present in all cases, and cleft palate and retinal detachment were absent in all. The authors suggested that the absence of cleft palate may distinguish recessive COL9A3-related Stickler syndrome from autosomal dominant forms. Unlike Hanson-Kahn et al. (2018), who noted that vitreous and retinal degeneration had not been reported with COL9A3 mutations, Markova et al. (2021) reported vitreoretinal degeneration in their COL9A3 patient.

## [LOW] Nash et al. (2021) — genotype_phenotype_correlation

Nash BM, Watson CJG, Hughes E, Hou AL, Loi TH, Bennetts B, Jelovic D, Polkinghorne PJ, Gorbatov M, Grigg JR, and others. Heterozygous COL9A3 variants cause severe peripheral vitreoretinal degeneration and retinal detachment. Eur J Hum Genet 29: 881-886, 2021. [PubMed: 33633367] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** Nash et al. (2021) report heterozygous COL9A3 variants (c.1107+1G>C splice variant; c.388G>A, G130S) in 2 families with autosomal dominant peripheral vitreoretinal degeneration and retinal detachment. This is a distinct, dominant COL9A3-related phenotype rather than recessive Stickler syndrome type VI, so it is relevant only as an allelic contrast. This matters because the entry currently says vitreoretinal degeneration has not been reported with COL9A3 mutations.

*genotype_phenotype_correlations*: In contrast to the biallelic loss-of-function COL9A3 mutations in autosomal recessive Stickler syndrome, Nash et al. (2021) described 2 families with autosomal dominant inheritance whose main features were peripheral vitreoretinal lattice degeneration and retinal detachment. Affected members carried heterozygous variants in the COL9A3 gene that segregated with disease. In Family 1, the variant was a splice site mutation (c.1107+1G-C, NM_001853.4), and cDNA studies showed that it caused an in-frame deletion in the COL2 domain. In Family 2, the variant was a c.388G-A transition resulting in a gly130-to-ser (G130S) substitution in the COL3 domain. The authors stated that both variants pointed to a critical role for type IX collagen in the vitreous base of the eye.

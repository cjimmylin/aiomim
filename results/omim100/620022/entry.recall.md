### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Stickler syndrome type VI (STL6) is caused by homozygous mutation in the COL9A3 gene (120270) on chromosome 20q13.33.

### Description

Stickler syndrome type VI is an autosomal recessive connective tissue disorder. Hanson-Kahn et al. (2018) noted that its main features are eye involvement, especially high myopia, and sensorineural hearing loss, sometimes with skeletal findings. As Faletra et al. (2014) noted, it is one of the recessive forms of Stickler syndrome caused by defects in type IX collagen.

### Clinical Features

Faletra et al. (2014) described a family with autosomal recessive Stickler syndrome.

Hanson-Kahn et al. (2018) described a single proband with autosomal recessive Stickler syndrome. His only sib, a sister, was unaffected. He had myopia and moderate to severe sensorineural hearing loss.

### Inheritance

Hanson-Kahn et al. (2018) showed that Stickler syndrome type VI is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern, based on the families reported by Faletra et al. (2014) and Hanson-Kahn et al. (2018). ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Hanson-Kahn et al. (2018) noted that other autosomal recessive forms of Stickler syndrome are caused by mutations in the COL9A1 gene (Stickler syndrome type IV) and in the COL9A2 gene (Stickler syndrome type V).

### Molecular Genetics

In a family with autosomal recessive Stickler syndrome, Faletra et al. (2014) found a loss-of-function mutation in the COL9A3 gene.

In a single proband with autosomal recessive Stickler syndrome, Hanson-Kahn et al. (2018) found a homozygous mutation in the COL9A3 gene. His parents and his unaffected sib were all heterozygous for the mutation.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive spastic paraplegia-51 (SPG51) is caused by homozygous mutation in the AP4E1 gene (607244) on chromosome 15q21. ⚠︎

### Description

Autosomal recessive spastic paraplegia-51 is a severe neurodevelopmental disorder. It begins with neonatal or infantile hypotonia that later becomes spasticity of the limbs, often spastic tetraplegia. Other features include severe intellectual disability, poor or absent speech, microcephaly, seizures and mild facial dysmorphism. It belongs to the group of disorders caused by deficiency of the adaptor protein complex-4 (AP-4), sometimes called 'AP-4 deficiency syndrome.' Defects in the other AP-4 subunits, AP4B1, AP4M1 and AP4S1, cause clinically similar disorders.

### Nomenclature

The disorder was first called spastic quadriplegic cerebral palsy-4 (CPSQ4). Because it is progressive and classed with the hereditary spastic paraplegias, it was renamed SPG51. ✗

### Clinical Features

Moreno-De-Luca et al. (2011) described a consanguineous family in which several members had a cerebral palsy-like syndrome. Features included spastic quadriplegia, microcephaly and severe intellectual disability. Some patients also had seizures. ⚠︎

Abou Jamra et al. (2011) reported consanguineous families with AP-4 deficiency, including a family with AP4E1 mutation. Affected individuals had severe intellectual disability, absent or minimal speech, early hypotonia that developed into progressive spasticity, and short stature. Some also had microcephaly and seizures. ✗

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of SPG51 in the families reported by Moreno-De-Luca et al. (2011) and Abou Jamra et al. (2011) fit autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Heterogeneity

Clinically similar autosomal recessive disorders are caused by mutation in the genes for the other subunits of the AP-4 complex: AP4B1 (SPG47), AP4M1 (SPG50) and AP4S1 (SPG52).

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of a consanguineous family with spastic quadriplegic cerebral palsy, Moreno-De-Luca et al. (2011) identified a homozygous loss-of-function mutation in the AP4E1 gene. The mutation segregated with the disorder in the family. ⚠︎

Abou Jamra et al. (2011) studied consanguineous families with intellectual disability and spastic paraplegia. In one family they identified a homozygous truncating mutation in the AP4E1 gene. In other families of the same study they found mutations in other AP-4 subunit genes. ⚠︎

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive spastic paraplegia-51 (SPG51) is caused by homozygous deletion involving the AP4E1 gene (607244) on chromosome 15q21.2. ⚠︎

### Description

Autosomal recessive spastic paraplegia-51 is a severe neurodevelopmental disorder. It begins with neonatal or infantile hypotonia that progresses to spasticity of the limbs, often spastic tetraplegia, and is accompanied by severe intellectual disability, poor or absent speech, microcephaly and seizures (Moreno-De-Luca et al., 2011). Mild facial dysmorphism has also been reported (Kong et al., 2013). The disorder belongs to the group of disorders caused by deficiency of the adaptor protein complex-4 (AP-4), which Moreno-De-Luca et al. (2011) called 'AP-4 deficiency syndrome.' Defects in the other AP-4 subunits, AP4B1, AP4M1 and AP4S1, cause clinically similar disorders (Abou Jamra et al., 2011). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Moreno-De-Luca et al. (2011) described a consanguineous family in which 2 sibs had a condition described as cerebral palsy. Both had spastic quadriplegia, microcephaly, severe intellectual disability and seizures.

Abou Jamra et al. (2011) reported consanguineous families with AP-4 deficiency, including a family with an AP4E1 mutation. Affected individuals had severe intellectual disability, absent speech, early hypotonia that progressed to spastic paraplegia, and short stature. Some also had microcephaly. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of SPG51 in the families reported by Moreno-De-Luca et al. (2011) and Abou Jamra et al. (2011) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Heterogeneity

Kong et al. (2013) noted that clinically similar autosomal recessive spastic paraplegias are caused by mutation in the genes encoding the other subunits of the AP-4 complex: AP4B1 (SPG47), AP4M1 (SPG50) and AP4S1 (SPG52).

### Molecular Genetics

In 2 sibs from a consanguineous family with spastic quadriplegic cerebral palsy, Moreno-De-Luca et al. (2011) identified a homozygous deletion of chromosome 15q21.2 that included the 5-prime end of 2 genes, AP4E1 and SPPL2A. The deletion segregated with the disorder in the family.

Abou Jamra et al. (2011) studied consanguineous families with intellectual disability and spastic paraplegia. In one family they identified a splice mutation in the AP4E1 gene (c.542+1_542+4delGTAA) that resulted in a frameshift and truncation (Glu181GlyfsTer20). In other families of the same study they found mutations in other AP-4 subunit genes.

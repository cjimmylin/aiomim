### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that ectodermal dysplasia-17 with or without limb malformations (ECTD17) is caused by heterozygous or homozygous mutation in the LEF1 gene on chromosome 4q25.

### Description

Ectodermal dysplasia-17 with or without limb malformations (ECTD17) is a disorder caused by variants in LEF1, which encodes a transcription factor acting downstream of the WNT-beta-catenin signaling pathway. Features include hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia with severe oligodontia, hypotrichosis, and hypohidrosis. Some patients also have limb malformations, such as radial ray defects, polydactyly, or split hand/foot (Lévy et al., 2020; Dufour et al., 2022).

### Clinical Features

Lévy et al. (2020) reported 2 unrelated patients with hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia. Both had severe oligodontia affecting the primary and permanent teeth, as well as hypotrichosis and hypohidrosis. Both patients also had taurodontism and a particular alveolar bone defect.

Dufour et al. (2022) reported 12 individuals with LEF1 variants. Eleven were from 4 unrelated families, and 1 was from a consanguineous family. The phenotypic spectrum included ectodermal dysplasia and various limb malformations, such as radial ray defects, polydactyly, or split hand/foot.

Hassan et al. (2024) reported a family with hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia, which they considered to expand the spectrum of LEF1-related disorders.

### Inheritance

The de novo occurrence of the deletions including LEF1 in the patients reported by Lévy et al. (2020) indicated autosomal dominant inheritance through haploinsufficiency. Dufour et al. (2022) stated that, depending on the type and location of the LEF1 variant, the disorder can be inherited in either an autosomal dominant or an autosomal recessive manner. ⚠︎

### Cytogenetics

Lévy et al. (2020) identified de novo deletions at chromosome 4q25 that included the LEF1 gene in 2 unrelated patients with hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia. They concluded that the findings provided further evidence that LEF1 haploinsufficiency causes ectodermal dysplasia. They noted that no pathogenic variants involving LEF1 had previously been reported in humans.

### Molecular Genetics

Using high-throughput sequencing, Dufour et al. (2022) identified monoallelic LEF1 variants in 11 affected individuals from 4 unrelated families. They also identified a biallelic LEF1 variant in an affected individual from a consanguineous family. The authors noted that LEF1 had earlier been suspected as a candidate gene for ectodermal dysplasia in 2 individuals with chromosome 4q microdeletions. Their functional studies used immunofluorescence, immunoblotting, pulldown assays, and RNA sequencing. Transcriptomic studies showed altered WNT signaling associated with the LEF1 variants.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

From their functional studies, Dufour et al. (2022) proposed 2 molecular mechanisms. Haploinsufficiency or loss of DNA binding was responsible for a mild to moderate phenotype. In contrast, loss of beta-catenin binding caused by biallelic variants was associated with a severe phenotype.

### Pathogenesis

Dufour et al. (2022) found by transcriptomic studies that the LEF1 variants were associated with altered WNT signaling.

### Animal Model

Lévy et al. (2020) noted that targeted inactivation of the Lef1 gene in mice completely blocks the development of multiple ectodermal appendages.

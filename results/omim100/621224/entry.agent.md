### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that ectodermal dysplasia-17 with or without limb malformations (ECTD17) is caused by heterozygous or homozygous mutation in the LEF1 gene. Heterozygous deletions encompassing LEF1 have also been reported in patients with ectodermal dysplasia. ⚠︎

### Description

Ectodermal dysplasia-17 with or without limb malformations (ECTD17) is a disorder caused by variants in LEF1, which encodes a transcription factor acting downstream of the WNT-beta-catenin signaling pathway. Its phenotypic spectrum includes ectodermal dysplasia and various limb malformations, such as radial ray defects, polydactyly, or split hand/foot (Dufour et al., 2022). Ectodermal features reported include severe oligodontia of primary and permanent dentition, hypotrichosis, and hypohidrosis (Levy et al., 2020).

### Clinical Features

Levy et al. (2020) reported 2 unrelated patients with severe oligodontia of primary and permanent dentition, hypotrichosis, and hypohidrosis compatible with hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia. Taurodontism and a particular alveolar bone defect were also observed in both patients.

Dufour et al. (2022) reported 12 individuals with LEF1 variants, 11 from 4 unrelated families and 1 from a consanguineous family. The phenotypic spectrum included various limb malformations, such as radial ray defects, polydactyly, or split hand/foot, and ectodermal dysplasia.

Hassan et al. (2024) reported a family with hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia, expanding the spectrum of LEF1-related disorders.

### Inheritance

Dufour et al. (2022) found that, depending on the type and location of LEF1 variants, the inheritance of ECTD17 can be either autosomal dominant or autosomal recessive. The deletions encompassing LEF1 reported by Levy et al. (2020) occurred de novo.

### Cytogenetics

In 2 unrelated patients with hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia, Levy et al. (2020) identified de novo deletions of chromosome 4q25 encompassing the LEF1 gene. The authors noted that no pathogenic variants involving LEF1 had previously been reported in humans and concluded that LEF1 haploinsufficiency plays a role in ectodermal dysplasia.

### Molecular Genetics

Using high-throughput sequencing, Dufour et al. (2022) detected monoallelic variants in LEF1 in 11 affected individuals from 4 unrelated families, and a biallelic variant in an affected individual from a consanguineous family. Cellular consequences were characterized by immunofluorescence, immunoblotting, pulldown assays, and/or RNA sequencing. Functional data indicated 2 molecular mechanisms: haploinsufficiency or loss of DNA binding, and loss of beta-catenin binding caused by biallelic variants. Transcriptomic studies revealed an alteration of WNT signaling.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Dufour et al. (2022) found that haploinsufficiency or loss of DNA binding was responsible for a mild to moderate phenotype, whereas loss of beta-catenin binding caused by biallelic LEF1 variants was associated with a severe phenotype.

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that ectodermal dysplasia-17 with or without limb malformations (ECTD17) is caused by heterozygous or homozygous mutation in the LEF1 gene on chromosome 4q25. ⚠︎

### Description

Ectodermal dysplasia-17 with or without limb malformations (ECTD17) is a disorder caused by variants in LEF1. Its features include ectodermal dysplasia, with severe oligodontia, hypotrichosis, and hypohidrosis, and various limb malformations, such as radial ray defects, polydactyly, or split hand/foot. Inheritance can be autosomal dominant or autosomal recessive, depending on the type and location of the LEF1 variant (Lévy et al., 2020; Dufour et al., 2022).

### Clinical Features

Lévy et al. (2020) reported 2 unrelated patients with severe oligodontia of the primary and permanent dentition, hypotrichosis, and hypohidrosis, a picture compatible with hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia. Both patients also had taurodontism and a particular alveolar bone defect.

Dufour et al. (2022) reported 12 individuals carrying LEF1 variants: 11 affected individuals from 4 unrelated families and 1 affected individual from a consanguineous family. The phenotypic spectrum included ectodermal dysplasia and various limb malformations, such as radial ray defects, polydactyly, or split hand/foot.

Hassan et al. (2024) reported a family with hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia in the setting of LEF1-related disorders.

### Inheritance

Lévy et al. (2020) found that the 4q25 deletions encompassing LEF1 in their 2 patients had arisen de novo. Dufour et al. (2022) found that ECTD17 can be inherited as either an autosomal dominant or an autosomal recessive trait, depending on the type and location of the LEF1 variant. They identified monoallelic variants in 4 families and a biallelic variant in a consanguineous family.

### Cytogenetics

In 2 unrelated patients with hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia, Lévy et al. (2020) identified de novo deletions at chromosome 4q25 that encompassed the LEF1 gene. They proposed that LEF1 haploinsufficiency causes ectodermal dysplasia and noted that no pathogenic variants involving LEF1 had previously been reported in humans.

### Molecular Genetics

Using high-throughput sequencing, Dufour et al. (2022) detected monoallelic LEF1 variants in 11 affected individuals from 4 unrelated families, and a biallelic LEF1 variant in an affected individual from a consanguineous family. They studied the cellular consequences of the variants with immunofluorescence, immunoblotting, pulldown assays, and/or RNA sequencing. Their functional data pointed to 2 molecular mechanisms. Haploinsufficiency or loss of DNA binding was associated with a mild to moderate phenotype, whereas loss of beta-catenin binding caused by biallelic variants was associated with a severe phenotype. Transcriptomic studies showed altered WNT signaling.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Dufour et al. (2022) found that haploinsufficiency or loss of DNA binding caused by LEF1 variants was associated with a mild to moderate phenotype. In contrast, loss of beta-catenin binding caused by biallelic variants was associated with a severe phenotype.

### Animal Model

Lévy et al. (2020) noted that in mice, targeted inactivation of the Lef1 gene completely blocks the development of multiple ectodermal appendages.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GABRB1 gene (137190) on chromosome 4p12.

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is a severe neurologic disorder. Seizures begin in infancy and include infantile spasms. They occur with impaired psychomotor development, which has been reported as severe to profound (Janve et al., 2016; Burgess et al., 2019). The disorder results from de novo heterozygous mutation in GABRB1, the gene encoding the beta-1 subunit of the GABA-A receptor (Janve et al., 2016).

### Clinical Features

Janve et al. (2016) reported that the patient carrying a GABRB1 mutation had infantile spasms.

Burgess et al. (2019) reported a patient with a GABRB1 mutation whose seizures began in infancy. The patient showed developmental slowing and was classified as having severe to profound impairment.

### Inheritance

The GABRB1 mutation reported by Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) occurred de novo. Janve et al. (2016) stated that the patient was heterozygous for the de novo GABRB1 mutation.

### Molecular Genetics

Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) carried out trio exome sequencing of patients with infantile spasms or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome and their unaffected parents. In one patient with epileptic encephalopathy, they identified a de novo mutation in the GABRB1 gene.

Janve et al. (2016) reported that the de novo GABRB1 mutation, a heterozygous phe246-to-ser (F246S) substitution, had been found in a patient with infantile spasms. They expressed this mutation and other de novo GABRB mutations associated with epileptic encephalopathy in heterologous (HEK293T) cells. All of the mutations, including the GABRB1 mutation, impaired GABA-A receptor function. Janve et al. (2016) suggested that GABRB1 is a candidate human epilepsy gene.

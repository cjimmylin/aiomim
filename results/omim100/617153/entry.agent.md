### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GABRB1 gene.

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is a severe neurologic disorder with onset of seizures in infancy. Reported patients have had infantile spasms (Epi4K Consortium et al., 2013; Janve et al., 2016) or epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures with severe to profound developmental impairment (Burgess et al., 2019). Janve et al. (2016) described the epileptic encephalopathies as a diverse group of severe childhood epilepsy syndromes with intractable seizures, neurodevelopmental delay and regression, resistance to treatment, and poor clinical outcomes.

### Clinical Features

The Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) sequenced the exomes of 264 probands with infantile spasms (149 patients) or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome (115 patients) and their parents. Patients were enrolled through the Epilepsy Phenome/Genome Project. Patients with infantile spasms were required to have hypsarrhythmia on EEG. One proband carried a de novo mutation in GABRB1. Janve et al. (2016) stated that this patient had infantile spasms. They noted that the infantile spasms phenotype was severe in this patient and in a patient with a GABRB3 mutation.

Burgess et al. (2019) studied a cohort of 135 patients from 128 unrelated families with epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (EIMFS). One patient (patient 63) had a de novo GABRB1 variant. Seizures in this patient began at 4 months of age, and the patient was 2 years old at the time of the study. The patient had focal seizures at onset, severe to profound developmental impairment, and hypotonia. EIMFS was defined in that study by focal seizures that migrated between the hemispheres, clinically and/or electrically, with onset before 6 months of age.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous GABRB1 mutations in DEE45 patients occurred de novo (Epi4K Consortium et al., 2013; Janve et al., 2016; Burgess et al., 2019). Burgess et al. (2019) classified GABRB1 as a de novo dominant cause of EIMFS.

### Molecular Genetics

The Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) performed exome sequencing on 264 parent-proband trios with infantile spasms or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome and confirmed candidate de novo mutations by Sanger sequencing. Six de novo mutations affected subunits of the GABA(A) receptor: 4 in GABRB3 and 1 each in GABRA1 and GABRB1.

Janve et al. (2016) reported that the de novo GABRB1 mutation from the Epi4K study was a phe246-to-ser (F246S) substitution in a patient with infantile spasms. The patient was heterozygous for the mutation. F246 is an invariant residue across all GABA(A) receptor subunits and lies in transmembrane domain 1. The authors coexpressed mutant beta-1 subunits with alpha-1 and gamma-2L subunits in HEK293T cells. When only mutant beta-1 was expressed, peak GABA-evoked current density fell by 25%. In the heterozygous expression condition, current density was not affected. The mutation slowed current deactivation, and in single-channel recordings it increased mean open time about 3.75-fold and burst duration about 3.2-fold. Under the homozygous expression condition it increased holding currents about 5-fold, consistent with spontaneous channel openings. It also increased the open probability of spontaneous single-channel openings 4-fold. Surface levels of the mutant subunit were slightly but significantly reduced, without a change in total levels. Janve et al. (2016) concluded that all 5 de novo GABRB mutations studied impaired GABA(A) receptor function, and they suggested GABRB1 as a candidate human epilepsy gene.

Lien et al. (2016) reported a second patient with a de novo GABRB1 mutation and epileptic encephalopathy.

In a cohort of 135 patients with EIMFS, Burgess et al. (2019) identified a heterozygous de novo ile247-to-thr (I247T) variant in GABRB1 (chr4:47405633T-C, GRCh37) in 1 patient (patient 63). The variant was found by whole-exome sequencing and was absent from the ExAC and gnomAD databases. PolyPhen classified it as benign and SIFT as deleterious. It was classified as likely pathogenic under ACMG criteria.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DEVELOPMENTAL AND EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY 45**  
GABRB1, PHE246SER [p.F246S]

The Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) found a de novo GABRB1 mutation in 1 of 264 probands with infantile spasms or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome who were studied by trio exome sequencing. Janve et al. (2016) identified this mutation as a phe246-to-ser (F246S) substitution in a patient with infantile spasms. The patient was heterozygous for the mutation. F246 is an invariant residue in transmembrane domain 1. In HEK293T cells, the mutation had little effect on peak GABA-evoked current, but it slowed deactivation and prolonged mean open time and burst duration. It also caused spontaneous channel openings, with increased holding currents and spontaneous open probability.

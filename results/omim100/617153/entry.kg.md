### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GABRB1 gene (137190) on chromosome 4p12. ⚠︎

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is a severe neurologic disorder with seizure onset in infancy. Reported features include infantile spasms, focal seizures with interhemispheric migration, profound to severe developmental impairment, and hypotonia (Janve et al., 2016; Lien et al., 2016; Burgess et al., 2019).

### Clinical Features

The Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) studied patients from the Epilepsy Phenome/Genome Project who had infantile spasms (n = 149) or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome (n = 115). Patients with infantile spasms had to have hypsarrhythmia on EEG. Patients with Lennox-Gastaut syndrome had to have a slow or disorganized EEG background with generalized spike-and-wave activity or generalized paroxysmal fast activity. In all patients, brain MRI or CT was read as normal, mild diffuse atrophy, or focal cortical dysplasia. None had moderate-to-severe developmental impairment before seizure onset. Janve et al. (2016) later reported that the GABRB1 mutation identified in this cohort was in a child with infantile spasms. ⚠︎

Lien et al. (2016) reported a second patient with epileptic encephalopathy and a de novo GABRB1 mutation.

Burgess et al. (2019) reported a patient with epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (EIMFS) and a de novo GABRB1 variant who was studied at age 2 years. Focal seizures began at 4 months of age and migrated between the hemispheres, which was an inclusion criterion for the cohort. The patient also had profound to severe developmental impairment and hypotonia. The patient did not have epileptic spasms or microcephaly.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous GABRB1 mutations in patients with DEE45 occurred de novo (Epi4K Consortium et al., 2013; Janve et al., 2016; Burgess et al., 2019). This is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

The Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) sequenced the exomes of 264 probands with infantile spasms or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome and of their parents. They found a de novo mutation in the GABRB1 gene in 1 patient. This was 1 of 6 de novo mutations affecting GABA-A receptor subunits in the cohort; the other 5 were 4 in GABRB3 and 1 in GABRA1. The authors suggested that rare mutations in different genes may converge on shared pathways.

Janve et al. (2016) studied a de novo heterozygous mutation in the GABRB1 gene, phe246-to-ser (F246S), which the Epi4K Consortium had found in a child with infantile spasms. F246 lies in transmembrane domain 1, and the residue is the same in all GABA-A receptor subunits. Janve et al. (2016) expressed the mutant with alpha-1 and gamma-2L subunits in HEK293T cells. In the homozygous condition, the mutation reduced GABA-evoked peak current density by 25%; in the heterozygous condition, it had no effect on peak current density. The mutation slowed current deactivation. The authors concluded that the mutation impaired GABA-A receptor function and suggested that GABRB1 is a candidate human epilepsy gene.

Lien et al. (2016) found a de novo mutation in the GABRB1 gene in a second patient with epileptic encephalopathy.

Burgess et al. (2019) used whole-exome sequencing in a patient (patient 63) with EIMFS and found a de novo heterozygous variant in the GABRB1 gene, ile247-to-thr (I247T). The variant was absent from the ExAC and gnomAD databases and was classified as likely pathogenic under ACMG criteria. In the full cohort of 135 patients with EIMFS, Burgess et al. (2019) found causative variants in 93 (69%), spread across 23 genes. Nine of these genes, including GABRB1 and GABRA1, were new to EIMFS. The authors emphasized the role of GABA-related mechanisms and noted that 3 patients carried variants in GABRB3, a gene already known to cause EIMFS.

### Pathogenesis

Janve et al. (2016) characterized the GABRB1 F246S mutation in single-channel recordings. Mutant receptors opened less often but had a normal open probability. The mutation produced a gain of function, with mean open time about 3.75-fold longer and burst duration about 3.2-fold longer. It also lowered single-channel conductance to about 21 pS. The authors proposed that a faster rise time and slower deactivation would lengthen inhibitory postsynaptic currents but would reduce charge transfer during later currents, resulting in a net loss of GABAergic inhibition.

In the homozygous condition, the F246S mutation increased whole-cell holding currents about 5-fold, and zinc partly blocked these currents (Janve et al., 2016). Without GABA, the mutation increased spontaneous single-channel open probability 4-fold. Surface levels of the mutant beta-1 subunit were slightly but significantly lower, while total levels were unchanged. The authors suggested that receptors containing the mutant subunit opened spontaneously. Structural modeling predicted rearrangements in the coupling zone that spread to the transmembrane domains.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DEVELOPMENTAL AND EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY 45**  
GABRB1, PHE246SER [p.F246S]

Janve et al. (2016) studied a de novo heterozygous phe246-to-ser (F246S) mutation in the GABRB1 gene, which the Epi4K Consortium had found in a child with infantile spasms (DEE45). F246 is in transmembrane domain 1, and the residue is the same in all GABA-A receptor subunits. The mutant was expressed with alpha-1 and gamma-2L subunits in HEK293T cells. In the homozygous condition, it reduced GABA-evoked peak current density by 25%, and in the heterozygous condition it had no effect on peak current density. It slowed deactivation and lengthened mean open time and burst duration. It also lowered single-channel conductance and increased GABA-independent spontaneous channel opening. Surface levels of the mutant subunit were slightly reduced. The authors proposed that the net effect was a loss of GABAergic inhibition.

**.0002 DEVELOPMENTAL AND EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY 45**  
GABRB1, ILE247THR [p.I247T]

Burgess et al. (2019) used whole-exome sequencing in a patient with epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (DEE45) and found a de novo heterozygous variant in the GABRB1 gene (chr4:47405633T-C, GRCh37), predicted to cause an ile247-to-thr (I247T) substitution. The patient's focal seizures began at 4 months of age, and the patient had profound to severe developmental impairment and hypotonia. The variant was absent from the ExAC and gnomAD databases. SIFT predicted it to be deleterious and PolyPhen predicted it to be benign. It was classified as likely pathogenic under ACMG criteria.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GABRB1 gene (137190) on chromosome 4p12. ⚠︎

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 is a severe neurologic disorder that begins in infancy. The reported seizure types include infantile spasms and the migrating focal seizures of epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (EIMFS). Affected individuals can have severe developmental impairment and hypotonia.

### Clinical Features

Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) screened for de novo mutations in patients with 2 classic epileptic encephalopathies, infantile spasms (149 patients) and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome (115 patients). One patient in this cohort, who had 1 of these 2 epileptic encephalopathies, carried a de novo GABRB1 mutation.

Janve et al. (2016) studied a patient with infantile spasms who carried a de novo heterozygous GABRB1 mutation. ⚠︎

Lien et al. (2016) reported a second patient with epileptic encephalopathy and a de novo GABRB1 mutation.

Burgess et al. (2019) studied a cohort of 135 patients with EIMFS. One of them (patient 63) had a de novo GABRB1 variant. This patient had seizure onset at 4 months of age, with focal seizures at onset. At last report, at 2 years of age, the patient had profound to severe developmental impairment and hypotonia. Burgess et al. (2019) noted that the GABRB1 phenotypic spectrum now extended to EIMFS, and they emphasized a role for GABA-related mechanisms in this disorder.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous GABRB1 mutations reported by Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013), Janve et al. (2016), and Burgess et al. (2019) occurred de novo. This is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Heterogeneity

Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) found significant genetic heterogeneity underlying infantile spasms and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome. They concluded that the responsible gene may be difficult to predict from the clinical presentation.

### Molecular Genetics

Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) sequenced the exomes of 264 trios in which the child had infantile spasms or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome. Six patients had de novo mutations in GABA-A receptor subunit genes: 4 in GABRB3 and 1 each in GABRA1 and GABRB1. The GABRB1 mutation was confirmed by Sanger sequencing in the trio and was absent from both parents. They suggested that individual rare mutations in different genes may converge on shared pathways.

Janve et al. (2016) carried out a functional study of a de novo heterozygous phe246-to-ser (F246S) mutation in the GABRB1 gene, which had been identified in a patient with infantile spasms. In HEK293T cells, the mutation caused only a minor reduction (about 25%) in GABA-evoked peak current in the homozygous expression condition, and no change in the heterozygous condition. However, it slowed current deactivation and increased single-channel mean open time and burst duration. Mutant receptors opened spontaneously, with a 4-fold increase in spontaneous open probability and increased holding currents. Surface levels of the mutant beta-1 subunit were slightly reduced, while total levels were unchanged.

Lien et al. (2016) identified a de novo GABRB1 mutation in a second patient with epileptic encephalopathy.

Burgess et al. (2019) used whole-exome sequencing in a cohort of 135 patients with EIMFS. In 1 patient they identified a de novo heterozygous ile247-to-thr (I247T) variant in the GABRB1 gene (Chr4:47405633T-C). The variant was absent from the ExAC and gnomAD databases and was classified as likely pathogenic. GABRB1 was 1 of 9 genes newly implicated in EIMFS. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Janve et al. (2016) suggested that the faster rise time and slower deactivation caused by the GABRB1 F246S mutation would lengthen the first inhibitory postsynaptic current (IPSC). They proposed that it would also reduce charge transfer during later IPSCs, causing a net loss of GABAergic inhibition. They suggested GABRB1 as a candidate human epilepsy gene.

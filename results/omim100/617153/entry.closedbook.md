### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GABRB1 gene (137190) on chromosome 4p12. ⚠︎

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is a severe neurologic disorder in which seizures begin in infancy, including infantile spasms, and are accompanied by impaired psychomotor development and intellectual disability. The disorder results from de novo heterozygous mutation in GABRB1, which encodes the beta-1 subunit of the GABA-A receptor. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

The Epi4K Consortium and Epilepsy Phenome/Genome Project (2013) carried out exome sequencing of patients with infantile spasms or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome and their unaffected parents. One of these patients had seizures that began in infancy, along with developmental delay. ✗

### Inheritance

The heterozygous GABRB1 mutation in the reported patient arose de novo (Epi4K Consortium and Epilepsy Phenome/Genome Project, 2013). This is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. ✗

### Molecular Genetics

In a patient with epileptic encephalopathy, the Epi4K Consortium and Epilepsy Phenome/Genome Project (2013) found a de novo heterozygous missense mutation in the GABRB1 gene (phe246-to-ser; F246S) using trio exome sequencing. ⚠︎

Janve et al. (2016) studied the function of de novo GABRB mutations associated with epileptic encephalopathy, including the GABRB1 mutation, by expressing them in heterologous cells. The mutations altered GABA-A receptor function.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that brachyolmia type 4 with mild epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes (BCYM4) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the PAPSS2 gene (also called ATPSK2) on chromosome 10q23-q24. ⚠︎

### Description

Brachyolmia type 4 with mild epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes is an autosomal recessive skeletal dysplasia that mainly affects the spine. Affected individuals have short-trunk short stature with normal intelligence and facies. Radiographic features include rectangular or flattened vertebral bodies with irregular endplates and narrow intervertebral discs. Involvement of the epiphyses and metaphyses of the tubular bones is mild and variable (Ahmad et al., 1998; Miyake et al., 2012). Miyake et al. (2012) concluded that PAPSS2 mutations cause a family of skeletal dysplasias, with phenotypes ranging from brachyolmia to spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia (SEMD). Noordam et al. (2009) found PAPSS2 deficiency in a girl with premature pubarche and androgen excess. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Ahmad et al. (1998) described a large inbred kindred from a remote area of Pakistan, spanning 8 generations, with a distinct form of spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia. They evaluated 16 affected individuals (11 males and 5 females). Clinical findings included short stature evident at birth, short bowed lower limbs, mild brachydactyly, kyphoscoliosis, an abnormal gait, enlarged knee joints, precocious osteoarthropathy, and normal intelligence. Radiographs showed delayed epiphyseal ossification at the hips and knees and platyspondyly with irregular end plates. They also showed narrowed joint spaces and diffuse early osteoarthritic changes, mainly in the spine and hands. Mild metaphyseal abnormalities were seen, mostly at the hips and knees. The authors considered the phenotype distinct from other autosomal recessive forms of SEMD because metaphyseal involvement was mild, because of the type of brachydactyly, and because loose joints and other clinical findings were absent.

Noordam et al. (2009) reported a girl with premature pubarche, hyperandrogenic anovulation, very low dehydroepiandrosterone sulfate (DHEAS) levels, and increased androgen levels.

Miyake et al. (2012) studied 3 affected members of a Turkish family with autosomal recessive brachyolmia, plus 3 other patients with a similar phenotype (2 Japanese and 1 Korean). All 6 patients had short-trunk short stature that became conspicuous during childhood, with normal intelligence and facies. Radiographic features included rectangular vertebral bodies with irregular endplates and narrow intervertebral discs. Other features were precocious calcification of the rib cartilages, short femoral neck, and mildly shortened metacarpals. The spinal changes were very similar in all 6 patients, but epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes of the tubular bones varied.

### Biochemical Features

In a girl with compound heterozygous PAPSS2 mutations, Noordam et al. (2009) found very low DHEAS levels and increased androgen levels. The sulfotransferase SULT2A1 converts DHEA to its inactive sulfate ester, DHEAS, and needs 3-prime-phosphoadenosine-5-prime-phosphosulfate (PAPS) to do so. The authors concluded that PAPSS2 deficiency is a monogenic adrenocortical cause of androgen excess.

### Inheritance

Ahmad et al. (1998) found that the pedigree of a large inbred Pakistani kindred strongly suggested autosomal recessive inheritance. Consanguineous loops could account for all affected individuals being homozygous for the abnormal allele. Miyake et al. (2012) also described autosomal recessive inheritance of brachyolmia in a Turkish family and found homozygous or compound heterozygous PAPSS2 mutations in affected individuals.

### Mapping

In a large inbred Pakistani family with recessively inherited SEMD, Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) mapped the disease gene to chromosome 10q23-q24. This region is syntenic with the locus for the brachymorphic mutation on mouse chromosome 19.

### Molecular Genetics

In the inbred Pakistani SEMD family originally described by Ahmad et al. (1998), Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) found a nonsense mutation in the ATPSK2 gene. This gene encodes an ATP sulfurylase/APS kinase, which activates inorganic sulfate to form PAPS, the universal donor for posttranslational protein sulfation. ⚠︎

In a girl with premature pubarche, hyperandrogenic anovulation, very low DHEAS levels, and increased androgen levels, Noordam et al. (2009) found compound heterozygous mutations in the PAPSS2 gene. In vitro, the authors coincubated human SULT2A1 with wildtype or mutant PAPSS2 protein, and the results confirmed that the mutations were inactivating.

Miyake et al. (2012) used exon capture and next-generation sequencing in 3 affected members of a Turkish family with autosomal recessive brachyolmia. They found a homozygous loss-of-function mutation in the PAPSS2 gene (c.337_338insG; p.A113GfsX18). The authors then studied 3 more patients with similar brachyolmia phenotypes (2 Japanese and 1 Korean) and found loss-of-function mutations in PAPSS2 in each of them. One patient was homozygous for IVS3+2delT. The second was compound heterozygous for c.616-634del19 (p.V206SfsX9) and c.1309-1310delAG (p.R437GfsX19). The third was compound heterozygous for c.480_481insCGTA (p.K161RfsX6) and c.661delA (p.I221SfsX40).

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

In 6 patients with PAPSS2 mutations, Miyake et al. (2012) found very similar spinal changes but variable epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes of the tubular bones. They concluded that PAPSS2 mutations cause a family of skeletal dysplasias, with phenotypes ranging from brachyolmia to SEMD.

### Animal Model

Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) found a missense mutation in the brachymorphic mouse in the part of the Atpsk2 gene that encodes APS kinase activity. Atpsk2 is the mouse ortholog of human ATPSK2. The authors noted that both the human and mouse phenotypes are specific to cartilage. They took this as further evidence that sulfate activation plays a critical role in the maturation of cartilage extracellular matrix molecules.

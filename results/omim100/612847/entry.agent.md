### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that brachyolmia type 4 with mild epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes (BCYM4) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the PAPSS2 gene (also called ATPSK2) on chromosome 10q23-q24. ⚠︎

### Description

Brachyolmia type 4 with mild epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes is an autosomal recessive skeletal dysplasia that mainly affects the spine. Patients have short-trunk short stature, normal intelligence, rectangular or flattened vertebral bodies with irregular endplates and narrow intervertebral discs, and variable epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes of the tubular bones (Ahmad et al., 1998; Miyake et al., 2012). Miyake et al. (2012) stated that PAPSS2 mutations give rise to a range of phenotypes from brachyolmia to spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia.

### Clinical Features

Ahmad et al. (1998) described an inbred kindred spanning 8 generations from a remote area of Pakistan with a distinct form of spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia (SEMD). They evaluated 16 affected individuals, 11 males and 5 females. Clinical findings were short stature evident at birth, short bowed lower limbs, mild brachydactyly, kyphoscoliosis, abnormal gait, enlarged knee joints, precocious osteoarthropathy, and normal intelligence. On radiographs there was delayed epiphyseal ossification at the hips and knees, platyspondyly with irregular end plates, narrowed joint spaces, and diffuse early osteoarthritic changes, mostly in the spine and hands. Mild metaphyseal abnormalities were seen, predominantly at the hips and knees. Ahmad et al. (1998) considered this phenotype distinct from other autosomal recessive forms of SEMD because of the mild metaphyseal involvement, the type of brachydactyly, and the absence of loose joints or other clinical findings.

Noordam et al. (2009) reported a girl with premature pubarche, hyperandrogenic anovulation, very low dehydroepiandrosterone sulfate (DHEAS) levels, and raised androgen levels.

Miyake et al. (2012) studied 3 affected members of a Turkish family with autosomal recessive brachyolmia, as well as 3 more patients with a similar phenotype (2 Japanese and 1 Korean). All 6 had short-trunk short stature that became noticeable during childhood, with normal intelligence and facies. Radiographic features included rectangular vertebral bodies with irregular endplates and narrow intervertebral discs, precocious calcification of the rib cartilages, short femoral necks, and mildly shortened metacarpals. The spinal changes were very similar in all 6 patients, but epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes of the tubular bones varied.

### Biochemical Features

In a girl with compound heterozygous PAPSS2 mutations, Noordam et al. (2009) found very low DHEAS levels and increased androgen levels. They explained that the DHEA sulfotransferase SULT2A1 converts DHEA to its inactive sulfate ester, DHEAS, which prevents DHEA from being converted to an active androgen, and that SULT2A1 needs 3-prime-phosphoadenosine-5-prime-phosphosulfate (PAPS) for its catalytic activity.

### Inheritance

Ahmad et al. (1998) concluded from the pedigree of a large inbred Pakistani kindred that this form of SEMD has autosomal recessive inheritance. Consanguineous loops could explain why all affected individuals were homozygous for the abnormal allele. Miyake et al. (2012) also found autosomal recessive inheritance in a Turkish family with brachyolmia. ⚠︎

### Mapping

In a large inbred Pakistani family with recessive SEMD, Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) mapped the disease gene to chromosome 10q23-q24. This region is syntenic with the locus for the brachymorphic mutation on mouse chromosome 19.

### Molecular Genetics

In the Pakistani SEMD family, Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) identified a nonsense mutation in the ATPSK2 gene (PAPSS2), which encodes an ATP sulfurylase/APS kinase. This enzyme catalyzes the metabolic activation of inorganic sulfate to PAPS, the universal donor for posttranslational protein sulfation.

Noordam et al. (2009) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the PAPSS2 gene in a girl with premature pubarche and androgen excess. When human SULT2A1 was coincubated in vitro with wildtype or mutant PAPSS2 proteins, the mutations were shown to be inactivating. Noordam et al. (2009) concluded that PAPSS2 deficiency is a monogenic adrenocortical cause of androgen excess.

Using exon capture and next-generation sequencing in 3 affected members of a Turkish family with autosomal recessive brachyolmia, Miyake et al. (2012) identified a homozygous loss-of-function mutation in the PAPSS2 gene (c.337_338insG; p.A113GfsX18). In 3 further patients with similar brachyolmia phenotypes (2 Japanese and 1 Korean), they found loss-of-function mutations in PAPSS2. One patient was homozygous for IVS3+2delT. The other 2 were compound heterozygous, one for c.616-634del19 (p.V206SfsX9) and c.1309-1310delAG (p.R437GfsX19), and the other for c.480_481insCGTA (p.K161RfsX6) and c.661delA (p.I221SfsX40).

### Animal Model

In the brachymorphic mouse, Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) found a missense mutation in Atpsk2, the mouse ortholog, in the region encoding APS kinase activity. They suggested that the cartilage-specific nature of the human and mouse phenotypes further supports a critical role for sulfate activation in the maturation of cartilage extracellular matrix molecules.

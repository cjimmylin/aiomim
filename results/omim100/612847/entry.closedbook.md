### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that brachyolmia type 4 with mild epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes (BCYM4) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the PAPSS2 gene (603005) on chromosome 10q23.

### Description

Brachyolmia type 4 is an autosomal recessive skeletal dysplasia. Its main features are short-trunk short stature, platyspondyly and mild changes in the epiphyses and metaphyses of the long bones. It is caused by deficiency of PAPS synthase-2, which produces the universal sulfate donor 3-prime-phosphoadenosine 5-prime-phosphosulfate (PAPS). Some affected individuals also have a defect in the sulfation of dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), with low DHEA sulfate, androgen excess and premature pubarche. ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

The disorder was first described as spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia (SEMD), Pakistani type. When PAPSS2 mutations were later found in patients diagnosed with brachyolmia, the phenotype was reclassified within the brachyolmia group. ✗

### Clinical Features

Ahmad et al. (1998) described a large consanguineous Pakistani family with an autosomal recessive spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia. Affected members had short stature with a short trunk, bowed lower limbs, enlarged knee joints and kyphoscoliosis. Radiographs showed platyspondyly and mild changes in the epiphyses and metaphyses. ⚠︎

Noordam et al. (2009) reported a girl with premature pubarche and hyperandrogenism. Her DHEA sulfate levels were low despite high DHEA, indicating impaired DHEA sulfation. She also had short stature and a mild spondylodysplasia. ✗

Miyake et al. (2012) and Iida et al. (2013) reported patients with brachyolmia who had PAPSS2 mutations. Their main features were short-trunk short stature and platyspondyly, and some had mild epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes. ⚠︎

### Biochemical Features

Noordam et al. (2009) found that their patient's ability to sulfate DHEA was impaired. As a result, DHEA was diverted toward active androgens, which explained her androgen excess and premature pubarche. Oostdijk et al. (2015) studied further PAPSS2-deficient individuals and heterozygous carriers. They confirmed that PAPSS2 deficiency impairs DHEA sulfation and leads to androgen excess. ✗

### Inheritance

The consanguineous Pakistani family reported by Ahmad et al. (1998) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Later reports of homozygous and compound heterozygous PAPSS2 mutations confirmed this pattern. ✗

### Mapping

Ahmad et al. (1998) mapped the disorder in the Pakistani family to chromosome 10q23-q24 by linkage analysis. ✗

### Molecular Genetics

Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) studied the Pakistani family originally reported by Ahmad et al. (1998). In affected members they found a homozygous nonsense mutation in PAPSS2, which lies in the linked region. In the same study, they showed that the brachymorphic (bm) mouse carries a missense mutation in the orthologous Papss2 gene. ✗

In the girl with premature pubarche and spondylodysplasia, Noordam et al. (2009) found compound heterozygous inactivating mutations in PAPSS2. ⚠︎

Miyake et al. (2012) and Iida et al. (2013) found biallelic PAPSS2 mutations in patients with brachyolmia.

### Animal Model

The brachymorphic (bm) mouse is an autosomal recessive mutant with short limbs, a short tail and undersulfated cartilage proteoglycans. Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) showed that it carries a missense mutation in Papss2, the mouse ortholog of the human disease gene. ✗

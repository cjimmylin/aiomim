### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that brachyolmia type 4 with mild epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes (BCYM4) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the PAPSS2 gene (603005) on chromosome 10q23.2-q23.31. ⚠︎

### Description

Brachyolmia type 4 with mild epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes (BCYM4) is an autosomal recessive skeletal dysplasia. Its features include short-trunk short stature (Miyake et al., 2012), platyspondyly, and mild changes in the epiphyses and metaphyses (Ahmad et al., 1998). The disorder is caused by loss-of-function mutations in the gene that encodes PAPS synthase-2 (Miyake et al., 2012). This ATP sulfurylase/APS kinase activates inorganic sulfate to 3-prime-phosphoadenosine 5-prime-phosphosulfate (PAPS), the universal sulfate donor (Faiyaz ul Haque et al., 1998). PAPSS2 deficiency was also reported in a girl with premature pubarche, very low DHEA sulfate levels and androgen excess (Noordam et al., 2009). ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

Ahmad et al. (1998) described the disorder as a distinct form of spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia (SEMD) in a kindred from Pakistan. Miyake et al. (2012) later found PAPSS2 mutations in patients with brachyolmia.

### Clinical Features

Ahmad et al. (1998) described a large inbred Pakistani kindred with a distinct form of spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia. Affected members had short stature evident at birth, bowed lower limbs, enlarged knee joints and kyphoscoliosis. Radiographs showed platyspondyly with irregular end plates, delayed epiphyseal ossification, and mild metaphyseal abnormalities.

Noordam et al. (2009) reported a girl with premature pubarche and hyperandrogenism. She had very low DHEA sulfate (DHEAS) levels and increased androgen levels.

Miyake et al. (2012) reported patients with autosomal recessive brachyolmia who had PAPSS2 mutations. The 6 patients had short-trunk short stature. Their radiographic features included rectangular vertebral bodies with irregular endplates and narrow intervertebral discs. Epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes of the tubular bones were variable.

### Biochemical Features

The patient reported by Noordam et al. (2009) had very low DHEAS levels and increased androgen levels. In vitro, the authors coincubated human SULT2A1 with wild-type or mutant PAPSS2 proteins, and the results confirmed that the mutations were inactivating. Noordam et al. (2009) noted that sulfation converts the androgen precursor DHEA to its inactive sulfate ester, DHEAS, which prevents conversion of DHEA to an active androgen. They concluded that PAPSS2 deficiency is a monogenic adrenocortical cause of androgen excess.

### Inheritance

Ahmad et al. (1998) reported that analysis of the pedigree of the inbred Pakistani kindred strongly suggested autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) identified ATPSK2 (PAPSS2), which encodes an ATP sulfurylase/APS kinase, in the region of chromosome 10q23-q24 to which the gene for this dwarfing condition had been mapped. In the SEMD family they characterized a nonsense mutation in ATPSK2.

Noordam et al. (2009) found compound heterozygous mutations in PAPSS2 in a girl with premature pubarche. In vitro studies confirmed that the mutations were inactivating.

Miyake et al. (2012) found biallelic PAPSS2 mutations in patients with brachyolmia. One patient was homozygous and others were compound heterozygous. ⚠︎

### Animal Model

Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) identified Atpsk2 (Papss2) as the mouse ortholog of human ATPSK2 (PAPSS2). They showed that the brachymorphic (bm) mouse carries a missense mutation in the region of Atpsk2 that encodes the APS kinase activity.

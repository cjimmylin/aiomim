### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that brachyolmia type 4 with mild epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes (BCYM4) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the PAPSS2 gene (603005) on chromosome 10q23.2-q23.31.

### Description

Brachyolmia type 4 with mild epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes is an autosomal recessive skeletal dysplasia. It is characterized by short-trunk short stature with normal intelligence. Radiographs show rectangular vertebral bodies or platyspondyly with irregular endplates, narrow intervertebral discs, early calcification of rib cartilage, short femoral necks, and mild epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes of the tubular bones (Ahmad et al., 1998; Miyake et al., 2012). The phenotype ranges from brachyolmia to spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia (Miyake et al., 2012). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Ahmad et al. (1998) studied an inbred Pakistani kindred spanning 8 generations with a distinct form of spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia (SEMD). They evaluated 16 affected individuals, 11 males and 5 females. Clinical findings included short stature evident at birth, short bowed lower limbs, mild brachydactyly, kyphoscoliosis, an abnormal gait, enlarged knee joints, precocious osteoarthropathy, and normal intelligence. Radiographs showed delayed epiphyseal ossification at the hips and knees, platyspondyly with irregular end plates and narrowed joint spaces, diffuse early osteoarthritic changes, mainly in the spine and hands, and mild brachydactyly. Ahmad et al. (1998) considered this phenotype distinct from other autosomal recessive forms of SEMD because of the mild metaphyseal involvement, the type of brachydactyly, and the absence of loose joints or other clinical findings.

Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) studied a large inbred Pakistani family with a distinct, recessively inherited dwarfing form of SEMD.

Noordam et al. (2009) reported a girl with premature pubarche, hyperandrogenic anovulation, very low levels of dehydroepiandrosterone sulfate (DHEAS), and increased androgen levels.

Miyake et al. (2012) studied a Turkish family with autosomal recessive brachyolmia, of whom 3 members were affected, along with 3 more patients with similar brachyolmia phenotypes: 2 Japanese and 1 Korean. All 6 patients had short-trunk short stature that became noticeable in childhood, with normal intelligence and facies. Radiographs showed rectangular vertebral bodies with irregular endplates, narrow intervertebral discs, early calcification of rib cartilage, short femoral necks, and mildly short metacarpals.

### Biochemical Features

The girl reported by Noordam et al. (2009) had very low DHEAS levels and increased androgen levels.

### Inheritance

Ahmad et al. (1998) concluded that pedigree analysis of an 8-generation inbred Pakistani kindred strongly suggested autosomal recessive inheritance. Consanguineous loops could account for all affected individuals being homozygous for the abnormal allele. Miyake et al. (2012) also described the disorder as autosomal recessive and found homozygous or compound heterozygous PAPSS2 mutations in affected individuals.

### Mapping

In a large inbred Pakistani family with recessively inherited SEMD, Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) mapped the disease gene to chromosome 10q23-24. This region is syntenic with the brachymorphic locus on mouse chromosome 19.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of a large inbred Pakistani family with SEMD, Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) identified a nonsense mutation in the ATPSK2 gene (PAPSS2), which encodes ATP sulfurylase/APS kinase.

In a girl with premature pubarche, hyperandrogenic anovulation, very low DHEAS levels, and increased androgen levels, Noordam et al. (2009) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the PAPSS2 gene. The mutations were inactivating: when human SULT2A1 was coincubated in vitro with wild-type or mutant PAPSS2 proteins, the mutant proteins failed to support SULT2A1 activity. Noordam et al. (2009) concluded that PAPSS2 deficiency is a monogenic adrenocortical cause of androgen excess. ⚠︎

Miyake et al. (2012) used exon capture and next-generation sequencing in 3 affected members of a Turkish family with autosomal recessive brachyolmia. They identified a homozygous loss-of-function mutation in the PAPSS2 gene, c.337_338insG (p.A113GfsX18). In 3 more patients with similar brachyolmia phenotypes (2 Japanese and 1 Korean), they found other loss-of-function PAPSS2 mutations. One patient was homozygous for IVS3+2delT. The second was compound heterozygous for c.616-634del19 (p.V206SfsX9) and c.1309-1310delAG (p.R437GfsX19). The third was compound heterozygous for c.480_481insCGTA (p.K161RfsX6) and c.661delA (p.I221SfsX40).

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Miyake et al. (2012) noted that spinal changes were very similar among their 6 patients with PAPSS2 mutations, while epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes of the tubular bones varied. They concluded that PAPSS2 mutations cause a family of skeletal dysplasias, with phenotypes ranging from brachyolmia to SEMD.

### Pathogenesis

Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) noted that ATP sulfurylase/APS kinase activates inorganic sulfate to PAPS, the universal sulfate donor. The cartilage specificity of the human and mouse phenotypes supported a critical role of sulfate activation in the maturation of cartilage extracellular matrix and in skeletogenesis.

Noordam et al. (2009) noted that DHEA sulfotransferase (SULT2A1) converts DHEA to its inactive sulfate, DHEAS, and needs PAPS, which PAPSS2 makes, to work. As a result, PAPSS2 deficiency lowers DHEA sulfation and allows more DHEA to be converted to active androgens.

### Animal Model

In the brachymorphic mouse, Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) identified a missense mutation in the region of the Atpsk2 gene, the mouse ortholog of human ATPSK2, that encodes the APS kinase activity.

In 3 affected members of a Turkish family with autosomal recessive brachyolmia, Miyake et al. (2012) identified a homozygous 1-bp insertion (c.337_338insG) in the PAPSS2 gene. The insertion caused a frameshift and premature termination (p.A113GfsX18) and was a loss-of-function mutation. It was found by exon capture and next-generation sequencing. The patients had short-trunk short stature that became noticeable in childhood, normal intelligence, rectangular vertebral bodies with irregular endplates, narrow intervertebral discs, early calcification of rib cartilage, short femoral necks, and mildly short metacarpals.

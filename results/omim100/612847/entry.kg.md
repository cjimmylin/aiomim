### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that brachyolmia type 4 with mild epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes (BCYM4) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the PAPSS2 gene (603005) on chromosome 10q23. ⚠︎

### Description

Brachyolmia type 4 with mild epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes is an autosomal recessive skeletal dysplasia. It causes short-trunk short stature that becomes noticeable in childhood. Intelligence and facies are normal. Radiographic features include rectangular vertebral bodies with irregular endplates and narrow intervertebral discs, precocious calcification of the rib cartilages, short femoral necks, and mildly shortened metacarpals. Epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes of the tubular bones vary between patients (Miyake et al., 2012). Miyake et al. (2012) concluded that PAPSS2 mutations produce a skeletal dysplasia family with a gradation of phenotypes ranging from brachyolmia to spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia.

### Clinical Features

Ahmad et al. (1998) described a large, inbred, 8-generation kindred from a remote area of Pakistan with a distinct form of spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia (SEMD). They evaluated 16 affected individuals (11 males and 5 females). Clinical findings were short stature evident at birth, short bowed lower limbs, mild brachydactyly, kyphoscoliosis, an abnormal gait, enlarged knee joints, precocious osteoarthropathy, and normal intelligence. Radiographs showed the following: - delayed epiphyseal ossification at the hips and knees - platyspondyly with irregular end plates and narrowed joint spaces - diffuse early osteoarthritic changes, mainly in the spine and hands - mild brachydactyly - mild metaphyseal abnormalities, mostly at the hips and knees

Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) studied a large inbred Pakistani family with a distinct, recessively inherited form of SEMD, a dwarfing condition.

Noordam et al. (2009) described a girl with premature pubarche, hyperandrogenic anovulation, very low DHEAS levels, and increased androgen levels.

Miyake et al. (2012) described 6 patients with autosomal recessive brachyolmia: 3 affected members of a Turkish family, 2 Japanese patients, and 1 Korean patient. The patients had short-trunk short stature that became conspicuous during childhood, with normal intelligence and facies. Radiographic features included rectangular vertebral bodies with irregular endplates and narrow intervertebral discs, precocious calcification of the rib cartilages, short femoral necks, and mildly shortened metacarpals. The spinal changes were very similar in all 6 patients, whereas the epiphyseal and metaphyseal changes of the tubular bones varied.

### Inheritance

In the inbred Pakistani kindred they reported, Ahmad et al. (1998) concluded that pedigree analysis strongly suggested autosomal recessive inheritance. Consanguineous loops could explain why all affected individuals were homozygous for the abnormal allele. Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) also found recessive inheritance of SEMD in a large inbred Pakistani family. Miyake et al. (2012) found homozygous or compound heterozygous PAPSS2 mutations in patients with autosomal recessive brachyolmia. ⚠︎

### Mapping

In a large inbred Pakistani family with SEMD, Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) mapped the disease gene to chromosome 10q23-q24. This region is syntenic with the locus of the brachymorphic mutation on mouse chromosome 19.

### Heterogeneity

Ahmad et al. (1998) concluded that the phenotype in their Pakistani kindred was distinct from other autosomal recessive forms of SEMD. They based this on the mild metaphyseal involvement, the type of brachydactyly, and the absence of loose joints or other clinical findings.

Miyake et al. (2012) noted that brachyolmia is clinically and genetically heterogeneous: TRPV4 mutations cause an autosomal dominant form. They identified PAPSS2 as a disease gene for an autosomal recessive form.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of a large inbred Pakistani family with SEMD, Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) identified a nonsense mutation in the ATPSK2 gene (PAPSS2). The gene encodes a novel ATP sulfurylase/APS kinase that catalyzes the activation of inorganic sulfate to PAPS.

In a girl with premature pubarche and androgen excess, Noordam et al. (2009) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the PAPSS2 gene. In vitro, they coincubated human SULT2A1 with wildtype or mutant PAPSS2 proteins, which confirmed that the mutations were inactivating.

In 3 affected members of a Turkish family with autosomal recessive brachyolmia, Miyake et al. (2012) used exon capture followed by next-generation sequencing. They identified a homozygous loss-of-function mutation in the PAPSS2 gene, c.337_338insG (p.A113GfsX18). In 3 additional patients with a similar brachyolmia phenotype (2 Japanese and 1 Korean), they found PAPSS2 mutations as follows: - 1 patient was homozygous for a splice site mutation (IVS3+2delT). - 1 patient was compound heterozygous for 2 loss-of-function mutations, c.616-634del19 (p.V206SfsX9) and c.1309-1310delAG (p.R437GfsX19). - 1 patient was compound heterozygous for 2 loss-of-function mutations, c.480_481insCGTA (p.K161RfsX6) and c.661delA (p.I221SfsX40).

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Miyake et al. (2012) concluded that PAPSS2 mutations produce a skeletal dysplasia family with a gradation of phenotypes ranging from brachyolmia to spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia.

### Pathogenesis

Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) concluded that the human and mouse phenotypes were both specific to cartilage. In their view, this showed that sulfate activation to PAPS, the universal donor for protein sulfation, plays a critical role in the maturation of cartilage extracellular matrix molecules and in skeletogenesis.

Noordam et al. (2009) noted that SULT2A1 converts DHEA to its inactive sulfate ester, DHEAS, which prevents the conversion of DHEA to active androgens. SULT2A1 requires PAPS for its catalytic activity. They concluded that PAPSS2 deficiency is a monogenic adrenocortical cause of androgen excess.

### Animal Model

In the brachymorphic mouse, Faiyaz ul Haque et al. (1998) identified a missense mutation in Atpsk2, the orthologous locus on mouse chromosome 19. The mutation lies in the region encoding the APS kinase activity.

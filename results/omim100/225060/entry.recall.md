### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that cleft lip/palate-ectodermal dysplasia syndrome (CLPED1) is caused by mutation in the NECTIN1 gene (600644) on chromosome 11q23.3. Suzuki et al. (2000) identified PVRL1 (NECTIN1) as the gene responsible for this autosomal recessive syndrome. Sözen et al. (2001) found that heterozygosity for a PVRL1 nonsense mutation was associated with sporadic, nonsyndromic cleft lip/palate (orofacial cleft-7, OFC7), which is included here.

### Description

Cleft lip/palate-ectodermal dysplasia syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder (Suzuki et al., 2000). Rodini and Richieri-Costa (1990) reported ectodermal dysplasia, cleft lip/palate, mental retardation, and syndactyly of fingers 2-3, showing that some affected individuals have intellectual disability.

### Clinical Features

Zlotogora et al. (1987) reported 2 sibs with a syndrome that included cleft lip and palate, sparse scalp hair, and partial syndactyly of the fingers and toes. The older child also had mental retardation.

Suzuki et al. (1998) described the disorder as characterized by dental anomalies, hypotrichosis, onychodysplasia, syndactyly, and cleft lip/cleft palate.

Yoshida et al. (2015) reported a patient with hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia, with sparse, brittle, fine, dry hair and hypodontia.

### Inheritance

Suzuki et al. (2000) described CLPED1 as autosomal recessive and identified PVRL1 as the responsible gene.

### Mapping

Suzuki et al. (1998) studied families with the disorder (then called ED4) from Margarita Island. Using an affected-only DNA-pooling strategy, they carried out linkage disequilibrium mapping of the disease gene to chromosome 11q23, and haplotype analysis of the Margarita Island families further localized the gene.

### Molecular Genetics

By positional cloning, Suzuki et al. (2000) identified PVRL1 as the gene responsible for autosomal recessive CLPED1. PVRL1 encodes nectin-1, an immunoglobulin-related transmembrane cell-cell adhesion molecule. Nectin-1 is also the principal cell surface receptor for alpha-herpesviruses.

Sözen et al. (2001) studied individuals with sporadic, nonsyndromic cleft lip with or without cleft palate in northern Venezuela. They found a highly significant association between heterozygosity for the PVRL1 nonsense mutation W185X and nonsyndromic CL/P, which suggests that heterozygous carriers have an increased risk of nonsyndromic orofacial clefting (OFC7).

### Population Genetics

Bustos et al. (1991) described an ectodermal dysplasia in families from a relatively isolated and inbred population on Margarita Island, a Caribbean Sea island in northeastern Venezuela.

Suzuki et al. (2000) noted the high frequency of CLPED1 on Margarita Island in the Caribbean Sea.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that cleft lip/palate-ectodermal dysplasia syndrome (CLPED1) is caused by homozygous mutation in the PVRL1 gene (NECTIN1; 600644) on chromosome 11q23. Heterozygosity for a PVRL1 nonsense mutation has been associated with susceptibility to nonsyndromic orofacial cleft-7 (OFC7), which is included here. ✗

### Description

Cleft lip/palate-ectodermal dysplasia syndrome, also called Zlotogora-Ogur syndrome or Margarita Island ectodermal dysplasia, is an autosomal recessive disorder. Its main features are cleft lip and/or palate, ectodermal dysplasia affecting the hair (sparse, short, dry hair), teeth (hypodontia and dental anomalies) and nails, and partial cutaneous syndactyly of the fingers and/or toes. Some affected individuals have intellectual disability. ✗

### Nomenclature

The disorder is also known as Zlotogora-Ogur syndrome, after the authors of the early case reports, and as Margarita Island ectodermal dysplasia, because many cases come from Margarita Island, off the coast of Venezuela. ✗

### Clinical Features

Zlotogora et al. (1987) described sibs with cleft lip/palate, ectodermal dysplasia involving the hair, teeth and nails, syndactyly, and intellectual disability. ⚠︎

A high frequency of the syndrome was recognized on Margarita Island, Venezuela, where affected individuals had cleft lip/palate together with hypotrichosis, dental anomalies, nail dysplasia and partial syndactyly. Cleft lip/palate without other features was also unusually common in the island population. ✗

### Inheritance

Several families included affected sibs born to unaffected parents, some of whom were consanguineous. This pattern is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance, which was confirmed by the finding of homozygous PVRL1 mutations (Suzuki et al., 2000). ✗

### Mapping

In families from Margarita Island, Suzuki et al. (1998) mapped the disease locus to chromosome 11q23 by homozygosity mapping and linkage analysis. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In affected individuals from Margarita Island, Suzuki et al. (2000) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in the PVRL1 gene, trp185-to-ter (W185X). PVRL1 encodes nectin-1, a cell-cell adhesion molecule of adherens junctions that also serves as a receptor for herpesviruses. Suzuki et al. (2000) also found PVRL1 mutations in affected families from other populations. ✗

Sozen et al. (2001) studied individuals with nonsyndromic cleft lip with or without cleft palate in northern Venezuela. Heterozygosity for the W185X mutation was more common in patients than in controls, suggesting that heterozygous carriers have an increased risk of nonsyndromic orofacial clefting (OFC7). ⚠︎

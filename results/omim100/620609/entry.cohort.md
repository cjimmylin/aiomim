### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Long-Olsen-Distelmaier syndrome (LNGODS) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the RRAGC gene (608267) on chromosome 1p34.3. ⚠︎

### Description

Long-Olsen-Distelmaier syndrome is an early-onset disorder that has been reported in infants with de novo heterozygous missense mutations in RRAGC. Its main features are dilated cardiomyopathy, hepatopathy, and brain abnormalities. Reijnders et al. (2023) described it as an mTORopathy with an unfavorable prognosis. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Long et al. (2016) reported a female infant of white European ancestry who was diagnosed with fetal cardiomegaly at 30 weeks' gestation. She was delivered 1 week later because of fetal hydrops. Postnatal echocardiography showed severe biventricular and biatrial chamber enlargement and severe systolic dysfunction (left ventricular ejection fraction 25%; normal greater than 50%). It also showed a small apical ventricular septal defect and a moderate secundum atrial septal defect. Her cardiomyopathy was dilated with restrictive physiology. She had mild facial dysmorphism, consisting of wide-set eyes, downslanting palpebral fissures, a prominent forehead, mild macrocephaly, and low-set ears. She also had bilateral cataracts. Failure to thrive required gastrostomy, and she had oral aversion. Hypoglycemia, elevated lactate, and elevated hepatic transaminases were noted but were attributed to another cause. Respiratory chain studies in skin and muscle were normal. She developed multisystem organ failure and died at 22 months of age. Screening echocardiography showed that her parents were unaffected.

Reijnders et al. (2023) reported 3 unrelated infants with dilated cardiomyopathy, hepatopathy, and brain abnormalities. The brain abnormalities included pachygyria, polymicrogyria, and septo-optic dysplasia. The authors described the disorder as fatal in early childhood. ⚠︎

### Other Features

Reijnders et al. (2023) found increased cell size in fibroblasts from a patient carrying the T90N mutation.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous RRAGC mutations in patients with Long-Olsen-Distelmaier syndrome occurred de novo in the patient reported by Long et al. (2016) and in the 3 patients reported by Reijnders et al. (2023).

### Molecular Genetics

In a girl with Long-Olsen-Distelmaier syndrome, Long et al. (2016) identified a de novo heterozygous c.224C-A transversion in the RRAGC gene, resulting in a ser75-to-tyr (S75Y) substitution. SIFT and PolyPhen2 predicted the variant to be damaging, and after filtering it remained the single most plausible candidate. The mutation lay in the highly conserved P-loop (G1) of the GTPase domain and was absent from the ExAC database. ExAC controls carried no missense or truncating variants in the RRAGC GTPase binding domain. The authors could not exclude synergistic heterozygosity involving other candidate variants. In silico modeling and molecular dynamics simulation predicted that the S75Y mutant could not coordinate Mg(2+), would disrupt ligand interactions, and would expand the GDP-bound conformation, similar to the engineered GDP-bound mutant S75L. In AD293 cells overexpressing RagC S75Y, phosphorylation of S6K and S6 after amino acid deprivation was increased compared with wildtype. This indicated that the mutation made cells partly insensitive to amino acid deprivation and activated mTORC1 signaling.

Kim et al. (2021) noted that further evidence, including functional studies in animal models and additional RRAGC mutations in dilated cardiomyopathy cohorts, was needed to prove the pathogenicity of the S75Y variant. They also noted that recurrent somatic RRAGC missense mutations had been found in 17% of patients with follicular lymphoma. These mutations cluster around the nucleotide-binding domain and include S75C, S75N, and S75F, and they result in greater GDP binding affinity and hyperactive mTORC1 signaling. In silico modeling predicted that S75Y likewise increases the GDP-bound state, which supports a gain-of-function mechanism.

In 3 infants with Long-Olsen-Distelmaier syndrome, Reijnders et al. (2023) used trio exome sequencing to identify 3 different de novo heterozygous missense mutations in the RRAGC gene: T90N, P118L, and W115R. These variants had previously been reported as somatic variants in follicular lymphoma. Fibroblasts from the patient with the T90N variant showed increased cell size and dysregulated p70S6K and TFEB signaling. In these cells, mTOR subcellular localization was decoupled from metabolic state. Key findings were confirmed for all 3 variants in HEK293 cells, consistent with constitutive overactivation of mTORC1.

### Pathogenesis

Kim et al. (2021) reviewed that RagC forms heterodimeric complexes with RagA or RagB, and that the activity of these complexes depends on whether they are bound to GDP or GTP. The Rag proteins localize to the lysosomal surface, where they recruit mTORC1 and TFEB in response to amino acid availability. The S75 residue of RagC is evolutionarily conserved from yeast to human and lies in the P-loop motif that mediates GTP/GDP binding.

Kim et al. (2021) overexpressed RagC S75Y in neonatal rat ventricular cardiomyocytes using adenovirus. Cell size increased 1.5-fold, Nppa, Nppb, and Mhc7 were elevated, and Mhc6 was downregulated. Wildtype RagC had no effect on cell size. The S75Y cells showed hyperactive mTORC1 signaling, with increased phosphorylated p70S6K and S6. They also showed cytosolic retention of TFEB with reduced expression of TFEB target genes, impaired autophagy flux, accumulation of ubiquitin-conjugated proteins, and increased aggresomes. In co-immunoprecipitation studies in AD293 cells, RagC S75Y bound TFEB with greater affinity than wildtype RagC. Rapamycin attenuated TFEB phosphorylation at ser211 but neither disrupted the Rag-TFEB interaction nor promoted nuclear translocation of TFEB. The authors proposed that mTOR-independent binding between RagC S75Y and TFEB makes mTOR inhibition ineffective. They concluded that S75Y is a pathogenic gain-of-function mutation that leads to cardiomyopathy and that the primary pathologic step is defective mTOR-TFEB signaling. They noted that it remained to be determined whether follicular lymphoma occurs in patients with RagC S75Y cardiomyopathy.

### Animal Model

Kim et al. (2021) used TALEN technology to generate a zebrafish knock-in line carrying rragc S56Y, which corresponds to human RagC S75Y, in the heart-predominant rragca ortholog. Homozygous knock-in fish began to die at 5 months of age, and approximately 70% survived to 1 year. The fish had reduced body weight and reduced maximum swimming capacity. Ejection fraction and fractional shortening were decreased, the ratio of end-systolic volume to body weight was increased, the E/A ratio was decreased, and the myocardial performance index was increased. Ventricular surface area relative to body weight was enlarged, trabecular density was reduced, and ventricular myosin heavy chain (vmhc) transcript was elevated. In knock-in hearts, TFEB-controlled lysosomal genes were downregulated. LC3-II was severely reduced and did not respond to bafilomycin A1. Ubiquitin-conjugated proteins were elevated, and glycogen deposits were increased at 1 year of age. These findings indicated dysregulated autophagy-lysosomal clearance. Fish homozygous for either of 2 rragca knockout alleles had no noticeable phenotype and unchanged cardiac pump function up to at least 1 year of age. This supported a gain-of-function mechanism, and the authors suggested that compensation by rragd might explain the lack of a knockout phenotype.

Kim et al. (2021) found that the mTOR inhibitors rapamycin and torin did not normalize the enlarged size of RagC S75Y cardiomyocytes, although they inhibited mTORC1 activity. Likewise, mtor haploinsufficiency did not rescue cardiac pump dysfunction, cardiomyopathy marker expression, impaired growth, or reduced survival in rragc knock-in zebrafish. In contrast, ectopic TFEB expression normalized the size of RagC S75Y cardiomyocytes and restored autophagy flux. A cardiomyocyte-specific TFEB transgene restored ejection fraction, fractional shortening, and vmhc expression in knock-in zebrafish and significantly improved their survival. The authors suggested that TFEB activation, but not mTOR inhibition, is a therapeutic strategy for RagC S75Y cardiomyopathy.

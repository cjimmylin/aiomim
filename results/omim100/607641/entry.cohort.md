### Description

Distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-14 (HMND14) is a lower motor neuron disease. It has been associated with a missense mutation in the largest subunit of dynactin, a protein involved in axonal transport, and with reduced binding of the mutant protein to microtubules (Puls et al., 2003). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Puls et al. (2003) studied a family with lower motor neuron disease. The abstract-level evidence does not give the number of affected members or their detailed clinical findings. ⚠︎

### Mapping

In a family with lower motor neuron disease, Puls et al. (2003) found linkage of the disorder to a 4-Mb region on chromosome 2p13. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In the affected members of a family with lower motor neuron disease linked to chromosome 2p13, Puls et al. (2003) analyzed a gene in the linked interval that encodes the largest subunit of the axonal transport protein dynactin. They found a single base-pair change resulting in an amino acid substitution. They predicted that the substitution would distort the folding of the microtubule-binding domain of dynactin. In binding assays, the mutant dynactin protein bound less to microtubules. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Puls et al. (2003) found that mutant dynactin bound less to microtubules in binding assays. They concluded that dysfunction of dynactin-mediated axonal transport can lead to human motor neuron disease.

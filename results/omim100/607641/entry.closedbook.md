### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal dominant distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-14 (HMND14), also called distal hereditary motor neuronopathy type VIIB (HMN7B), is caused by heterozygous mutation in the dynactin gene (DCTN1; 601143) on chromosome 2p13. ✗

### Description

Autosomal dominant distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-14 is a slowly progressive lower motor neuron disease. It begins in adulthood with breathing difficulty caused by vocal fold paralysis. Later features include weakness of the face and weakness and atrophy of distal muscles, most notably in the hands. Sensation is spared. ✗

### Clinical Features

Puls et al. (2003) studied a large North American kindred with an autosomal dominant lower motor neuron disorder. Affected members first had breathing difficulty from bilateral vocal fold paralysis. Weakness of the face and weakness and atrophy of the hand muscles followed, and the disease later reached the distal lower limbs. Sensation was normal, and there were no signs of upper motor neuron involvement. ✗

Puls et al. (2005) gave a fuller clinical description of the same kindred and called the disorder a distal spinal and bulbar muscular atrophy. Disease severity and rate of progression differed among affected family members. ✗

### Inheritance

In the family reported by Puls et al. (2003), the disorder was inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern. ✗

### Mapping

Puls et al. (2003) mapped the disease locus in the kindred to chromosome 2p13. The interval contained the DCTN1 gene.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of the kindred with lower motor neuron disease and vocal fold paralysis, Puls et al. (2003) found a heterozygous gly59-to-ser (G59S) mutation in the DCTN1 gene, which encodes the p150(Glued) subunit of dynactin. The mutation lies in the highly conserved CAP-Gly microtubule-binding domain. It segregated with the disease. Functional studies showed that the mutant protein bound microtubules less well than normal, and that the mutant protein formed aggregates in cultured cells. The authors proposed that the mutation disrupts dynein/dynactin-dependent axonal transport in motor neurons. ✗

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Other mutations in the CAP-Gly domain of DCTN1 cause Perry syndrome, a different disorder with parkinsonism, depression, weight loss, and central hypoventilation (Farrer et al., 2009). Unlike G59S, these mutations did not appear to cause the same distal motor neuron phenotype. ✗

### Animal Model

Lai et al. (2007) made transgenic mice that expressed the G59S mutant p150(Glued). The mice developed late-onset, progressive motor neuron disease, and mutant protein accumulated in motor neurons. ✗

Laird et al. (2008) made knockin mice carrying the G59S mutation. Homozygous mice died early in development. Heterozygous mice developed motor neuron degeneration, with abnormalities at the neuromuscular junction. ✗

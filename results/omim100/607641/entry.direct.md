### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-14 (HMND14) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding the largest subunit of dynactin (DCTN1) on chromosome 2p13. ⚠︎

### Description

Distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-14 is a lower motor neuron disease. Puls et al. (2003) showed that it is caused by mutation in the gene encoding the largest subunit of dynactin, a protein involved in axonal transport.

### Mapping

Puls et al. (2003) linked a lower motor neuron disease to a 4-Mb region on chromosome 2p13.

### Molecular Genetics

Puls et al. (2003) screened a gene in the 2p13 linkage interval that encodes the largest subunit of the axonal transport protein dynactin in a family with lower motor neuron disease. They found a single base-pair change causing an amino acid substitution that was predicted to distort the folding of the dynactin microtubule-binding domain. In binding assays, the mutant protein bound microtubules less well than normal. Puls et al. (2003) concluded that dysfunction of dynactin-mediated transport can cause human motor neuron disease. ⚠︎

Münch et al. (2004) screened the genes encoding the p150 subunit of dynactin (DCTN1) and the cytoplasmic dynein heavy chain (DNCHC1) in 250 patients with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) and 150 unrelated control subjects. They found heterozygous missense mutations in DCTN1 in 1 apparently sporadic ALS patient (T1249I) and 1 person with familial ALS (M571T). They also found a third change (R785W) in 2 patients with familial ALS and in 2 unaffected relatives from the same kindred. Münch et al. (2004) suggested that these DCTN1 variants may be a previously unknown genomic risk factor for ALS.

### Pathogenesis

Impaired axonal transport in motor neurons has been proposed as a mechanism of neuronal degeneration in motor neuron disease. Puls et al. (2003) found that a mutant dynactin subunit bound microtubules less well than normal. They concluded that this supports a role for impaired dynactin-mediated transport in human motor neuron disease.

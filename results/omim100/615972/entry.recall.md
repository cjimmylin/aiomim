### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that nanophthalmos-4 (NNO4) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TMEM98 gene (615949) on chromosome 17q11.2.

### Description

Khorram et al. (2015) described autosomal dominant nanophthalmos as an inherited eye disorder in which the eye is structurally normal but smaller. They noted that nanophthalmic eyes also have thick sclera, which has been linked to a high risk of choroidal effusions after any intraocular surgery. Awadalla et al. (2014) named angle-closure glaucoma as one of the main complications. Yu et al. (2009) described simple microphthalmia, a structurally normal but small eye globe, as an uncommon congenital or developmental ocular anomaly that is usually bilateral, with a short axis and hyperopia of +7.00 to +13.00 diopters. Nanophthalmos-4 is the autosomal dominant form caused by mutation in TMEM98, which Awadalla et al. (2014) reported as the first gene identified to cause autosomal dominant nanophthalmos.

### Inheritance

Awadalla et al. (2014) studied one large white family in which nanophthalmos was inherited as an autosomal dominant trait.

### Heterogeneity

Khorram et al. (2015) noted that nanophthalmos is genetically heterogeneous and that both autosomal dominant and autosomal recessive forms have been reported. Several cases of recessive nanophthalmos have been attributed to mutations in the MFRP gene. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Awadalla et al. (2014) studied one large white family (NNO-SA1) with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos. Exome sequencing found a single novel missense variant within the linkage region. The variant lay in exon 8 of the transmembrane-98 (TMEM98) gene: a c.577G-C change that results in an ala193-to-pro (A193P) substitution. The variant segregated with the disease in the family.

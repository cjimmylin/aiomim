### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that nanophthalmos-4 (NNO4) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TMEM98 gene on chromosome 17q11.

### Description

Nanophthalmos is a rare developmental disorder of the eye in which the globe is abnormally small but has no other major structural malformation. Short axial length leads to high hyperopia. The sclera is often thickened, and patients are at increased risk of angle-closure glaucoma and of complications after intraocular surgery, such as uveal effusion. Nanophthalmos-4 is the autosomal dominant form caused by mutation in TMEM98. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Awadalla et al. (2014) found that nanophthalmos in the families they studied segregated as an autosomal dominant trait. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Nanophthalmos is genetically heterogeneous. Both autosomal dominant and autosomal recessive forms have been described, including the recessive form caused by mutation in the MFRP gene.

### Molecular Genetics

Awadalla et al. (2014) studied families with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos. In affected members, they identified heterozygous mutations in the TMEM98 gene, which encodes a transmembrane protein, and the mutations segregated with the disease. ⚠︎

### Animal Model

Later mouse studies suggested that TMEM98 interacts with MYRF, another gene involved in nanophthalmos, and linked both proteins to ocular growth. ✗

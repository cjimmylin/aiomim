### Description

Auriculocondylar syndrome is a craniofacial malformation disorder of the first and second pharyngeal arches. Its cardinal features are malformed external ears, typically a 'question mark' ear with a cleft between the helix and the lobule, and hypoplasia of the mandibular condyle with temporomandibular joint abnormalities and micrognathia. Other features can include round face, prominent cheeks, microstomia, glossoptosis, and respiratory or feeding difficulties in infancy. Expressivity is variable, both within and between families. ✗

### Heterogeneity

Auriculocondylar syndrome is genetically heterogeneous. Other forms that I recall with confidence are ARCND1, caused by mutation in the GNAI3 gene; ARCND2, caused by mutation in the PLCB4 gene; and ARCND3, caused by mutation in the EDN1 gene. All of these genes act in the endothelin-1/endothelin receptor type A signaling pathway, which patterns the mandibular arch. ⚠︎

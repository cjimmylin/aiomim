### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that auriculocondylar syndrome-4 (ARCND4) is caused by a heterozygous 430-kb tandem duplication on chromosome 7 that covers most of the HDAC9 gene and includes possible regulatory elements of the neighboring TWIST1 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Auriculocondylar syndrome (ARCND) is a rare disorder affecting structures derived from the first and second pharyngeal arches, mainly resulting in micrognathia and auricular malformations ('question mark ears'). Other features include mandibular condyle hypoplasia, microstomia, glossoptosis, postauricular tags and prominent cheeks, and there is wide clinical variability, including cases with isolated ear anomalies (Romanelli Tavares et al., 2022). ARCND4 is caused by a 430-kb duplication at the HDAC9/TWIST1 locus on chromosome 7, identified in a single large Brazilian family (Romanelli Tavares et al., 2022).

### Clinical Features

Masotti et al. (2008) described a multigeneration family (F1) with 11 affected individuals with auriculocondylar syndrome and confirmed intrafamilial clinical variability. Facial asymmetry, a feature not highlighted in other reports of the disorder, was highly prevalent among the patients.

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) studied a previously reported non-consanguineous 4-generation Brazilian family, which they designated F1, with 11 members showing the typical features of ARCND. Of those documented, most had question mark ears (8 of 10), microstomia (8 of 10), and micrognathia (6 of 10), with considerable intrafamilial variability. The patients did not have large, open fontanelles, the hallmark feature of trisomy 7p.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of ARCND4 in the family reported by Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) was consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance: the duplication segregated with the disease in a 4-generation family, and all unaffected individuals were negative for it, suggesting full penetrance in this family. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Masotti et al. (2008) performed linkage analysis in 2 large families with auriculocondylar syndrome, F1 and F2. After excluding 3 loci associated with first and second pharyngeal arch disorders (Treacher Collins syndrome, oculoauriculovertebral spectrum, and Townes-Brocks syndrome), a genomewide search showed evidence of linkage to 1p21.1-q23.3 in F2 (maximum lod score of 3.01 at theta = 0); this locus was not linked to the phenotype in F1, providing evidence for genetic heterogeneity.

In the Brazilian family F1, Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) performed linkage analysis using SNP genotyping in 9 affected and 3 unaffected individuals and found 3 regions with positive lod scores on chromosomes 7, 14, and 18. The highest lod score (2.88), close to the maximum theoretical lod score for the family (2.93), was observed in a region of about 17.6 Mb on chromosome 7 (chr7:14395902-32017194, GRCh38), which was narrowed by microsatellite genotyping to chr7:14395902-28158440.

### Heterogeneity

Masotti et al. (2008) found that the auriculocondylar syndrome locus on 1p21.1-q23.3 identified in family F2 was not linked to the phenotype in family F1, suggesting that at least 2 loci are responsible for the phenotype. Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) noted that no pathogenic variants in PLCB4, GNAI3, or EDN1 were found in their family F1, and identified a fourth ARCND locus on chromosome 7.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of the Brazilian ARCND family F1, Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) excluded pathogenic variants in the PLCB4, GNAI3, and EDN1 genes by Sanger sequencing. Whole-exome sequencing of 4 affected individuals identified, within the linkage regions, only a synonymous TRIL variant that did not segregate with the disease. Gene prioritization ranked TWIST1 as the top candidate within the chromosome 7 region, and targeted resequencing of a 2.4-Mb region around TWIST1 revealed no pathogenic variants in TWIST1 but detected a heterozygous tandem duplication of 430,302 bp (NC_000007.14:g.18437239_18867540dup) telomeric to TWIST1 and covering most of the HDAC9 gene. The duplication segregated with the disease in the family (lod score of 2.88). Capture-C analysis using the TWIST1 promoter as viewpoint showed multiple cis interactions between the TWIST1 promoter and possible regulatory elements within the duplicated region. ⚠︎

To test pathogenicity, Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) generated induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) from 3 affected family members and controls. Neural crest cells derived from patient iPSCs showed increased HDAC9 (3.15-fold) and TWIST1 (2.03-fold) mRNA expression and a significant decrease in migratory capacity (4.3-fold) compared with controls, whereas cell cycle distribution was not different. Mesenchymal stem cells derived from patient neural crest cells showed significantly diminished alkaline phosphatase activity after 9 days of osteoinduction (20.3-fold decrease) and a subtle decrease in matrix mineralization, indicating delayed or impaired osteogenic differentiation.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) found 19 patients in DECIPHER with copy-number gains in the region; most were much larger and encompassed multiple genes. Seven of the 19 had ear malformation among other features and 7 had micrognathia. One duplication (patient 276644; 179 kb), enclosed entirely within the ARCND duplicated region but not including the TWIST1 regulatory elements, was found in a patient without ARCND features.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 AURICULOCONDYLAR SYNDROME 4**  
HDAC9, 430-KB DUP [NC_000007.14:g.18437239_18867540dup]

In affected members of a non-consanguineous 4-generation Brazilian family with auriculocondylar syndrome-4 (ARCND4), Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) identified a heterozygous 430,302-bp tandem duplication (NC_000007.14:g.18437239_18867540dup) located telomeric to TWIST1 and covering most of the HDAC9 gene. Several HDAC9 transcript isoforms were duplicated in their entirety, whereas full-length catalytically active isoforms extended beyond the breakpoint and were likely disrupted. The duplication was found by targeted resequencing, characterized by breakpoint PCR, and segregated with the disease; all unaffected individuals were negative. Capture-C showed contacts between the TWIST1 promoter and the duplicated region, and patient iPSC-derived neural crest cells showed increased HDAC9 and TWIST1 expression and reduced migration. ⚠︎

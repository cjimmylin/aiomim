## AURICULOCONDYLAR SYNDROME 4; ARCND4

### TEXT

### Description

Masotti et al. (2008) described auriculocondylar syndrome as a disorder of the first and second pharyngeal arches. It is characterized by malformed ears ('question mark ears'), prominent cheeks, microstomia, an abnormal temporomandibular joint, and hypoplasia of the mandibular condyle. Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) also listed micrognathia as a characteristic feature, and glossoptosis as a less common one. Masotti et al. (2008) noted high phenotypic variation both within and between families.

### Heterogeneity

Masotti et al. (2008) found evidence for genetic heterogeneity of auriculocondylar syndrome, suggesting that at least 2 loci are responsible for the phenotype.

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) noted that PLCB4 is the gene most commonly mutated in individuals with auriculocondylar syndrome, followed by GNAI3 and EDN1. All 3 genes belong to the endothelin-1/endothelin receptor type A (EDN1-EDNRA) pathway, which is responsible for patterning the mandibular domain of the first pharyngeal arch.

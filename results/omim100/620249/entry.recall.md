### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that the mild variant of congenital myopathy-10 (CMYO10B) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the MEGF10 gene (612453) on chromosome 5q23.2.

### Description

Congenital myopathy-10B (CMYO10B) is an autosomal recessive muscle disorder. Boyden et al. (2012) found it to be milder than the previously reported severe MEGF10-related myopathy. In their patients, weakness was mainly axial and proximal and was present by 12 months of age. The patients also had scoliosis and joint contractures or hyperlaxity, and muscle biopsy showed minicores. Harris et al. (2018) noted that respiratory insufficiency may not appear until adulthood.

### Nomenclature

Harris et al. (2018) discussed the spectrum of MEGF10-related disease. Takayama et al. (2016) noted that MEGF10 mutations were known to cause both early-onset myopathy characterized by areflexia, respiratory distress, and dysphagia (EMARDD), which is the more severe phenotype, and a milder phenotype of minicore myopathy.

### Clinical Features

Boyden et al. (2012) reported patients with a recessive congenital myopathy and MEGF10 mutations. Their phenotype was milder than the severe congenital myopathy described in families with nonsense and frameshift MEGF10 mutations. The patients had childhood weakness, respiratory impairment, and scoliosis. Joint contractures were present in one family, and another patient had hyperflexible joints. Muscle biopsy showed minicores. ⚠︎

Harris et al. (2018) reported a 66-year-old man with a 20-year history of progressive proximal and distal weakness of the upper and lower limbs. They noted that in patients with 2 missense mutations, respiratory dysfunction occurs at 10 years of age or much later, as it did in their patient.

### Inheritance

In the family reported by Boyden et al. (2012), the transmission pattern of CMYO10B was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

In the affected members of a family with a congenital myopathy with minicores, Boyden et al. (2012) found compound heterozygous missense mutations in the MEGF10 gene. In a second patient they identified only a single heterozygous mutation. They suggested that the milder phenotype was associated with missense mutations, whereas the severe phenotype was associated with loss of MEGF10 expression.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Boyden et al. (2012) noted that patients with the milder myopathy carried at least one missense mutation in MEGF10. In contrast, patients with the severe phenotype usually had biallelic mutations predicted to cause loss of function. ⚠︎

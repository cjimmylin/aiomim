### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that the mild variant of congenital myopathy-10 (CMYO10B) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the MEGF10 gene (612453) on chromosome 5q23.

### Description

Congenital myopathy-10B (CMYO10B) is an autosomal recessive muscle disorder that is a milder form of the myopathy caused by MEGF10 mutations. Patients have onset of proximal muscle weakness in childhood, often with mildly delayed motor milestones, and the disorder is slowly progressive. Features include respiratory insufficiency, which may appear in adulthood, scoliosis, and joint contractures or hyperlaxity. Muscle biopsy may show myopathic changes with minicores. The severe end of the spectrum, also called early-onset myopathy, areflexia, respiratory distress, and dysphagia (EMARDD), is called CMYO10A. ✗

### Nomenclature

MEGF10-related myopathy forms a spectrum. The severe infantile form, EMARDD, is called CMYO10A. The milder form, which begins in childhood or later and may show minicores on biopsy, is called CMYO10B. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Boyden et al. (2012) reported patients with a recessive congenital myopathy and MEGF10 mutations. Their phenotype was milder than the severe EMARDD phenotype. They had childhood-onset weakness, respiratory impairment, and scoliosis, and muscle biopsy showed minicores.

Liewluck et al. (2016) reported patients with multiminicore myopathy caused by MEGF10 mutations. They had respiratory insufficiency beginning in adulthood, scoliosis, and hyperlaxity of the distal joints. ✗

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CMYO10B in the reported families was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance (Boyden et al., 2012).

### Molecular Genetics

In patients with a mild congenital myopathy with minicores, Boyden et al. (2012) found homozygous or compound heterozygous mutations in the MEGF10 gene. They suggested that the mild phenotype was associated with missense mutations, whereas the severe EMARDD phenotype was associated with mutations that cause loss of function. ⚠︎

Liewluck et al. (2016) found novel MEGF10 mutations in adult patients with multiminicore disease. ✗

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Boyden et al. (2012) noted that patients with the milder myopathy carried at least one missense mutation in MEGF10. In contrast, patients with severe EMARDD (CMYO10A) usually had biallelic mutations that were predicted to cause loss of function.

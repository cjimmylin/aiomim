# Proposed updates for OMIM 620249: CONGENITAL MYOPATHY 10B, MILD VARIANT

Literature from 2018 onwards that the entry does not cite: 45 papers screened, 7 read, 7 proposal(s). Each proposal is a draft for a curator to accept, edit or reject.

**Back-test as of 2018.** OMIM's curators later added 2 reference(s) from that year on; this run proposed 2 of them (recall 1.0), and 5 paper(s) OMIM does not cite.

## [MEDIUM] Croci et al. (2022) — new_variants, phenotype_expansion, genotype_phenotype_correlation, natural_history

Croci C, Traverso M, Baratto S, Iacomino M, Pedemonte M, Caroli F, Scala M, Bruno C, Fiorillo C. Congenital myopathy associated with a novel mutation in MEGF10 gene, myofibrillar alteration and progressive course. Acta Myol 41: 111-116, 2022. [PubMed: 36349186] *(read: fulltext)*

**Why:** Croci et al. (2022) report a new homozygous MEGF10 missense mutation, c.2096G-C (p.Cys699Ser), which is not in the variant table, in an Italian man born to consanguineous parents. Unlike the mild nonprogressive course already recorded, his disease began early and progressed slowly. He had dysphagia, global areflexia, rhinolalia and hyperlordosis, and his later biopsy showed myofibrillar changes (whorled fibers, cytoplasmic inclusions). Based on this case, the authors proposed that MEGF10-related phenotypes form a continuum.

*clinical_features*: Croci et al. (2022) reported a 22-year-old Italian man, born to healthy consanguineous parents, with a congenital myopathy. From the first months of life he had weak suction, elbow contractures and scoliosis. He was born at 33 weeks' gestation and needed resuscitation for asphyxia. Motor development and intellect were normal. He then developed slowly progressive muscle weakness with impaired walking, rhinolalia, dysphagia and respiratory involvement, and from age 16 years he needed nocturnal noninvasive ventilation. On examination he had axial hypotonia, generalized muscle hypotrophy, cleft palate, global areflexia, contractures of the elbows and ankles, and hyperlordosis. Serum CK, brain and spine MRI, and cardiac studies were normal. A first muscle biopsy at age 5 years showed nonspecific changes: variation in fiber size, internal nuclei and fibrosis. A later biopsy showed myofibrillar alterations, including whorled fibers, cytoplasmic inclusions and minicores. Electrophysiologic studies were normal apart from reduced amplitude of the muscle evoked potential. The authors proposed that the minicores seen in the late-onset group may result from longstanding myofibrillar pathology that begins in infancy.

*molecular_genetics*: In an Italian man with a progressive form of mvEMARDD, Croci et al. (2022) used exome sequencing to identify a homozygous missense mutation in the MEGF10 gene, c.2096G-C, causing a cys699-to-ser (C699S) substitution (NM_001256545.2). Sanger sequencing confirmed the variant, and both consanguineous parents were heterozygous carriers. The variant was absent from ClinVar and gnomAD and changes a conserved cysteine in an EGF-like domain. In silico tools predicted it to be damaging, and it was classified as likely pathogenic.

*genotype_phenotype_correlations*: Croci et al. (2022) noted that missense variants in MEGF10 have been associated with later onset and a milder course. Their patient, who was homozygous for the C699S missense mutation, nevertheless had early-onset myopathy with a progressive course. The authors proposed that MEGF10-related phenotypes form a continuum rather than a dichotomy between classic EMARDD and mvEMARDD.

## [MEDIUM] Fujii et al. (2022) — new_variants, phenotype_expansion, genotype_phenotype_correlation

Fujii K, Hirano M, Terayama A, Inada R, Saito Y, Nishino I, Nagai Y. Identification of a novel mutation and genotype-phenotype relationship in MEGF10 myopathy. Neuromuscul Disord 32: 436-440, 2022. [PubMed: 35370044] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** Fujii et al. (2022) report a new MEGF10 missense mutation, p.G739R, which is not in the variant table. It was found in a man whose progressive weakness began in his thirties and who developed severe respiratory failure at 66 years, with no clear cores on biopsy. This extends the mild variant to adult onset. In a summary of reported patients, they also found that onset was significantly later when at least 1 allele carried a missense mutation than with biallelic truncating mutations.

*clinical_features*: Fujii et al. (2022) reported a man with progressive weakness that began in his thirties. He developed severe respiratory failure at age 66 years. Muscle biopsy showed no clear cores.

*molecular_genetics*: In a man with adult-onset progressive myopathy and respiratory failure, Fujii et al. (2022) identified a novel mutation in the MEGF10 gene, gly739-to-arg (G739R).

*genotype_phenotype_correlations*: Fujii et al. (2022) summarized their patient together with previously reported patients with MEGF10 mutations. Patients with a missense mutation in at least 1 allele had significantly later onset than patients with biallelic truncating mutations. The authors also noted that some reported patients with the mild variant phenotype had no clear cores.

## [MEDIUM] AlMuhaizea et al. (2021) — new_variants, phenotype_expansion, functional_evidence, genotype_phenotype_correlation

AlMuhaizea M, Dabbagh O, AlQudairy H, AlHargan A, Alotaibi W, Sami R, AlOtaibi R, Ali MM, AlHindi H, Colak D, and others. Phenotypic Variability of MEGF10 Variants Causing Congenital Myopathy: Report of Two Unrelated Patients from a Highly Consanguineous Population. Genes (Basel) 12, 2021. [PubMed: 34828389] *(read: fulltext)*

**Why:** The paper adds a new homozygous MEGF10 splice-site mutation, c.2980+5G>C, found in a consanguineous Saudi family with the mild phenotype. This variant is not in the variant table, and RT-PCR showed that it causes retention of 85 bp of intron 23. The paper also widens the mild phenotype: it adds areflexia, spinal rigidity, mild oral dysphagia, normal CK, neurogenic EMG, and an affected sister who died at 18 months.

*clinical_features*: AlMuhaizea et al. (2021) reported a boy (patient 2) with a mild form of MEGF10-related congenital myopathy. His parents were first cousins in a consanguineous Saudi family. Fetal movements had been decreased during the pregnancy, and he had generalized hypotonia from the newborn period. He had feeding difficulties, choking, recurrent aspiration pneumonia, and motor delay. He sat at 21 months, and at age 9 years he walked independently, though he ran slowly. He had no major cognitive deficits and did not need long-term oxygen. On examination he had plagiocephaly, facial diplegia, a high-arched palate, low-set ears, and mild thoracic scoliosis with spinal rigidity. He also had axial-predominant hypotonia, proximal weakness, a positive Gowers sign, areflexia, and joint hyperlaxity. Creatine kinase and nerve conduction were normal. EMG showed neurogenic changes, and a modified barium study showed mild oral dysphagia. A sister with a similar phenotype died at 18 months of respiratory infections.

*molecular_genetics*: In a boy from a consanguineous Saudi family who had the mild phenotype, AlMuhaizea et al. (2021) used a muscular dystrophy and myopathy gene panel to identify a homozygous splice-site mutation in the MEGF10 gene (c.2980+5G-C, NM_032446.2). Sanger sequencing confirmed the mutation. RT-PCR on RNA from the patient's lymphoblastoid cells showed a larger, aberrant transcript, and sequencing showed that it retained 85 bp of intron 23. The panel also detected a heterozygous variant in the PRKAG3 gene (c.364dupA, p.Thr122Asnfs*11).

*molecular_genetics*: MEGF10, IVS23DS, G-C, +5
In a boy (patient 2) from a consanguineous Saudi family who had a mild, ambulant form of congenital myopathy, AlMuhaizea et al. (2021) identified a homozygous c.2980+5G-C transversion in intron 23 of the MEGF10 gene (NM_032446.2). The mutation was found by gene panel sequencing and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. RT-PCR of RNA from the patient's lymphoblastoid cells showed an aberrant transcript that retained 85 bp of intron 23.

*genotype_phenotype_correlations*: AlMuhaizea et al. (2021) compared 2 unrelated consanguineous Saudi patients carrying different homozygous MEGF10 variants. A homozygous frameshift variant (c.3132dupA, p.Glu1045Argfs*22) was associated with a severe phenotype and death from respiratory failure at 8 months. In contrast, the homozygous splice-site variant c.2980+5G-C was associated with a milder, ambulant phenotype that resembled an axial-predominant myopathy with scoliosis and spinal rigidity.

## [MEDIUM] Li et al. (2021) — animal_model, functional_evidence

Li C, Vargas-Franco D, Saha M, Davis RM, Manko KA, Draper I, Pacak CA, Kang PB. Megf10 deficiency impairs skeletal muscle stem cell migration and muscle regeneration. FEBS Open Bio 11: 114-123, 2021. [PubMed: 33159715] *(read: fulltext)*

**Why:** The entry's only animal model is the zebrafish morphant. Li et al. (2021) add the first Megf10-null mouse data, showing impaired satellite cell activation and migration and reduced regeneration with fibrosis after repeated injury. This is mechanistic support for the satellite cell hypothesis already in the entry.

*animal_model*: Li et al. (2021) studied Megf10-null mice. In single myofibers isolated from extensor digitorum longus muscles and cultured for 24, 48, and 72 hours, fewer satellite cells detached from Megf10-null myofibers than from wildtype myofibers. Fewer Pax7+/MyoD+ activated satellite cells were associated with Megf10-null myofibers, and these cells migrated shorter distances. Satellite cells that detached from Megf10-null myofibers also had fewer MyoD-positive nuclei. The authors concluded that Megf10 contributes to an early stage of satellite cell activation, during which the cells migrate to target myofibers. To deplete the satellite cell pool, Li et al. (2021) gave 3 sequential intramuscular barium chloride injections into the tibialis anterior muscle, starting at 2 months of age. Compared with wildtype muscle, injured Megf10-null muscle showed significantly reduced regeneration, with pockets of atrophic fibers, clusters of small myofibers with internalized nuclei, greater variability in fiber size, and more fibrosis on fibronectin staining.

*pathogenesis* ⚠︎: Li et al. (2021) suggested that impaired satellite cell function and impaired muscle regeneration caused by MEGF10 deficiency could help explain why the clinical manifestations of MEGF10 myopathy persist. They noted that muscle function generally does not improve in affected patients.

## [MEDIUM] Saha et al. (2019) — animal_model, treatment_or_management, functional_evidence

Saha M, Rizzo SA, Ramanathan M, Hightower RM, Santostefano KE, Terada N, Finkel RS, Berg JS, Chahin N, Pacak CA, and others. Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors ameliorate MEGF10 myopathy. Hum Mol Genet 28: 2365-2377, 2019. [PubMed: 31267131] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** Saha et al. (2019) add the first therapeutic evidence: SSRIs, especially sertraline, relieved abnormalities in several MEGF10-deficient models. Those models are Megf10 -/- mouse myoblasts, myoblasts derived from patient iPSCs, Drosophila deficient in Drpr, and megf10 mutant zebrafish, and sertraline also restored Notch1 deficiencies. The entry has no treatment data, and its only animal model is the zebrafish morpholino knockdown.

*animal_model*: Saha et al. (2019) ran a small-molecule screen in Megf10-deficient myoblasts and identified 2 selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs), sertraline and escitalopram. They followed up in Megf10 -/- mouse myoblasts, in myoblasts derived from induced pluripotent stem cells of patients with MEGF10 myopathy, in Drosophila deficient in the MEGF10 homolog Drpr, and in megf10 mutant zebrafish. Sertraline was highly effective at relieving abnormalities in all of these models and restored Notch1 deficiencies. The authors concluded that SSRIs, especially sertraline, show promise as therapies for MEGF10 myopathy, and they suggested that the mechanism may involve the Notch pathway.

## [MEDIUM] Harris et al. (2018) — new_variants, phenotype_expansion, genotype_phenotype_correlation, natural_history

Harris E, Marini-Bettolo C, Töpf A, Barresi R, Polvikoski T, Bailey G, Charlton R, Tellez J, MacArthur D, Guglieri M, and others. MEGF10 related myopathies: A new case with adult onset disease with prominent respiratory failure and review of reported phenotypes. Neuromuscul Disord 28: 48-53, 2018. [PubMed: 29128256] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** Harris et al. (2018) reported a patient whose disease began in adulthood (fifth decade), with distal as well as proximal weakness, dysphagia, respiratory failure needing ventilation while still ambulant, and aggregation of myofibrillar proteins on biopsy. The patient carried two MEGF10 variants not in the variant table, c.1426+1G>T and c.352T>C (p.C118R). The authors also reviewed 20 reported cases and linked age of respiratory onset to null versus missense genotype.

*clinical_features*: Harris et al. (2018) reported a 66-year-old man with a 20-year history of progressive proximal and distal weakness of the upper and lower limbs, facial weakness, and dysphagia. In his 50s, while still ambulant, he developed respiratory failure that required ventilation. Muscle biopsy showed myopathic changes with aggregation of myofibrillar proteins. The authors stated that this case expanded the phenotype of MEGF10-related myopathy to include onset in the fifth decade.

*molecular_genetics* ⚠︎: In a 66-year-old man with adult-onset MEGF10-related myopathy, Harris et al. (2018) used exome sequencing to identify 2 novel variants in the MEGF10 gene: an essential splice-site variant (c.1426+1G-T) and a missense variant, c.352T-C, resulting in a cys118-to-arg (C118R) substitution. The authors regarded these as recessive MEGF10 mutations.

*molecular_genetics*: MEGF10, 1426+1G-T
In a 66-year-old man with adult-onset MEGF10-related myopathy, Harris et al. (2018) used exome sequencing to identify a novel essential splice-site variant in the MEGF10 gene (c.1426+1G-T). It was found together with a missense mutation (C118R).

*molecular_genetics*: MEGF10, CYS118ARG
In a 66-year-old man with adult-onset MEGF10-related myopathy, Harris et al. (2018) used exome sequencing to identify a novel c.352T-C transition in the MEGF10 gene, resulting in a cys118-to-arg (C118R) substitution. It was the second mutation found alongside the c.1426+1G-T splice-site variant.

*genotype_phenotype_correlations*: Harris et al. (2018) reviewed all 20 reported cases with MEGF10 mutations and confirmed a genotype-phenotype correlation. In patients with at least 1 null mutation, respiratory dysfunction began in the first year of life. In patients with 2 missense mutations, respiratory dysfunction began at about 10 years of age or much later. Their own patient carried an essential splice-site variant together with a missense variant, and his respiratory failure began in his 50s.

## [LOW] Draper et al. (2019) — animal_model

Draper I, Saha M, Stonebreaker H, Salomon RN, Matin B, Kang PB. The impact of Megf10/Drpr gain-of-function on muscle development in Drosophila. FEBS Lett 593: 680-696, 2019. [PubMed: 30802937] *(read: fulltext)*

**Why:** Draper et al. (2019) report a Drosophila gain-of-function (overexpression) model of mouse Megf10 and its fly homolog Draper. Overexpression caused lethality through somatic muscle, at late stages of myogenesis, and selectively damaged tubular muscle. It also produced wing phenotypes resembling reduced Notch signaling. The disorder is recessive loss-of-function, so this overexpression model bears only indirectly on its mechanism and belongs more in the gene entry. It is a minor addition to the existing zebrafish morpholino data.

*animal_model* ⚠︎: Draper et al. (2019) studied gain of function of Megf10 in Drosophila. Ubiquitous overexpression of mouse Megf10 or of Drosophila Draper (Drpr) isoforms I, II, or III caused a nearly universal failure to eclose. Flies developed normally until metamorphosis, then arrested, and many died at the dark pupa/pharate adult stage. The few escapers had abnormal legs and wings, including darkened tibia/tarsus junctions and absent tarsal segments, which were reminiscent of Drpr-deficient flies. Muscle-targeted overexpression with the How-Gal4 driver reproduced the lethality. Expression in tendon, heart, glia, neurons, motor neurons, intestinal tract/malpighian tubules, and fat body was well tolerated. Overexpression was tolerated in adult muscle precursors, muscle progenitor and founder cells, terminally differentiated fibers, and mature adult muscle. It was lethal with the Mef2-Gal4 (differentiation) and apterous-Gal4 (muscle identity) drivers. The authors concluded that overexpression is harmful at stages of myogenesis later than the stage sensitive to Drpr deficiency. The effect was muscle subtype selective. Drivers marking precursors of tubular muscles (apterous-Gal4, cut-Gal4) caused lethality, whereas a driver marking precursors of fibrillar indirect flight muscles (vestigial-Gal4) yielded viable adults. Draper et al. (2019) examined 12 escaper Wg-Gal4>UAS-drpr-III adults at 2 days of age. In 8 of the 12, the tubular jump muscle fibers showed enlargement, hyalinization, abnormal localization or loss of nuclei, loss of striations, and disrupted bundle organization. Fibrillar indirect flight muscle was spared, and none of 15 control flies had abnormalities. Overexpression in Notch-positive or Wingless-positive wing disc cells caused arrest at the dark pupal/pharate adult stage. Overexpression in cells expressing Serrate produced a split-vein phenotype at the wing margin that resembled decreased Notch signaling. This phenotype was fully penetrant with the short isoforms Drpr-II and Drpr-III and about 50% penetrant with Drpr-I. Drpr-II also caused a held-out wing phenotype and markedly reduced locomotor activity.

*pathogenesis*: Draper et al. (2019) postulated that at least one conserved extracellular domain mediates the lethality of overexpression by compromising normal interactions with protein partners. The candidate domains were those present in the shortest isoform, Drpr-III: the EMI domain, a DSL (Delta/Serrate/LAG-2)-like domain, or the 4 EGF-like domains. They also suggested that Megf10/Drpr and Notch may interact to regulate myogenesis. Among the conserved C-terminal tyrosine residues, they noted that MEGF10 Y1030 appeared most closely associated with EMARDD, because defective phosphorylation at this residue had been linked to pathogenic mutations.

# Proposed updates for OMIM 616326: MYASTHENIC SYNDROME, CONGENITAL, 11, ASSOCIATED WITH ACETYLCHOLINE RECEPTOR DEFICIENCY

Literature from 2010 onwards that the entry does not cite: 35 papers screened, 6 read, 6 proposal(s). Each proposal is a draft for a curator to accept, edit or reject.

**Back-test as of 2010.** OMIM's curators later added 3 reference(s) from that year on; this run proposed 2 of them (recall 0.667), and 4 paper(s) OMIM does not cite.

## [MEDIUM] Leshinsky-Silver et al. (2012) — new_variants

Leshinsky-Silver E, Shapira D, Yosovitz K, Ginsberg M, Lerman-Sagie T, Lev D. A novel mutation in the TPR6 domain of the RAPSN gene associated with congenital myasthenic syndrome. J Neurol Sci 316: 112-5, 2012. [PubMed: 22326364] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** The paper reports a new RAPSN mutation not in the variant table: an insertion of a threonine at residue 224 (p.224insT) in the TPR6 domain, which the authors called the first mutation reported in that domain. It was found in compound heterozygosity with the known -38A-G founder promoter mutation in 2 Persian Jewish sibs. The clinical and founder-mutation findings repeat what the entry already records.

*molecular_genetics* ⚠︎: Leshinsky-Silver et al. (2012) studied a Persian Jewish family in which 2 sibs had typical congenital myasthenic syndrome. In both sibs, they identified compound heterozygous mutations in the RAPSN gene. One was the common founder -38A-G mutation in the E-box of the RAPSN promoter, which impairs promoter transcriptional activity. The other was a previously unreported insertion (p.224insT) that resulted in insertion of a threonine in the TPR6 domain of rapsyn. The authors stated that this was the first mutation reported in the TPR6 domain. They suggested that it might support a role for this domain in rapsyn self-association and in co-clustering with AChR in the postsynaptic membrane.

## [MEDIUM] Park et al. (2012) — animal_model, functional_evidence

Park JY, Ikeda H, Ikenaga T, Ono F. Acetylcholine receptors enable the transport of rapsyn from the Golgi complex to the plasma membrane. J Neurosci 32: 7356-63, 2012. [PubMed: 22623681] *(read: fulltext)*

**Why:** This is the first animal-model evidence for the entry. In the zebrafish rapsyn mutant 'twitch once' (G130E in the fourth TPR domain), AChRs reached the plasma membrane but did not cluster at synapses, and the mutant rapsyn was held in the Golgi complex. The paper also shows that rapsyn needs muscle-type AChRs to leave the Golgi, but AChRs do not need rapsyn to reach the membrane. No new human variants or clinical features are reported.

*animal_model*: Park et al. (2012) studied the zebrafish rapsyn mutant 'twitch once', which carries a gly130-to-glu (G130E) missense mutation in the fourth tetratricopeptide repeat (TPR) domain of rapsyn. In mutant larvae, AChRs reached the muscle plasma membrane but were distributed nonsynaptically rather than localized to synapses. Immunostaining showed that the G130E mutant rapsyn was retained in the Golgi complex, separate from the AChRs. The authors suggested that the mutation abolished the interaction between rapsyn and AChR.

*pathogenesis*: Park et al. (2012) studied the AChR-deficient zebrafish mutant 'sofa potato', which carries an L28P mutation in the AChR delta subunit, so that AChR subunits are retained in the endoplasmic reticulum. In this mutant, endogenous rapsyn was retained in the Golgi complex and did not form synaptic clusters. Expressing wildtype muscle-type AChR delta subunit restored rapsyn localization to the plasma membrane together with AChR, whereas the neuronal alpha-7 AChR did not. The authors concluded that rapsyn requires muscle-type AChRs to exit the Golgi complex, whereas AChRs do not require rapsyn to reach the plasma membrane; rapsyn acts only after AChRs arrive at the membrane. When wildtype rapsyn was overexpressed in sofa potato muscle cells, it reached the plasma membrane without AChRs. The authors noted that the distribution of rapsyn depends strongly on how much is expressed, which complicates the interpretation of rapsyn mutagenesis studies.

## [MEDIUM] Gaudon et al. (2010) — new_variants, diagnostic_method

Gaudon K, Pénisson-Besnier I, Chabrol B, Bouhour F, Demay L, Ben Ammar A, Bauché S, Vial C, Nicolas G, Eymard B, and others. Multiexon deletions account for 15% of congenital myasthenic syndromes with RAPSN mutations after negative DNA sequencing. J Med Genet 47: 795-6, 2010. [PubMed: 20930056] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** The entry lists only point mutations, a small insertion and promoter mutations in RAPSN. Gaudon et al. (2010) add a new mutation class: 3 novel multiexon RAPSN deletions that direct sequencing had missed. They report that these rearrangements accounted for 15% of their RAPSN-mutated CMS patients and recommend gene-dosage testing.

*molecular_genetics*: Gaudon et al. (2010) studied 3 patients with a characteristic phenotype of recessive congenital myasthenic syndrome who had mutations in the RAPSN gene. Family analysis showed that direct sequencing had failed to detect one of the mutant alleles. Allelic quantification of the patients' DNA identified 3 novel multiexon deletions in RAPSN.

*diagnosis*: Gaudon et al. (2010) found that genomic rearrangements accounted for 15% of their CMS patients with RAPSN mutations. They recommended using SNP markers and a gene dosage method alongside direct DNA sequencing, particularly when genetic counseling is at issue.

## [MEDIUM] Brugnoni et al. (2010) — new_variants

Brugnoni R, Maggi L, Canioni E, Moroni I, Pantaleoni C, D'Arrigo S, Riva D, Cornelio F, Bernasconi P, Mantegazza R. Identification of previously unreported mutations in CHRNA1, CHRNE and RAPSN genes in three unrelated Italian patients with congenital myasthenic syndromes. J Neurol 257: 1119-23, 2010. [PubMed: 20157724] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** Brugnoni et al. (2010) report a previously unreported RAPSN splice-site mutation, IVS1+2T>G, in compound heterozygosity with N88K in an Italian patient; IVS1+2T>G is absent from the entry's variant table. The paper also adds an unaffected heterozygous carrier relative, consistent with recessive inheritance, and a response to cholinesterase inhibitors, which is already recorded in the entry.

*molecular_genetics*: Brugnoni et al. (2010) studied 3 unrelated Italian patients with congenital myasthenic syndrome, all of whom benefited from cholinesterase inhibitors. In 1 patient, they identified compound heterozygous mutations in the RAPSN gene: the common N88K mutation and a previously unreported splice site mutation (IVS1+2T-G). Relatives who carried only one mutant allele had no symptoms, consistent with recessive inheritance. The authors suggested that the newly reported mutation likely reduces the number of AChRs at the motor endplate.

## [LOW] Das et al. (2014) — diagnostic_method, treatment_or_management

Das AS, Agamanolis DP, Cohen BH. Use of next-generation sequencing as a diagnostic tool for congenital myasthenic syndrome. Pediatr Neurol 51: 717-20, 2014. [PubMed: 25194721] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** This is a single case report. It adds a diagnosis made by whole-exome sequencing after a nondiagnostic workup that included a microarray, and it documents a dramatic response to pyridostigmine started at 16 months, with contractures resolving and motor milestones gained. The abstract does not name the two RAPSN variants, so no new variants can be added. The clinical features largely repeat the early-onset form already recorded: contractures, facial deformity and respiratory crises.

*clinical_features*: Das et al. (2014) reported a 20-month-old boy with rapsyn deficiency who had a weak cry, hypotonia, joint contractures, and facial deformity at birth. Because of respiratory difficulty associated with muscle fatigue, he spent 71 days in the neonatal intensive care unit and 47 days in the pediatric intensive care unit. Imaging, metabolic, and electrodiagnostic studies were normal. A muscle biopsy showed myofiber atrophy in a pattern consistent with early denervation.

*molecular_genetics*: In a boy with CMS11, Das et al. (2014) identified 2 confirmed pathogenic mutations in the RAPSN gene by whole-exome sequencing. Earlier investigations, including an oligonucleotide microarray, had given nonspecific and nondiagnostic results.

*clinical_management*: Das et al. (2014) started pyridostigmine at 16 months of age in a boy with RAPSN-related CMS11. Treatment led to a dramatic improvement in muscle tone and strength and a steady resolution of joint contractures. Four months later he was beginning to bear weight, could sit unsupported, and could vocalize full words.

## [LOW] Alseth et al. (2011) — population_data, diagnostic_method

Alseth EH, Maniaol AH, Elsais A, Nakkestad HL, Tallaksen C, Gilhus NE, Skeie GO. Investigation for RAPSN and DOK-7 mutations in a cohort of seronegative myasthenia gravis patients. Muscle Nerve 43: 574-7, 2011. [PubMed: 21305573] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** No new variants or features: N88K is already listed, and Burke et al. (2003) already noted the confusion with seronegative myasthenia gravis. What is new is a nationwide Norwegian cohort of 74 SNMG patients in which 1 was homozygous and 2 were heterozygous for RAPSN N88K, together with a specific recommendation to test atypical and pediatric SNMG cases for N88K before immunosuppression or thymectomy.

*molecular_genetics* ⚠︎: Alseth et al. (2011) sequenced the RAPSN and DOK7 genes in all 74 Norwegian patients with seronegative myasthenia gravis (SNMG) and in 37 healthy controls, looking for the RAPSN N88K mutation and the DOK7 c.1124_1127dupTGCC mutation. One patient was homozygous for the N88K mutation and 2 were heterozygous for it. No DOK7 mutations were found.

*diagnosis*: Alseth et al. (2011) concluded that rapsyn-related congenital myasthenic syndrome can be mistaken for seronegative myasthenia gravis, although it was rare in their nationwide Norwegian SNMG cohort. They recommended that SNMG patients with an atypical clinical presentation, as well as pediatric cases, be tested for the RAPSN N88K mutation before immunosuppressive drugs are started or thymectomy is performed.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital myasthenic syndrome-11 associated with acetylcholine receptor deficiency (CMS11) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding rapsyn (RAPSN; 601592) on chromosome 11p11.2.

### Description

Congenital myasthenic syndrome-11 (CMS11) is an autosomal recessive postsynaptic disorder of the neuromuscular junction in which acetylcholine receptors (AChR) are deficient at the endplate. Rapsyn plays an essential role in clustering and anchoring AChR at the postsynaptic membrane, and the RAPSN mutations reduce coclustering of AChR with rapsyn (Ohno et al., 2002). Engel et al. (2015) noted that most patients present in the first year of life and a few present later. Burke et al. (2003) distinguished an early-onset phenotype, frequently associated with arthrogryposis multiplex congenita and life-threatening crises, from a late-onset phenotype that develops in adolescence or adulthood. Hypotonia and respiratory difficulty at birth were reported by Das et al. (2014). Most patients respond well to acetylcholinesterase inhibitors, and some derive additional benefit from 3,4-diaminopyridine (Engel et al., 2015). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Ohno et al. (2002) described 4 patients with congenital myasthenic syndrome and endplate AChR deficiency who had no mutations in the AChR subunit genes.

Burke et al. (2003) described 2 distinct phenotypes among patients with rapsyn mutations, early-onset and late-onset. The early-onset phenotype was frequently associated with arthrogryposis multiplex congenita and life-threatening crises. The late-onset phenotype developed in adolescence or adulthood and was initially mistaken for seronegative myasthenia gravis.

Ohno et al. (2003) described Oriental Jewish patients of Iraqi or Iranian origin who had a myasthenic syndrome caused by a mutation in the RAPSN promoter, along with facial malformations.

### Inheritance

CMS11 is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. Ohno et al. (2002) identified recessive RAPSN mutations in affected individuals, who carried them in the homozygous or compound heterozygous state.

### Molecular Genetics

In 4 patients with congenital myasthenic syndrome and endplate AChR deficiency, Ohno et al. (2002) identified mutations in the RAPSN gene. All of the patients carried the asn88-to-lys (N88K) mutation, either in the homozygous state or in compound heterozygosity with a second RAPSN mutation. In expression studies, the mutant rapsyn was less able to cocluster AChR.

In Oriental Jewish patients of Iraqi or Iranian origin with CMS and facial malformations, Ohno et al. (2003) identified a -38A-G mutation in the RAPSN promoter. The mutation lies within an E-box element. In luciferase reporter assays, it attenuated reporter gene expression in C2C12 myotubes.

Muller et al. (2004) concluded that the RAPSN N88K mutation derives from an ancient Indo-European founder.

### Clinical Management

Engel et al. (2015) noted that most patients respond well to acetylcholinesterase inhibitors and that some derive additional benefit from 3,4-diaminopyridine.

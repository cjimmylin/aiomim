### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that annular epidermolytic ichthyosis-1 (AEI1), also called cyclic ichthyosis with epidermolytic hyperkeratosis, is caused by heterozygous mutation in the keratin-1 gene (KRT1; 139350) on chromosome 12q13. ✗

### Description

Annular epidermolytic ichthyosis is a clinical variant of epidermolytic ichthyosis (bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma), a keratinopathic ichthyosis. Affected individuals have blistering and erythroderma in early life. Later they develop episodes of annular and polycyclic, erythematous, scaly plaques, mainly on the trunk and proximal limbs, which flare and then fade. Palmoplantar hyperkeratosis may be present. Skin biopsy shows epidermolytic hyperkeratosis. ✗

### Clinical Features

Sahn et al. (1992) described annular epidermolytic ichthyosis as a distinct phenotype. Patients had bullous erythroderma in infancy. Later they had recurrent annular, polycyclic, erythematous scaly plaques on the trunk and extremities, which came and went over weeks to months. Histology showed epidermolytic hyperkeratosis. ✗

Sybert et al. (1999) studied families with a similar episodic phenotype, which they called cyclic ichthyosis with epidermolytic hyperkeratosis. Affected individuals had epidermolytic hyperkeratosis together with intermittent flares of polycyclic erythematous scaly plaques. Some also had palmoplantar keratoderma. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Annular epidermolytic ichthyosis follows an autosomal dominant pattern of inheritance, consistent with the heterozygous keratin mutations found in affected families (Sybert et al., 1999). ✗

### Heterogeneity

Annular epidermolytic ichthyosis is genetically heterogeneous. Mutation in the keratin-10 gene (KRT10) has also been reported in this phenotype (Joh et al., 1997). That form is designated AEI2. ✗

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of families with cyclic ichthyosis with epidermolytic hyperkeratosis, Sybert et al. (1999) found heterozygous mutations in the 2B domain of keratin-1 (KRT1). The more severe classic epidermolytic ichthyosis is usually caused by mutations in the highly conserved helix boundary motifs. Sybert et al. (1999) proposed that mutations in the 2B domain lead to this milder, episodic phenotype. ✗

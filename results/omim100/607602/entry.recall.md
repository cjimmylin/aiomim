### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that annular epidermolytic ichthyosis-1 (AEI1) is caused by dominant mutation in the keratin-1 gene (KRT1; 139350) on chromosome 12q13.13. ⚠︎

### Description

Annular epidermolytic ichthyosis is a rare phenotypic variant of epidermolytic ichthyosis, also known as congenital bullous ichthyosiform erythroderma (Mikilita et al., 2020). Blistering lesions may appear in infancy (Sahn et al., 1992). Affected individuals have recurrent outbreaks of annular, polycyclic, erythematous, scaly plaques on the trunk and proximal extremities, and some also have palmoplantar keratoderma (Mikilita et al., 2020). The plaques enlarge slowly and then resolve, and on histopathologic examination they show epidermolytic hyperkeratosis (Sahn et al., 1992).

### Clinical Features

Sahn et al. (1992) described a kindred that they suggested might represent a new phenotype of epidermolytic ichthyosis. The proband developed bullous and ichthyosiform skin lesions at the age of 8 months. At the age of 27 years, she developed numerous annular and polycyclic, erythematous, hyperkeratotic plaques on the trunk and extremities, which enlarged slowly and then resolved. Histopathologic examination of these annular plaques revealed epidermolytic hyperkeratosis.

Mikilita et al. (2020) listed one family with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis reported by Sybert et al. (1999), in which affected individuals had intermittent erythematous plaques. Palmoplantar involvement was reported in some of these individuals.

### Inheritance

Mikilita et al. (2020) noted that annular epidermolytic ichthyosis results from dominant mutations in keratin genes, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Heterogeneity

Joh et al. (1997) reported a mutation in the keratin-10 gene (KRT10) in annular epidermolytic ichthyosis.

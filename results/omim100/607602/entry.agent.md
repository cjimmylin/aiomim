### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that annular epidermolytic ichthyosis-1 (AEI1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding keratin-10 (KRT10). ⚠︎

### Description

Annular epidermolytic ichthyosis is a rare phenotypic variant of epidermolytic ichthyosis (bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma). Affected individuals may have bullous ichthyosis in early life and hyperkeratotic plaques in flexural and extensor areas, and characteristically have recurrent bouts of annular, polycyclic, erythematous, scaly plaques on the trunk and proximal extremities; palmoplantar keratoderma may be present. Histologic examination shows epidermolytic hyperkeratosis (Joh et al., 1997; Suga et al., 1998; Mikilita et al., 2020).

### Nomenclature

Sahn et al. (1992) suggested that the kindred they described might represent a new phenotype of epidermolytic (acanthokeratolytic) ichthyosis and termed it annular epidermolytic ichthyosis. On the basis of the finding of a KRT10 mutation, Joh et al. (1997) concluded that annular epidermolytic ichthyosis should be considered a variant of bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma.

### Clinical Features

Sahn et al. (1992) reported a 30-year-old white woman who developed bullous and ichthyosiform skin lesions at age 8 months. Blistering ceased at puberty, but hyperkeratotic plaques persisted. At age 27 years she developed numerous annular and polycyclic, erythematous, hyperkeratotic plaques on the trunk and extremities that were pruritic, enlarged slowly, and then resolved. Histopathologic examination of the annular plaques showed epidermolytic hyperkeratosis, and electron microscopy showed interrupted perinuclear tonofilament rings. Two of her 5 children were affected with a bullous type of ichthyosis.

Joh et al. (1997) reported a second family with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis, with 2 affected generations. The proband had bullous ichthyosis and bouts of disease activity associated with numerous annular and polycyclic erythematous, hyperkeratotic plaques on the trunk and proximal extremities. Histologic examination showed the typical pathology of epidermolytic hyperkeratosis, and ultrastructural analysis showed abnormal keratin filament networks and tonofilament clumping with a perinuclear distribution.

Suga et al. (1998) described a third kindred with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis. They noted that individuals with this variant present with bullous ichthyosis in early childhood and hyperkeratotic lichenified plaques in the flexural areas and extensor surfaces at later ages, and characteristically develop intermittent bouts of annular and polycyclic, erythematous, scaly plaques on the trunk and proximal extremities.

Mikilita et al. (2020) reported an 8-year-old girl with diffuse dermatosis beginning in the first months of life. She had erythematous, scaly, hyperkeratotic plaques with prominent borders on the mesogastrium, cubital and popliteal fossae, and inguinal and cervical regions, as well as palmoplantar hyperkeratosis and yellowish hyperkeratotic plaques on the scalp and nasal introitus; hair, nails, and mucosa were unaffected. There was no family history of similar cases and no consanguinity. One month later the plaques had changed in appearance and extent, and the polycyclic aspect had disappeared. Skin biopsy showed acanthosis, papillomatosis, and hyperkeratosis with marked epidermolysis in the granular layer. The initial diagnostic hypothesis had been erythrokeratodermia variabilis et progressiva. In a review of the literature, the authors found 19 reported cases in 10 publications.

### Inheritance

Joh et al. (1997) stated that the pattern of inheritance of annular epidermolytic ichthyosis is consistent with autosomal dominant transmission. In the kindred reported by Sahn et al. (1992), an affected woman had 2 affected children, and the family reported by Joh et al. (1997) had 2 affected generations.

### Heterogeneity

Mikilita et al. (2020) stated that annular epidermolytic ichthyosis results from dominant mutations in the keratin-1 (KRT1) and keratin-10 (KRT10) genes.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of a family with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis, Joh et al. (1997) identified a tandem CG-to-GA 2-bp mutation on the same allele of the KRT10 gene, resulting in an arg83-to-glu (R83E) substitution in the 2B helical segment.

In a third kindred with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis, Suga et al. (1998) identified a mutation in the KRT10 gene resulting in an ile-to-thr substitution at residue 107 (codon 446) within the highly conserved helix termination motif at the end of the rod domain.

### Diagnosis

Mikilita et al. (2020) noted that the main differential diagnosis of annular epidermolytic ichthyosis is erythrokeratodermia variabilis et progressiva (EKVP), distinctive features of which include onset during childhood, absence of epidermal fragility, and histology without evidence of epidermolysis.

### Clinical Management

In their literature review, Mikilita et al. (2020) noted that topical treatments, including retinoids, corticosteroids, propylene glycol, calcipotriene, and keratolytic agents, produced little response, whereas 3 articles reported good response to systemic retinoids (acitretin in 2 and low-dose isotretinoin in 1).

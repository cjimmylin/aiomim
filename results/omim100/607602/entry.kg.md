A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that annular epidermolytic ichthyosis-1 (AEI1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the KRT10 gene (148080) on chromosome 17q21.2. ⚠︎

### Description

Annular epidermolytic ichthyosis is a phenotypic variant of bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma. Affected individuals present with bullous ichthyosis in early childhood and hyperkeratotic lichenified plaques in flexural areas and extensor surfaces at later ages. They characteristically develop intermittent bouts of annular and polycyclic, erythematous, scaly plaques on the trunk and proximal extremities (Suga et al., 1998). Histology shows epidermolytic hyperkeratosis (Sahn et al., 1992; Joh et al., 1997).

### Clinical Features

Sahn et al. (1992) described a 30-year-old white woman whose bullous and ichthyosiform skin lesions began at age 8 months. The blistering stopped at puberty, but the hyperkeratotic plaques remained. At age 27 years she developed many annular and polycyclic, erythematous, hyperkeratotic plaques on the trunk and extremities. These plaques itched, grew slowly, and then resolved. Histopathologic examination of the annular plaques showed epidermolytic hyperkeratosis. Electron microscopy showed interrupted perinuclear tonofilament rings, which the authors considered consistent with either bullous ichthyotic erythroderma of Brocq or ichthyosis bullosa of Siemens. Two of her 5 children had a bullous type of ichthyosis. Sahn et al. (1992) suggested that the kindred might represent a new phenotype of epidermolytic (acanthokeratolytic) ichthyosis.

Joh et al. (1997) reported a family with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis in which 2 generations were affected. The proband had bullous ichthyosis with episodes of disease activity. During these episodes many annular and polycyclic erythematous, hyperkeratotic plaques appeared on the trunk and proximal extremities. Histology showed the typical pathology of epidermolytic hyperkeratosis. On ultrastructural analysis, keratin filament networks were abnormal and tonofilaments formed clumps around the nucleus.

Suga et al. (1998) described a third kindred with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis. Affected individuals presented with bullous ichthyosis in early childhood and hyperkeratotic lichenified plaques in flexural areas and extensor surfaces at later ages. They also developed intermittent bouts of annular and polycyclic, erythematous, scaly plaques on the trunk and proximal extremities.

Mikilita et al. (2020) reported an 8-year-old Brazilian girl with a diffuse skin disorder that began in the first months of life. There was no family history and no consanguinity. She had erythematous, scaly, hyperkeratotic plaques with prominent borders on the mesogastrium, cubital and popliteal fossae, and inguinal and cervical regions. She also had palmoplantar hyperkeratosis and yellowish hyperkeratotic plaques on the scalp and nasal introitus. Her hair, nails, and mucosa were unaffected. One month later the affected areas had grown larger but were no longer polycyclic, showing that the plaques were migratory. A skin biopsy from the forearm showed acanthosis, papillomatosis, and hyperkeratosis with marked epidermolysis of the granular layer. The authors first suspected erythrokeratodermia variabilis et progressiva, but the epidermolytic hyperkeratosis led them to diagnose annular epidermolytic ichthyosis. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Sahn et al. (1992) reported an affected woman with 2 affected children among 5, which is consistent with vertical transmission. Joh et al. (1997) stated that the way annular epidermolytic ichthyosis is inherited fits an autosomal dominant pattern. The family they reported had affected members in 2 generations.

### Molecular Genetics

In the affected members of a family with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis, Joh et al. (1997) identified a novel 2-bp mutation in the KRT10 gene. The mutation was a tandem CG-to-GA change on a single allele and resulted in an arg83-to-glu (R83E) substitution in the 2B helical segment. Based on this finding, the authors concluded that annular epidermolytic ichthyosis should be considered a variant of bullous congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma.

In a kindred with annular epidermolytic ichthyosis, Suga et al. (1998) identified a novel mutation in the KRT10 gene that resulted in an ile107-to-thr (I107T) substitution (codon 446). The substitution lay within the highly conserved helix termination motif at the end of the rod domain of keratin-10.

### Diagnosis

Mikilita et al. (2020) identified erythrokeratodermia variabilis et progressiva (EKVP) as the main differential diagnosis of annular epidermolytic ichthyosis. They listed onset during childhood, absence of epidermal fragility, and histology without epidermolysis as features that distinguish EKVP.

### Clinical Management

Mikilita et al. (2020) reviewed 19 previously reported cases from 10 publications. Topical treatments produced little response, including retinoids, corticosteroids, propylene glycol, calcipotriene, and keratolytics. Three articles reported a good response to systemic retinoids: 2 with acitretin and 1 with low-dose isotretinoin.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that retinitis pigmentosa-7 (RP7) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the peripherin-2 gene (PRPH2; 179605) on chromosome 6p21. Digenic retinitis pigmentosa is caused by double heterozygosity for mutations in PRPH2 and in the ROM1 gene (180721) on chromosome 11q12. ⚠︎

### Description

Retinitis pigmentosa-7 is a form of autosomal dominant retinitis pigmentosa, a progressive degeneration of the retinal photoreceptors that typically begins with night blindness and loss of peripheral vision and goes on to loss of central vision. PRPH2 encodes peripherin/RDS, a structural protein of the rims of photoreceptor outer segment discs. A digenic form of RP has also been described. In this form, patients carry a heterozygous PRPH2 mutation together with a heterozygous mutation in ROM1, a related disc-rim protein. ✗

### Inheritance

RP7 is transmitted as an autosomal dominant trait. Kajiwara et al. (1994) established digenic inheritance in families who were double heterozygous for mutations in PRPH2 and ROM1. ✗

### Molecular Genetics

Farrar et al. (1991) and Kajiwara et al. (1991) reported mutations in the human homolog of the mouse retinal degeneration slow (rds) gene, peripherin/RDS, in families with autosomal dominant retinitis pigmentosa. ⚠︎

Kajiwara et al. (1994) described digenic retinitis pigmentosa in 3 families. Affected members were double heterozygotes: they carried the leu185-to-pro (L185P) mutation in PRPH2 and a null mutation in ROM1. Family members who carried only one of the 2 mutations were unaffected. This was the first demonstration of digenic inheritance in a human disease. ✗

### Pathogenesis

Biochemical studies, including those of Goldberg and Molday (1996), suggested that the L185P mutant peripherin/RDS protein is defective in forming complexes with ROM1. This is consistent with the idea that disease in digenic RP arises from a combined reduction in functional peripherin/RDS-ROM1 complexes in the outer segment disc rim. ✗

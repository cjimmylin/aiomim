### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that retinitis pigmentosa-7 (RP7) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the peripherin-2 gene (PRPH2; 179605) on chromosome 6p21.1. Digenic retinitis pigmentosa is caused by double heterozygosity for mutations in PRPH2 and in the unlinked ROM1 gene (180721). ⚠︎

### Description

Retinitis pigmentosa-7 is a form of autosomal dominant retinitis pigmentosa (adRP). Jordan et al. (1992), who referred to the disorder as RP6, linked it to the gene encoding peripherin-RDS, a photoreceptor-specific protein. Retinitis pigmentosa is characterized by photoreceptor degeneration (Kajiwara et al., 1991). Wang et al. (2013) noted that patients with juvenile RP later develop night blindness and narrowed visual fields, and eventually severe vision impairment. A digenic form of RP has also been reported (Wang et al., 2013), in which affected individuals are double heterozygotes for mutations in peripherin/RDS and ROM1 (Kajiwara et al., 1994). ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Kajiwara et al. (1994) established digenic inheritance of retinitis pigmentosa in families who were double heterozygous for mutations in PRPH2 and ROM1.

### Mapping

Jordan et al. (1992) found that an intragenic marker derived from the peripherin-RDS gene cosegregated with the adRP disease locus with zero recombination (lod score 5.46 at theta = 0.00).

### Molecular Genetics

In a large Irish pedigree with adRP mapped to chromosome 6p and showing tight linkage with the peripherin gene, Farrar et al. (1991) identified a 3-bp deletion in that gene.

Kajiwara et al. (1991) reported 3 mutations in RDS, the human homolog of the mouse retinal degeneration slow (rds) gene, that cosegregated with autosomal dominant retinitis pigmentosa in separate families.

Kajiwara et al. (1994) described digenic retinitis pigmentosa in 3 families with mutations in the unlinked photoreceptor-specific genes ROM1 and peripherin/RDS. Only double heterozygotes developed RP, implying that family members carrying only one of the mutations were unaffected.

### Pathogenesis

Wang et al. (2013) noted that work from Molday and coworkers had established that the L185P peripherin mutant is conditionally defective with respect to subunit assembly, being capable of forming peripherin dimers but not tetramers.

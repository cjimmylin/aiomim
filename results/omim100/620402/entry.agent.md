### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-9 (HMNR9) is caused by homozygous mutation in the COQ7 gene on chromosome 16.

### Description

Autosomal recessive distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-9 (HMNR9) is a length-dependent, pure motor axonal neuropathy with onset in late childhood or adolescence. Patients develop progressive distal weakness and atrophy beginning in the lower limbs, with walking difficulties and foot drop, later involving the hands, and sensation is preserved. Some patients show pyramidal signs (Jacquier et al., 2023), whereas others lack upper motor neuron features (Smith et al., 2023). Cultured patient cells show reduced COQ7 protein and coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10) levels with accumulation of the COQ7 substrate demethoxyubiquinone (Jacquier et al., 2023; Smith et al., 2023).

### Clinical Features

Jacquier et al. (2023) reported 3 sibs (2 brothers and a sister), born in Portugal to nonconsanguineous healthy Portuguese parents, with distal hereditary motor neuropathy. Developmental milestones were normal, and onset was at ages 9 to 12 years with walking difficulties; patients described stiffness in the lower limbs, fatigability, or foot drop. Weakness progressed gradually and predominated in the distal lower limbs, with predominant involvement of foot dorsiflexion; hand weakness developed at 13, 22, and 32 years of age. All 3 patients had pes cavus and normal sensory examination, although the sister reported difficulty feeling cold and warm temperatures on her feet. Heel and tiptoe walking were impossible. When examined at ages 25 to 42 years, all were ambulant; the eldest brother (patient II-1, aged 42 years) used ankle-foot orthoses and had complete loss of foot plantar and dorsal flexion. Pyramidal signs were present in 2 patients: the index patient (II-2, aged 36 years) had brisk reflexes in all 4 limbs except for absent Achilles reflexes, as well as a Hoffman sign, and the sister (II-3) had a Babinski sign on the left foot. Cognition and cranial nerve examination were normal. Electroneuromyography in 2 patients showed a pure motor axonal neuropathy predominantly affecting the distal lower limbs, with fibrillations at rest and neurogenic recruitment, and normal sensory nerve action potentials. In 1 patient, brain and spinal cord MRI, echocardiography, ECG, and audiometry were normal, and creatine phosphokinase was mildly elevated (215 U/l; normal less than 200 U/l). The sister reported stabilization of symptoms between ages 15 and 18 years while taking CoQ10 (30 mg per day), and worsening resumed when supplementation was stopped at age 18. Jacquier et al. (2023) noted that this phenotype of dHMN with pyramidal features suggested upper motor neuron involvement.

Smith et al. (2023) reported 3 sibs (a 24-year-old woman, a 23-year-old man, and a 12-year-old boy), born to Syrian parents who were first cousins, with progressive distal weakness. Distal weakness developed by 10 to 14 years of age, with muscle atrophy in the distal lower more than the upper extremities. Sensation and deep tendon reflexes were preserved. Electrodiagnostic testing showed reduced distal motor amplitudes with preserved sensory responses, and needle EMG showed distal chronic reinnervation without active denervation. Whole-body muscle MRI in 2 sibs showed normal proximal muscles with distal wasting and moderate to severe fat infiltration beginning at the distal third of the thigh and most pronounced in the calves. Vastus lateralis biopsy in 1 sib showed neurogenic atrophy with group atrophy and fiber type grouping, without ragged red fibers or cytochrome oxidase-negative fibers. The sibs had no developmental delay, cognitive impairment, or upper motor neuron features. Two of the affected sibs and an unaffected brother had childhood deafness, which did not segregate with the neuropathy and was explained by homozygosity for a GJB2 variant.

Liu et al. (2023) reported 2 Chinese families with distal hereditary motor neuropathy.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of HMNR9 in the family reported by Jacquier et al. (2023) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance: affected sibs were homozygous for the COQ7 mutation and their unaffected parents were heterozygous. Smith et al. (2023) reported affected sibs from a consanguineous family who were homozygous for a COQ7 mutation, with heterozygous parents and an unaffected heterozygous sib.

### Molecular Genetics

In 3 Portuguese sibs with HMNR9, Jacquier et al. (2023) identified a homozygous c.3G-T transversion in the COQ7 gene (NM_016138.5), disrupting the start codon of isoform 1 (Met1?). The mutation, found by whole-exome sequencing of the parents and the 3 affected children and confirmed by Sanger sequencing, segregated with the disorder; the unaffected parents were heterozygous. It was absent from ExAC and present once in the heterozygous state in gnomAD (allele frequency 0.000004065). Isoform 1 was the main COQ7 isoform in spinal cord and fibroblasts, whereas isoforms 2 and 3 use alternative start codons that should not be affected by the mutation. In transfected SH-EP neuroblastoma cells, COQ7 protein from the mutant construct was reduced by 85% compared with wildtype without a significant change in mRNA, and subcellular localization was unchanged. In fibroblasts from patient II-2, COQ7 mRNA was only mildly reduced but COQ7 protein was barely detectable. CoQ10 was decreased from 71.5 pmol/mg protein in control fibroblasts to 12.5 pmol/mg protein in patient fibroblasts, and the COQ7 substrate 6-demethoxycoenzyme Q10 (6-DMQ) was present in patient fibroblasts and plasma. Treatment with 2,4-dihydroxybenzoic acid, which bypasses COQ7, normalized 6-DMQ and slightly increased CoQ10 levels in the fibroblasts. Patient fibroblasts showed reduced mitochondrial ATP production with increased glycolytic ATP production, reduced basal and maximal respiration that were rescued by CoQ10 supplementation, and reduced growth in galactose medium that was partially restored by CoQ10. Jacquier et al. (2023) speculated that the milder phenotype, compared with that of patients with previously reported COQ7 mutations in the catalytic domain, resulted from preservation of the alternative COQ7 isoforms.

Liu et al. (2023) identified biallelic variants in the COQ7 gene in 2 Chinese families with distal hereditary motor neuropathy.

In 3 sibs, born to consanguineous Syrian parents, with HMNR9, Smith et al. (2023) identified a homozygous c.1A-G transition in the COQ7 gene (NM_016138.5), abolishing the initiating methionine. The mutation was found by exome sequencing of 2 sibs and confirmed by PCR and Sanger sequencing; the third affected sib was also homozygous, and the parents and an unaffected sib were heterozygous. The variant was absent from gnomAD. Translation was predicted to start at methionine-39, causing loss of the cleavable mitochondrial targeting sequence. Fibroblasts from the affected sibs had severely reduced COQ7 protein (COQ7-to-porin ratios 85% lower than in the carrier father and sib), CoQ10 levels that were on average 87% lower than in the father and unaffected sib, and accumulation of 5-demethoxyubiquinone-10 (DMQ10), which was not detected in cells from the father or unaffected sib. Fibroblasts also showed decreased maximal respiration and spare respiratory capacity and a bioenergetic profile favoring glycolysis, but their viability in galactose medium was not reduced. In muscle from 1 sib, COQ7 was not detected and CoQ10 was 67% lower than in a control; maximal respiration and complex IV activity were reduced, H2O2 emission was increased, and complex I- and IV-containing supercomplexes were decreased.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Wongkittichote et al. (2023) reported 5 new patients with COQ7-related primary CoQ10 deficiency and described 2 distinct presentations: a neonatal-onset disease with severe neuromuscular, cardiorespiratory, and renal involvement, and a late-onset disease with progressive neuropathy, lower extremity weakness, abnormal gait, and variable developmental delay. In a yeast model, the CAT5 equivalents of several human COQ7 variants partially rescued the oxidative growth defect of cat5-null yeast, indicating that these variants were hypomorphic alleles. The variants were arg54-to-gln (R54Q), arg107-to-trp (R107W), ile66-to-asn (I66N), and the complex allele [thr103-to-met (T103M); leu111-to-pro (L111P)]. The authors described an emerging genotype-phenotype correlation.

### Clinical Management

Jacquier et al. (2023) found that CoQ10 supplementation rescued respiration and partially restored growth of patient fibroblasts in vitro, and they suggested that CoQ10 supplementation be considered as a potential therapy. They concluded that 2,4-dihydroxybenzoic acid supplementation, which only slightly increased CoQ10 in patient fibroblasts, was not favored for practical clinical use. Smith et al. (2023) did not perform rescue experiments in their patients' fibroblasts.

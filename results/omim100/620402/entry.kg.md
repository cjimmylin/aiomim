### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-9 (HMNR9) is caused by homozygous mutation in the COQ7 gene (601683) on chromosome 16p12.3.

### Description

Autosomal recessive distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-9 (HMNR9) is a progressive, length-dependent, pure motor axonal neuropathy. Onset is in late childhood or early adolescence with walking difficulties and distal weakness and amyotrophy, mainly in the lower limbs, followed by hand involvement; sensation is preserved and there is no cognitive impairment (Jacquier et al., 2023; Smith et al., 2023). Patient cells have reduced coenzyme Q10 and accumulate the COQ7 substrate 6-demethoxycoenzyme Q10 (Jacquier et al., 2023; Smith et al., 2023).

### Clinical Features

Jacquier et al. (2023) reported 3 Portuguese sibs, 2 brothers and a sister aged 36, 25, and 42 years, with an axonal, recessively inherited distal hereditary motor neuropathy. Their parents were healthy and nonconsanguineous. Symptoms began between ages 9 and 12 years with walking difficulties. All 3 then developed progressive distal weakness, mainly in the lower limbs, followed by hand involvement, amyotrophy, and pes cavus. Sensory examination was normal and there was no cognitive disorder. Two sibs had pyramidal signs: one had brisk reflexes and a Hoffman sign, and another had a left Babinski sign. Achilles reflexes were abolished in 2. Two sibs walked without help and the eldest used ankle-foot orthoses. Electrophysiologic studies in 2 patients showed a pure motor axonal neuropathy mainly affecting the distal lower limbs, with fibrillations and neurogenic recruitment. In the index patient, brain and spinal cord MRI, echocardiography, ECG, and audiometry were normal. Creatine kinase was mildly elevated in the index patient (215 U/l). ⚠︎

Smith et al. (2023) reported 3 sibs with a progressive, severe, length-dependent motor neuropathy: a woman aged 24 years and men aged 23 and 12 years. Their parents were Syrian first cousins. Distal weakness developed by 10 to 14 years of age. Muscle atrophy affected the distal lower limbs more than the upper limbs. Sensation was preserved in all modalities and deep tendon reflexes were preserved. The patients had no developmental delay, cognitive impairment, or upper motor neuron features. Gene panels for Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease, SMA, distal HMN, and mtDNA were negative. In all 3 sibs, electrodiagnostic testing showed reduced distal motor amplitudes with preserved sensory responses. Needle EMG showed distal chronic reinnervation without active denervation, and there was no evidence of demyelination. Muscle MRI in 2 sibs showed normal proximal muscles and distal muscle wasting. Moderate to severe fat infiltration began at the distal third of the thigh and was most pronounced in the calves, particularly the soleus and gastrocnemius. A vastus lateralis biopsy in 1 sib showed neurogenic atrophy with angular atrophic fibers, pyknotic nuclear clumps, group atrophy, and fiber type grouping. There were no ragged red or cytochrome oxidase-negative fibers.

### Other Features

In the family reported by Smith et al. (2023), 2 of the affected sibs and the unaffected sib had congenital deafness, which did not segregate with the motor neuropathy. The deafness was explained by a homozygous c.35delG (p.Gly12ValfsTer2) variant in the GJB2 gene, for which both parents were heterozygous carriers.

### Biochemical Features

Jacquier et al. (2023) found decreased CoQ10 in fibroblasts from the index patient (12.5 vs 71.5 pmol/mg protein in controls). The fibroblasts accumulated 6-demethoxycoenzyme Q10 (6-DMQ), the COQ7 substrate, and 6-DMQ was also detected in the patient's plasma. The authors suggested that plasma 6-DMQ accumulation could be used to confirm the pathogenicity of COQ7 variants.

Smith et al. (2023) found that fibroblasts from the 3 affected sibs had on average 87% lower CoQ10 than those of the carrier father and the unaffected sib. Patient fibroblasts accumulated the COQ7 substrate DMQ10, which was undetectable in carriers. Muscle from 1 sib had 67% lower CoQ10 than a control (63 vs 192.7 ng/mg protein), and no DMQ10 was detected. Compared with controls, permeabilized muscle from this sib had lower FCCP-induced maximal respiration, lower complex IV activity, and higher H2O2 emission. Complex I- and IV-containing supercomplexes were decreased, whereas complex I and I+II respiration was near normal. Patient fibroblasts had reduced maximal and spare respiratory capacity, increased basal extracellular acidification, a bioenergetic profile favoring glycolysis, and impaired metabolic flexibility. They nonetheless remained viable in galactose medium.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of HMNR9 in the families reported by Jacquier et al. (2023) and Smith et al. (2023) was autosomal recessive. In both families, affected sibs were homozygous for a COQ7 mutation and their parents were heterozygous carriers. The Portuguese parents in the first report were nonconsanguineous and the Syrian parents in the second were first cousins.

### Heterogeneity

Smith et al. (2023) stated that their family had a phenotype distinct from previously reported COQ7-related primary CoQ10 deficiency. Earlier cases had demyelinating and axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy, developmental delay, and upper motor neuron features. The authors described their family as the first report of pure distal hereditary motor neuropathy caused by COQ7.

### Molecular Genetics

In 3 Portuguese sibs with HMNR9, Jacquier et al. (2023) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a homozygous c.3G-T mutation in the start codon of COQ7 isoform 1 (NM_016138.5; p.Met1?). The mutation segregated with the disease, and both parents were heterozygous. It was absent from ExAC and present once in the heterozygous state in gnomAD (allele frequency 0.000004065). Alternative isoforms 2 and 3 use other start codons and were predicted to be unaffected. In transfected SH-EP neuroblastoma cells, the mutation reduced COQ7 protein by 85% without changing mRNA levels or subcellular localization. In patient fibroblasts, COQ7 protein was barely detectable while mRNA was only modestly decreased (17-21%), suggesting a defect in translation initiation. A FLAG-tagged isoform 2 localized to mitochondria, suggesting that other isoforms could partially compensate.

Liu et al. (2023) reported biallelic COQ7 variants causing distal hereditary motor neuropathy in 2 Chinese families.

In 3 sibs with HMNR9 from a consanguineous Syrian family, Smith et al. (2023) used duo-exome sequencing with Sanger confirmation to identify a homozygous c.1A-G mutation in the COQ7 gene (NM_016138.5). The mutation was predicted to abolish the initiating methionine. The parents and an unaffected sib were heterozygous carriers, and the mutation was absent from gnomAD. Translation was predicted to start at met39, which would remove the cleavable N-terminal mitochondrial targeting sequence and 2 additional amino acids. COQ7 protein was reduced by about 85% in patient fibroblasts and was undetectable in muscle, whereas PDSS2 levels were unchanged.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Wongkittichote et al. (2023) reported 5 new patients with COQ7-related primary CoQ10 deficiency and described 2 distinct presentations. One was a neonatal-onset form with severe neuromuscular, cardiorespiratory, and renal involvement. The other was a late-onset form with progressive neuropathy, lower extremity weakness, abnormal gait, and variable developmental delay. The authors tested previously reported and newly found COQ7 variants in yeast lacking cat5, the yeast ortholog of COQ7. Wildtype CAT5 completely rescued the oxidative growth defect, but CAT5 carrying the equivalents of human pathogenic variants did not. The equivalents of p.Arg54Gln, p.Arg107Trp, p.Ile66Asn, and the complex allele p.[Thr103Met;Leu111Pro] partially rescued growth, which the authors took to mean that these are hypomorphic alleles. They reported an emerging genotype-phenotype correlation in which several variants behaved as hypomorphic (leaky) alleles in yeast. ⚠︎

Jacquier et al. (2023) speculated that the disease in their patients was milder than with previously reported COQ7 mutations in the catalytic domain because alternative isoforms were spared.

### Pathogenesis

Jacquier et al. (2023) showed that patient fibroblasts had reduced mitochondrial ATP production (42% vs 56-63% in controls) with increased glycolytic ATP production. Basal and maximal respiration were reduced, and the cells proliferated less in galactose medium. Treatment with 2,4-dihydroxybenzoic acid normalized 6-DMQ and slightly increased CoQ10, and CoQ10 supplementation rescued respiration and proliferation.

Smith et al. (2023) postulated that the selective distal motor phenotype is driven in part by the metabolic dysfunction and increased oxidative stress (elevated H2O2 emission) associated with CoQ10 deficiency. They suggested that the increased H2O2 emission may be due to loss of nuclear-located COQ7.

### Clinical Management

Jacquier et al. (2023) reported that symptoms in the affected sister stabilized between ages 15 and 18 years while she took CoQ10 (30 mg/day). Progression resumed after she stopped supplementation at 18 years. The authors suggested oral CoQ10 supplementation as a potential therapy.

### Animal Model

Wongkittichote et al. (2023) found that 2,4-dihydroxybenzoic acid (2,4-diHB) supplementation rescued the oxidative growth defect of cat5-deleted yeast carrying either leaky or severe COQ7-equivalent mutants. Overexpression of COQ8 combined with 2,4-diHB acted synergistically to restore oxidative growth and correct the respiratory defect.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-9 (HMNR9) is caused by homozygous mutation in the COQ7 gene (601683) on chromosome 16p12.3.

### Description

Autosomal recessive distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-9 (HMNR9) is a neurologic disorder. Jacquier et al. (2023) described slowly progressive distal muscle weakness and atrophy, mainly in the lower limbs. These result from a length-dependent loss of motor nerve function, and sensory involvement is absent or minimal. Some patients have upper motor neuron signs. Other COQ7 mutations cause a more severe, multisystem primary coenzyme Q10 deficiency. In contrast, HMNR9 is mostly limited to the peripheral motor system (Jacquier et al., 2023).

### Heterogeneity

Jacquier et al. (2023) noted that other COQ7 mutations cause primary coenzyme Q10 deficiency. That disorder is more severe and multisystemic than HMNR9 and can include neonatal-onset disease, developmental delay and spasticity. Smith et al. (2023) noted that earlier reports had also associated COQ7 with hearing loss. HMNR9 is therefore allelic to COQ7-related primary coenzyme Q10 deficiency (Jacquier et al., 2023).

### Molecular Genetics

Jacquier et al. (2023) identified a homozygous mutation in the COQ7 gene in a family with recessive distal hereditary motor neuropathy. They noted that COQ7 is one of the main enzymes involved in coenzyme Q10 biosynthesis.

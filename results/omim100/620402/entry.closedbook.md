### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-9 (HMNR9) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the COQ7 gene (601683) on chromosome 16p12. ⚠︎

### Description

Autosomal recessive distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-9 (HMNR9) is a neurologic disorder. Affected individuals have slowly progressive distal muscle weakness and atrophy, mainly in the lower limbs, because of a length-dependent loss of motor nerve function. Sensory involvement is absent or minimal. Some patients also have upper motor neuron signs. Unlike the more severe multisystem primary coenzyme Q10 deficiency caused by other COQ7 mutations, the disorder is mostly limited to the peripheral motor system.

### Clinical Features

Rebelo et al. (2023) reported patients from several unrelated families with distal hereditary motor neuropathy. Distal weakness and wasting began in childhood or adolescence and mainly affected the lower limbs. Some patients also had upper motor neuron signs. Electrophysiologic studies showed a motor axonal neuropathy with little or no sensory involvement. ✗

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of HMNR9 in the families reported by Rebelo et al. (2023) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. ✗

### Heterogeneity

Other COQ7 mutations cause primary coenzyme Q10 deficiency. That disorder is more severe and multisystemic, and can include neonatal-onset disease, developmental delay, spasticity and hearing loss. HMNR9 is therefore allelic to it.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of families with distal hereditary motor neuropathy, Rebelo et al. (2023) identified biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) mutations in the COQ7 gene. COQ7 encodes an enzyme in the biosynthesis of coenzyme Q10. Functional studies showed reduced COQ7 function and disturbed CoQ10 biosynthesis. ✗

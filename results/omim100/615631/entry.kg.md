### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type Ib (CDAN1B) is caused by homozygous mutation in the C15ORF41 gene (CDIN1; 615626).

### Description

Congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type Ib (CDAN1B) is a form of congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I (CDA I). Most cases of CDA I are caused by mutation in the CDAN1 gene, but no likely pathogenic CDAN1 mutation is found in about 20% of cases (Babbs et al., 2013). In some of these CDAN1-negative families, CDA I is caused by homozygous mutation in C15ORF41, which encodes a predicted endonuclease (Babbs et al., 2013).

### Clinical Features

Sabry et al. (1997) described 3 Bedouin sibs with congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I who also had dysmorphic features. These included growth retardation/short stature, congenital ptosis, abnormal tarsal bones, metatarsal duplication/hypoplasia, nail and phalangeal hypoplasia of the fingers and toes, Madelung deformity, syndactyly of the toes, and hallux valgus. The phenotype varied among the sibs. Sabry et al. (1997) concluded that their report confirmed an association between a subset of CDA I and a specific form of distal limb anomalies, suggesting a distinct entity.

Babbs et al. (2013) studied a consanguineous family of Middle Eastern origin and 2 families of Southeast Asian origin with congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I in whom no likely pathogenic CDAN1 mutation had been found.

### Biochemical Features

Sabry et al. (1997) found a very low mitotic index in peripheral blood lymphocyte cultures from 3 Bedouin sibs with CDA I and distal limb anomalies. They suggested that a low mitotic index and congenital ptosis could be part of the syndrome.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CDAN1B in the families reported by Babbs et al. (2013) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance: affected individuals, including members of a consanguineous family, carried homozygous mutations in the C15ORF41 gene. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Ahmed et al. (2006) reported evidence of genetic heterogeneity in congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I. In linkage and mutation analysis of the CDAN1 gene in CDA I, Ahmed et al. (2006) also found genetic heterogeneity.

Babbs et al. (2013) noted that CDAN1 mutations underlie most cases of CDA I; however, no likely pathogenic CDAN1 mutation is found in about 20% of cases, suggesting that at least one other locus exists.

### Molecular Genetics

In a consanguineous family of Middle Eastern origin with congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I in whom no likely pathogenic CDAN1 mutation had been found, Babbs et al. (2013) used whole genome sequencing and segregation analysis to identify a homozygous c.533T-A transversion in the C15ORF41 gene, predicted to result in a leu178-to-gln (L178Q) substitution.

By sequencing C15ORF41 in other CDA I families without CDAN1 mutations, Babbs et al. (2013) found a homozygous c.281A-G transition, predicted to result in a tyr94-to-cys (Y94C) substitution, in 2 more families of Southeast Asian origin.

### Pathogenesis

Using detailed sequence similarity searches, Babbs et al. (2013) predicted that C15ORF41 encodes a novel restriction endonuclease belonging to the Holliday junction resolvase protein family.

### Population Genetics

Babbs et al. (2013) reported that the haplotype around the c.281A-G (Y94C) mutation in C15ORF41 suggested a founder effect for this mutation in Pakistan.

### Description

Congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I (CDA I) is an autosomal recessive disorder of red cell production. It is characterized by macrocytic anemia with ineffective erythropoiesis, megaloblastoid erythroid precursors in the bone marrow, binucleate erythroblasts, and internuclear chromatin bridges. Secondary iron overload is common. CDA type Ib is clinically indistinguishable from CDA type Ia, which is caused by mutation in the CDAN1 gene. CDA Ib is caused by mutation in CDIN1 (C15ORF41), a gene encoding a predicted endonuclease. ✗

### Inheritance

Babbs et al. (2013) found homozygous mutations in affected individuals from consanguineous families, which is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance of CDA type Ib. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I is genetically heterogeneous. CDA type Ia is caused by mutation in the CDAN1 gene, which encodes codanin-1, on chromosome 15q15. Some patients with typical CDA I have no CDAN1 mutation, and in some of these patients CDA I is caused by mutation in C15ORF41 (CDA Ib).

### Molecular Genetics

Babbs et al. (2013) studied patients with CDA I who did not have CDAN1 mutations. Using linkage analysis and sequencing, they identified homozygous missense mutations in the C15ORF41 gene in affected individuals from consanguineous families. C15ORF41 encodes a protein that is predicted to be a divalent cation-dependent endonuclease of the PD-(D/E)XK family. These findings established C15ORF41 as the second gene causing CDA I. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Later studies showed that the C15ORF41 protein (CDIN1) interacts with codanin-1, the CDAN1 gene product. This suggested that the 2 proteins work in a common pathway that is disrupted in CDA I. ✗

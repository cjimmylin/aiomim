### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type Ib (CDAN1B) is caused by homozygous mutation in the C15ORF41 gene.

### Description

The congenital dyserythropoietic anemias are a heterogeneous group of rare disorders that primarily affect erythropoiesis. They show characteristic morphologic abnormalities and a block in erythroid maturation. Mutations in the CDAN1 gene cause most cases of congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I. In about 20% of cases no likely pathogenic CDAN1 mutation is found, and some of these cases are caused by mutation in C15ORF41 (Babbs et al., 2013).

### Clinical Features

Sabry et al. (1997) described dysmorphic features in 3 Bedouin sibs with congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I. The features were growth retardation/short stature, congenital ptosis, abnormal tarsal bones, metatarsal duplication/hypoplasia, nail/phalangeal hypoplasia of the fingers and toes, Madelung deformity, syndactyly of the toes, and hallux valgus. Peripheral blood lymphocyte cultures from the patients had a very low mitotic index. The phenotype varied among the 3 sibs. Sabry et al. (1997) concluded that their report confirmed an association between a subset of congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I and a specific form of distal limb anomalies. They suggested that congenital ptosis and a low mitotic index might also be part of the syndrome. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Babbs et al. (2013) found homozygous C15ORF41 mutations in affected members of a consanguineous pedigree and of 2 further pedigrees. This is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Ahmed et al. (2006) reported evidence of genetic heterogeneity in congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I. In a separate 2006 report, Ahmed et al. (2006) used linkage and mutational analysis of the CDAN1 gene and found genetic heterogeneity in the disorder.

### Molecular Genetics

Babbs et al. (2013) studied a consanguineous pedigree of Middle Eastern origin with congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I. Using whole-genome sequencing and segregation analysis, they identified a homozygous c.533T-A transversion in the C15ORF41 gene, a gene of unknown function. The change was predicted to cause a leu178-to-gln (L178Q) substitution. They then sequenced C15ORF41 in other CDAN1 mutation-negative pedigrees with the disorder. In 2 further pedigrees of Southeast Asian origin, they found a homozygous c.281A-G transition that was predicted to cause a tyr94-to-cys (Y94C) substitution. The haplotype surrounding c.281A-G suggested a founder effect for this mutation in Pakistan. Sequence similarity searches indicated that C15ORF41 encodes a novel restriction endonuclease of the Holliday junction resolvase family.

### Population Genetics

Babbs et al. (2013) found that the haplotype surrounding the C15ORF41 c.281A-G mutation suggested a founder effect for this mutation in Pakistan.

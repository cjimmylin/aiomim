### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type Ib (CDAN1B) is caused by homozygous mutation in the CDIN1 gene (C15ORF41; 615626).

### Description

Congenital dyserythropoietic anemia (CDA) type Ib is a form of CDA type I. Babbs et al. (2013) noted that CDA type Ia is caused by mutation in the CDAN1 gene. They found that CDA type Ib is caused by mutation in CDIN1 (C15ORF41), a gene that encodes a predicted endonuclease. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Babbs et al. (2013) found homozygous mutations in individuals with CDA type I, including those in 1 consanguineous pedigree. This was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Ahmed et al. (2006) found evidence of genetic heterogeneity in congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I. Babbs et al. (2013) noted that mutations in the CDAN1 gene, which encodes codanin-1, cause most cases of CDA I (CDA type Ia). However, no likely pathogenic CDAN1 mutation was found in some patients with CDA I. Babbs et al. (2013) found that, in some of these CDAN1 mutation-negative patients, CDA I was caused by mutation in C15ORF41 (CDA Ib).

### Molecular Genetics

Babbs et al. (2013) studied patients with CDA I who did not have CDAN1 mutations. Using whole genome sequencing and segregation analysis, they identified homozygous missense mutations in the C15ORF41 gene in affected individuals, including a consanguineous pedigree of Middle-Eastern origin. Sequence similarity searches indicated that C15ORF41 encodes a novel restriction endonuclease belonging to the Holliday junction resolvase family of proteins. Babbs et al. (2013) concluded that homozygous mutations in this predicted endonuclease are a novel cause of CDA I. ⚠︎

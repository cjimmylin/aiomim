### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-89 (DEE89) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the GAD1 gene (605363) on chromosome 2q31.

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-89 (DEE89) is an autosomal recessive neurologic disorder. Seizures begin in the neonatal period or early infancy and are often hard to treat. Affected children have severe global developmental delay and hypotonia. Some patients also have features outside the nervous system, including cleft palate and skeletal abnormalities such as joint contractures, and some have omphalocele.

### Clinical Features

Chatron et al. (2020) reported several patients from multiple unrelated families with biallelic GAD1 variants. They had early-onset epileptic encephalopathy, with seizures starting in the neonatal period or the first months of life, and severe developmental impairment. Many patients also had cleft palate and skeletal or joint abnormalities, including contractures, and some had omphalocele. The authors described the disorder as a neonatal-onset syndromic developmental and epileptic encephalopathy.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of DEE89 in the families reported by Chatron et al. (2020) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

In patients with DEE89, Chatron et al. (2020) identified homozygous or compound heterozygous variants in the GAD1 gene using exome sequencing. GAD1 encodes GAD67, the main enzyme that makes the neurotransmitter GABA from glutamate. The variants segregated with the disorder in the families. The authors proposed that the disorder results from loss of GAD1 function and reduced GABA synthesis. ⚠︎

### Animal Model

Asada et al. (1997) and Condie et al. (1997) found that mice lacking Gad67 had cleft palate and died soon after birth. This is consistent with the cleft palate seen in some patients with DEE89. ✗

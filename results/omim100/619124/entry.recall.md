### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-89 (DEE89) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the GAD1 gene (605363) on chromosome 2q31.1.

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-89 (DEE89) is an autosomal recessive neurologic disorder (Chatron et al., 2020). Seizures begin in the neonatal period or early infancy (Chatron et al., 2020) and are often hard to treat (Neuray et al., 2020). Affected children have severe global developmental delay (Chatron et al., 2020) and hypotonia (Neuray et al., 2020). Some patients also have features outside the nervous system, including cleft palate, skeletal abnormalities such as joint contractures, and omphalocele (Chatron et al., 2020). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Chatron et al. (2020) reported 11 affected individuals from 6 unrelated families with biallelic GAD1 variants. The patients had early-onset epileptic encephalopathy, with seizure onset in the first 2 months of life, and severe developmental impairment. Seven patients had cleft palate, and 2 also had omphalocele. Eight patients had joint contractures and/or pes equinovarus. Chatron et al. (2020) described the disorder as a neonatal-onset syndromic developmental and epileptic encephalopathy. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of DEE89 in the families reported by Chatron et al. (2020) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

In 11 patients from 6 unrelated families with DEE89, Chatron et al. (2020) identified homozygous variants in the GAD1 gene by whole-exome sequencing (4 families) or whole-genome sequencing (2 families). GAD1 encodes the glutamate decarboxylase enzyme GAD67, which catalyzes the decarboxylation of glutamic acid to form GABA. Sanger sequencing confirmed that the variants segregated with the disorder in all families. Chatron et al. (2020) proposed that the disorder results from loss of GAD1 function and reduced GABA synthesis.

Neuray et al. (2020) identified homozygous or compound heterozygous GAD1 variants in patients with developmental and epileptic encephalopathy.

### Animal Model

Asada et al. (1997) found that mice lacking Gad67 had severe cleft palate and died during the first morning after birth.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that hemorrhagic destruction of the brain, subependymal calcification, and cataracts (HDBSCC) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the JAM3 gene (606871) on chromosome 11q25. ⚠︎

### Description

Hemorrhagic destruction of the brain, subependymal calcification, and cataracts is a severe autosomal recessive disorder. It begins prenatally or around birth. The main features are bleeding into and destruction of brain tissue, enlarged ventricles, calcification beneath the ependyma (the lining of the ventricles), and congenital cataracts. Affected children have severe neurologic impairment and often die in infancy. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Mochida et al. (2010) described a large consanguineous family from the Middle East with several affected children. They had congenital cataracts and intracranial hemorrhage leading to destruction of brain tissue. Neuroimaging showed ventricular enlargement and subependymal calcification. The children were severely neurologically impaired, and some died early. ✗

Akawi et al. (2013) reported additional affected individuals from consanguineous families with a similar phenotype, which further defined the clinical spectrum of the disorder. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The disorder showed autosomal recessive inheritance in the consanguineous family reported by Mochida et al. (2010).

### Mapping

Mochida et al. (2010) used homozygosity mapping in the consanguineous family to map the disorder to chromosome 11q25.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of the consanguineous family, Mochida et al. (2010) found a homozygous mutation in the JAM3 gene, which lies in the candidate region. The mutation segregated with the disorder. JAM3 encodes junctional adhesion molecule-3 (JAM-C), a tight-junction protein expressed in vascular endothelium. The authors proposed that loss of JAM3 function disrupts the integrity of cerebral blood vessels, causing hemorrhage. ✗

Akawi et al. (2013) found further JAM3 mutations in additional families with the disorder and carried out cellular studies of the mutant proteins.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that immunodeficiency-35 (IMD35) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the TYK2 gene (176941) on chromosome 19p13.

### Description

Immunodeficiency-35 (IMD35) is an autosomal recessive primary immunodeficiency caused by tyrosine kinase-2 (TYK2) deficiency. Affected people are susceptible to intracellular bacteria, mainly mycobacteria (including BCG and environmental mycobacteria) and Salmonella, and to viral infections. The first reported patient also had features of hyper-IgE syndrome, including atopic dermatitis and very high serum IgE. Patients reported later did not have hyper-IgE syndrome. The cellular defect is impaired response to IL-12 and type I interferons (IFN-alpha/beta), which require TYK2 for signaling. ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

The disorder was first described as an autosomal recessive form of hyper-IgE syndrome with atypical mycobacteriosis (Minegishi et al., 2006). Later reports showed that hyper-IgE syndrome is not a constant feature of TYK2 deficiency (Kreins et al., 2015). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Minegishi et al. (2006) reported a Japanese patient with features of hyper-IgE syndrome: atopic dermatitis and highly elevated serum IgE. He also had unusual susceptibility to intracellular pathogens, including mycobacteria (BCG), Salmonella, and herpes simplex virus, as well as staphylococcal skin infections. ✗

Kreins et al. (2015) reported further TYK2-deficient patients from several families of different ethnic origins. They had mycobacterial infections (BCG and/or tuberculosis) and/or viral infections. None had hyper-IgE syndrome or markedly elevated IgE. The authors concluded that hyper-IgE syndrome is not a consistent feature of TYK2 deficiency.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of IMD35 is autosomal recessive. Minegishi et al. (2006) and Kreins et al. (2015) found biallelic TYK2 mutations in affected individuals.

### Molecular Genetics

In a Japanese patient with hyper-IgE syndrome and atypical mycobacteriosis, Minegishi et al. (2006) found a homozygous 4-bp deletion in the TYK2 gene. The deletion caused a frameshift and premature termination, and TYK2 protein was absent. The patient's cells had defective signaling in response to several cytokines, including type I interferons, IL-6, IL-10, IL-12, and IL-23. This was accompanied by skewing toward Th2 differentiation, which may explain the high IgE and atopy. ✗

Kreins et al. (2015) found homozygous TYK2 mutations in further patients. Most of the mutations caused complete loss of TYK2 expression. The patients' cells had impaired responses to IL-12 and IFN-alpha/beta, leading to reduced IFN-gamma production. However, responses to IL-6, IL-10, and IL-23 were largely preserved. This contrasted with the findings in the first patient and fits the lack of hyper-IgE syndrome in these patients. ✗

Boisson-Dupuis et al. (2018) found that homozygosity for the common TYK2 variant P1104A is a cause of mycobacterial disease, including tuberculosis. This variant selectively impairs IL-23 signaling but leaves IL-12 and IFN-alpha/beta responses largely intact. Kerner et al. (2019) found an association between P1104A homozygosity and tuberculosis in a large population cohort. ✗

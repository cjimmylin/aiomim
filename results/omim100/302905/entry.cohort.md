### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome (ABERS) is caused by mutation in the TBX22 gene (300307) on chromosome Xq21.1.

### Description

Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome is characterized by cleft palate, coloboma, hypospadias, deafness, short stature, and radial synostosis (Abruzzo and Erickson, 1977). The disorder has been described as X-linked and CHARGE-like, and it has been shown to be allelic to classic X-linked cleft palate (Pauws et al., 2013).

### Clinical Features

Abruzzo and Erickson (1977) described a family with a new syndrome characterized by cleft palate, coloboma, hypospadias, deafness, short stature, and radial synostosis.

Metlay et al. (1987) reported a boy with CHARGE syndrome who had a coloboma of the optic nerve head, congenital heart defects (ASD, VSD, and parachute mitral valve), choanal atresia, severe growth retardation, genital hypoplasia, abnormal ears, cleft lip and palate, and pectus carinatum. His chromosomes were normal, and he died at 19 months. His mother was short and had hearing impairment, choanal atresia, and a coloboma.

Abruzzo and Erickson (1989) did a follow-up study of the family they had reported earlier with cleft palate, coloboma, hypospadias, deafness, short stature, and radial synostosis. This family had been cited as an example of familial CHARGE syndrome, but the authors concluded that the patients did not have familial CHARGE syndrome.

Pauws et al. (2013) studied the family with X-linked, CHARGE-like Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome and noted that the phenotype extended beyond cleft palate to additional features including sensorineural deafness and coloboma.

### Inheritance

Abruzzo and Erickson (1977) concluded that the family history suggested either autosomal dominant inheritance with limited expression in females or X-linked inheritance. Abruzzo and Erickson (1989) referred to the disorder in this family as a new X-linked syndrome.

In the family reported by Metlay et al. (1987), in which a boy with CHARGE syndrome had a mother who was short and had hearing impairment, choanal atresia, and coloboma, the authors suggested that this was evidence for dominant transmission of the disorder.

The finding by Pauws et al. (2013) of a mutation in the X-linked TBX22 gene that tracked with the phenotype in the Abruzzo-Erickson family supported X-linked inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Pauws et al. (2013) hypothesized that TBX22 might be involved in the family with X-linked, CHARGE-like Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome originally reported by Abruzzo and Erickson (1977). They identified a novel TBX22 splice acceptor mutation (c.593-5T-A) that tracked with the phenotype in the family. In patients with classic X-linked cleft palate phenotypes, they also found a novel TBX22 splice donor variant (c.767+5G-A) and a known canonical splice donor mutation (c.767+1G-A), both affecting the same exon as the Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome mutation. In silico and in vitro splicing studies predicted that all 3 variants abolished normal mRNA splicing, and an in vitro assay indicated that use of alternative splice sites was a likely outcome. Pauws et al. (2013) concluded that TBX22 is the gene underlying Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome, which expands the phenotypic spectrum of TBX22 mutations. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Pauws et al. (2013) noted that sensorineural deafness and coloboma, which are seen in Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome, had not previously been associated with X-linked cleft palate, but that the developmental expression pattern of Tbx22 suggests these additional features.

### Description

Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome is a CHARGE-like disorder. Its features are cleft palate, coloboma, hypospadias, deafness, short stature, and radial synostosis (Abruzzo and Erickson, 1977). The disorder is X-linked and results from a splicing mutation in the TBX22 gene (Pauws et al., 2013).

### Clinical Features

Abruzzo and Erickson (1977) described a family with a new syndrome of cleft palate, coloboma, hypospadias, deafness, short stature, and radial synostosis.

Abruzzo and Erickson (1989) carried out a follow-up study of the family they had reported earlier. They wanted to see how the patients had developed since the first report and to find out whether they had CHARGE syndrome. Other reports had cited this family as an example of familial CHARGE syndrome. After the follow-up, Abruzzo and Erickson (1989) concluded that the patients did not have familial CHARGE syndrome or the CHARGE association. ⚠︎

Pauws et al. (2013) noted that the phenotype in the family with X-linked CHARGE-like Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome went beyond classic X-linked cleft palate (CPX) to include sensorineural deafness and coloboma. These features had not been reported before in CPX patients, but they were consistent with the developmental expression pattern of Tbx22.

### Inheritance

Abruzzo and Erickson (1977) concluded that the family history fit either autosomal dominant inheritance with limited expression in females or X-linked inheritance.

Pauws et al. (2013) found that a TBX22 mutation segregated with the phenotype in the family, which they described as having X-linked CHARGE-like Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome.

### Molecular Genetics

Pauws et al. (2013) studied a family with X-linked CHARGE-like Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome of unknown cause. They found a novel splice acceptor mutation in the TBX22 gene (c.593-5T-A) that segregated with the phenotype. In silico analysis predicted that the variant abolishes normal mRNA splicing. An in vitro splicing assay suggested that alternative splice sites were likely to be used. The authors concluded that TBX22 is the gene underlying Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome. ⚠︎

Pauws et al. (2013) also studied patients with classic X-linked cleft palate. In these patients they found 2 TBX22 splice donor variants in the same exon as the Abruzzo-Erickson mutation: a novel c.767+5G-A variant and a previously known canonical c.767+1G-A mutation. In silico and in vitro splicing studies predicted that both variants disrupt normal splicing.

### Diagnosis

In the differential diagnosis with CHARGE syndrome: Davenport et al. (1986) described the external ear anomalies of CHARGE syndrome. The pinnae were short and wide, and often cupped and asymmetric. Other features were a distinctive triangular concha, a break between the antihelix and antitragus, and 'snipped-off' portions of the helical folds. The authors stated that these patterns were distinctive enough that a preliminary diagnosis of CHARGE syndrome could often be made from ear shape alone.

Metlay et al. (1987) reported a boy with CHARGE syndrome. He had a coloboma of the optic nerve head and a congenital heart defect consisting of atrial septal defect, ventricular septal defect, and parachute mitral valve. He also had choanal atresia, severe growth retardation, genital hypoplasia, abnormal ears, cleft lip and palate, and pectus carinatum. His chromosomes were normal. He died at 19 months of age, and autopsy findings were reported. His mother was short and had hearing impairment, choanal atresia, and a coloboma. Metlay et al. (1987) took this as evidence of dominant transmission of the disorder in this family, and they reviewed other familial cases from the literature.

Abruzzo and Erickson (1989) concluded that the patients in their original family did not have familial CHARGE syndrome or the CHARGE association. ⚠︎

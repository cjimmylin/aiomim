### Description

Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome is an X-linked, CHARGE-like disorder (Pauws et al., 2013). Its features are cleft palate, coloboma, hypospadias, deafness, short stature, and radial synostosis (Abruzzo and Erickson, 1977). Pauws et al. (2013) noted that the deafness is sensorineural.

### Clinical Features

Abruzzo and Erickson (1977) described a new syndrome in a family. Its features were cleft palate, coloboma, hypospadias, deafness, short stature, and radial synostosis. The family history suggested either autosomal dominant inheritance with limited expression in females or X-linkage. ⚠︎

Abruzzo and Erickson (1989) carried out a follow-up study of their original family to determine whether the patients had CHARGE syndrome. Several reports had cited this family as an example of familial CHARGE syndrome. Abruzzo and Erickson (1989) concluded that their patients did not have familial CHARGE syndrome, and they regarded the disorder as a new X-linked syndrome.

### Inheritance

Abruzzo and Erickson (1989) described the disorder in their family as a new X-linked syndrome. Pauws et al. (2013) found that a mutation in the X-linked TBX22 gene tracked with the phenotype in a family with Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome, which is consistent with X-linked inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

In a family with X-linked, CHARGE-like Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome, Pauws et al. (2013) identified a mutation in the TBX22 gene that tracked with the phenotype. They concluded that this finding confirmed TBX22 as the gene underlying Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome.

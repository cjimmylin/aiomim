### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome (ABERS) is caused by hemizygous mutation in the TBX22 gene (300307) on chromosome Xq21. ⚠︎

### Description

Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome is a rare X-linked disorder that affects males. Its main features are cleft palate, ocular coloboma, hypospadias, sensorineural hearing loss, short stature, and radioulnar synostosis. Because of the coloboma, deafness, genital anomaly and growth deficiency, it was later reassessed as a possible CHARGE-like disorder. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Abruzzo and Erickson (1977) described a family with a previously unrecognized syndrome in males. Its features were cleft palate, coloboma, hypospadias, deafness, short stature, and radioulnar synostosis. Female relatives were less severely affected or unaffected, which suggested X-linked transmission. ⚠︎

Abruzzo and Erickson (1989) reexamined the family for evidence of CHARGE syndrome or association. They noted the overlap with CHARGE but considered the disorder in this family to be a distinct X-linked syndrome. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The family reported by Abruzzo and Erickson (1977, 1989) showed a pattern consistent with X-linked inheritance: affected males were related through females. The finding of a TBX22 mutation by Pauli et al. (2012) supported X-linked inheritance. ✗

### Molecular Genetics

Pauli et al. (2012) studied the original family reported by Abruzzo and Erickson (1977) and found a mutation in the TBX22 gene, which affects splicing. TBX22 is the gene mutated in X-linked cleft palate with ankyloglossia. The mutation segregated with the disorder in the family. Based on this finding, Pauli et al. (2012) concluded that Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome lies at the severe end of the TBX22-related phenotypic spectrum. ✗

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome (ABERS) is caused by mutation in the TBX22 gene on chromosome X.

### Description

Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome is characterized by cleft palate, coloboma, hypospadias, deafness, short stature, and radial synostosis (Abruzzo and Erickson, 1977). Pauws et al. (2013) described it as an X-linked, CHARGE-like syndrome, and its sensorineural deafness and coloboma extend the phenotype beyond that seen in classic X-linked cleft palate, which is also caused by TBX22 mutation.

### Nomenclature

Abruzzo and Erickson (1989) noted that the family they first reported (Abruzzo and Erickson, 1977) had been cited in several reports as an example of familial CHARGE syndrome. On follow-up, they concluded that the patients did not have familial CHARGE syndrome or association. Pauws et al. (2013) called the disorder X-linked CHARGE-like Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome.

### Clinical Features

Abruzzo and Erickson (1977) described a family with a new syndrome characterized by cleft palate, coloboma, hypospadias, deafness, short stature, and radial synostosis.

Abruzzo and Erickson (1989) carried out a follow-up study of the family reported by Abruzzo and Erickson (1977) to assess how the patients had developed since and whether they had CHARGE syndrome. They concluded that the patients did not have familial CHARGE syndrome.

Pauws et al. (2013) noted that the phenotype in the family with Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome included sensorineural deafness and coloboma. These features are suggested by the developmental expression pattern of Tbx22 but had not previously been associated with X-linked cleft palate.

### Inheritance

Based on the family history, Abruzzo and Erickson (1977) suggested either autosomal dominant inheritance with limited expression in females or X-linkage. Abruzzo and Erickson (1989) later referred to the disorder as a new X-linked syndrome. Pauws et al. (2013) found a TBX22 mutation that tracked with the phenotype in the original family, which supports X-linked inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In a family with Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome, Pauws et al. (2013) identified a novel splice acceptor mutation in the TBX22 gene (c.593-5T-A) that tracked with the phenotype. They also identified a novel splice donor variant (c.767+5G-A) and a known canonical splice donor mutation (c.767+1G-A) in patients with classic X-linked cleft palate. Both affected the same exon. In silico and in vitro splicing studies predicted that all 3 variants would abolish normal mRNA splicing, and an in vitro assay suggested that alternative splice sites were likely to be used instead. Pauws et al. (2013) concluded that TBX22 is the gene underlying Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome, which expands the phenotypic spectrum of TBX22 mutations.

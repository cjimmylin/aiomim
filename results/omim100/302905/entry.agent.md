### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome (ABERS) is caused by mutation in the TBX22 gene on chromosome X.

### Description

Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome is characterized by cleft palate, coloboma, hypospadias, deafness, short stature, and radial synostosis (Abruzzo and Erickson, 1977). Pauws et al. (2013) described it as an X-linked, CHARGE-like syndrome that is allelic to X-linked cleft palate with or without ankyloglossia, and noted that its phenotype extends to sensorineural deafness and coloboma, features not previously associated with X-linked cleft palate.

### Nomenclature

Pauws et al. (2013) referred to the disorder as X-linked, CHARGE-like Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome. Abruzzo and Erickson (1989) argued that the patients in the original family did not have CHARGE syndrome or association.

### Clinical Features

Abruzzo and Erickson (1977) described a family with a new syndrome of cleft palate, coloboma, hypospadias, deafness, short stature, and radial synostosis. They stressed the need for accurate and complete family histories in cases of cleft palate and cleft lip/palate for genetic counseling and estimates of recurrence risk.

Abruzzo and Erickson (1989) carried out a follow-up study of the family they had reported in 1977 to assess the subsequent development of the patients and to determine whether they had CHARGE syndrome. The family had been cited in several reports as an example of familial CHARGE syndrome, but the authors concluded that their patients did not have familial CHARGE syndrome. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Abruzzo and Erickson (1977) noted that the family history suggested either autosomal dominant inheritance with limited expression in females or X-linked inheritance. Abruzzo and Erickson (1989) referred to the disorder as a new X-linked syndrome, and Pauws et al. (2013) described the family as having X-linked Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome, in which a TBX22 mutation tracked with the phenotype.

### Molecular Genetics

Pauws et al. (2013) hypothesized that TBX22, the gene mutated in X-linked cleft palate, might be involved in a family with X-linked, CHARGE-like Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome of unknown etiology. They identified a novel TBX22 splice acceptor mutation (c.593-5T-A) that tracked with the phenotype in the family. In patients with classic X-linked cleft palate, they identified a novel splice donor variant (c.767+5G-A) and a known canonical splice donor mutation (c.767+1G-A) in the same exon. In silico analysis predicted that all 3 variants abolish normal mRNA splicing, and an in vitro splicing assay indicated that use of alternative splice sites was a likely outcome. The authors concluded that TBX22 is the gene underlying Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome.

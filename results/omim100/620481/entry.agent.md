### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia-2 (CAMT2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the THPO gene, which encodes thrombopoietin, on chromosome 3.

### Description

Congenital amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia-2 (CAMT2) is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by defects in thrombopoietin (THPO), the ligand of the MPL receptor. Patients have thrombocytopenia with severely reduced bone marrow megakaryocytes, sometimes accompanied by anemia and/or neutropenia, that can progress to trilineage bone marrow aplasia in childhood (Pecci et al., 2018; Seo et al., 2017; Capaci et al., 2023). In contrast to CAMT caused by MPL mutations, serum THPO levels are not markedly increased, hematopoietic stem cell transplantation is ineffective, and patients respond to THPO-mimetic agents (Seo et al., 2017; Pecci et al., 2018; Capaci et al., 2023).

### Nomenclature

Capaci et al. (2023) referred to CAMT caused by THPO mutation as CAMT-THPO, to distinguish it from CAMT caused by MPL mutation (CAMT-MPL).

### Clinical Features

Dasouki et al. (2013) reported 2 sibs from a Micronesian family with idiopathic, autosomal recessive aplastic anemia. Both parents and a heterozygous sib had reduced platelet counts, whereas a sib with the wildtype sequence had a normal platelet count. In a later discussion of this family, Pecci et al. (2018) stated that bone marrow aplasia had been recognized at ages 16 and 28 years in the 2 patients, that 2 other sibs had died of aplastic anemia in adolescence, and that it had not been reported whether the aplasia was preceded by congenital thrombocytopenia.

Seo et al. (2017) reported 5 individuals from 3 unrelated families with severe thrombocytopenia that progressed to trilineage bone marrow failure. Four of the children underwent hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, and all had poor graft function with persistent severe cytopenias, even after repeated transplants from different donors. THPO was absent from the patients' serum. In the 2 surviving patients, trilineage hematopoiesis improved after treatment with a THPO receptor agonist.

Pecci et al. (2018) reported 3 affected sibs born to Egyptian parents who were first cousins. The proband, a boy, presented at age 3.5 years with ecchymotic patches. He had very severe thrombocytopenia (platelets 3 x 10(9)/l), hyporegenerative anemia, and severe neutropenia, with normal platelet size. He also had preauricular skin tags and a left divergent squint. The bone marrow was hypocellular with very rare immature megakaryocytes, and the karyotype was normal. Fanconi anemia and paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria were excluded, and idiopathic aplastic anemia was diagnosed. He then had recurrent major hemorrhages (epistaxis, gum and rectal bleeding) and recurrent severe infections, which required frequent platelet and red cell transfusions. He did not respond to cyclosporine A or oxymetholone. His brother had severe isolated thrombocytopenia (26 x 10(9)/l) at 7 months of age, with a markedly decreased number of bone marrow megakaryocytes, and had recurrent spontaneous bleeding from age 1.6 years. A younger sister developed a hematoma at the vaccination site at age 2 months and was found to have anemia and severe thrombocytopenia. Serum THPO levels in the 2 older sibs before treatment were within the normal range (10.9 and 14.1 pg/ml). Pecci et al. (2018) noted that this was inappropriately low, since serum THPO is markedly increased in bone marrow aplasia, including CAMT due to MPL mutations. They considered the clinical picture indistinguishable from CAMT caused by MPL mutations and suggested that the hematologic picture worsens with age.

Capaci et al. (2023) reported a boy born to first-cousin Muslim Palestinian parents. He presented at age 3.5 years with easy bruising and thrombocytopenia (platelet count 21 x 10(9)/L) with a normal mean platelet volume. His platelet count shortly after birth had been 70 x 10(9)/L, and at age 2 years, when he was diagnosed with urethral stenosis, it was 20 x 10(9)/L. Apart from mild occasional neutropenia, he had no other cytopenias. Two bone marrow biopsies showed normocellular marrow with severe megakaryocyte hypoplasia, a few small immature forms, and mild dyserythropoiesis. Chromosome breakage testing excluded Fanconi anemia, and the bone marrow karyotype was normal. His serum THPO concentration was 126 pg/mL (reference range 15-80 pg/mL for children under 15 years). The authors considered this lower than expected given his severely reduced megakaryocyte and platelet mass. His mother had mild thrombocytopenia, with platelet counts stable at around 80 x 10(9)/L, and his younger sib had platelet counts of 130 and 160 x 10(9)/L.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CAMT2 in the families reported by Dasouki et al. (2013), Pecci et al. (2018), and Capaci et al. (2023) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Heterozygous carriers in the families reported by Dasouki et al. (2013) and Capaci et al. (2023) had mild thrombocytopenia.

### Molecular Genetics

In 2 sibs from a Micronesian family with autosomal recessive aplastic anemia, Dasouki et al. (2013) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a homozygous c.112C-T mutation in the THPO gene, resulting in an arg38-to-cys (R38C) substitution (R17C in the mature protein) in the receptor-binding domain. In silico modeling predicted that the extra cysteine could disrupt the normal disulfide bonds and impair receptor binding. Medium containing the mutant protein was 2- to 3-fold less able to sustain the proliferation of THPO-dependent UT7-TPO cells. Both parents and a sib who were heterozygous for the mutation had reduced platelet counts.

In 5 individuals from 3 unrelated families with severe thrombocytopenia that progressed to bone marrow failure, Seo et al. (2017) used exome and targeted sequencing to identify homozygous mutations in the THPO gene. Affected children in 2 families were homozygous for R99W, and the affected child in the third family was homozygous for R157X. Both mutations led to absence of THPO from the patients' serum.

In 3 sibs from a consanguineous Egyptian family with CAMT, Pecci et al. (2018) found no variants in the MPL gene. Sanger sequencing identified a homozygous c.355C-T mutation in the THPO gene, resulting in an arg119-to-cys (R119C) substitution (R98 in the mature protein). The methods section gives the change as c.335C-T. The mutation segregated with the disorder: the parents were heterozygous, and it was absent from the SNP, 1000 Genomes, and ExAC databases. In transfected HEK293T cells, the R119C and R38C mutants were expressed and were as stable as wildtype. However, THPO concentrations in the culture supernatants were about 23-fold lower than wildtype, suggesting a defect in secretion. At equal concentrations, R119C THPO had about 45 to 53% of wildtype activity in supporting UT7-TPO cell proliferation, and it showed defective activation of STAT5, ERK1/2, and AKT downstream of MPL.

In a boy with CAMT born to consanguineous Palestinian parents, Capaci et al. (2023) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a homozygous c.-323C-T substitution (chr3:g.184096040G-A, GRCh37) in the promoter region of the THPO gene, 45 nucleotides upstream of the transcription start site of transcript variant 1. The parents and a younger sib were heterozygous, and 2 healthy sibs were homozygous for the wildtype allele. The variant was listed in dbSNP but not in gnomAD, and no variants were found in MPL, MECOM, HOXA11, RBM8A, or other bone marrow failure genes. The c.-323C nucleotide lay within predicted binding sites for ETS1 and STAT4. In HEK293T cells, the variant reduced reporter activity by almost 80%. In vitro chromatin immunoprecipitation showed less binding of ETS1 and STAT4 to the mutant promoter. Knockdown of ETS1 and STAT4 in HepG2 cells strongly reduced THPO mRNA and protein levels.

### Diagnosis

Pecci et al. (2018) and Capaci et al. (2023) noted that serum THPO is markedly increased in bone marrow aplasia, including CAMT due to MPL mutations, but was not increased in patients with THPO mutations. They suggested that measuring serum THPO could help distinguish patients with THPO mutations. Capaci et al. (2023) recommended that molecular analysis of THPO, including its regulatory regions, be part of the diagnostic workup for suspected CAMT.

### Clinical Management

Seo et al. (2017) found that bone marrow failure caused by THPO mutations did not respond to hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, owing to a mechanism extrinsic to hematopoietic cells. In the 2 surviving patients, trilineage hematopoiesis improved with a THPO receptor agonist.

Pecci et al. (2018) treated all 3 affected sibs with romiplostim. All 3 had trilineage hematologic responses, remission of bleeding and infections, and became independent of transfusions. These responses were maintained for up to 6.5 years, and no side effects were observed.

Capaci et al. (2023) treated their patient with eltrombopag, starting at 1.4 mg/kg/day and increasing to 2.8 mg/kg/day. His platelet count rose to 125 x 10(9)/L, and his easy bruising resolved.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 APLASTIC ANEMIA / CONGENITAL AMEGAKARYOCYTIC THROMBOCYTOPENIA 2**  
THPO, ARG38CYS [c.112C>T; p.Arg38Cys; p.R38C; R17C]

In 2 sibs from a Micronesian family with autosomal recessive aplastic anemia, Dasouki et al. (2013) identified a homozygous c.112C-T transition in the THPO gene, resulting in an arg38-to-cys (R38C) substitution (R17C in the mature protein, after removal of the 21-amino acid signal peptide) in the receptor-binding domain. Medium containing the mutant protein was 2- to 3-fold less able to sustain proliferation of UT7-TPO cells. The heterozygous parents and a heterozygous sib had reduced platelet counts.

Pecci et al. (2018) showed that the R38C mutant, like R119C, was efficiently expressed and stable in HEK293T cells but was present at about 23-fold lower concentration in culture supernatants, indicating defective secretion. It also had reduced activity in UT7-TPO proliferation assays.

**.0002 CONGENITAL AMEGAKARYOCYTIC THROMBOCYTOPENIA 2**

In affected children from 2 of 3 unrelated families with severe thrombocytopenia that progressed to trilineage bone marrow failure, Seo et al. (2017) identified a homozygous arg99-to-trp (R99W) mutation in the THPO gene using exome and targeted sequencing. The mutation resulted in a lack of THPO in the patients' serum.

**.0003 CONGENITAL AMEGAKARYOCYTIC THROMBOCYTOPENIA 2**

In the affected child from 1 of 3 unrelated families with severe thrombocytopenia that progressed to trilineage bone marrow failure, Seo et al. (2017) identified a homozygous arg157-to-ter (R157X) mutation in the THPO gene. The mutation resulted in a lack of THPO in the patient's serum.

**.0004 CONGENITAL AMEGAKARYOCYTIC THROMBOCYTOPENIA 2**  
THPO, ARG119CYS [c.355C>T; p.Arg119Cys; p.R119C]

In 3 sibs with CAMT from a consanguineous Egyptian family, Pecci et al. (2018) identified a homozygous c.355C-T transition in the THPO gene, resulting in an arg119-to-cys (R119C) substitution (R98 in the mature protein) at a highly conserved residue in helix C of the receptor-binding domain. The methods section gives the change as c.335C-T. The parents were heterozygous, and the variant was absent from the SNP, 1000 Genomes, and ExAC databases. In functional studies, secretion of the mutant protein was markedly impaired, its stability was normal, and its activity in stimulating MPL was about 50% of wildtype. Serum THPO levels in the patients were not increased.

**.0005 CONGENITAL AMEGAKARYOCYTIC THROMBOCYTOPENIA 2**  
THPO, -323C-T [c.-323C>T; chr3:g.184096040G>A]

In a boy with CAMT born to Palestinian first-cousin parents, Capaci et al. (2023) identified a homozygous c.-323C-T substitution (chr3:g.184096040G-A, GRCh37) in the promoter region of the THPO gene, found by whole-exome sequencing. His heterozygous mother and a heterozygous younger sib had mild thrombocytopenia. The variant abolished predicted ETS1 and STAT4 binding sites and reduced promoter-driven reporter activity by almost 80%. It also impaired binding of the 2 transcription factors in chromatin immunoprecipitation assays. The patient's serum THPO level (126 pg/mL) was lower than expected for amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia.

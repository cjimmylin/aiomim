### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia-2 (CAMT2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the thrombopoietin gene (THPO; 600044) on chromosome 3q27.

### Description

Congenital amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia-2 (CAMT2) is an autosomal recessive bone marrow failure disorder. It begins with thrombocytopenia and a lack of megakaryocytes and can progress to pancytopenia and aplastic anemia. It is caused by loss of function of thrombopoietin, the ligand of the MPL receptor. Thrombopoietin is mainly made in the liver, so hematopoietic stem cell transplantation does not correct the defect. Patients have responded to thrombopoietin receptor agonists such as romiplostim.

### Clinical Features

Dasouki et al. (2015) described a consanguineous Micronesian family in which several members had autosomal recessive aplastic anemia and pancytopenia. ⚠︎

Seo et al. (2017) reported patients with inherited bone marrow failure caused by THPO mutations. Their bone marrow failure did not respond to hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, but blood counts improved with thrombopoietin receptor agonist treatment.

Pecci et al. (2018) reported children with congenital amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia due to a homozygous THPO mutation. Treatment with romiplostim was effective.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CAMT2 in the families reported by Dasouki et al. (2015) and later authors was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Heterogeneity

Congenital amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia-1 (CAMT1) is caused by mutation in the gene encoding the thrombopoietin receptor (MPL).

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of a consanguineous Micronesian family with aplastic anemia, Dasouki et al. (2015) used exome sequencing to identify a homozygous missense mutation in the THPO gene (R38C). ⚠︎

Seo et al. (2017) identified homozygous THPO mutations, including R99W, in patients with bone marrow failure.

Pecci et al. (2018) identified a homozygous THPO missense mutation in patients with CAMT who responded to romiplostim.

### Clinical Management

Seo et al. (2017) and Pecci et al. (2018) found that thrombopoietin receptor agonists such as romiplostim improved hematopoiesis in patients with THPO mutations. In contrast, hematopoietic stem cell transplantation did not correct the defect, because thrombopoietin is produced outside the bone marrow.

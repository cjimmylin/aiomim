### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia-2 (CAMT2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the gene encoding thrombopoietin (THPO; 600044) on chromosome 3q27.1.

### Description

Congenital amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia-2 (CAMT2) is an autosomal recessive bone marrow failure disorder. It begins with thrombocytopenia and a lack of megakaryocytes and can progress to pancytopenia and aplastic anemia (Pecci et al., 2018). Dasouki et al. (2013) attributed the disorder to loss of function of thrombopoietin, the ligand of the MPL receptor. Pecci et al. (2018) noted that thrombopoietin is mainly made in the liver, so hematopoietic stem cell transplantation is not expected to correct the defect, and patients have responded to the thrombopoietin receptor agonist romiplostim. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Dasouki et al. (2013) described 2 sibs from a Micronesian family with autosomal recessive aplastic anemia.

Seo et al. (2017) reported patients with inherited bone marrow failure caused by THPO mutations. Their bone marrow failure did not respond to hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, but blood counts improved with thrombopoietin receptor agonist treatment.

Pecci et al. (2018) reported a family with 3 children who had congenital amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia caused by a homozygous THPO mutation. Treatment with romiplostim was effective in all 3 affected children.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CAMT2 in the family reported by Dasouki et al. (2013) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Recessive inheritance was also seen in the family reported by Pecci et al. (2018), in which the affected sibs were homozygous and the parents heterozygous for the THPO mutation.

### Heterogeneity

Pecci et al. (2018) noted that congenital amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia-1 (CAMT1) is caused by mutation in the gene encoding the thrombopoietin receptor (MPL).

### Molecular Genetics

In 2 sibs from a Micronesian family with aplastic anemia, Dasouki et al. (2013) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a homozygous c.112C-T missense mutation in the THPO gene, resulting in an arg38-to-cys (R38C) substitution (R17C when the 21-amino acid signal peptide is excluded).

Seo et al. (2017) identified homozygous THPO mutations, including R99W, in patients with bone marrow failure.

In patients with CAMT, Pecci et al. (2018) identified a homozygous c.355C-T missense mutation in the THPO gene, resulting in an arg119-to-cys (R119C) substitution. The patients responded to romiplostim.

### Clinical Management

Pecci et al. (2018) found that the thrombopoietin receptor agonist romiplostim improved hematopoiesis in their patients with THPO mutations and in patients reported by Seo et al. (2017). Seo et al. (2017) found that hematopoietic stem cell transplantation did not correct the defect in patients with THPO mutations. Pecci et al. (2018) explained this by the fact that thrombopoietin is produced outside the hematopoietic cells. ⚠︎

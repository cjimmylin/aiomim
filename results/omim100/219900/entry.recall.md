### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that late-onset juvenile or adolescent nephropathic cystinosis is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the CTNS gene (606272) on chromosome 17p13.2. ⚠︎

### Description

Thoene et al. (1999) described an intermediate form of cystinosis. It results either from one mild CTNS mutation combined with one mutation known to cause nephropathic cystinosis, or from homozygosity for a predicted mild mutation. Attard et al. (1999) stated that cystinosis is caused by a defect in the transport of cystine across the lysosomal membrane. They noted that structure predictions suggested the gene product, cystinosin, is a novel integral lysosomal membrane protein. According to Attard et al. (1999), late-onset cystinosis is a rarer form of the disorder, with symptoms beginning between 12 and 15 years of age. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Langman et al. (1985) reported renal failure in a sibship with late-onset cystinosis.

Attard et al. (1999) described late-onset cystinosis as having its onset of symptoms between 12 and 15 years of age.

### Inheritance

Thoene et al. (1999) found that the intermediate form of cystinosis occurs either in compound heterozygotes for a mild CTNS mutation and a mutation known to cause nephropathic cystinosis, or in homozygotes for a predicted mild mutation. These biallelic genotypes imply autosomal recessive inheritance. Because affected individuals carry mutations known to cause nephropathic cystinosis, the intermediate form is allelic to nephropathic cystinosis. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Thoene et al. (1999) identified mutations in the CTNS gene in patients with intermediate cystinosis. The disease occurred either from one mild mutation combined with one known to cause nephropathic cystinosis, or from homozygosity for a predicted mild mutation.

Attard et al. (1999) identified CTNS mutations in patients with late-onset cystinosis. In their patients with infantile nephropathic cystinosis, the mutations on both alleles were predicted to cause loss of cystinosin function. These mutations included a common deletion of approximately 65 kb. ⚠︎

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Attard et al. (1999) found that the severity of the cystinosis phenotype varied with the mutations in the CTNS gene. Of 25 patients with infantile nephropathic cystinosis, 12 had two severely truncating mutations. The other 13 had missense mutations or in-frame deletions, which were predicted to disrupt transmembrane domains and cause loss of protein function. In two late-onset patients, the mutations affected functionally unimportant regions of cystinosin, which accounted for their milder phenotype.

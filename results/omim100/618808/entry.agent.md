### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that intellectual developmental disorder with poor growth and with or without seizures or ataxia (IDPOGSA) is caused by homozygous mutation in the ABCA2 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

IDPOGSA is an autosomal recessive disorder associated with biallelic truncating variants in ABCA2. Maddirevula et al. (2019) described affected individuals with global developmental delay and intellectual disability, and Aslam and Naz (2019) reported sibs from a consanguineous family with cerebellar ataxia and dysarthria, a presentation they regarded as an extension of the phenotypic spectrum.

### Clinical Features

Maddirevula et al. (2019) searched a database of 799 research and 1,750 clinical exomes for homozygous likely deleterious variants in genes with tentative disease links. They identified 2 families with global developmental delay and intellectual disability. These families carried homozygous truncating variants in ABCA2. The authors considered this phenotype sufficiently different from the previously reported amyotrophic lateral sclerosis to possibly represent a distinct allelic disorder. Facial photographs of the index patient (16DG0071) showed no apparent gross dysmorphism.

Aslam and Naz (2019) reported 2 sibs from a consanguineous Pakistani family who had a movement disorder. Their symptoms were consistent with cerebellar ataxia with dysarthria. Magnetic resonance imaging of the older sib showed no brain abnormalities. Low density lipoprotein levels were elevated in blood samples from both affected individuals. The authors noted that the phenotype closely matched that of Abca2 knockout mice.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of IDPOGSA in the families reported by Maddirevula et al. (2019) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Homozygous truncating ABCA2 variants, identified through autozygome and exome analysis, fully segregated with the disorder in 2 families. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Maddirevula et al. (2019) used autozygome and exome analysis to study patients with suspected Mendelian phenotypes. In 2 families with global developmental delay and intellectual disability, they identified homozygous truncating mutations in the ABCA2 gene: c.740dupT (Gly248ArgfsTer38) and c.1027C-T (Gln343Ter). The mutations fully segregated with the disorder in the families.

Aslam and Naz (2019) performed whole-exome sequencing in 2 sibs from a consanguineous Pakistani family with cerebellar ataxia and dysarthria. They identified a frameshift mutation in the ABCA2 gene: c.4993delG (Val1665TyrfsTer36). Sanger sequencing showed that the mutation segregated with the disorder. It was absent from all publicly available databases and from ethnically matched controls. The abstract did not state the zygosity of the mutation.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that intellectual developmental disorder with poor growth and with or without seizures or ataxia (IDPOGSA) is caused by homozygous mutation in the ABCA2 gene (600047) on chromosome 9q34. ⚠︎

### Description

IDPOGSA is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by mutation in the ABCA2 gene. Reported features include global developmental delay and intellectual disability (Maddirevula et al., 2019). In one family, cerebellar ataxia with dysarthria was the reported presentation (Aslam and Naz, 2019).

### Clinical Features

Maddirevula et al. (2019) described 2 families with global developmental delay and intellectual disability. The index patient (16DG0071) had no obvious gross dysmorphism. The authors suggested that this phenotype was a distinct allelic disorder, different from the amyotrophic lateral sclerosis previously reported in association with ABCA2.

Aslam and Naz (2019) reported 2 sibs from a consanguineous Pakistani family with cerebellar ataxia and dysarthria. Brain MRI of the older sib was normal. Both affected sibs had elevated blood levels of low density lipoproteins.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of IDPOGSA in the families reported by Maddirevula et al. (2019) and Aslam and Naz (2019) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Maddirevula et al. (2019) found homozygous ABCA2 mutations that segregated with the disorder, and the sibs reported by Aslam and Naz (2019) were born to consanguineous parents. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of 2 families with global developmental delay and intellectual disability, Maddirevula et al. (2019) used autozygome and exome analysis to identify homozygous truncating mutations in the ABCA2 gene. In one family the mutation was a frameshift, c.740dupT (p.Gly248Argfs*38; NM_212533.2). In the other it was a nonsense mutation, c.1027C-T (p.Gln343*; NM_212533.2). In each family the mutation fully segregated with the phenotype.

In 2 sibs from a consanguineous Pakistani family with ataxia and dysarthria, Aslam and Naz (2019) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a frameshift mutation in the ABCA2 gene, c.4993delG (p.Val1665TyrfsTer36; NM_212533.2). Sanger sequencing showed that the mutation segregated with the disorder. It was absent from all publicly available databases and from ethnically matched controls.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Aslam and Naz (2019) noted that recessive ABCA2 variants had previously been reported in patients with intellectual disability and global developmental delay. They concluded that their family had a distinct phenotype of ataxia with dysarthria, which reflected pleiotropic effects of ABCA2 mutation. They also stated that this phenotype closely matched that of Abca2 knockout mice.

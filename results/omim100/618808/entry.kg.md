### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that intellectual developmental disorder with poor growth and with or without seizures or ataxia (IDPOGSA) is caused by homozygous mutation in the ABCA2 gene (600047) on chromosome 9q34. ⚠︎

### Description

IDPOGSA is an autosomal recessive disorder characterized by global developmental delay and intellectual disability (Maddirevula et al., 2019). Some affected individuals have cerebellar ataxia with dysarthria (Aslam and Naz, 2019). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Maddirevula et al. (2019) described 2 families with global developmental delay and intellectual disability. The index patient (16DG0071) did not have obvious gross dysmorphism. The authors suggested that this phenotype is a distinct allelic disorder, different from the amyotrophic lateral sclerosis previously reported with ABCA2.

Aslam and Naz (2019) reported 2 sibs from a consanguineous Pakistani family who had cerebellar ataxia with dysarthria. Brain MRI of the older sib was normal. Both affected individuals had elevated levels of low density lipoproteins in the blood.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of IDPOGSA in the families reported by Maddirevula et al. (2019) and by Aslam and Naz (2019) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Maddirevula et al. (2019) identified homozygous ABCA2 variants that fully segregated with the phenotype in 2 families, and Aslam and Naz (2019) identified a segregating ABCA2 variant in affected sibs from a consanguineous family. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of 2 families with global developmental delay and intellectual disability, Maddirevula et al. (2019) used autozygome and exome analysis to identify homozygous truncating mutations in the ABCA2 gene. In one family, they found a 1-bp duplication (c.740dupT; NM_212533.2), resulting in a frameshift and premature termination (Gly248ArgfsTer38); in the second family, they found a nonsense mutation (c.1027C-T; NM_212533.2), resulting in a gln343-to-ter (Q343X) substitution. In each family, the variant fully segregated with the phenotype.

In 2 sibs from a consanguineous Pakistani family with ataxia and dysarthria, Aslam and Naz (2019) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a 1-bp deletion in the ABCA2 gene (c.4993delG; NM_212533.2), resulting in a frameshift and premature termination (Val1665TyrfsTer36). Sanger sequencing showed that the mutation segregated with the disorder. It was absent from all publicly available databases and from ethnically matched controls.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Aslam and Naz (2019) noted that recessive ABCA2 variants had previously been reported in patients with intellectual disability and global developmental delay. They concluded that their family showed a distinct ataxia-with-dysarthria phenotype, reflecting pleiotropic effects of ABCA2 mutation, and stated that the phenotype closely matched that of Abca2 knockout mice.

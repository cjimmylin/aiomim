### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that epidermolysis bullosa simplex-3, localized or generalized intermediate, with BP230 deficiency (EBS3) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous (biallelic) mutation in the DST gene (113810) on chromosome 6p12.1. ⚠︎

### Description

EBS3 is an autosomal recessive form of epidermolysis bullosa simplex. It is caused by mutations in DST, which encodes an epithelial isoform of dystonin, BPAG1-e (BP230) (Ganani et al., 2021; Groves et al., 2010). The skin is fragile, with blisters and erosions that arise after trauma or spontaneously (Groves et al., 2010).

### Clinical Features

Groves et al. (2010) reported a 38-year-old man with an inherited skin fragility disorder. Throughout his life he had had generalized blisters and erosions, induced by trauma or arising spontaneously, especially around the ankles. Beginning at age 37 years he had episodes of numbness in his limbs. He had also been diagnosed with CADASIL, and the authors noted that this made it harder to tell whether the neurologic symptoms were due to the DST mutation.

### Inheritance

Groves et al. (2010) found a homozygous DST mutation in an affected man and concluded that the disorder is autosomal recessive. Liu et al. (2012) also reported autosomal recessive EBS due to loss of BPAG1-e expression. Ganani et al. (2021) stated that DST-associated EBS is transmitted as an autosomal recessive trait.

### Molecular Genetics

Groves et al. (2010) studied a 38-year-old man with autosomal recessive EBS. They identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in the DST gene, gln1124-to-ter (Q1124X), which lies in the region encoding the coiled-coil domain of the epithelial isoform BPAG1-e (BP230). In the patient's skin, the mutation led to loss of the hemidesmosomal inner plaques and complete absence of BPAG1-e immunostaining. Immunolabeling for plectin, beta-4 integrin, and type XVII collagen was reduced. The authors concluded that BPAG1-e pathology underlies a new form of autosomal recessive EBS.

Liu et al. (2012) reported autosomal recessive EBS caused by loss of BPAG1-e expression.

Ganani et al. (2021) studied 5 patients with EBS from 3 families. Using whole-exome and direct sequencing, they identified biallelic mutations in the DST gene. Two of the variants had not been reported before: c.7097dupA (Y2366X) and c.7429delC, which causes a frameshift (Leu2477SerfsTer13).

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Ganani et al. (2021) reviewed 6 previously reported cases, which brought the total number of individuals with EBS due to DST variants to 11. They noted that the patients had distinct phenotypes regardless of the causative variant.

### Pathogenesis

Groves et al. (2010) studied the skin of a patient homozygous for the DST Q1124X mutation. The hemidesmosomal inner plaques were lost, and immunostaining for BPAG1-e was completely absent. Labeling for plectin, beta-4 integrin, and type XVII collagen was reduced.

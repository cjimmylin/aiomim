### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that epidermolysis bullosa simplex-3, localized or generalized intermediate, with BP230 deficiency (EBS3) is caused by homozygous mutation in the DST gene (113810) on chromosome 6p12, which encodes the epithelial isoform of bullous pemphigoid antigen-1 (BPAG1-e, also known as BP230).

### Description

EBS3 is an autosomal recessive form of epidermolysis bullosa simplex. It is caused by loss of the epithelial isoform of dystonin (BPAG1-e/BP230), a plakin-family protein of the hemidesmosomal inner plaque that links keratin intermediate filaments to the hemidesmosome. Affected individuals have trauma-induced skin blistering, mainly on the feet and other acral sites. The blistering is relatively mild and can be localized or more generalized. Blisters form within the basal keratinocytes. Patients do not have the neurologic disease caused by loss of the neuronal dystonin isoforms. ✗

### Clinical Features

Groves et al. (2010) reported a patient from a consanguineous family who had trauma-induced blistering of the skin, mainly affecting the feet. Ultrastructural examination of skin showed cleavage within the basal keratinocytes just above the hemidesmosomes. Hemidesmosomes lacked a recognizable inner plaque, and keratin filaments were poorly attached to them. Immunostaining showed no BPAG1-e in the skin. ✗

Liu et al. (2012) described another patient with autosomal recessive EBS and loss of BPAG1-e expression in the skin. The patient had trauma-induced blistering, and skin biopsy showed intraepidermal splitting in the basal cell layer. ✗

Later reports described more patients with DST-related EBS. Their blistering ranged from localized acral blistering to more widespread blistering, and none had sensory or autonomic neuropathy. ✗

### Inheritance

The finding of homozygous DST mutations in affected individuals from consanguineous families (Groves et al., 2010; Liu et al., 2012) indicated that EBS3 is inherited as an autosomal recessive trait. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Groves et al. (2010) studied a patient with EBS from a consanguineous family and found a homozygous nonsense mutation in the DST gene. The mutation lay in the exons that encode the coiled-coil domain of the epithelial isoform, BPAG1-e, and the parents were heterozygous carriers. The mutation caused complete loss of BPAG1-e protein in the skin. ✗

Liu et al. (2012) identified a homozygous loss-of-function mutation in the DST gene in a patient with autosomal recessive EBS. The mutation resulted in absent BPAG1-e expression in skin. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

BPAG1-e binds keratin intermediate filaments through its C-terminal domain and binds hemidesmosomal components, including integrin beta-4, through its N-terminal plakin domain. When BPAG1-e is lost, keratin filaments are no longer anchored to the hemidesmosome. As a result, the basal keratinocytes are fragile and split above the hemidesmosomes under mechanical stress. Mutations that affect only the epithelial isoform spare the neuronal and muscle isoforms of dystonin. This explains why patients with EBS3 have no neurologic disease. ✗

### Animal Model

Guo et al. (1995) found that mice with targeted disruption of the Bpag1 gene developed skin blistering caused by fragility of the basal keratinocytes, with hemidesmosomes lacking an inner plaque. These mice also developed severe sensory neurodegeneration, because the knockout affected the neuronal isoforms as well. ✗

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 EPIDERMOLYSIS BULLOSA SIMPLEX 3, LOCALIZED OR GENERALIZED INTERMEDIATE, WITH BP230 DEFICIENCY**  
DST, GLN1124TER [c.3370C>T; p.Gln1124Ter]

Groves et al. (2010) studied a patient with autosomal recessive EBS from a consanguineous family and identified a homozygous C-to-T transition in the DST gene. The change was reported as c.3370C-T and was predicted to cause a gln1124-to-ter (Q1124X) substitution in the coiled-coil domain of BPAG1-e. The parents were heterozygous for the mutation, and BPAG1-e protein was absent from the patient's skin. ✗

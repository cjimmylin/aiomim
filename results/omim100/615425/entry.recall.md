### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that epidermolysis bullosa simplex-3, localized or generalized intermediate, with BP230 deficiency (EBS3) is caused by homozygous mutation in the DST gene (113810) on chromosome 6p12.1, which encodes the epithelial isoform of bullous pemphigoid antigen-1 (BPAG1-e, also known as BP230). ⚠︎

### Description

EBS3 is an autosomal recessive form of epidermolysis bullosa simplex caused by loss of the epithelial isoform of dystonin, BPAG1-e (BP230), which results in loss of the hemidesmosomal inner plaques (Groves et al., 2010). Groves et al. (2010) described generalized trauma-induced blisters and erosions, particularly around the ankles. Epidermolysis bullosa simplex is characterized by skin fragility due to intraepidermal separation (Ganani et al., 2021).

### Clinical Features

Groves et al. (2010) reported a patient with generalized trauma-induced blisters and erosions, particularly around the ankles. Hemidesmosomal inner plaques were lost, and skin immunostaining for BPAG1-e was completely absent. The patient also had episodic numbness in his limbs, which started at the age of 37 years. Groves et al. (2010) suggested that these neurologic symptoms may also be due to the DST mutation.

Liu et al. (2012) reported autosomal recessive epidermolysis bullosa simplex due to loss of BPAG1-e expression.

Ganani et al. (2021) identified DST mutations in five patients belonging to three families with epidermolysis bullosa simplex.

### Inheritance

Groves et al. (2010) found a homozygous nonsense mutation in the DST gene in a patient with inherited skin fragility and identified this disorder as a new form of autosomal recessive epidermolysis bullosa simplex.

### Molecular Genetics

In a patient with EBS, Groves et al. (2010) found a homozygous nonsense mutation (Q1124X) in the DST gene, within the region encoding the coiled-coil domain of the epithelial isoform BPAG1-e. The mutation caused a complete absence of skin immunostaining for BPAG1-e.

In a patient with autosomal recessive EBS, Groves et al. (2010) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in the DST gene, resulting in a gln1124-to-ter (Q1124X) substitution in the coiled-coil domain of BPAG1-e. Skin immunostaining for BPAG1-e was completely absent in the patient.

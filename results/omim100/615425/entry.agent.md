### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that epidermolysis bullosa simplex-3, localized or generalized intermediate, with BP230 deficiency (EBS3) is caused by homozygous or other biallelic mutation in the DST gene, which encodes dystonin, including its epithelial isoform BPAG1-e (BP230). ⚠︎

### Description

Epidermolysis bullosa simplex (EBS) is a heterogeneous group of inherited disorders characterized by skin fragility due to intraepidermal separation. Ganani et al. (2021) noted that most cases result from heterozygous mutations in KRT5 or KRT14, whereas a minority of affected individuals carry mutations in non-keratin genes, including DST, which encodes an epithelial isoform of dystonin. DST-associated EBS is transmitted as an autosomal recessive trait.

### Clinical Features

Groves et al. (2010) reported a 38-year-old man with lifelong generalized trauma-induced spontaneous blisters and erosions, particularly around the ankles. He also experienced episodic numbness in his limbs beginning at age 37 years. The authors suggested that these neurologic symptoms might also be due to the DST mutation, but noted that he had a concomitant diagnosis of CADASIL, a cerebral small-vessel arteriopathy, which complicated genotype-phenotype interpretation. In the patient's skin, hemidesmosomal inner plaques were lost, immunostaining for BPAG1-e was completely absent, and labeling for plectin, the beta-4 integrin subunit, and type XVII collagen was reduced.

Ganani et al. (2021) reported 5 EBS patients from 3 families carrying biallelic DST mutations and reviewed an additional 6 cases from the literature, bringing the total number of individuals with EBS due to DST variants to 11. Patients displayed distinctive phenotypes regardless of the causative variant.

### Inheritance

Groves et al. (2010) identified a homozygous DST mutation in a patient with a new form of autosomal recessive EBS, and Liu et al. (2012) also reported autosomal recessive EBS due to loss of BPAG1-e expression. Ganani et al. (2021) stated that DST-associated EBS is transmitted as an autosomal recessive trait.

### Molecular Genetics

In a 38-year-old man with EBS3, Groves et al. (2010) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation (Q1124X) in the DST gene, in the region encoding the coiled-coil domain of the epithelial isoform BPAG1-e (BP230). The mutation was associated with loss of hemidesmosomal inner plaques and complete absence of BPAG1-e immunostaining in the skin.

Using whole-exome and direct sequencing, Ganani et al. (2021) identified biallelic DST mutations in 5 patients from 3 families with EBS. Two of the variants had not been reported before: c.7097dupA (Y2366X) and c.7429delC (L2477SfsTer13).

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

In their series of 5 patients and review of 6 previously reported cases, Ganani et al. (2021) found that patients displayed distinctive phenotypes regardless of the causative DST variant.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 EPIDERMOLYSIS BULLOSA SIMPLEX 3, LOCALIZED OR GENERALIZED INTERMEDIATE, WITH BP230 DEFICIENCY**  
DST, GLN1124TER [p.Gln1124X]

In a 38-year-old man with lifelong generalized trauma-induced blisters and erosions, Groves et al. (2010) identified a homozygous gln1124-to-ter (Q1124X) mutation in the DST gene, within the region encoding the coiled-coil domain of BPAG1-e. The patient's skin showed loss of hemidesmosomal inner plaques, complete absence of BPAG1-e immunostaining, and reduced labeling for plectin, beta-4 integrin, and type XVII collagen.

The abstract did not specify which patients carried this variant or its zygosity.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that ReNU syndrome (RENU) is caused by heterozygous mutation, typically arising de novo, in the RNU4-2 gene, which encodes the U4 small nuclear RNA, on chromosome 12q24.

### Description

ReNU syndrome is a neurodevelopmental disorder marked by global developmental delay, moderate to severe intellectual disability, hypotonia, and severely impaired or absent speech. Many patients also have seizures, microcephaly, short stature, distinctive facial features, and structural brain anomalies on imaging. The disorder is caused by de novo heterozygous mutations in a short, highly constrained region of the noncoding RNU4-2 gene, which encodes U4 snRNA, a component of the major spliceosome. It appears to be one of the most common monogenic causes of neurodevelopmental disorders. ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

The name ReNU was proposed as an abbreviation built from RNU4-2 and neurodevelopmental disorder (Chen et al., 2024; Greene et al., 2024). ✗

### Clinical Features

Chen et al. (2024) identified de novo variants in RNU4-2 in a large series of individuals with neurodevelopmental disorders, first in the Genomics England 100,000 Genomes Project and then in further cohorts. The patients had global developmental delay, intellectual disability, hypotonia, and severe speech impairment. Seizures, microcephaly, short stature, and brain imaging abnormalities were common, and the patients shared overlapping facial features.

Greene et al. (2024) independently found RNU4-2 variants in patients with neurodevelopmental disorders in the 100,000 Genomes Project and described a similar phenotype. They concluded that the disorder is among the most common monogenic neurodevelopmental disorders. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The heterozygous RNU4-2 mutations in ReNU syndrome occurred de novo in the patients reported by Chen et al. (2024) and Greene et al. (2024). ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Chen et al. (2024) used whole-genome sequencing in patients with neurodevelopmental disorders and found an excess of de novo variants in RNU4-2. The variants clustered in a short critical region of about 18 nucleotides. This region includes the stem III region and the adjacent T-loop, which are involved in U4-U6 pairing and in positioning the 5-prime splice site in the spliceosome. The most frequent variant was a single-nucleotide insertion, n.64_65insT. The variants were absent from population databases. RNA sequencing of patient blood samples showed changes in 5-prime splice site usage. Earlier studies had missed this gene because RNU4-2 is a small noncoding gene that exome capture kits cover poorly. ✗

Greene et al. (2024) independently identified de novo variants in the same region of RNU4-2 in patients from the 100,000 Genomes Project. The recurrent n.64_65insT variant was the most common. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Chen et al. (2024) proposed that variants in the critical region of U4 snRNA disrupt how the U4/U6 di-snRNP interacts with the 5-prime splice site during spliceosome assembly. This would alter 5-prime splice site selection, consistent with the splicing changes they observed in patient samples. ⚠︎

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive intellectual developmental disorder-43 (MRT43) is caused by homozygous mutation in the WASHC4 gene (KIAA1033) on chromosome 12q23. ⚠︎

### Description

Autosomal recessive intellectual developmental disorder-43 (MRT43) is a nonsyndromic form of intellectual disability. It is caused by loss of function of SWIP (WASHC4), a subunit of the WASH complex, which regulates actin polymerization at endosomes and endosomal trafficking.

### Clinical Features

Ropers et al. (2011) reported a large consanguineous Iranian family in which several members had nonsyndromic intellectual disability that was inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of MRT43 in the family reported by Ropers et al. (2011) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of a consanguineous Iranian family with intellectual disability, Ropers et al. (2011) found a homozygous mutation in the KIAA1033 gene, which encodes SWIP (WASHC4). They identified it by linkage mapping followed by sequencing of candidate genes. The mutation segregated with the disorder in the family. Patient cells had lower levels of SWIP and of other WASH complex subunits, which suggested that the mutation destabilizes the WASH complex. ✗

### Animal Model

Courtland et al. (2021) made knockin mice carrying the Swip mutation that corresponds to the human P1019R mutation. The mutant mice had less WASH complex. Their neurons showed endolysosomal abnormalities, and the mice developed cognitive and motor deficits.

It results in a pro1019-to-arg (P1019R) substitution. The mutation segregated with the disorder and reduced the levels of the WASH complex in patient cells. ✗

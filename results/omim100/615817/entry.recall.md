### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive intellectual developmental disorder-43 (MRT43) is caused by mutation in the WASHC4 gene (KIAA1033; 615748) on chromosome 12q23.3.

### Description

Autosomal recessive intellectual developmental disorder-43 (MRT43) is a nonsyndromic form of intellectual disability (Ropers et al., 2011). It is caused by loss of function of SWIP (WASHC4) (Courtland et al., 2021). SWIP is a member of the WASH complex, which is involved in actin polymerization and endosomal transport (Ropers et al., 2011). The WASH complex activates Arp2/3-dependent actin branching at the outer surface of endosomes (Courtland et al., 2021). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Ropers et al. (2011) reported a large consanguineous family with autosomal recessive nonsyndromic intellectual disability.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of MRT43 in the family reported by Ropers et al. (2011) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

In a large consanguineous family with autosomal recessive intellectual disability, Ropers et al. (2011) used homozygosity mapping, targeted exon enrichment, and high-throughput sequencing to identify a mutation in the gene encoding SWIP (WASHC4). The mutation led to significantly reduced SWIP levels and to destabilization of the entire WASH complex. Ropers et al. (2011) concluded that their findings suggested that SWIP is a novel gene for autosomal recessive intellectual disability.

### Animal Model

Courtland et al. (2021) made knockin mice carrying the Swip mutation that corresponds to the human pro1019-to-arg (P1019R) mutation. The mutant mice had less WASH complex. Their neurons showed endolysosomal abnormalities, and the mice developed cognitive and motor deficits.

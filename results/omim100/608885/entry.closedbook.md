### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that stomatin-deficient cryohydrocytosis with neurologic defects (SDCHCN) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the SLC2A1 gene (138140), which encodes the GLUT1 glucose transporter, on chromosome 1p34. ⚠︎

### Description

Stomatin-deficient cryohydrocytosis with neurologic defects is a rare disorder that combines a hereditary stomatocytosis with features of GLUT1 deficiency syndrome. The red cells are stomatocytic, leak sodium and potassium, and leak even more at low temperature (cryohydrocytosis). This causes hemolytic anemia and pseudohyperkalemia, meaning a falsely high potassium level in stored blood samples. The red cell membrane lacks stomatin. The neurologic features include seizures and intellectual disability (mental retardation). Some patients also have cataracts and massive hepatosplenomegaly.

### Nomenclature

Flatt et al. (2011) argued that this disorder is a form of GLUT1 deficiency syndrome in which the mutant transporter also leaks cations. The alternative title 'GLUT1 deficiency syndrome with pseudohyperkalemia and hemolysis' reflects that view. ✗

### Clinical Features

Fricke et al. (2004) reported new cases of stomatin-deficient hereditary stomatocytosis. They found that the cryohydrocytosis form of stomatin-deficient stomatocytosis came with neurologic dysfunction: seizures and developmental delay or intellectual disability. Cataracts and hepatosplenomegaly were also present. The red cells leaked cations, and the leak increased at low temperature. Stomatin was missing from the red cell membrane. ⚠︎

Bawazir et al. (2012) reported an infant with pseudohyperkalemia, hemolysis, and seizures. The infant was found to have a cation-leaky form of GLUT1 deficiency syndrome caused by an SLC2A1 mutation.

### Biochemical Features

Red cells from affected patients leaked sodium and potassium passively. The leak was greater at 0 degrees C than at 37 degrees C, which defines cryohydrocytosis. Stomatin was missing or greatly reduced in the red cell membrane. Like other stomatin-deficient stomatocytoses, the disorder is not caused by mutation in the stomatin gene itself. Flatt et al. (2011) found the glucose transporter GLUT1 in the red cell membrane but with reduced function. ✗

### Inheritance

The mutations reported by Flatt et al. (2011) were heterozygous and in at least one case arose de novo. This fits autosomal dominant inheritance. ✗

### Molecular Genetics

Flatt et al. (2011) studied patients with stomatin-deficient cryohydrocytosis and neurologic defects. In 2 unrelated patients they found heterozygous mutations in the SLC2A1 gene: a missense mutation, gly286-to-asp (G286D), and a 3-bp in-frame deletion that removes an isoleucine near residue 435/436. They expressed the mutant proteins in Xenopus oocytes. The mutant transporters let monovalent cations leak through, and glucose transport was reduced. The authors concluded that the mutant GLUT1 accounts for both the red cell cation leak and the neurologic features of GLUT1 deficiency. ✗

### Pathogenesis

Flatt et al. (2011) proposed that certain SLC2A1 mutations turn GLUT1 into a cation-leaky transporter. Reduced glucose transport across the blood-brain barrier causes the neurologic phenotype, and the cation leak in red cells causes the cryohydrocytosis and hemolysis. Why stomatin is lost from the membrane remains unclear; it may be a secondary effect. ✗

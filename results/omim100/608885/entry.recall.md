### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that stomatin-deficient cryohydrocytosis with neurologic defects (SDCHCN) is caused by mutation in the SLC2A1 gene (138140), which encodes the GLUT1 glucose transporter, on chromosome 1p34.2.

### Description

Stomatin-deficient cryohydrocytosis with neurologic defects is a rare disorder. Fricke et al. (2004) knew of only 2 cases. Flatt et al. (2011) described it as a form of stomatocytosis that is also a form of GLUT1 deficiency syndrome. The red cells are stomatocytic and deficient in stomatin (Flatt et al., 2011). They leak sodium and potassium, and they are extremely leaky at 0 degrees C, which gives the phenotype known as cryohydrocytosis (Fricke et al., 2004). Bawazir et al. (2012) showed that the leak causes hemolysis and causes pseudohyperkalemia, a falsely high potassium level in stored blood samples. The 2 patients reported by Fricke et al. (2004) had a congenital syndrome of mental retardation, seizures, cataracts, and massive hepatosplenomegaly. ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

Flatt et al. (2011) argued that stomatin-deficient cryohydrocytosis is a form of GLUT1 deficiency syndrome. In this form, the SLC2A1 mutations cause both loss of glucose transport and a cation leak.

### Clinical Features

Fricke et al. (2004) reported new cases of stomatin-deficient hereditary stomatocytosis. Two patients had stomatin-deficient cryohydrocytosis. Both showed a congenital syndrome of mental retardation, seizures, cataracts, and massive hepatosplenomegaly. Their red cells showed pronounced cation leaks at 37 degrees C, with markedly abnormal intracellular sodium and potassium concentrations. The leak was at its lowest at around 20 to 25 degrees C, so the cells were extremely leaky at 0 degrees C. The red cells were deficient in stomatin, but immunocytochemistry showed that the deficiency was not complete. ⚠︎

Bawazir et al. (2012) reported an infant with pseudohyperkalemia, hemolysis, and seizures. The infant had a cation-leaky form of GLUT1 deficiency syndrome caused by an SLC2A1 mutation.

### Biochemical Features

Flatt et al. (2011) described stomatin-deficient cryohydrocytosis as a form of stomatocytosis with a cold-induced cation leak. Stomatin was missing or greatly reduced in the red cells. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Flatt et al. (2011) noted that the hereditary stomatocytoses are a series of dominantly inherited hemolytic anemias. They did not state this specifically for stomatin-deficient cryohydrocytosis. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Flatt et al. (2011) studied 2 previously reported patients with stomatin-deficient cryohydrocytosis and found 2 mutations in the SLC2A1 gene. They expressed the mutant proteins in Xenopus oocytes. The mutations caused both loss of glucose transport and a cation leak.

### Pathogenesis

Flatt et al. (2011) proposed that certain SLC2A1 mutations turn GLUT1 into a transporter that leaks cations, as shown by expression studies in Xenopus oocytes. They noted that GLUT1 is the main glucose transporter in the mammalian blood-brain barrier and that GLUT1 deficiency syndromes cause an array of neurologic symptoms.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that retinitis pigmentosa-56 (RP56) is caused by homozygous mutation in the IMPG2 gene on chromosome 3.

### Description

Retinitis pigmentosa-56 (RP56) is an autosomal recessive form of retinitis pigmentosa, a heterogeneous group of inherited retinal diseases caused by progressive degeneration of the photoreceptor cells. Most patients have an early-onset form of RP with progressive visual field loss and deterioration of visual acuity (Bandah-Rozenfeld et al., 2010).

### Clinical Features

Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) studied 2 families, each with 3 affected sibs, one of Iraqi Jewish ancestry and one Dutch, as well as 10 additional index cases of Dutch, Israeli, Italian, and Pakistani origin. Most patients with IMPG2 mutations had an early-onset form of RP with progressive visual field loss and deterioration of visual acuity. The patient carrying a missense mutation, however, was diagnosed with maculopathy.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of RP56 in the families reported by Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance: affected sibs shared large homozygous regions encompassing IMPG2 and carried homozygous mutations that cosegregated with the disease.

### Mapping

By autozygosity mapping, Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) identified 2 families, each with 3 affected sibs, who shared large overlapping homozygous regions on chromosome 3 harboring the IMPG2 gene.

### Molecular Genetics

In 2 families with autosomal recessive RP identified by autozygosity mapping, Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) found homozygous mutations in the IMPG2 gene that cosegregated with the disease. The 3 affected sibs of Iraqi Jewish ancestry carried a nonsense mutation, and the Dutch family carried a 1.8-kb genomic deletion that removes exon 9 and results in the absence of 7 amino acids in a conserved SEA domain of the IMPG2 protein. In transiently transfected COS-1 cells, a construct expressing the wildtype SEA domain was properly targeted to the plasma membrane, whereas the mutant lacking the 7 amino acids appeared to be retained in the endoplasmic reticulum. Mutation analysis in 10 additional index cases of Dutch, Israeli, Italian, and Pakistani origin who had homozygous regions encompassing IMPG2 revealed 5 additional mutations: 4 nonsense mutations and 1 missense mutation affecting a highly conserved phenylalanine residue. Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) concluded that mutations in a structural component of the interphotoreceptor matrix can cause autosomal recessive RP.

Allelic Disorder In a screen of 49 families with vitelliform macular dystrophy without identified mutation in the BEST1 and PRPH2 genes, Meunier et al. (2014) found that IMPG2 was the causal gene in 1 family (2 patients), which had autosomal dominant transmission and carried a c.3230G-T (p.Cys1077Phe) mutation in IMPG2. Patients with IMPG1 or IMPG2 mutations had late onset and moderate visual impairment.

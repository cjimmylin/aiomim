### Description

Retinitis pigmentosa (RP) is a heterogeneous group of inherited retinal diseases caused by progressive degeneration of the photoreceptor cells. RP56 is an autosomal recessive form caused by mutation in IMPG2, which encodes interphotoreceptor matrix proteoglycan-2, a structural component of the interphotoreceptor matrix. Most affected individuals have early-onset RP with progressive visual field loss and worsening visual acuity (Bandah-Rozenfeld et al., 2010).

### Clinical Features

Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) studied 2 families, each with 3 affected sibs, and 10 additional index cases of Dutch, Israeli, Italian, and Pakistani origin. Most patients with IMPG2 mutations had early-onset RP with progressive visual field loss and deterioration of visual acuity. However, the patient with a missense mutation was diagnosed with maculopathy.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of RP56 in the families reported by Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) was autosomal recessive: affected individuals carried homozygous IMPG2 mutations that cosegregated with the disease.

### Mapping

Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) used autozygosity mapping to identify 2 families, each with 3 affected sibs. In each family, the affected sibs shared large overlapping homozygous regions on chromosome 3 that contained the IMPG2 gene.

### Molecular Genetics

In 2 families with autosomal recessive RP, Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) sequenced the IMPG2 gene in the index cases and found homozygous mutations that cosegregated with the disease. The 3 affected sibs of Iraqi Jewish ancestry had a homozygous nonsense mutation. The Dutch family had a homozygous 1.8-kb genomic deletion that removes exon 9, resulting in the loss of 7 amino acids from a conserved SEA domain of the IMPG2 protein. In transiently transfected COS-1 cells, a construct expressing the wildtype SEA domain was correctly targeted to the plasma membrane, whereas the mutant lacking the 7 amino acids appeared to be retained in the endoplasmic reticulum. Mutation analysis in 10 additional index cases of Dutch, Israeli, Italian, and Pakistani origin with homozygous regions encompassing IMPG2 identified 5 more mutations: 4 nonsense mutations and 1 missense mutation affecting a highly conserved phenylalanine residue.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Among the patients with IMPG2 mutations reported by Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010), most had early-onset RP. The single patient with a missense mutation, which affected a highly conserved phenylalanine residue, was diagnosed with maculopathy.

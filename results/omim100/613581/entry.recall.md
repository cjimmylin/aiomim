### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that retinitis pigmentosa-56 (RP56) is caused by homozygous mutation in the IMPG2 gene (607056) on chromosome 3.

### Description

Retinitis pigmentosa-56 (RP56) is an autosomal recessive form of retinitis pigmentosa (Bandah-Rozenfeld et al., 2010). Retinitis pigmentosa is a heterogeneous group of inherited retinal diseases caused by progressive degeneration of the photoreceptor cells. Patients have progressive visual-field loss (Bandah-Rozenfeld et al., 2010). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) studied 2 families with autosomal recessive retinitis pigmentosa, each with 3 affected sibs. Most patients with IMPG2 mutations had progressive visual-field loss. The patient with the missense mutation was diagnosed with maculopathy rather than retinitis pigmentosa.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of RP56 in the families reported by Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Mapping

Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) used homozygosity (autozygosity) mapping in families with autosomal recessive retinitis pigmentosa and identified a homozygous region on chromosome 3q that contained the IMPG2 gene.

### Heterogeneity

Meunier et al. (2014) reported a heterozygous mutation in the IMPG2 gene in a family with vitelliform macular dystrophy with autosomal dominant transmission.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of families with autosomal recessive retinitis pigmentosa, Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) found homozygous mutations in the IMPG2 gene, which encodes interphotoreceptor matrix proteoglycan-2, a constituent of the interphotoreceptor matrix. The mutations cosegregated with the disease in the families. Most of the mutations were nonsense mutations predicted to truncate the protein. ⚠︎

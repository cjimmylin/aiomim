### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that retinitis pigmentosa-56 (RP56) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the IMPG2 gene (607056) on chromosome 3q12. ⚠︎

### Description

Retinitis pigmentosa-56 (RP56) is an autosomal recessive form of retinitis pigmentosa, a progressive degeneration of the retina in which rod photoreceptors are mainly affected first. Patients have night blindness and progressive loss of peripheral vision, and the macula may also be involved. ✗

### Clinical Features

Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) studied several families with autosomal recessive retinitis pigmentosa. Affected individuals had night blindness and constriction of the visual field, with fundus findings typical of RP, including bone spicule pigmentation and attenuated retinal vessels. Macular involvement was also seen in some patients. ✗

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of RP56 in the families reported by Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Mapping

Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) used homozygosity mapping in families with autosomal recessive RP and identified a homozygous region on chromosome 3q that contained the IMPG2 gene.

### Heterogeneity

Heterozygous mutations in the IMPG2 gene have also been reported to cause a distinct, dominantly inherited phenotype, vitelliform macular dystrophy. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of families with autosomal recessive retinitis pigmentosa, Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) found homozygous or compound heterozygous mutations in the IMPG2 gene. IMPG2 encodes interphotoreceptor matrix proteoglycan-2, a part of the interphotoreceptor matrix that surrounds the photoreceptor outer segments. Most of the mutations were predicted to truncate the protein, and the mutations segregated with the disease in the families. ⚠︎

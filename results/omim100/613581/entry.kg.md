### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that retinitis pigmentosa-56 (RP56) is caused by homozygous mutation in the IMPG2 gene (607056) on chromosome 3q12.

### Description

Retinitis pigmentosa-56 (RP56) is an autosomal recessive retinal dystrophy. Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) reported that most affected patients had early-onset disease, with visual-field loss and declining visual acuity that both got worse over time.

### Clinical Features

Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) studied 2 families with retinitis pigmentosa, one of Iraqi Jewish ancestry and one Dutch, each with 3 affected sibs, and 10 more index cases of Dutch, Israeli, Italian, and Pakistani origin. Most patients with IMPG2 mutations had early-onset retinitis pigmentosa. Their visual-field loss and declining visual acuity both progressed over time.

### Inheritance

Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) found that RP56 shows autosomal recessive inheritance. Affected sibs in the families they studied were homozygous for IMPG2 mutations, and these mutations cosegregated with the disease.

### Mapping

Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) used autozygosity mapping to identify 2 families, one Iraqi Jewish and one Dutch, each with 3 affected sibs with retinitis pigmentosa. The affected sibs shared large overlapping homozygous regions on chromosome 3, and these regions contained the IMPG2 gene.

### Molecular Genetics

Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) studied 3 affected sibs of Iraqi Jewish ancestry with autosomal recessive retinitis pigmentosa. The sibs were homozygous for a nonsense mutation in the IMPG2 gene, which cosegregated with the disease in the family.

In a Dutch family with autosomal recessive retinitis pigmentosa, Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) found a homozygous 1.8-kb genomic deletion in the IMPG2 gene that removes exon 9. The deletion causes the loss of 7 amino acids in a conserved SEA domain, and it cosegregated with the disease. The authors tested the effect in transfected COS-1 cells. The wildtype SEA domain was targeted to the plasma membrane, but the mutant SEA domain lacking the 7 amino acids appeared to be retained in the endoplasmic reticulum.

Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) also analyzed IMPG2 in 10 more index cases of Dutch, Israeli, Italian, and Pakistani origin whose homozygous regions included IMPG2. They found 5 more mutations: 4 nonsense mutations and 1 missense mutation affecting a highly conserved phenylalanine residue.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Bandah-Rozenfeld et al. (2010) reported that the patient with the IMPG2 missense mutation was diagnosed with maculopathy rather than retinitis pigmentosa. The patients with nonsense mutations or the exon 9 deletion had retinitis pigmentosa. ⚠︎

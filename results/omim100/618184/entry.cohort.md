### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy-2 (CHN2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the MPZ gene (159440) on chromosome 1q23.3. ⚠︎

### Description

Congenital hypomyelination is a hereditary demyelinating peripheral neuropathy. It starts in early infancy and causes distal muscle weakness, hypotonia, areflexia, and severely slowed nerve conduction velocities (Mandich et al., 1999). Onset may be prenatal, neonatal, or early infantile. Most reported patients die within the first years of life, and histopathology shows absent or thin myelin sheaths (Smit et al., 2008).

### Clinical Features

Warner et al. (1996) studied patients with Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1B (CMT1B), Dejerine-Sottas syndrome (DSS), or congenital hypomyelination (CH). Each of these patients carried an MPZ mutation.

Mandich et al. (1999) described the clinical, morphologic, and immunohistochemical features of a patient with CH. The patient's clinical features were quite distinct from those of the more frequent disorders CMT1B and DSS.

Szigeti et al. (2003) reported a patient with congenital hypomyelination. The clinical picture was confounded by cranial nerve dysfunction, respiratory failure, and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. A muscle biopsy showed abnormal muscle fiber differentiation. ⚠︎

Kochanski et al. (2004) described the clinical and morphologic features of a patient with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy (CHN). They described CHN as a severe neuropathy with onset in early infancy. In CHN, sural nerve biopsy showed nonmyelinated and poorly myelinated axons, with basal lamina onion bulbs and no myelin breakdown products.

Smit et al. (2008) reported a long-term follow-up study of a family with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy. Affected members were found in 2 generations. The authors noted that CHN is a rare condition with prenatal, neonatal, or early infantile onset of hypotonia, paresis, and areflexia, and that most reported patients die within the first years of life. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Mandich et al. (1999) and Szigeti et al. (2003) each reported a de novo MPZ mutation in a patient with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy. Smit et al. (2008) described a family in which a heterozygous MPZ mutation was confirmed in 2 generations, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Warner et al. (1996) identified 5 novel mutations in the MPZ gene in patients with CMT1B, DSS, or congenital hypomyelination. MPZ encodes P0, the major structural protein of peripheral nerve myelin. The authors suggested that these disorders may not be distinct pathophysiologic entities. Instead, they may form a spectrum of related 'myelinopathies' caused by an underlying defect in myelination.

Mandich et al. (1999) found a de novo gln215-to-ter (Q215X) mutation in the MPZ gene in a patient with congenital hypomyelination. They concluded that this confirmed that CH is allelic with CMT1B and DSS, which have a less severe phenotype and a different clinical and neuropathologic profile. ⚠︎

Szigeti et al. (2003) identified a complex de novo heterozygous mutation in the MPZ gene in a patient with congenital hypomyelination. The mutation consisted of a 3-bp deletion of CTA at nucleotides 550 to 552 and an insertion of G at nucleotide 550. By conceptual translation it caused a frameshift. ⚠︎

Kochanski et al. (2004) identified a novel thr124-to-lys (T124K) mutation in the MPZ gene in a patient with CHN.

Smit et al. (2008) identified a heterozygous mutation in the MPZ gene in a family with CHN and confirmed it in 2 generations.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Warner et al. (1996) hypothesized that the differences in clinical severity among MPZ mutations depend on the type of mutation and its effect on protein function, that is, loss of function versus a dominant-negative effect.

### Pathogenesis

Szigeti et al. (2003) reported muscle biopsy findings in a patient with CHN due to an MPZ frameshift mutation. The findings suggested that abnormal innervation affects early postnatal muscle fiber differentiation.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy-2 (CHN2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding myelin protein zero (MPZ). ⚠︎

### Description

Congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy is a rare, severe hereditary demyelinating peripheral neuropathy with prenatal, neonatal, or early infantile onset of hypotonia, distal muscle weakness, and areflexia, with severe slowing of nerve conduction velocities (Mandich et al., 1999; Smit et al., 2008). Nerve biopsy shows absent or thin myelin sheaths (Smit et al., 2008). Smit et al. (2008) noted that most of the few patients described in the literature died within the first years of life. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Mandich et al. (1999) described the clinical, morphologic, and immunohistochemical features of a patient with congenital hypomyelination. The clinical features were considered quite distinct from those of the more frequent Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1B or Dejerine-Sottas syndrome.

Szigeti et al. (2003) reported a patient with congenital hypomyelination associated with cranial nerve dysfunction, respiratory failure, and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, which confounded the clinical picture. Muscle biopsy findings suggested an effect of abnormal innervation on early postnatal muscle fiber differentiation.

Kochanski et al. (2004) described the clinical and morphologic features of a patient with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy. They noted that sural nerve biopsy in this disorder characteristically shows nonmyelinated and poorly myelinated axons with basal lamina onion bulbs and lack of myelin breakdown products.

Smit et al. (2008) reported a long-term follow-up study of a family with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy affecting 2 generations.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous MPZ mutations in the patients reported by Mandich et al. (1999) and Szigeti et al. (2003) occurred de novo. Smit et al. (2008) confirmed a heterozygous MPZ mutation in 2 generations of a family, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Kochanski et al. (2004) noted that congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy can be inherited as an autosomal dominant or recessive trait and that mutations in the MTMR2, PMP22, EGR2, and MPZ genes had been found in affected patients.

### Molecular Genetics

Warner et al. (1996) identified 5 novel mutations in the MPZ gene in patients with Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1B, Dejerine-Sottas syndrome, or congenital hypomyelination. They suggested that these disorders represent a spectrum of related myelinopathies, and hypothesized that differences in clinical severity are related to the type of mutation and its effect on protein function (loss of function versus dominant negative).

In a patient with congenital hypomyelination, Mandich et al. (1999) identified a de novo gln215-to-ter (Q215X) mutation in the MPZ gene, confirming that congenital hypomyelination is allelic with disorders of less severe phenotype.

In a patient with congenital hypomyelination, Szigeti et al. (2003) identified a complex de novo mutation in the MPZ gene, consisting of a 3-bp deletion (CTA) of nucleotides 550 to 552 and insertion of a G at nucleotide 550, predicted to result in a frameshift. ⚠︎

In a patient with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy, Kochanski et al. (2004) identified a novel thr124-to-lys (T124K) mutation in the MPZ gene.

In a family with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy, Smit et al. (2008) identified a heterozygous mutation in the MPZ gene, confirmed in 2 generations.

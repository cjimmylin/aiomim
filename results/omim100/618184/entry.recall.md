### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy-2 (CHN2) is caused by mutation in the gene encoding myelin protein zero (MPZ; 159440) on chromosome 1q23.3.

### Description

Kochanski et al. (2004) described congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy (CHN) as a severe neuropathy. Smit et al. (2008) noted that onset is prenatal, neonatal or early infantile. Mandich et al. (1999) listed its features as hypotonia, distal muscle weakness, areflexia and severe slowing of nerve conduction velocities. According to Kochanski et al. (2004), sural nerve biopsy shows nonmyelinated and poorly myelinated axons and no myelin breakdown products. Mandich et al. (1999) concluded that congenital hypomyelination is allelic with less severe disorders, including Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1B (CMT1B) and Dejerine-Sottas syndrome (DSS).

### Inheritance

Kochanski et al. (2004) noted that CHN is inherited as an autosomal dominant or recessive trait. Mandich et al. (1999) reported a de novo MPZ mutation in a patient with congenital hypomyelination.

### Heterogeneity

Congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy is genetically heterogeneous. Kochanski et al. (2004) noted that mutations in several genes have been found in patients with CHN, including the EGR2 gene.

### Molecular Genetics

Warner et al. (1996) studied patients with inherited peripheral neuropathies and identified mutations in the MPZ gene in patients with CMT1B, DSS, or congenital hypomyelination (CH). They suggested that these disorders may not be distinct pathophysiologic entities. Instead, they proposed that the disorders represent a spectrum of related myelinopathies.

Mandich et al. (1999) identified a de novo MPZ mutation in a patient with congenital hypomyelination. They concluded that CH is allelic with disorders that have a less severe phenotype, such as CMT1B and DSS.

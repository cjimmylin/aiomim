### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy-2 (CHN2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding myelin protein zero (MPZ; 159440) on chromosome 1q23.3.

### Description

Congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy is a rare condition with onset of hypotonia, paresis, and areflexia before birth, in the neonatal period, or in early infancy. On histopathology, myelin sheaths are absent or thin. Most reported patients have died within the first years of life (Smit et al., 2008). Mutations had been described in the MPZ, EGR2, PMP22, and MTMR2 genes (Smit et al., 2008).

### Clinical Features

Mandich et al. (1999) described the clinical, morphologic, and immunohistochemical features of a patient with congenital hypomyelination. They characterized congenital hypomyelination as a hereditary demyelinating peripheral neuropathy with onset in early infancy, distal muscle weakness, hypotonia, areflexia, and severely slowed nerve conduction velocities. They reported that the patient's clinical features were quite distinct from those of CMT1B or Dejerine-Sottas syndrome.

Szigeti et al. (2003) reported a patient with congenital hypomyelination who had cranial nerve dysfunction, respiratory failure, and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. The cardiomyopathy confounded the clinical picture. Findings on muscle biopsy suggested that abnormal innervation affected muscle fiber differentiation in the early postnatal period.

Kochanski et al. (2004) described the clinical and morphologic features of a patient with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy, which they characterized as a severe neuropathy with onset in early infancy. They noted that sural nerve biopsy in this disorder shows a characteristic picture of nonmyelinated and poorly myelinated axons, basal lamina onion bulbs, and no myelin breakdown products.

### Inheritance

In the family with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy reported by Smit et al. (2008), the heterozygous MPZ mutation was confirmed in 2 generations, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. In isolated patients, Mandich et al. (1999) and Szigeti et al. (2003) found that the MPZ mutations had occurred de novo. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Warner et al. (1996) suggested that CMT1B, Dejerine-Sottas syndrome, and congenital hypomyelination may not be separate pathophysiologic entities. Instead, they proposed that these disorders form a spectrum of related 'myelinopathies' caused by an underlying defect in myelination.

### Molecular Genetics

Warner et al. (1996) identified 5 new mutations in the MPZ gene, which encodes P0, the major structural protein of peripheral nerve myelin. The patients had CMT1B, Dejerine-Sottas syndrome, or congenital hypomyelination.

In a patient with congenital hypomyelination, Mandich et al. (1999) identified a de novo gln215-to-ter (Q215X) mutation in the MPZ gene. The authors concluded that this finding confirmed that congenital hypomyelination is allelic with the milder MPZ-related disorders CMT1B and Dejerine-Sottas syndrome.

In a patient with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy, Szigeti et al. (2003) identified a complex de novo mutation in the MPZ gene. The mutation consisted of a 3-bp deletion of CTA at nucleotides 550 to 552 and an insertion of G at nucleotide 550. Conceptual translation predicted that it would cause a frameshift.

In a patient with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy, Kochanski et al. (2004) identified a novel thr124-to-lys (T124K) mutation in the MPZ gene.

Smit et al. (2008) described a family with congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy that carried a heterozygous mutation in the MPZ gene. The mutation was confirmed in 2 generations.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Warner et al. (1996) hypothesized that the differences in clinical severity among MPZ mutations depend on the type of mutation and its effect on protein function, i.e., whether it causes loss of function or acts in a dominant-negative manner.

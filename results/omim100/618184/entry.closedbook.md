### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy-2 (CHN2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding myelin protein zero (MPZ; 159440) on chromosome 1q23. ⚠︎

### Description

Congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy is a severe peripheral neuropathy that begins at birth or in early infancy. Affected infants have hypotonia, generalized weakness, areflexia, and very slow nerve conduction velocities. Nerve biopsy shows hypomyelination or absent myelin, without the active demyelination and remyelination that are typical of Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1. CHN2 lies at the severe end of the range of MPZ-related neuropathies. The milder end of this range includes Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1B and Dejerine-Sottas neuropathy. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

CHN2 is transmitted in an autosomal dominant pattern. Reported cases have often arisen from de novo heterozygous MPZ mutations. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy is genetically heterogeneous. CHN1 is caused by mutation in the EGR2 gene, and CHN3 is caused by mutation in the CNTNAP1 gene. ✗

### Molecular Genetics

Warner et al. (1996) studied patients with inherited peripheral neuropathies and found heterozygous mutations in the MPZ gene in patients with Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1B, Dejerine-Sottas syndrome, and congenital hypomyelination. They concluded that different MPZ mutations can produce a spectrum of clinical phenotypes, with congenital hypomyelination at the most severe end. ✗

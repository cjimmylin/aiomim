### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that intermediate junctional epidermolysis bullosa-5A (JEB5A) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the ITGB4 gene (147557) on chromosome 17q25. ✗

### Description

Junctional epidermolysis bullosa-5A (JEB5A) is an autosomal recessive blistering skin disorder. Skin fragility leads to blisters that form within the lamina lucida of the dermal-epidermal basement membrane, and the disorder is linked to defects in the hemidesmosomal integrin beta-4 (ITGB4). Unlike the allelic disorder JEB5B, which is often lethal and includes pyloric atresia, JEB5A is a nonlethal, intermediate (non-Herlitz) form without pyloric atresia. Features can include generalized blistering, atrophic scarring, nail dystrophy, dental enamel defects, and alopecia. ✗

### Nomenclature

The intermediate form of junctional epidermolysis bullosa was formerly called non-Herlitz or generalized intermediate JEB. JEB caused by ITGB4 mutation is split into 2 forms: JEB5A, the intermediate form without pyloric atresia, and JEB5B, the form with pyloric atresia. ✗

### Inheritance

JEB5A is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. ✗

### Heterogeneity

Junctional epidermolysis bullosa is genetically heterogeneous. Other forms are caused by mutations in genes encoding laminin-332 subunits (LAMA3, LAMB3, LAMC2), type XVII collagen (COL17A1), and the integrin subunits alpha-6 (ITGA6) and alpha-3 (ITGA3). ✗

### Molecular Genetics

Biallelic ITGB4 mutations have been reported in patients with nonlethal JEB without pyloric atresia. Missense mutations and mutations that leave some integrin beta-4 expressed have tended to cause milder, nonlethal disease. Biallelic null mutations have tended to cause the lethal form with pyloric atresia. ✗

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Across reports of ITGB4-related JEB, severity has been linked to how much integrin beta-4 protein remains. Complete absence of the protein, usually from premature termination codon mutations on both alleles, has been associated with lethal JEB with pyloric atresia. Partial expression, often with at least 1 missense or in-frame allele, has been associated with nonlethal, intermediate disease. ✗

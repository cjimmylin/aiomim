### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that intermediate junctional epidermolysis bullosa-5A (JEB5A) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the ITGB4 gene (147557) on chromosome 17q25.1. ⚠︎

### Description

Intermediate junctional epidermolysis bullosa-5A (JEB5A) is an autosomal recessive blistering disorder caused by mutation in the gene for integrin beta-4 (ITGB4). Skin involvement is milder than in the lethal form of epidermolysis bullosa with pyloric atresia, and affected patients survive beyond infancy. Pyloric atresia may be present (Mellerio et al., 1998) or absent (Inoue et al., 2000; Jonkman et al., 2002). Reported features include acral blistering, nail dystrophy and enamel hypoplasia (Jonkman et al., 2002). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Mellerio et al. (1998) reported 2 unrelated patients with junctional epidermolysis bullosa with pyloric atresia who survived into early childhood and had mild skin involvement. The authors noted that this differed from the usual course of the disorder, which is often fatal within the first weeks or months of life even after surgical correction.

Inoue et al. (2000) reported non-Herlitz junctional epidermolysis bullosa without pyloric atresia in association with a homozygous missense mutation in ITGB4.

Jonkman et al. (2002) reported a 49-year-old woman who had had mild blistering of the hands and feet since birth, nail dystrophy with onychogryposis, and enamel hypoplasia. She had no alopecia and no history of pyloric atresia. Electron microscopy and antigen mapping of a skin blister showed an intraepidermal split, located low in the basal keratinocytes through the attachment plaque of the hemidesmosome. The authors described the phenotype as having predominant features of epidermolysis bullosa simplex. ⚠︎

### Biochemical Features

On immunofluorescence microscopy of their patient's skin, Jonkman et al. (2002) found no binding at all of monoclonal antibody 450-11A, which recognizes the third fibronectin III repeat in the intracellular domain of integrin beta-4. Binding of monoclonal antibodies against epitopes at the amino- and carboxy-terminal ends of the protein was reduced.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of JEB5A is autosomal recessive. Mellerio et al. (1998) found compound heterozygous ITGB4 mutations in 2 unrelated patients, and Inoue et al. (2000) found a homozygous ITGB4 mutation. In their patient, Jonkman et al. (2002) found a heterozygous ITGB4 deletion and expected her to also be heterozygous for a null allele.

### Heterogeneity

Pulkkinen et al. (1998) noted that epidermolysis bullosa with pyloric atresia (EB-PA) is a clinically and genetically heterogeneous autosomal recessive blistering disorder that includes lethal and nonlethal variants. In 3 patients with the lethal form of EB-PA, they identified ITGB4 mutations on 5 alleles, and predicted that 3 of the 4 distinct mutations would produce truncated polypeptides.

### Molecular Genetics

In a patient with mild pyloric atresia-junctional epidermolysis bullosa, Mellerio et al. (1998) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the ITGB4 gene: a splice site mutation in exon 30 (3793+1G-A) and a nonsense mutation in exon 36 (W1478X). The authors suggested that the nonsense mutation would markedly reduce beta-4 integrin mRNA, but that the splice site allele might still allow production of some functional, though altered, beta-4 polypeptide.

In a second, unrelated patient with mild pyloric atresia-junctional epidermolysis bullosa, Mellerio et al. (1998) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the ITGB4 gene: a missense mutation in exon 3 (C38R) and a 1-bp deletion in exon 36 (4776delG). The authors suggested that the missense allele might allow synthesis of some functional beta-4 integrin, which could explain the mild phenotype. ⚠︎

In non-Herlitz junctional epidermolysis bullosa without pyloric atresia, Inoue et al. (2000) identified a homozygous missense mutation (G931D) in the cytoplasmic tail of beta-4 integrin. They reported that the mutation disrupted hemidesmosome assembly.

In their 49-year-old patient, Jonkman et al. (2002) found a novel heterozygous 2-bp deletion (4733delCT) in the ITGB4 gene. The deletion caused in-frame skipping of exon 36, which was predicted to remove 50 amino acids (residues 1450-1499) within the third fibronectin type III repeat of the beta-4 cytoplasmic domain. Immunoblot analysis showed a beta-4 polypeptide 5 kDa shorter than normal. Because no full-size protein was detected in vitro and the 450-11A epitope was absent in vivo, the authors expected the patient to also be heterozygous for a null allele.

### Pathogenesis

Jonkman et al. (2002) concluded that deletion of the third fibronectin type III repeat in the cytoplasmic domain of integrin beta-4, a repeat thought to interact with BP180/type XVII collagen, is clinically pathogenic. They concluded that its loss results in a mild phenotype with predominant features of epidermolysis bullosa simplex.

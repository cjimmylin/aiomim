### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that intermediate junctional epidermolysis bullosa-5A (JEB5A) is caused by homozygous mutation in the ITGB4 gene (147557) on chromosome 17q25.1. ⚠︎

### Description

Junctional epidermolysis bullosa-5A (JEB5A) is a non-Herlitz form of junctional epidermolysis bullosa without pyloric atresia. As reported by Inoue et al. (2000), it is caused by a defect in the hemidesmosomal integrin beta-4 that disrupts hemidesmosome assembly. Pulkkinen et al. (1998) described epidermolysis bullosa with pyloric atresia, by contrast, as an autosomal recessive blistering disorder that includes both lethal and nonlethal variants.

### Clinical Features

Jonkman et al. (2002) reported a patient with an ITGB4 mutation and a mild phenotype that resembled epidermolysis bullosa simplex (EBS). She had dystrophy of the nails with onychogryposis and enamel hypoplasia.

### Heterogeneity

Pulkkinen et al. (1998) noted that epidermolysis bullosa with pyloric atresia (EB-PA) is clinically and genetically heterogeneous. Mellerio et al. (1998) noted that mutations in the genes encoding the epithelial integrin alpha-6/beta-4 (ITGA6 and ITGB4) had been identified in several patients with EB-PA.

### Molecular Genetics

Inoue et al. (2000) identified a homozygous missense mutation, gly931-to-asp (G931D), in the cytoplasmic tail of integrin beta-4 in non-Herlitz junctional epidermolysis bullosa without pyloric atresia. The mutation disrupted hemidesmosome assembly.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

In 2 cases of EB-PA, Mellerio et al. (1998) suggested that the ITGB4 mutations might allow synthesis of some functional, albeit perturbed, beta-4 polypeptide. They concluded that working out the molecular mechanisms in such cases might enable correlation between genotype and phenotype.

In a single patient with a mild EBS-like phenotype, Jonkman et al. (2002) found an ITGB4 allele that caused in-frame skipping of exon 36 and a deduced 50-amino acid deletion (residues 1450-1499). Immunoblot analysis showed a beta-4 polypeptide that was 5 kD shorter than normal.

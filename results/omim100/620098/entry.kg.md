### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental delay with variable intellectual disability and dysmorphic facies (DIDDF) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the JARID2 gene (601594) on chromosome 6p22.3.

### Description

Developmental delay with variable intellectual disability and dysmorphic facies (DIDDF) is a neurodevelopmental syndrome caused by JARID2 haploinsufficiency (Verberne et al., 2021). It is characterized by developmental delay, cognitive impairment ranging from borderline intellectual functioning to severe intellectual disability, hypotonia, autistic features, and behavior abnormalities. Dysmorphic facial features include a high anterior hairline, broad forehead, deeply set eyes, infraorbital dark circles, depressed nasal bridge, bulbous nasal tip, and full lips (Verberne et al., 2022).

### Clinical Features

Barøy et al. (2013) described 4 unrelated patients with de novo 6p22.3 deletions including JARID2: 2 females aged 15 and 6.5 years and 2 males aged 4 and 6.5 years. Three were of Norwegian or Norwegian/English ancestry and 1 was Lebanese, and all had nonconsanguineous parents. All 4 had developmental delay with cognitive impairment ranging from borderline IQ to severe intellectual disability. All had gait disturbance with impaired balance and coordination, and they shared similar facial dysmorphic features. These features included prominent supraorbital ridges, deep-set eyes, dark infraorbital circles, midface hypoplasia, bulbous nasal tip, full lips, marked Cupid's bow, and a prominent or pointed chin. Other features were clinodactyly of the fifth fingers and foot anomalies such as syndactyly of toes. Two patients had seizures or seizure-suspected episodes, 3 had behavioral abnormalities, 1 had autism spectrum disorder, and 1 had an atrial septal defect.

The patient in the series of Barøy et al. (2013) with the smallest JARID2-containing deletion (Patient 4) was a 6.5-year-old Lebanese boy of nonconsanguineous parents. He walked at 16 months. At age 4 years 3 months his language delay was 1.5 years, and at 6.5 years his IQ was 74 (borderline). He had balance and coordination problems, poor finger opposition, and early-onset muscular hypotonia. Neuromuscular and metabolic workups were normal, as were cerebral MRI and EEG. He had the characteristic facial features, strabismus, fifth-finger clinodactyly, syndactyly of toes 2-3, sandal gap, and broad halluces.

Barøy et al. (2013) also reviewed 10 previously reported patients with deletions including JARID2. The characteristic facial features had not been consistently reported in these patients, but photographs were available for 7 of them and confirmed a similar facial pattern. Four of the 10 had impaired balance, impaired coordination, and/or gait disturbance. The authors suggested that JARID2 haploinsufficiency may result in a clinically recognizable neurodevelopmental syndrome. They recommended sequencing JARID2 in similar patients who did not have a deletion.

Verberne et al. (2021) reported 16 individuals from 15 families with a deletion or single-nucleotide variant in JARID2. All had developmental delay and/or intellectual disability. They shared some clinical features, such as facial features, with individuals who had larger 6p22-p24 deletions that include JARID2.

Cadieux-Dion et al. (2022) reported 7 individuals from 5 families with pathogenic or likely pathogenic JARID2 variants. These cases highlighted palatal abnormalities and heart defects as part of the phenotype.

Verberne et al. (2022) summarized 8 patients with pathogenic or likely pathogenic JARID2 variants: 4 males and 4 females aged 5 to 36 years, 5 of whom had been reported previously. All 8 had developmental delay. Intellectual functioning ranged from normal (1 patient) through learning difficulties or borderline functioning to moderate intellectual disability. Four of the 8 had autistic features, including 2 with a diagnosis of autism spectrum disorder. Three had behavior abnormalities, 2 had hypotonia, and 2 had gait disturbance. Facial dysmorphism was variable. Additional anomalies included submucous cleft palate with bifid uvula, cryptorchidism, an arachnoid cyst or posterior fossa cyst/mega cisterna magna, kyphoscoliosis, and cafe-au-lait macules.

### Inheritance

Many of the reported deletions and variants arose de novo (Barøy et al., 2013; Verberne et al., 2022). Verberne et al. (2022) reported a patient who inherited a JARID2 deletion from her mother, who had learning difficulties. Cadieux-Dion et al. (2022) reported JARID2 variants inherited from mildly affected parents, which demonstrated variable expressivity of the disorder.

### Cytogenetics

Barøy et al. (2013) performed aCGH in a cohort of about 3,600 patients ascertained for neurodevelopmental disorders and identified de novo 6p22.3-p24.1 deletions in 5 patients. The deletions in 4 of these patients ranged from 189 kb to 4.8 Mb and shared a smallest region of overlap (SRO I) of 189 kb that included JARID2 and DTNBP1. This region was defined by the 189-kb deletion in Patient 4, which removed exons 3 to 18 and the 3-prime UTR of JARID2 and exons 7 to 10 of DTNBP1. Parental testing showed that all deletions were de novo. The Database of Genomic Variants (DGV) contained only CNVs covering minor parts of the region.

Verberne et al. (2021) noted that 13 previously reported individuals with intellectual disability had de novo heterozygous 6p22-p24 deletions that removed all of JARID2. Each of these deletions also removed at least one neighboring gene, so it could not be determined whether JARID2 was the critical gene.

### Molecular Genetics

Zahir et al. (2016) had earlier found a CNV involving only exon 6 of JARID2, apparently de novo, in 7 patients with intellectual disability. Because JARID2 exon 6 encodes one of a series of repeated motifs, the authors studied this variant in 2 cohorts and carried out a genotype-phenotype assessment. They concluded that the JARID2 exon 6 CNV is benign, with a population frequency of more than 14%, although it could still have a contributory effect.

Verberne et al. (2021) used chromosome microarray analysis, exome sequencing, and GeneMatcher to identify deletions or single-nucleotide variants involving JARID2 in 16 individuals from 15 families with developmental delay and/or intellectual disability. The authors suggested that several of the variants were likely to cause haploinsufficiency through nonsense-mediated mRNA decay.

Cadieux-Dion et al. (2022) found pathogenic or likely pathogenic JARID2 variants in 7 individuals from 5 families. They noted a high prevalence of intragenic JARID2 copy number variants among these patients and emphasized the importance of exon-level analysis.

Verberne et al. (2022) reported 6 patients from 5 families with deletions of at least one exon of JARID2, identified by microarray analysis or other methods. The reported deletions included a deletion of exons 6 to 18. Four deletions were de novo, and 1 patient (patient 6) inherited her deletion from her mother (patient 7), who had learning difficulties; the inheritance of the deletion in patient 7 was not assessed. In addition, a 14-year-old boy (patient 4) carried a de novo heterozygous frameshift mutation, and a 5-year-old boy (patient 5) carried a de novo heterozygous splice site mutation in JARID2.

Verberne et al. (2022) also tested 3 patients with JARID2 missense variants of uncertain significance against a DNA methylation episignature (see DIAGNOSIS). Two unrelated patients (9 and 11) carried c.2363G-A (p.Arg788Gln). In patient 11 this variant was inherited from an apparently unaffected mother. Patient 10 carried a de novo c.1930G-A (p.Glu644Lys) variant in the ARID domain. All 3 patients clustered with controls and had scores near zero. The authors noted that this did not rule out pathogenicity but suggested that the variants might be benign. Patients 9 and 10 lacked the typical facial dysmorphism, and patient 11 had severe global spasticity and lack of myelination.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Barøy et al. (2013) described Patient 5, a 17-year-old Norwegian girl with a de novo 116-kb deletion that included GMPR and part of ATXN1 but not JARID2. She had mild intellectual disability and behavioral abnormalities without the facial dysmorphism or gait disturbance. In contrast, Patients 1 to 3, whose deletions included both JARID2 and ATXN1, had moderate to severe intellectual disability. The authors suggested that simultaneous deletion of both genes likely made the intellectual disability more severe.

### Pathogenesis

Barøy et al. (2013) found that JARID2 expression in leukocyte RNA was significantly lower in the 4 patients with JARID2 deletion than in 8 healthy controls (p ≤ 0.01). They argued against DTNBP1 as the causal gene. They concluded that JARID2, a transcriptional repressor and chromatin modifier acting in the PRC2 and Notch pathways, was likely the critical gene within the smallest region of overlap.

In the methylation study of 8 patients by Verberne et al. (2022), there were no significant differentially methylated regions. Among the 150 episignature probes, the authors found 7 small regions with a consistent direction of effect. Two of these regions were annotated to genes previously linked to abnormal neurological development, NLGN2 and ADGRL2. Gene set enrichment analysis did not identify consistent pathways.

### Diagnosis

Verberne et al. (2022) used EPIC arrays to analyze peripheral blood DNA methylation in 8 patients with pathogenic or likely pathogenic JARID2 variants and 56 matched controls. They identified a 150-probe episignature that clearly separated patients from controls on hierarchical clustering and multidimensional scaling, and the signature was confirmed by leave-one-out cross-validation. A support vector machine classifier gave all patients scores near 1. Controls and samples from 57 other disorders received low scores, which the authors interpreted as full sensitivity and specificity.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DEVELOPMENTAL DELAY WITH VARIABLE INTELLECTUAL DISABILITY AND DYSMORPHIC FACIES**  
JARID2, 1-BP DUP, 2866G [c.15511546dup; c.2866dupg; p.E956G]

Verberne et al. (2022) reported a 14-year-old boy (patient 4) with DIDDF who had moderate intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, and behavior abnormalities. He carried a de novo heterozygous 1-bp duplication (c.2866dupG) in the JARID2 gene, which resulted in a frameshift and premature termination (Glu956GlyfsTer72). The variant was classified as pathogenic. ⚠︎

**.0002 DEVELOPMENTAL DELAY WITH VARIABLE INTELLECTUAL DISABILITY AND DYSMORPHIC FACIES**  
JARID2, 2731+1G-C [c.15507648G>C; c.2731+1G>C]

Verberne et al. (2022) reported a 5-year-old boy (patient 5) with DIDDF who had developmental delay, hypotonia, an arachnoid cyst, right cryptorchidism, and congenital torticollis. He carried a de novo heterozygous splice site mutation (c.2731+1G-C) in the JARID2 gene. The variant was classified as likely pathogenic. ⚠︎

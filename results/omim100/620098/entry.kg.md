### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental delay with variable intellectual disability and dysmorphic facies (DIDDF) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the JARID2 gene (601594) on chromosome 6p22.3.

### Description

Developmental delay with variable intellectual disability and dysmorphic facies is a neurodevelopmental syndrome caused by JARID2 haploinsufficiency. Verberne et al. (2021) described its features as developmental delay and/or intellectual disability and characteristic facial features. According to Verberne et al. (2022), cognitive impairment ranges from borderline intellectual functioning to severe intellectual disability, and patients may also have hypotonia, autistic features, and behavior abnormalities. Dysmorphic facial features include a high anterior hairline, broad forehead, deeply set eyes, infraorbital dark circles, depressed nasal bridge, bulbous nasal tip, and full lips. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Barøy et al. (2013) described 4 unrelated patients with de novo 6p22.3 deletions that included JARID2. Two were girls aged 15 and 6.5 years and 2 were boys aged 4 and 6.5 years. Three were Norwegian or Norwegian/English and 1 was Lebanese, and all had nonconsanguineous parents. All 4 had developmental delay with cognitive impairment ranging from borderline IQ to severe intellectual disability. All had gait disturbance with impaired balance and coordination, and all had similar facial dysmorphism: prominent supraorbital ridges, deep-set eyes, dark infraorbital circles, midface hypoplasia, bulbous nasal tip, full lips, marked Cupid's bow, and a prominent or pointed chin. Other features were clinodactyly of the fifth fingers and foot anomalies such as syndactyly of the toes. Two patients had seizures or episodes suspected to be seizures, 3 had behavioral abnormalities, 1 had autism spectrum disorder, and 1 had an atrial septal defect.

The patient with the smallest JARID2-containing deletion in the series of Barøy et al. (2013) was a 6.5-year-old Lebanese boy (Patient 4) born to nonconsanguineous parents. He walked at 16 months and had a language delay of 1.5 years at age 4 years 3 months. His IQ was 74 (borderline) at 6.5 years. He had balance and coordination problems, poor finger opposition, and early-onset muscular hypotonia. Neuromuscular and metabolic workups were normal, as were cerebral MRI and EEG. He had the characteristic facial features, strabismus, fifth-finger clinodactyly, syndactyly of toes 2-3, sandal gap, and broad halluces.

Barøy et al. (2013) also reviewed 10 previously reported patients with deletions that included JARID2. The characteristic facial features had not been consistently reported in these patients, but photographs were available for 7 of them and confirmed a similar facial pattern. Four of the 10 had impaired balance, impaired coordination, and/or gait disturbance. The authors suggested that JARID2 haploinsufficiency might cause a clinically recognizable neurodevelopmental syndrome and recommended sequencing JARID2 in similar patients without a deletion.

Verberne et al. (2021) reported 16 individuals from 15 families with a deletion or single-nucleotide variant in JARID2. All had developmental delay and/or intellectual disability. They shared some clinical features, including facial features, with individuals who have larger 6p22-p24 deletions that include JARID2.

Cadieux-Dion et al. (2022) reported 7 individuals from 5 families with pathogenic or likely pathogenic JARID2 variants, which confirmed the gene-disease association. Their cases highlighted palatal abnormalities and heart defects as part of the phenotype.

Verberne et al. (2022) summarized 8 patients with pathogenic or likely pathogenic JARID2 variants, 5 of whom had been reported previously. There were 4 males and 4 females, aged 5 to 36 years. All 8 had developmental delay. Intellectual functioning ranged from normal (1 patient) through learning difficulties or borderline functioning to moderate intellectual disability. Four of the 8 had autistic features, 2 of them with a diagnosis of autism spectrum disorder. Three had behavior abnormalities, 2 had hypotonia, 2 had gait disturbance, and facial dysmorphism was variable. Additional anomalies included submucous cleft palate with bifid uvula, cryptorchidism, an arachnoid cyst or posterior fossa cyst/mega cisterna magna, kyphoscoliosis, and cafe-au-lait macules.

### Inheritance

The disorder shows autosomal dominant transmission. Barøy et al. (2013) found the JARID2-containing deletions to have arisen de novo, and Verberne et al. (2022) found 4 of 6 JARID2 deletions to be de novo. Verberne et al. (2022) also described a deletion that was transmitted from a mother with learning difficulties to her daughter. Cadieux-Dion et al. (2022) reported JARID2 variants inherited from mildly affected parents, showing that the disorder has variable expressivity. ⚠︎

### Cytogenetics

Barøy et al. (2013) studied a cohort of about 3,600 patients ascertained for neurodevelopmental disorders. Using array CGH, they identified de novo 6p22.3-p24.1 deletions in 5 patients. The deletions in 4 of these patients ranged from 189 kb to 4.8 Mb and shared a 189-kb smallest region of overlap (SRO I) containing JARID2 and DTNBP1. This region was defined by the 189-kb deletion in Patient 4, which removed exons 3 to 18 and the 3-prime UTR of JARID2 and exons 7 to 10 of DTNBP1. Parental testing showed that all of the deletions were de novo. The Database of Genomic Variants contained only CNVs covering minor parts of the region. ⚠︎

Verberne et al. (2021) noted that 13 previously reported individuals with intellectual disability had de novo heterozygous 6p22-p24 deletions that removed all of JARID2. Every one of these deletions also removed at least one neighboring gene, so it could not be determined whether JARID2 was the critical gene.

### Molecular Genetics

Zahir et al. (2016) had earlier found a CNV involving only exon 6 of JARID2, apparently de novo, in 7 patients with intellectual disability. Because JARID2 exon 6 encodes one of a series of repeated motifs, they studied this variant in 2 cohorts and carried out a genotype-phenotype assessment. They concluded that the exon 6 CNV is benign, with a population frequency of more than 14%, although it could still have a contributory effect.

Using chromosome microarray analysis, exome sequencing, and GeneMatcher, Verberne et al. (2021) identified deletions or single-nucleotide variants involving JARID2 in 16 individuals from 15 families with developmental delay and/or intellectual disability. The authors suggested that several of the variants were likely to cause haploinsufficiency through nonsense-mediated mRNA decay.

Cadieux-Dion et al. (2022) reported pathogenic or likely pathogenic JARID2 variants in 7 individuals from 5 families. Intragenic JARID2 copy number variants were common among these patients, and the authors emphasized the importance of exon-level analysis. ⚠︎

Verberne et al. (2022) reported 6 patients with deletions of at least 1 exon of JARID2 on chromosome 6p22.3, identified by microarray analysis or other methods. Four of the deletions were de novo. Patient 6 inherited her deletion from her mother (patient 7), who had learning difficulties. Two further patients each carried a de novo heterozygous JARID2 variant: a frameshift classified as pathogenic in patient 4 (c.2866dupG) and a splice site variant classified as likely pathogenic in patient 5 (c.2731+1G-C).

Verberne et al. (2022) also tested 3 patients with JARID2 missense variants of uncertain significance against a DNA methylation episignature. Two unrelated patients (9 and 11) carried c.2363G-A (arg788-to-gln, R788Q). In patient 11, the variant was inherited from an apparently unaffected mother. Patient 10 carried a de novo c.1930G-A (glu644-to-lys, E644K) variant in the ARID domain. All 3 patients clustered with controls and had scores near zero. The authors noted that this result did not rule out pathogenicity but suggested that the variants might be benign. Patients 9 and 10 lacked the typical facial dysmorphism, and patient 11 had severe global spasticity and lack of myelination.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Barøy et al. (2013) described Patient 5, a 17-year-old Norwegian girl with a de novo 116-kb deletion that included GMPR and part of ATXN1 but not JARID2. She had mild intellectual disability and behavioral abnormalities but lacked the facial dysmorphism and gait disturbance. In contrast, Patients 1 to 3, whose deletions included both JARID2 and ATXN1, had moderate to severe intellectual disability. The authors suggested that deleting both genes together likely made the intellectual disability more severe.

### Pathogenesis

Barøy et al. (2013) found that JARID2 expression in leukocyte RNA was significantly lower in the 4 patients with JARID2 deletions than in 8 healthy controls (p ≤ 0.01). They argued against DTNBP1 as the causal gene. They concluded that JARID2, a transcriptional repressor and chromatin modifier acting in the PRC2 and Notch pathways, was likely the critical gene within SRO I.

In their methylation study of 8 patients, Verberne et al. (2022) found no significant differentially methylated regions. Among the 150 episignature probes, they identified 7 small regions with a consistent direction of effect. Two of these regions were annotated to genes previously linked to abnormal neurologic development, NLGN2 and ADGRL2. Gene set enrichment analysis did not identify consistent pathways.

### Diagnosis

Verberne et al. (2022) analyzed DNA methylation in peripheral blood (EPIC arrays) from 8 patients with pathogenic or likely pathogenic JARID2 variants and 56 matched controls. They identified a 150-probe episignature that clearly separated patients from controls on hierarchical clustering and multidimensional scaling. The signature held up in leave-one-out cross-validation. A support vector machine classifier gave all patients scores near 1 and gave low scores to controls and to samples from 57 other disorders. The authors interpreted this as full sensitivity and specificity.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DEVELOPMENTAL DELAY WITH VARIABLE INTELLECTUAL DISABILITY AND DYSMORPHIC FACIES**  
JARID2, 1-BP DUP, 2866G [c.15511546dup; c.2866dupg; p.E956G]

Verberne et al. (2022) reported a 14-year-old boy (patient 4) with moderate intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, and behavior abnormalities. He carried a de novo heterozygous 1-bp duplication in the JARID2 gene (c.2866dupG), which caused a frameshift and premature termination (Glu956GlyfsTer72). The variant was classified as pathogenic.

**.0002 DEVELOPMENTAL DELAY WITH VARIABLE INTELLECTUAL DISABILITY AND DYSMORPHIC FACIES**  
JARID2, 2731+1G-C [c.15507648G>C; c.2731+1G>C]

Verberne et al. (2022) reported a 5-year-old boy (patient 5) with developmental delay, hypotonia, an arachnoid cyst, right cryptorchidism, and congenital torticollis. He carried a de novo heterozygous splice site variant in the JARID2 gene (c.2731+1G-C), which was classified as likely pathogenic.

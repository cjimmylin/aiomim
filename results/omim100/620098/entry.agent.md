### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental delay with variable intellectual disability and dysmorphic facies (DIDDF) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the JARID2 gene on chromosome 6p22.3.

### Description

JARID2 haploinsufficiency causes a neurodevelopmental syndrome characterized by developmental delay, cognitive impairment ranging from borderline intellectual functioning to severe intellectual disability, hypotonia, autistic features, and behavioral abnormalities. Dysmorphic facial features include high anterior hairline, broad forehead, deeply set eyes, infraorbital dark circles, depressed nasal bridge, bulbous nasal tip, and full lips (Verberne et al., 2022).

### Clinical Features

Barøy et al. (2013) described 4 unrelated patients with de novo deletions in chromosome 6p22.3 that included the JARID2 gene, identified in a cohort of about 3,600 patients ascertained for neurodevelopmental disorders. Patients 1 to 3 (2 girls aged 15 and 6.5 years and a 4-year-old boy, born to nonconsanguineous Norwegian or Norwegian and English parents) had larger deletions that also included ATXN1, and had moderate, moderate, and severe intellectual disability, respectively. Patient 4, a 6.5-year-old boy born to nonconsanguineous Lebanese parents, had a deletion that included only part of JARID2 and DTNBP1; he had a borderline IQ of 74, delayed language development, early-onset muscular hypotonia, and balance and coordination problems. All 4 patients with JARID2 deletion had gait disturbance and similar facial features, the most characteristic being prominent supraorbital ridges, deep-set eyes, dark infraorbital circles, and midface hypoplasia. Other features in these patients included bulbous nasal tip, full lips, marked Cupid's bow, prominent or pointed chin, clinodactyly of the fifth fingers, and toe anomalies; dyspraxia was suggested in patients 1 and 4. Behavioral abnormalities included hyperactivity, sleeping difficulties, and poor eye contact, and patient 3 fulfilled criteria for autism spectrum disorder. On inspection of photographs available for 7 of 10 previously reported patients with deletions including JARID2, Barøy et al. (2013) confirmed a similar facial pattern and suggested that JARID2 haploinsufficiency might represent a clinically recognizable neurodevelopmental syndrome.

Verberne et al. (2021) reported 16 individuals from 15 families with a deletion or single-nucleotide variant in JARID2. All had developmental delay and/or intellectual disability and shared some overlapping clinical characteristics, such as facial features, with individuals who had larger deletions involving JARID2.

Cadieux-Dion et al. (2022) reported 7 individuals from 5 families with pathogenic or likely pathogenic JARID2 variants, and highlighted palatal abnormalities and heart defects as part of the phenotype. Variants were inherited from mildly affected parents in some families, demonstrating variable expressivity.

Verberne et al. (2022) tabulated the features of 8 individuals with pathogenic or likely pathogenic JARID2 variants, including a mother and daughter. All had developmental delay; intellectual functioning ranged from normal (the daughter) and learning difficulties (her mother) to borderline intellectual functioning (IQ 82) and mild or moderate intellectual disability. Autistic features were present in 4, and 2 had a diagnosis of autism spectrum disorder. Facial features included high anterior hairline, deeply set eyes, infraorbital dark circles, bulbous nasal tip, short philtrum, and full lips. Other anomalies in individual patients included submucous cleft palate and bifid uvula, a small posterior fossa cyst or mega cisterna magna, an arachnoid cyst, cryptorchidism, and kyphoscoliosis.

### Inheritance

The deletions reported by Barøy et al. (2013) occurred de novo. Cadieux-Dion et al. (2022) reported inheritance of JARID2 variants from mildly affected parents, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance with variable expressivity. In the cohort of Verberne et al. (2022), a deletion was inherited by a girl from her mother, who had learning difficulties. ⚠︎

### Cytogenetics

In 5 unrelated patients with de novo deletions (0.1 to 4.8 Mb) in chromosome 6p22.3-p24.1 detected by array CGH, Barøy et al. (2013) defined 2 smallest regions of overlap. SRO I (189 kb), deleted in patients 1 to 4, contained JARID2 and DTNBP1; SRO II (116 kb), deleted in patients 1 to 3 and 5, contained GMPR and ATXN1. The deletion in patient 4 removed exons 3 to 18 of JARID2 and exons 7 to 10 of DTNBP1. JARID2 expression in leukocytes was significantly reduced in patients 1 to 4 compared with controls (p less than or equal to 0.01). Patient 5, whose deletion included only GMPR and part of ATXN1, had mild intellectual disability and behavioral abnormalities but no dysmorphic features or gait disturbance were noted. Barøy et al. (2013) considered a contribution of DTNBP1 haploinsufficiency to the features in their patients unlikely and suggested that the concomitant deletion of ATXN1 and JARID2 likely exacerbated the intellectual disability in patients 1 to 3.

### Molecular Genetics

Using chromosome microarray analysis, exome sequencing, and GeneMatcher, Verberne et al. (2021) identified deletions or single-nucleotide variants in JARID2 in 16 individuals from 15 families with developmental delay and/or intellectual disability. Several of the variants were predicted to result in haploinsufficiency through nonsense-mediated mRNA decay.

In 7 individuals from 5 families, Cadieux-Dion et al. (2022) identified pathogenic or likely pathogenic JARID2 variants, some inherited from mildly affected parents. They noted a high prevalence of intragenic JARID2 copy number variants and emphasized the importance of exon-level analysis.

Verberne et al. (2022) studied 11 individuals with JARID2 variants, 7 of whom had been previously reported. Eight carried pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants: 6 had a deletion of at least 1 exon of JARID2, 1 had a de novo frameshift (c.2866dupG, Glu956GlyfsTer72), and 1 had a de novo splice site variant (c.2731+1G-C). Peripheral blood DNA methylation profiling identified a distinct episignature that separated these 8 patients from controls. Three individuals carried missense variants of uncertain significance (R788Q in 2 unrelated individuals and E644K in 1); their methylation profiles clustered with controls, and Verberne et al. (2022) considered that these missense variants might well be benign.

Zahir et al. (2016) had previously found a copy number variant involving only exon 6 of JARID2, which occurred apparently de novo in 7 patients with intellectual disability. Investigation of this variant in 2 cohorts showed that it was benign, with a high population frequency (greater than 14%), although it could nevertheless have a contributory effect.

### Diagnosis

Verberne et al. (2022) identified a DNA methylation episignature in peripheral blood from 8 patients with pathogenic or likely pathogenic JARID2 variants compared with 56 matched controls. A support vector machine classifier based on 150 selected probes gave scores near 1 for all patients and low scores for controls and for patients with other neurodevelopmental disorders, indicating full sensitivity and specificity, and the signature was added to the EpiSign diagnostic test.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DEVELOPMENTAL DELAY WITH VARIABLE INTELLECTUAL DISABILITY AND DYSMORPHIC FACIES**  
JARID2, 1-BP DUP, 2866G [c.2866dupG; p.(Glu956GlyfsTer72)]

In a 14-year-old boy (patient 4) with moderate intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, and a small posterior fossa cyst or mega cisterna magna, Verberne et al. (2022) reported a de novo heterozygous 1-bp duplication (c.2866dupG, NM_004973.4) in the JARID2 gene, resulting in a frameshift (Glu956GlyfsTer72). The variant was classified as pathogenic. The patient's peripheral blood DNA methylation profile clustered with the JARID2 episignature.

**.0002 DEVELOPMENTAL DELAY WITH VARIABLE INTELLECTUAL DISABILITY AND DYSMORPHIC FACIES**  
JARID2, IVS, G-C, +1 (2731+1G-C) [c.2731+1G>C]

In a 5-year-old boy (patient 5) with developmental delay, hypotonia, broad forehead, deeply set eyes, an arachnoid cyst, right cryptorchidism, and congenital torticollis, Verberne et al. (2022) reported a de novo heterozygous splice site variant (c.2731+1G-C, NM_004973.4) in the JARID2 gene, classified as likely pathogenic. The patient was included in the discovery cohort for the JARID2 DNA methylation episignature.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Noonan syndrome-like disorder with loose anagen hair-1 (NSLH1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the SHOC2 gene (602775) on chromosome 10q25. ⚠︎

### Description

Noonan syndrome-like disorder with loose anagen hair-1 is a RASopathy that shares features with Noonan syndrome, including characteristic facial features, short stature, and congenital heart defects (particularly dysplasia of the mitral valve and septal defects). Distinguishing features are hair abnormalities: hair that is easily pluckable, sparse, thin, and slow-growing (loose anagen hair). Other features include growth hormone deficiency, darkly pigmented skin with eczema or ichthyosis, hypernasal voice, and cognitive deficits, often with hyperactive behavior.

### Clinical Features

Mazzanti et al. (2003) described patients with a Noonan-like phenotype associated with loose anagen hair, which they proposed as a distinct entity. In addition to Noonan-like facial features and short stature, the patients had easily pluckable, sparse, slow-growing hair, and growth hormone deficiency was noted. ⚠︎

Cordeddu et al. (2009) delineated the clinical phenotype of patients carrying the same SHOC2 mutation, noting a distinctive combination of Noonan-like features with loose anagen hair, short stature frequently associated with growth hormone deficiency, darkly pigmented skin with eczema or ichthyosis, hypernasal voice, cardiac defects with frequent mitral valve dysplasia and septal defects, and cognitive deficits with hyperactive behavior.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous SHOC2 mutation identified by Cordeddu et al. (2009) occurred de novo in the patients tested, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

A similar phenotype, Noonan syndrome-like disorder with loose anagen hair-2 (NSLH2), is caused by mutation in the PPP1CB gene.

### Molecular Genetics

Cordeddu et al. (2009) identified a recurrent heterozygous c.4A-G transition in the SHOC2 gene, resulting in a ser2-to-gly (S2G) substitution, in patients with Noonan-like syndrome with loose anagen hair. The mutation arose de novo. Functional studies showed that the substitution introduced an N-myristoylation site, resulting in aberrant myristoylation of SHOC2 and its constitutive targeting to the plasma membrane, which impaired its translocation to the nucleus upon growth factor stimulation and enhanced MAPK activation in a cell type-specific manner. ⚠︎

Komatsuzaki et al. (2010) identified the SHOC2 S2G mutation in Japanese patients with a phenotype in the Noonan syndrome spectrum including loose anagen hair. ⚠︎

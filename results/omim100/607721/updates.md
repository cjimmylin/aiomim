# Proposed updates for OMIM 607721: NOONAN SYNDROME-LIKE DISORDER WITH LOOSE ANAGEN HAIR 1

Literature from 2014 onwards that the entry does not cite: 59 papers screened, 8 read, 8 proposal(s). Each proposal is a draft for a curator to accept, edit or reject.

**Back-test as of 2014.** OMIM's curators later added 4 reference(s) from that year on; this run proposed 2 of them (recall 0.5), and 6 paper(s) OMIM does not cite.

## [MEDIUM] Motta et al. (2019) — new_variants, functional_evidence, phenotype_expansion

Motta M, Giancotti A, Mastromoro G, Chandramouli B, Pinna V, Pantaleoni F, Di Giosaffatte N, Petrini S, Mazza T, D'Ambrosio V, and others. Clinical and functional characterization of a novel RASopathy-causing SHOC2 mutation associated with prenatal-onset hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. Hum Mutat 40: 1046-1056, 2019. [PubMed: 31059601] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** The only SHOC2 variant in the entry so far is S2G. This paper adds a new SHOC2 mutation, c.807_808delinsTT (p.Gln269_His270delinsHisTyr), in a patient whose hypertrophic cardiomyopathy began before birth. Its functional data point to a mechanism that does not involve membrane targeting: the mutant binds MRAS and PPP1CB more strongly and increases MAPK signaling, but unlike S2G it is not constitutively at the plasma membrane.

*molecular_genetics*: Motta et al. (2019) reported a RASopathy-causing SHOC2 mutation, c.807_808delinsTT (p.Gln269_His270delinsHisTyr). They described it as the third such SHOC2 mutation and found it in association with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy that began before birth. Structural analyses suggested that the mutation could change how SHOC2's two leucine-rich repeat domains are oriented relative to each other. Compared with wildtype, the mutant protein bound MRAS and PPP1CB more strongly and increased signaling through the MAPK cascade, which supports an activating effect.

*gene_function*: Motta et al. (2019) found that the SHOC2 Q269_H270delinsHY mutant bound MRAS and PPP1CB more strongly than wildtype and increased MAPK signaling. Unlike SHOC2 S2G, it was not constitutively targeted to the plasma membrane. The authors concluded that different mechanisms of SHOC2 dysregulation converge on increased MAPK signaling.

*molecular_genetics* ⚠︎: SHOC2, 2-BP DELINS, 807TT

In a patient with a RASopathy and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy that began before birth, Motta et al. (2019) identified a c.807_808delinsTT change in the SHOC2 gene, resulting in a gln269-his270 delins his-tyr (Q269_H270delinsHY) substitution. Structural analyses predicted that the change would alter the relative orientation of SHOC2's two leucine-rich repeat domains. In functional studies, the mutant bound MRAS and PPP1CB more strongly and increased MAPK signaling, but unlike S2G it was not constitutively targeted to the plasma membrane.

## [MEDIUM] Jang et al. (2019) — animal_model

Jang H, Oakley E, Forbes-Osborne M, Kesler MV, Norcross R, Morris AC, Galperin E. Hematopoietic and neural crest defects in zebrafish shoc2 mutants: a novel vertebrate model for Noonan-like syndrome. Hum Mol Genet 28: 501-514, 2019. [PubMed: 30329053] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** The entry's only animal model is C. elegans expressing human SHOC2 S2G. Jang et al. (2019) add the first vertebrate loss-of-function model: zebrafish shoc2 mutants, in which loss of Shoc2 was essential for development and disrupted neural crest development and hematopoiesis.

*animal_model*: Jang et al. (2019) characterized a zebrafish shoc2 mutant. They showed that Shoc2 is essential for development and that its loss is detrimental to neural crest development and to hematopoiesis. Noting that the scaffold protein Shoc2 amplifies ERK1/2 pathway activity and modulates a variety of signaling inputs, and that mouse Shoc2 knockouts are embryonic lethal, the authors suggested that the zebrafish mutant provides a vertebrate system for structure-function analyses and genetic screens relevant to Noonan-like syndrome with loose anagen hair.

## [MEDIUM] Garavelli et al. (2015) — phenotype_expansion, natural_history

Garavelli L, Cordeddu V, Errico S, Bertolini P, Street ME, Rosato S, Pollazzon M, Wischmeijer A, Ivanovski I, Daniele P, and others. Noonan syndrome-like disorder with loose anagen hair: a second case with neuroblastoma. Am J Med Genet A 167A: 1902-7, 2015. [PubMed: 25846317] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** This paper adds neuroblastoma, found in infancy in a child with molecularly confirmed NSLH. Neither neuroblastoma nor any solid tumor is in the entry's feature list. The paper also screened pediatric cancers for somatic SHOC2 c.4A>G and found none, which extends the entry's existing negative leukemia data to neuroblastoma, brain tumors, rhabdomyosarcoma, ALL and myeloid leukemia.

*clinical_features*: Garavelli et al. (2015) reported a child with molecularly confirmed Noonan syndrome-like disorder with loose anagen hair who developed a neuroblastoma, first suspected at 3 months of age by abdominal ultrasound examination. The authors noted that this was the second instance of neuroblastoma in NSLH. They suggested that individuals heterozygous for the SHOC2 c.4A-G mutation may be predisposed to this malignancy.

*molecular_genetics*: After observing neuroblastoma in a patient with NSLH, Garavelli et al. (2015) scanned the region of the SHOC2 coding sequence encompassing the c.4A-G change in selected pediatric cohorts with malignancies documented to occur in RASopathies: neuroblastoma, brain tumors, rhabdomyosarcoma, acute lymphoblastic leukemia, and myeloid leukemia. They identified no functionally relevant cancer-associated variant and concluded that their results did not support a major role for somatic SHOC2 mutations in these pediatric cancers.

## [MEDIUM] Takenouchi et al. (2014) — phenotype_expansion

Takenouchi T, Sakamoto Y, Miwa T, Torii C, Kosaki R, Kishi K, Takahashi T, Kosaki K. Severe craniosynostosis with Noonan syndrome phenotype associated with SHOC2 mutation: clinical evidence of crosslink between FGFR and RAS signaling pathways. Am J Med Genet A 164A: 2869-72, 2014. [PubMed: 25123707] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** The paper reports a further patient with the known de novo SHOC2 S2G mutation. In that patient it describes severe multisuture craniosynostosis, fetal pleural effusion, fetal hydrops and atrial tachycardia, and none of these is among the clinical features the entry already lists. The variant is already in the entry, so this is a phenotype expansion only.

*clinical_features*: Takenouchi et al. (2014) described a boy with short stature, developmental delay, and severe craniosynostosis involving the right coronal, both lambdoid, and the sagittal sutures. In retrospect, he also had features suggesting a dysregulated RAS signaling cascade: fetal pleural effusion, fetal hydrops, and atrial tachycardia. Abnormal cranial shape had already been reported with the SHOC2 S2G mutation. The authors suggested that craniosynostosis itself might be a new feature associated with this mutation.

*molecular_genetics*: In a boy with a Noonan syndrome phenotype and severe craniosynostosis, Takenouchi et al. (2014) identified a de novo c.4A-G mutation in exon 1 of the SHOC2 gene, resulting in a ser2-to-gly (S2G) substitution.

*pathogenesis*: Takenouchi et al. (2014) suggested that the combination of severe craniosynostosis and RASopathy features in their patient pointed to a crosslink between the RAS and FGFR signaling cascades. They proposed that patients with craniosynostosis and any RASopathy feature may need mutation screening of molecules in the FGFR-RAS signaling cascade.

## [MEDIUM] Hannig et al. (2014) — new_variants, functional_evidence, genotype_phenotype_correlation, phenotype_expansion

Hannig V, Jeoung M, Jang ER, Phillips JA, Galperin E. A Novel SHOC2 Variant in Rasopathy. Hum Mutat 35: 1290-4, 2014. [PubMed: 25137548] *(read: fulltext)*

**Why:** Hannig et al. (2014) report the first SHOC2 variant other than S2G in the entry: M173I (c.519G>A). They describe it as a loss-of-function change that impairs PP1c recruitment. It was inherited from a mildly affected father and caused a milder phenotype, without short stature, heart defects, hyperpigmentation or loose anagen hair. The paper also adds functional data and a genotype-phenotype contrast with S2G.

*clinical_features* ⚠︎: Hannig et al. (2014) described a 5-year-old girl of European ancestry, born to nonconsanguineous parents. She was referred for right optic nerve hypoplasia, bilateral nystagmus, speech delay, poor hair growth, and hyperactive behavior. Her stature was average (47th percentile), and she had macrocephaly (98th percentile), bilateral ptosis, wide-spaced nipples, and clubbed nails. Her hair was sparse and slow-growing but not loose, and her skin pigmentation was normal. MRI showed multiple irregularities of unknown significance in the midbrain, pons, and medulla. Echocardiogram and renal ultrasound were normal. Her father, who carried the same SHOC2 variant, was 6 feet 3 inches tall and had intellectual disability, a high-arched palate, sparse hair, and clubbed nails. His features were very subtle and did not clearly denote a Rasopathy. Both parents had required special education classes.

*molecular_genetics*: Hannig et al. (2014) studied a girl with a mild Rasopathy phenotype that overlapped Noonan syndrome, cardiofaciocutaneous syndrome, and Noonan-like syndrome with loose anagen hair. Using Rasopathy gene panel sequencing, they identified a heterozygous c.519G-A transition in the SHOC2 gene, resulting in a met173-to-ile (M173I) substitution. She had inherited the variant from her mildly affected father. It was not found in 6,500 asymptomatic individuals. Met173 lies in the fourth leucine-rich repeat and is conserved among vertebrate orthologs. In functional studies, the mutant was a loss-of-function variant that impaired PP1c recruitment and RAF-1 activation.

*gene_function*: Hannig et al. (2014) found that wildtype SHOC2 fully restored EGF-induced ERK1/2 phosphorylation in SHOC2-depleted Cos1 cells. The M173I mutant restored it to only about 10% above basal levels. The mutant had normal protein stability, normal subcellular localization, and normal binding to M-RAS and RAF-1. However, it failed to coprecipitate PP1c effectively and produced only a mild increase in RAF-1 S338 phosphorylation. The authors concluded that M173I is a loss-of-function variant that impairs PP1c recruitment and RAF-1 activation.

*genotype_phenotype_correlations* ⚠︎: Hannig et al. (2014) noted that the phenotype of patients with the M173I mutation differed significantly from that of patients with the S2G mutation. Their proband lacked short stature, cardiac anomalies, hyperpigmentation, ichthyosis, and loose anagen hair, and her developmental delay was relatively mild. The authors concluded that SHOC2 mutations can cause a spectrum of Rasopathy phenotypes of varying severity. They suggested that individuals with mild symptoms may be underdiagnosed.

## [LOW] Couser et al. (2018) — phenotype_expansion

Couser NL, Keelean-Fuller D, Davenport ML, Haverfield E, Masood MM, Henin M, Aylsworth AS. Cleft palate and hypopituitarism in a patient with Noonan-like syndrome with loose anagen hair-1. Am J Med Genet A 176: 2024-2027, 2018. [PubMed: 30240112] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** This is a single case report of a woman with the recurrent SHOC2 S2G mutation. It adds features the entry does not list: posterior cleft palate, micrognathia, hearing loss, and hypopituitarism with TSH and gonadotropin deficiency on top of growth hormone deficiency. The variant is already in the table, and the authors' description summary repeats what the entry says, so neither is drafted.

*clinical_features* ⚠︎: Couser et al. (2018) reported a female patient with NSLH1 and the SHOC2 S2G mutation. She was born with a posterior cleft palate, micrognathia, and mild hypotonia. In childhood and young adulthood she developed hearing loss, strabismus, and hypopituitarism with deficiencies of growth hormone, thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), and gonadotropins. The authors noted that true cleft palate did not appear to have been reported before in Noonan syndrome or NSLH. They also noted that endocrine abnormalities other than growth hormone deficiency had rarely been documented. They suggested that the SHOC2 mutation may be responsible for the cleft palate and the hypopituitarism.

## [LOW] Baldassarre et al. (2014) — phenotype_expansion

Baldassarre G, Mussa A, Banaudi E, Rossi C, Tartaglia M, Silengo M, Ferrero GB. Phenotypic variability associated with the invariant SHOC2 c.4A>G (p.Ser2Gly) missense mutation. Am J Med Genet A 164A: 3120-5, 2014. [PubMed: 25331583] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** This paper adds 2 more patients with the SHOC2 c.4A>G (S2G) mutation that is already listed. The only new point is that the two had very different cardiac severity and neurocognitive profiles, and the authors argue the phenotypic spectrum is wider than previously recognized. It adds no new variant, gene or individual clinical feature, and its description repeats what the entry already says.

*clinical_features*: Baldassarre et al. (2014) reported 2 patients with molecularly confirmed Noonan-like syndrome with loose anagen hair whose phenotypes differed greatly, particularly in the severity of the cardiac phenotype and in the neurocognitive profile. The authors concluded that the phenotypic spectrum associated with this invariant mutation is wider than previously recognized.

*molecular_genetics*: In 2 patients with NSLH whose phenotypes differed markedly, Baldassarre et al. (2014) identified the recurrent SHOC2 c.4A-G transition, resulting in a ser2-to-gly (S2G) substitution.

## [LOW] Zmolikova et al. (2014) — phenotype_expansion, treatment_or_management

Zmolikova M, Puchmajerova A, Hecht P, Lebl J, Trkova M, Krepelova A. Coarctation of the aorta in Noonan-like syndrome with loose anagen hair. Am J Med Genet A 164A: 1218-21, 2014. [PubMed: 24458596] *(read: abstract)*

**Why:** This single case report has the known SHOC2 c.4A>G (S2G) mutation, so it adds no new variant. What is new for the entry is coarctation of the aorta with a small ventricular septal defect and increased nuchal translucency seen prenatally, none of which the entry lists, plus one observation of a partial response to growth hormone therapy.

*clinical_features*: Zmolikova et al. (2014) reported a girl with molecularly confirmed Noonan-like syndrome with loose anagen hair who had coarctation of the aorta. Prenatal findings were increased nuchal translucency and aortic coarctation with a small ventricular septal defect. Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy was found after birth. She was born precipitously at 37 weeks and needed resuscitation. She had facial dysmorphism typical of Noonan syndrome, redundant skin over the nape and back, short stature, and relative macrocephaly. She also had failure to thrive with a dystrophic appearance and developmental delay, mainly in motor milestones. Beginning a few weeks after birth, her hair was very thin, sparse, and slow-growing. The authors noted that coarctation of the aorta is not a typical heart defect in this disorder and concluded that their observation extended the phenotypic spectrum.

*molecular_genetics* ⚠︎: In a girl with Noonan-like syndrome with loose anagen hair and coarctation of the aorta, Zmolikova et al. (2014) molecularly confirmed the c.4A-G mutation in the SHOC2 gene, predicting a ser2-to-gly (S2G) substitution.

*clinical_management*: Zmolikova et al. (2014) found low IGF-1 levels and borderline growth hormone deficiency in a girl with Noonan-like syndrome with loose anagen hair and the SHOC2 S2G mutation. Growth hormone therapy started at 16 months had a partial effect and prevented further decline in growth.

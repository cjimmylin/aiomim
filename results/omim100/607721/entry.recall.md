### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Noonan syndrome-like disorder with loose anagen hair-1 (NSLH1) is caused by mutation in the SHOC2 gene (602775) on chromosome 10q25.2.

### Description

Noonan syndrome-like disorder with loose anagen hair-1 is a RASopathy (Gripp et al., 2013). It shares features with Noonan syndrome, including characteristic facial features, short stature, and congenital heart defects, particularly dysplasia of the mitral valve and septal defects (Mazzanti et al., 2003; Cordeddu et al., 2009). Its distinguishing features are hair abnormalities: the hair is easily pluckable, sparse, thin, and slow-growing (loose anagen hair) (Cordeddu et al., 2009). Other features include growth hormone deficiency, darkly pigmented skin with eczema or ichthyosis, hypernasal voice, and cognitive deficits, often with hyperactive behavior (Mazzanti et al., 2003; Cordeddu et al., 2009).

### Clinical Features

Mazzanti et al. (2003) described 3 children with short stature, a shared facial phenotype, and loose anagen hair. They proposed that the condition was a distinct, previously unreported entity, which they called 'Noonan-like syndrome with loose anagen hair.' The children's appearance was not completely typical of Noonan syndrome. The authors also noted severe growth hormone deficiency.

Cordeddu et al. (2009) delineated the clinical phenotype of patients carrying the same SHOC2 mutation. The patients had a distinctive combination of Noonan-like features and loose anagen hair. They also had short stature, frequently associated with proven growth hormone deficiency; darkly pigmented skin with eczema or ichthyosis; and a characteristically hypernasal voice. Cardiac anomalies were present in most cases, and mitral valve dysplasia and septal defects were significantly overrepresented compared with the general Noonan syndrome population. The patients also had cognitive deficits and distinctive hyperactive behavior.

### Inheritance

Cordeddu et al. (2009) showed that the SHOC2 mutation had arisen de novo in the 12 families from which parental DNA was available. In these patients, DNA from several tissues all carried the S2G mutation, which was evidence that the defect had been inherited through the germline. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Bertola et al. (2017) described a similar phenotype, PPP1CB-related Noonan syndrome with loose anagen hair (NSLH2), which is caused by mutation in the PPP1CB gene.

### Molecular Genetics

In patients with Noonan-like syndrome with loose anagen hair, Cordeddu et al. (2009) identified a recurrent c.4A-G transition in the SHOC2 gene, resulting in a ser2-to-gly (S2G) substitution. The mutation had arisen de novo in the families from which parental DNA was available. In functional studies, the substitution introduced an N-myristoylation site, which led to aberrant myristoylation of SHOC2. The mutant protein was constitutively targeted to the plasma membrane, and its translocation to the nucleus upon growth factor stimulation was impaired. Expression of SHOC2 S2G in vitro enhanced MAPK activation in a cell type-specific manner.

Komatsuzaki et al. (2010) identified the SHOC2 S2G mutation in patients with a phenotype in the Noonan syndrome spectrum that included loose anagen hair.

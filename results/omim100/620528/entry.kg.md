### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal dominant distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-11 (HMND11) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the SPTAN1 gene (182810) on chromosome 9q34.

### Description

Autosomal dominant distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-11 (HMND11) is a juvenile-onset distal hereditary motor neuropathy caused by heterozygous nonsense mutations in the SPTAN1 gene, which encodes alpha-II spectrin. Penetrance may be variable, and severity differs greatly between patients (Beijer et al., 2019).

### Clinical Features

Beijer et al. (2019) reported 3 families with juvenile-onset distal hereditary motor neuropathy. Penetrance was variable in 2 of the 3 families, and the severity of the phenotype differed greatly between patients.

### Inheritance

Beijer et al. (2019) reported autosomal dominant inheritance of distal hereditary motor neuropathy in 3 unrelated families with heterozygous SPTAN1 nonsense mutations. Penetrance was variable in 2 of the 3 families.

Dong et al. (2021) reported a de novo SPTAN1 nonsense variant causing hereditary motor neuropathy in a Chinese family.

### Molecular Genetics

Beijer et al. (2019) used next-generation sequencing to study affected members of 3 unrelated families with juvenile-onset autosomal dominant distal hereditary motor neuropathy. They found heterozygous nonsense mutations in the SPTAN1 gene. In patient cells, mutant SPTAN1 mRNA was broken down by nonsense-mediated decay, and alpha-II spectrin protein levels were reduced.

Dong et al. (2021) reported a novel de novo nonsense variant in the SPTAN1 gene that caused hereditary motor neuropathy in a Chinese family.

Ylikallio et al. (2020) reported a de novo SPTAN1 mutation in a patient with axonal sensorimotor neuropathy and a developmental disorder. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

In patient cells, Beijer et al. (2019) found that mutant SPTAN1 mRNA carrying the nonsense mutations was broken down by nonsense-mediated decay and that alpha-II spectrin protein levels were reduced. They contrasted this with the dominant-negative SPTAN1 mutations previously described as causing a spectrum of epilepsy phenotypes. ⚠︎

### Description

Autosomal dominant distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-11 (HMND11) is a neurologic disorder in which degeneration of lower motor neurons causes slowly progressive distal muscle weakness and atrophy, mainly in the lower limbs. Sensory function is largely spared. Onset is usually in childhood or adolescence. ✗

### Clinical Features

Beijer et al. (2023) described several unrelated families with autosomal dominant distal hereditary motor neuropathy that began in childhood or adolescence. Affected individuals had slowly progressive weakness and wasting of the distal lower limbs, with foot deformities. Electrophysiologic studies showed a motor axonal neuropathy, and sensory involvement was absent or minimal. ✗

### Inheritance

The families reported by Beijer et al. (2023) showed autosomal dominant transmission of HMND11.

### Molecular Genetics

Beijer et al. (2023) found heterozygous nonsense mutations in the SPTAN1 gene, which encodes alpha-II spectrin, in affected members of families with juvenile-onset distal hereditary motor neuropathy. The mutations segregated with the disorder in the families. ⚠︎

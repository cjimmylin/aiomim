### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia type III (HHC3) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the AP2S1 gene (602242) on chromosome 19q13.32.

### Description

Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia type III is an autosomal dominant disorder of extracellular calcium homeostasis. It is characterized by lifelong hypercalcemia with inappropriately low urinary calcium excretion (Nesbit et al. (2013)). HHC3 is genetically distinct from HHC1, which Hannan et al. (2010) attributed to mutation in the calcium-sensing receptor gene (CASR). Nesbit et al. (2013) showed that HHC3 results from mutation in AP2S1, the sigma-2 subunit of adaptor protein-2, which has a role in endocytosis of the calcium-sensing receptor.

### Clinical Features

McMurtry et al. (1992) described a large kindred with familial benign (hypocalciuric) hypercalcemia. Affected members were hypercalcemic. Their serum immunoreactive PTH levels became supranormal by about age 30 years. Their serum inorganic phosphate levels were lower than those of unaffected kindred members. Some affected adults had radiographic changes suggestive of osteomalacia. The authors suggested that they might have observed an unusual variant of familial benign hypercalcemia.

### Inheritance

Nesbit et al. (2013) described familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia type III as an autosomal dominant disorder. They showed that the AP2S1 mutation cosegregated with the disorder in members of the 2 kindreds they studied.

### Mapping

Lloyd et al. (1999) performed a genomewide search in an Oklahoma family with a variant of familial benign hypercalcemia (FBHOk). Linkage to chromosomes 3q and 19p had previously been excluded in this family. Lloyd et al. (1999) established linkage between the disorder and loci on chromosome 19q13.

### Heterogeneity

Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia is genetically heterogeneous. Hannan et al. (2010) noted that HHC1 is caused by mutation in CASR on chromosome 3q21.1. Nesbit et al. (2013) noted that the HHC2 locus maps to chromosome 19p, and they showed that HHC3 is caused by mutation in AP2S1 on chromosome 19q13.3.

### Molecular Genetics

Nesbit et al. (2013) studied the previously reported Oklahoma kindred and patients with familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia in whom CASR mutations had not been detected. They identified heterozygous missense mutations in the AP2S1 gene that all affected the same residue, arg15 (R15C, R15H, and R15L). In vitro, the mutations decreased the sensitivity of cells expressing the calcium-sensing receptor to extracellular calcium and reduced receptor endocytosis. Nesbit et al. (2013) concluded that AP2-sigma-2 has a role in calcium-sensing receptor endocytosis and signaling, and that disruption of this role by the arg15 mutants results in HHC3. ⚠︎

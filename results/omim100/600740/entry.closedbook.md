### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia type III (HHC3) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the AP2S1 gene (602242) on chromosome 19q13.

### Description

Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia type III is an autosomal dominant disorder of extracellular calcium homeostasis. It is marked by lifelong hypercalcemia with inappropriately normal or raised parathyroid hormone (PTH) levels and low urinary calcium excretion. It is genetically distinct from HHC1, which is caused by mutation in the calcium-sensing receptor gene (CASR). HHC3 results from mutation in the sigma-2 subunit of adaptor protein-2 (AP2S1), which takes part in clathrin-mediated endocytosis of the calcium-sensing receptor. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

McMurtry et al. (1992) described a large kindred from Oklahoma with familial benign hypercalcemia. Affected members had hypercalcemia, raised serum PTH, hypophosphatemia and low urinary calcium excretion, and some had features of osteomalacia. These features set the kindred apart from typical families with familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia, and the disorder became known as the Oklahoma variant. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

In the Oklahoma kindred, HHC3 was transmitted as an autosomal dominant trait. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Lloyd et al. (1999) used linkage analysis in the Oklahoma kindred to map the familial benign hypercalcemia Oklahoma variant to chromosome 19q13. They excluded linkage to the CASR locus on chromosome 3q and to the HHC2 locus on 19p. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia is genetically heterogeneous. HHC1 is caused by mutation in CASR on chromosome 3q, HHC2 by mutation in GNA11 on chromosome 19p, and HHC3 by mutation in AP2S1 on chromosome 19q. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Nesbit et al. (2013) studied the Oklahoma kindred and patients with familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia who did not carry CASR mutations. They identified heterozygous missense mutations in the AP2S1 gene, all affecting the same residue, arg15 (R15C, R15H and R15L). In vitro, the mutations reduced the sensitivity of cells expressing the calcium-sensing receptor to extracellular calcium and reduced receptor endocytosis. These results suggested that the arg15 mutations impair AP2-mediated internalization of the calcium-sensing receptor.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Later reports suggested that patients with AP2S1 mutations may have higher serum calcium levels and lower urinary calcium excretion than patients with HHC1, and some have had cognitive or developmental problems. The details of these reports are not given here. ✗

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia type III (HHC3) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the AP2S1 gene on chromosome 19q13.

### Description

Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia type 3 (FHH3) is an autosomal dominant disorder of extracellular calcium homeostasis affecting the parathyroids, kidneys, and bone. Like FHH1 and FHH2, it is characterized by lifelong elevation of serum calcium with inappropriately low urinary calcium excretion, but FHH3 has distinctive features that include increased serum PTH concentrations, hypophosphatemia, and osteomalacia (Nesbit et al., 2013). Howles et al. (2016) described FHH3 as the most severe of the 3 variants clinically.

### Nomenclature

Trump et al. (1995) designated the form of familial benign hypercalcemia segregating in a kindred from Oklahoma the Oklahoma variant, FBH(Ok). Hannan et al. (2010) noted that FBHH3 is also referred to as the Oklahoma variant.

### Clinical Features

McMurtry et al. (1992) described a large kindred with familial benign hypercalcemia in which 46 members spanning 5 generations, aged 1.5 to 91 years, were surveyed biochemically and/or by chart review; 19 were affected. In the 15 hypercalcemic subjects examined biochemically, serum immunoreactive PTH levels, measured in 3 separate immunoassays, became supranormal by about age 30 years and appeared to increase further thereafter. Serum alkaline phosphatase and creatinine were normal, but inorganic phosphate levels were lower than in unaffected kindred members. Three of 5 affected adults older than 40 years who were studied radiographically had changes suggestive of osteomalacia, and iliac crest biopsy after tetracycline labeling in a 51-year-old woman confirmed defective skeletal mineralization. The authors concluded that the disorder may not be totally benign and that osteomalacia, perhaps due to mild hypophosphatemia, can develop in adulthood.

Trump et al. (1995) studied a 5-generation kindred from Oklahoma (19 affected, 2 obligate carriers, and 20 unaffected) in which familial benign hypercalcemia was associated with a developmental elevation in serum PTH levels.

In their further study of the Oklahoma kindred, Hannan et al. (2010) established the phenotype by documenting hypercalcemia with a ratio of calcium clearance to creatinine clearance below 0.01. Elevated serum PTH had been observed in some affected individuals older than 30 years, and 3 adults older than 40 years showed evidence of osteomalacia.

Nesbit et al. (2010) reported a second FHH3 kindred. The proband, a 43-year-old woman without a CASR mutation in the coding region or splice sites, presented with a corrected serum calcium of 2.74 mmol/liter (normal, 2.15-2.55), serum PTH of 47 pg/ml (normal, 10-65), and a urinary calcium clearance:creatinine clearance ratio of 0.006. Of 24 members from 3 generations of her kindred, 16 were hypercalcemic with normal or elevated serum PTH and mild hypophosphatemia, features consistent with FHH3.

Fujisawa et al. (2013) reported a Japanese female infant found to have hypercalcemia on routine testing for poor weight gain on breast feeding. At 49 days of age, serum calcium (adjusted by Payne's formula) was 13.1 mg/dL, intact PTH 27 pg/mL, and urinary calcium-to-creatinine ratio 1.29 mg/mg. There was no evidence of hyperparathyroidism, PTHrP-producing neoplasm, or vitamin D excess. Thus, unlike typical FHH, the infant had hypercalciuria.

Howles et al. (2016) reported 3 unrelated probands with FHH3. A 34-year-old woman with a 12-year history of hypercalcemia presented with fatigue, headaches, and persistent generalized aches that did not resolve after parathyroidectomy; a 22-year-old man presented with hypercalcemia, fatigue, and generalized rib pain; and a 52-year-old woman with an approximately 20-year history of hypercalcemia presented with headaches, abdominal pain, vomiting, fatigue, and musculoskeletal pain that did not resolve after pamidronate infusion or parathyroidectomy.

### Biochemical Features

Nesbit et al. (2013) tabulated biochemical findings in 13 FHH3 families or patients with AP2S1 mutations. In the Oklahoma kindred (13 affected females and 3 affected males), mean serum calcium was 2.88 mmol/l and mean phosphate 0.77 mmol/l, with a mean urinary calcium:creatinine clearance ratio of 0.003; in the second kindred (11 females and 5 males), mean serum calcium was 2.72 mmol/l and mean phosphate 0.95 mmol/l (normal ranges, calcium 2.10-2.60 mmol/l and phosphate 0.70-1.40 mmol/l).

### Inheritance

FHH3 segregated in multigenerational kindreds (Trump et al., 1995; Nesbit et al., 2010), and linkage was analyzed under an autosomal dominant model (Hannan et al., 2010). Nesbit et al. (2013) found heterozygous AP2S1 mutations in affected individuals, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. The mutation in the infant reported by Fujisawa et al. (2013) was presumably de novo.

### Mapping

Using 17 polymorphic markers in the 5-generation Oklahoma kindred, Trump et al. (1995) excluded linkage to the candidate regions 3q21-q24, 19p13.3, 11q13, and 11p15 (lod scores less than -2.00 at theta = 0.05-0.25); multipoint crossover analysis and the LINKMAP program confirmed the exclusion.

By a genomewide search with microsatellite polymorphisms in the Oklahoma family, Lloyd et al. (1999) established linkage between FBHOk and 8 loci on chromosome 19q13, with a maximum lod score of 6.67 (theta = 0.00) at D19S606. Recombinants mapped the locus to a less than 12-cM interval flanked by D19S908 and D19S866. Sequencing of the coding region, 5-prime UTR, and part of the promoter of the calmodulin III gene, which lies in this interval, in an affected individual detected no abnormality.

Hannan et al. (2010) refined the FBHH3 locus with 24 polymorphic loci (17 microsatellites and 7 SNPs) in 34 members from 5 generations of the Oklahoma kindred (19 affected). The peak lod score was 6.92 at 0% recombination with D19S606, and recombinants placed FBHH3 in a 4.1-Mb interval flanked by D19S112 and rs245111. The syntenic region on mouse chromosome 7 contains 4 Casr-related sequences (Gprc2a-rs), but human homologs were not found; comparative analysis showed loss from the human genome of an 11.6-Mb region containing these 4 sequences. Sequencing of the coding regions and intron-exon boundaries of 12 other candidate genes from the interval that are expressed in parathyroid and/or kidney found no abnormalities in an affected individual.

In a second FHH3 kindred, Nesbit et al. (2010) demonstrated cosegregation of FHH with polymorphic loci on chromosome 19q13.3, with a peak lod score of 5.98 at 0% recombination with D19S412. Recombinants mapped FHH to a 3.46-Mbp interval flanked centromerically by rs1990932 and telomerically by D19S604.

### Heterogeneity

Trump et al. (1995) excluded linkage of FBH in the Oklahoma kindred to the FBH regions on 3q21-q24 and 19p13.3 and to the regions containing the MEN1 (11q13) and PTH (11p15) genes, indicating further genetic heterogeneity and a third locus for FBH.

### Molecular Genetics

Nesbit et al. (2013) performed exome sequencing in an affected member of each of the 2 unrelated FHH3 kindreds (the Oklahoma kindred and the kindred reported by Nesbit et al., 2010). Analysis of variants within the 3.46-Mbp 19q13.3 interval identified in both patients a single variant, a C-to-T transition in exon 2 of the AP2S1 gene predicting an arg15-to-cys (R15C) substitution. The change was confirmed by Sanger sequencing, and restriction enzyme analysis showed cosegregation in members (reported as 32 affected and 23 unaffected) from 4 generations of the 2 kindreds. Sequencing of AP2S1 in 50 unrelated FHH patients in whom CASR mutations had been excluded identified 11 heterozygous missense mutations, all affecting arg15: 4 R15C, 3 arg15-to-his (R15H), and 4 arg15-to-leu (R15L). Arg15 is conserved in AP2-sigma-2 orthologs and in human AP sigma subunit paralogs, and the authors estimated that AP2S1 Arg15 mutations may underlie FHH in more than 20% of patients without CASR mutations. In HEK293 cells stably expressing CASR, all 3 mutants shifted the concentration-response curves to extracellular calcium to the right, with significantly higher EC50 values than wildtype, indicating decreased sensitivity to extracellular calcium; the R15C mutant gave an EC50 significantly higher than vector alone, suggesting a possible dominant-negative effect. Following calcium stimulation, cells transfected with R15C had less reduction in cell-surface CASR than cells transfected with wildtype AP2S1, indicating reduced CASR endocytosis. Removal of the C-terminal CASR dileucine motif also decreased cellular sensitivity to extracellular calcium. ⚠︎

In a Japanese infant with hypercalcemia and hypercalciuria, Fujisawa et al. (2013) found no pathologic mutation in CASR or GNA11 but identified a presumably de novo heterozygous R15L mutation in the AP2S1 gene.

Howles et al. (2016) reported 3 unrelated probands with FHH3 who had heterozygous AP2S1 mutations resulting in R15C, R15H, or R15L substitutions. In cells expressing CASR and the mutant AP2-sigma proteins, cinacalcet (10 nM) corrected the abnormal intracellular calcium signaling and MAPK responses to extracellular calcium.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Nesbit et al. (2013) found that patients with the R15C, R15H, and R15L AP2S1 mutations, aged 8 days to 78 years, had similar elevations in serum calcium and PTH and reductions in urinary calcium excretion, indicating no correlation between mutation and clinical phenotype.

### Diagnosis

McMurtry et al. (1992) noted that in their kindred FBH in adults could be especially difficult to distinguish from primary hyperparathyroidism because serum PTH levels may be elevated. Fujisawa et al. (2013) concluded from their infant patient that lack of hypocalciuria does not necessarily argue against the presence of AP2S1 mutations, and suggested that the early infantile age of their patient played a role in the hypercalciuria.

### Clinical Management

In their infant with an AP2S1 mutation, Fujisawa et al. (2013) found that a low calcium formula containing 2.6 mg/dL of calcium led to catch-up growth and decreases in serum calcium and urinary calcium-to-creatinine ratio; feeding with a 2:1 mixture of low calcium and standard formula maintained serum calcium at approximately 12 mg/dL without markedly increasing serum PTH.

Howles et al. (2016) administered oral cinacalcet, 30 to 60 mg daily, to 3 symptomatic FHH3 probands over a 33- to 45-month period. Treatment led to more than 20% reductions in serum calcium and abatement of symptoms; serum phosphate increased and PTH decreased, although both remained within the normal range. Nausea, vomiting, and hypocalcemia did not develop.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 HYPOCALCIURIC HYPERCALCEMIA, FAMILIAL, TYPE III**  
AP2S1, ARG15CYS [Arg15Cys]

In affected members of 2 unrelated FHH3 kindreds, including the Oklahoma kindred, Nesbit et al. (2013) identified a heterozygous C-to-T transition in exon 2 of the AP2S1 gene, resulting in an arg15-to-cys (R15C) substitution. The mutation was found by exome sequencing and segregated with the disorder in the 2 kindreds. The same mutation was found in 4 of 50 unrelated FHH patients without CASR mutations. In HEK293 cells expressing CASR, the mutant increased the EC50 for extracellular calcium beyond that seen with vector alone, suggesting a possible dominant-negative effect, and reduced calcium-stimulated CASR endocytosis.

Howles et al. (2016) reported a symptomatic FHH3 proband with a heterozygous R15C mutation whose serum calcium decreased and whose symptoms abated with cinacalcet treatment.

**.0002 HYPOCALCIURIC HYPERCALCEMIA, FAMILIAL, TYPE III**  
AP2S1, ARG15LEU [Arg15Leu; p.Arg15Leu]

Among 50 unrelated FHH patients without CASR mutations, Nesbit et al. (2013) found 4 with a heterozygous CGC-to-CTC change in the AP2S1 gene, resulting in an arg15-to-leu (R15L) substitution. The mutant decreased the sensitivity of CASR-expressing HEK293 cells to extracellular calcium.

In a Japanese infant with hypercalcemia and hypercalciuria, Fujisawa et al. (2013) identified a presumably de novo heterozygous R15L mutation in AP2S1.

Howles et al. (2016) reported a symptomatic FHH3 proband with a heterozygous R15L mutation who responded to cinacalcet.

**.0003 HYPOCALCIURIC HYPERCALCEMIA, FAMILIAL, TYPE III**  
AP2S1, ARG15HIS [Arg15His]

Among 50 unrelated FHH patients without CASR mutations, Nesbit et al. (2013) found 3 with a heterozygous CGC-to-CAC change in the AP2S1 gene, resulting in an arg15-to-his (R15H) substitution. Although arginine and histidine are both positively charged, the mutant significantly increased the EC50 of CASR-expressing HEK293 cells for extracellular calcium compared with wildtype.

Howles et al. (2016) reported a symptomatic FHH3 proband with a heterozygous R15H mutation who responded to cinacalcet.

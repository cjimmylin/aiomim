### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that MEHMO syndrome (MEHMO) is caused by hemizygous mutation in the EIF2S3 gene (300161) on chromosome Xp22.

### Description

MEHMO syndrome is an X-linked recessive intellectual developmental disorder in males. Its name comes from its main features: mental retardation, epileptic seizures, hypogonadism and hypogenitalism, microcephaly, and obesity. Affected males have severe developmental delay, and many die early in childhood. Some patients also have glucose dysregulation and pituitary hormone deficiencies.

### Clinical Features

Steinmuller et al. (1998) described a family in which several males in more than one generation had severe mental retardation, epileptic seizures, hypogonadism with hypogenitalism, microcephaly, and obesity. They named the condition with the acronym MEHMO. Some affected boys died in early childhood. ⚠︎

Borck et al. (2012) reported a family in which males had X-linked syndromic intellectual disability with microcephaly, among other features.

Moortgat et al. (2016) restudied the family originally reported by Steinmuller et al. (1998). ✗

### Inheritance

MEHMO syndrome is inherited in an X-linked recessive pattern. Only males were affected, and the disease passed through unaffected carrier females, as seen in the family reported by Steinmuller et al. (1998). ✗

### Mapping

In the original family, linkage analysis placed the MEHMO locus on the short arm of the X chromosome, in Xp22. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In affected males of a family with X-linked syndromic intellectual disability, Borck et al. (2012) found a hemizygous missense mutation in the EIF2S3 gene, which encodes the gamma subunit of eukaryotic translation initiation factor 2 (eIF2). The mutation was ile222-to-thr (I222T). Functional studies in yeast showed that it weakened the binding of eIF2-gamma to the eIF2-beta subunit. This disrupted the integrity of the eIF2 complex and impaired translation initiation. ⚠︎

In affected males of the original MEHMO family (Steinmuller et al., 1998), Moortgat et al. (2016) found a hemizygous frameshift mutation in the EIF2S3 gene near the C terminus of the protein (I465fs). Functional studies supported impaired eIF2 function. ✗

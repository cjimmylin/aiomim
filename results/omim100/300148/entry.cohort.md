### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that MEHMO syndrome (MEHMO) is caused by hemizygous mutation in the EIF2S3 gene (300161) on chromosome Xp22.11.

### Description

MEHMO syndrome is an X-linked intellectual disability syndrome. Its name comes from its cardinal features: mental retardation, epileptic seizures, hypogonadism and hypogenitalism, microcephaly, and obesity (Steinmuller et al., 1998; DeLozier-Blanchet et al., 1999). In the family originally described, affected patients lived for less than 2 years (Steinmuller et al., 1998).

### Clinical Features

Delozier-Blanchet et al. (1989) described 2 brothers with a severe syndrome of postnatal growth retardation and mental retardation. Features included extreme microcephaly, obesity developing during infancy, microgonadism, and a characteristic amphora-shaped facies. Neurologic examination was highly abnormal, showing hypertonia, hyperreflexia, nystagmus, and extremely irritable and agitated behavior. The first child died at 4.5 years of age; he had also had neonatal hypoglycemia and chronic constipation. The authors noted that the brothers most resembled patients with Borjeson-Forssman-Lehmann syndrome (BFL). However, the dwarfism and mental retardation were much more severe than in any reported BFL patient, and the neurologic and dysmorphic features differed significantly. They considered 2 possibilities: a particularly severe, perhaps allelic, variant of BFL, or a previously undescribed disorder.

Steinmuller et al. (1998) described a large 3-generation family with an X-linked syndrome that they named MEHMO. Its clinical hallmarks were mental retardation, epileptic seizures, hypogonadism and hypogenitalism, microcephaly, and obesity. Affected patients lived for less than 2 years.

Leshinsky-Silver et al. (2002) described a child with MEHMO and lactic acidosis. A muscle biopsy showed markedly reduced activities of mitochondrial respiratory chain complexes I, III, and IV. Histologic staining showed mitochondrial proliferation and lipid storage. On electron microscopy the mitochondria were abnormal and enlarged, with concentric cristae and electron-dense bodies.

Borck et al. (2012) reported a family of Moroccan Jewish ancestry in the maternal line in which 3 males had an intellectual disability syndrome that appeared to be X-linked. Shared features were moderate to severe intellectual disability, microcephaly, short stature, and facial dysmorphism. Each affected individual also had features not seen in the others. Patient III:1 had cleft lip and palate and behavioral impairments. Patient III:2 had generalized seizures. Patient II:3 had postpubertal microgenitalism and obesity. Brain MRI of patient III:2 at 1 year of age showed microcephaly, a very thin corpus callosum, and mild enlargement and asymmetry of the lateral ventricles.

Moortgat et al. (2016) reported 2 unrelated families with suspected X-linked syndromic intellectual disability. In both families, affected males had severe intellectual disability, microcephaly, growth retardation, and epilepsy.

Skopkova et al. (2017) reported 4 families with MEHMO syndrome. Family 1 was Slovak and nonconsanguineous. In the index patient, microcephaly was detected prenatally at 28 weeks' gestation. Partial complex seizures were diagnosed at 4 months and did not respond to anticonvulsants. MEHMO was diagnosed at 10 months, based on microcephaly, seizures, hypogenitalism (micropenis and cryptorchidism), and severe mental and motor delay. Other findings included: - central obesity, short stature, and dysphagia - peripheral hypertonia with central hypotonia - dysmorphic features, including full cheeks, large ears, downturned corners of the mouth, epiblepharon, long eyelashes, thick eyebrows, tapered fingers, and talipes - delayed myelination, cerebral atrophy, and a thin corpus callosum - non-autoimmune diabetes mellitus treated with insulin from 10 months of age - panhypopituitarism and neonatal hypoglycemia - atrial septal defect, elevated liver enzymes, and severe combined dyslipidemia - recurrent bronchitis and bronchopneumonia - abnormal muscle mitochondria with decreased activity of respiratory complex II During intercurrent illness his condition worsened markedly, with more frequent seizures and poorer diabetes control. The authors noted that this resembled vanishing white matter disease.

The index patient of family 2 in the report of Skopkova et al. (2017) was the first child of nonconsanguineous Slovak parents. No other family members were known to be affected, and his mother was unaffected. He had low birth weight, microcephaly, and neonatal hypoglycemia. Seizures were diagnosed at 6 months; they did not respond to therapy and had no EEG correlate. Other findings were hypogenitalism, central obesity, short stature, hypotonia, partial hypopituitarism, and non-autoimmune diabetes mellitus treated with insulin from 10 months of age. Brain imaging showed delayed myelination, cerebral atrophy, and a thin corpus callosum. His mental and motor delay was severe: at 2 years his development corresponded to the third month of life, and he had no voluntary movements or social interaction. ⚠︎

Family 3 in the report of Skopkova et al. (2017) was the German family previously described by Steinmuller et al. (1998). It included 4 affected males, who died at ages 2 years, 7 months, 2 years, and 10 months. Microcephaly was diagnosed prenatally in the index patient (IV:3). Features included: - epileptic seizures that did not respond to therapy, diagnosed in the index patient at 7 weeks - hypogenitalism - severe mental and motor delay - central obesity, hypertonia, and postnatal growth retardation - dysmorphic features, including a narrow forehead, full cheeks, facial telangiectasias, downturned corners of the mouth, large ears, edematous hands and feet, tapered fingers, single palmar creases, and bilateral talipes - increased acylcarnitine and fatty acids One affected male had each of the following: low birth weight, diabetes with ketoacidosis at 6 months of age, and a small thyroid. Female carriers in this family were unaffected. ⚠︎

The index patient of family 4 in the report of Skopkova et al. (2017) was a boy of Northern European ancestry from the United States, born to nonconsanguineous parents and last examined at 4 years 7 months. He had microcephaly, hypospadias, cryptorchidism, and developmental delay. He had a long period of failure to thrive, followed by obesity with a BMI at the 97th centile. Other findings were short stature, central hypotonia with peripheral hypertonia and spasticity, chronic lung disease, dysphagia, delayed gastric emptying, grade 5 vesicoureteral reflux, and congenital scoliosis. He had no epileptic seizures and no diabetes, and his EEG was normal. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Delozier-Blanchet et al. (1989) noted that an X-linked recessive gene was a tempting explanation, given the importance of the X chromosome in mental retardation. They concluded, however, that the disorder in the 2 affected brothers was probably recessive, either autosomal or X-linked.

Steinmuller et al. (1998) mapped the disorder to the X chromosome in a large 3-generation family, establishing X-linked inheritance. Moortgat et al. (2016) found that female obligate carriers were heterozygous for EIF2S3 mutations while affected males were hemizygous. Skopkova et al. (2017) found that female carriers of the EIF2S3 mutation in the Steinmuller family were unaffected. These findings are consistent with X-linked recessive inheritance.

### Mapping

Steinmuller et al. (1998) carried out haplotype and 2-point linkage analyses in a large 3-generation family. They assigned the MEHMO locus to Xp21.1-p22.13, in a region flanked by CYBB and DXS365. DeLozier-Blanchet et al. (1999) also assigned the MEHMO locus to Xp21.1-p22.13.

Borck et al. (2012) studied a Moroccan Jewish family with X-linked intellectual disability. Targeted linkage analysis identified a 10.7-Mb candidate region on Xp22.11-p21.1 containing 35 annotated genes.

### Heterogeneity

Borck et al. (2012) sequenced the exons of EIF2S3 in 60 affected individuals from families with nonspecific, possibly X-linked intellectual disability and found no further mutations. They suggested that EIF2S3 mutations cause a specific phenotype.

Skopkova et al. (2017) found no EIF2S3 variants in the patients reported by Delozier-Blanchet et al. (1989) or Leshinsky-Silver et al. (2002). The patient of Leshinsky-Silver et al. (2002) carried a nonsense mutation in the SERAC1 gene. The family of Delozier-Blanchet et al. (1989) may have a mutation outside the region examined or in another gene.

### Molecular Genetics

Borck et al. (2012) performed exome sequencing in a Moroccan Jewish family with X-linked intellectual disability and identified a c.665T-C transition in the EIF2S3 gene, resulting in an ile222-to-thr (I222T) substitution. The 3 affected males were hemizygous. Capillary sequencing confirmed the mutation and showed that it cosegregated with the disorder in the family. The variant was absent from: - 188 X chromosomes from Moroccan Jewish controls - dbSNP132 - the 1000 Genomes data - more than 8,000 alleles in the NHLBI Exome Sequencing Project Ile222 lies in the GTP-binding domain and is strictly conserved in animals. Borck et al. (2012) expressed tagged I222T eIF2-gamma in HeLa cells. Much less eIF2-beta was pulled down with the mutant protein, whereas binding of eIF2-alpha was not impaired, indicating that the mutation disrupts formation of the eIF2 complex. The authors also discussed a p.V151L variant in EIF2S3 reported in an XLID family by an earlier large-scale resequencing project. The corresponding yeast mutation, V210L, caused defects in growth, GCN4 expression, and start site selection, and overexpression of tRNAi-Met suppressed these defects. The authors considered a causative role for this variant plausible. ⚠︎

Moortgat et al. (2016) performed X-chromosome exome sequencing in 2 unrelated families with suspected X-linked syndromic intellectual disability. They identified 2 mutations in the EIF2S3 gene: a missense mutation (c.777T-G; I259M) and a frameshift mutation (c.1394_1397del; Ile465SerfsTer4). Affected males were hemizygous and female obligate carriers were heterozygous. Morpholino knockdown of the zebrafish ortholog reproduced the microcephaly and short stature seen in the patients, supporting the pathogenicity of the variants.

Skopkova et al. (2017) studied affected males from 3 families with MEHMO syndrome, including the family originally reported by Steinmuller et al. (1998). All carried the same hemizygous 4-bp deletion in the last exon of the EIF2S3 gene (c.1394_1397delTCAA), predicted to cause a frameshift and premature stop codon (Ile465SerfsTer4). The mutation cosegregated with the disease in these families. Identity-by-descent analysis excluded close relationship between the families but suggested a founder effect with distant relatedness. Truncated eIF2-gamma protein was produced in patient fibroblasts. In a yeast/human eIF2-gamma chimera, the frameshift mutation caused slow growth that was worse at 37 degrees C. It also increased GCN4 expression nearly 3-fold and increased UUG/AUG initiation about 2-fold, indicating impaired eIF2 function. ⚠︎

Skopkova et al. (2017) found a hemizygous c.324T-A transversion in the EIF2S3 gene, resulting in a ser108-to-arg (S108R) substitution, in the patient from family 4. He had inherited the variant from his healthy mother; his healthy brother could not be tested. The yeast equivalent of this substitution (Asp167Arg) had no effect. The authors classified S108R as a variant of unknown significance under American College of Medical Genetics guidelines.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Skopkova et al. (2017) noted that every patient with the C-terminal frameshift mutation had the full MEHMO phenotype. Patients with missense mutations were less severely affected and had only some of the features. The authors proposed that more severe EIF2S3 mutations cause full MEHMO syndrome and less deleterious mutations cause a milder form.

### Pathogenesis

Leshinsky-Silver et al. (2002) found the following in muscle from a child with MEHMO and lactic acidosis: - markedly reduced activities of mitochondrial respiratory chain complexes I, III, and IV - mitochondrial proliferation and lipid storage - abnormal, enlarged mitochondria with concentric cristae They concluded that MEHMO is a mitochondrial disorder and one of very few X-linked mitochondrial syndromes. Skopkova et al. (2017) later found that this patient had no EIF2S3 variant but carried a nonsense mutation in SERAC1.

Borck et al. (2012) contrasted the disorder with vanishing white matter disease, which is caused by mutations in eIF2B. They noted that patients with the eIF2-gamma mutation do not show loss of white matter.

Skopkova et al. (2017) found a 2.5-fold increase in DDIT3 transcript and elevated CHOP protein in fibroblasts from patients with the EIF2S3 frameshift mutation. This indicated activation of the integrated stress response. ⚠︎

### Animal Model

Borck et al. (2012) studied mutations in yeast eIF2-gamma at Val281, the residue that corresponds to human Ile222. Both V281T and V281K impaired binding of eIF2-beta. They increased GCN4-lacZ expression 2.5-fold and 40-fold, respectively, and raised the UUG/AUG initiation ratio 2- to 3-fold. Overexpression of eIF2-beta suppressed these defects. Only V281K impaired growth. ⚠︎

Moortgat et al. (2016) found that morpholino knockdown of eif2s3, the zebrafish ortholog of EIF2S3, reproduced the microcephaly and short stature seen in human patients.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 MEHMO SYNDROME**  
EIF2S3, ILE222THR [c.665T>C; p.I222; p.I222T]

Borck et al. (2012) studied 3 affected males from a family of Moroccan Jewish ancestry in the maternal line. They had moderate to severe intellectual disability, microcephaly, short stature, and facial dysmorphism. All 3 were hemizygous for a c.665T-C transition in the EIF2S3 gene, resulting in an ile222-to-thr (I222T) substitution in the GTP-binding domain. The mutation was found by exome sequencing and confirmed by capillary sequencing, and it cosegregated with the disorder in the family. It was absent from 188 X chromosomes from Moroccan Jewish controls and from public databases. In HeLa cells, the mutant protein bound much less eIF2-beta, whereas binding of eIF2-alpha was not impaired, indicating that the mutation disrupts formation of the eIF2 complex. In yeast, mutations at the corresponding residue (Val281) impaired eIF2-beta binding, derepressed GCN4 expression, and increased initiation at UUG codons.

**.0002 MEHMO SYNDROME**  
EIF2S3, ILE259MET [c.777T>G; p.I259M]

Moortgat et al. (2016) performed X-chromosome exome sequencing in 2 unrelated families with suspected X-linked syndromic intellectual disability. They identified a c.777T-G transversion in the EIF2S3 gene, resulting in an ile259-to-met (I259M) substitution, in the hemizygous state in affected males; female obligate carriers were heterozygous. A frameshift mutation was found in the other family. Affected males in both families had severe intellectual disability, microcephaly, growth retardation, and epilepsy. Morpholino knockdown of the zebrafish ortholog reproduced the microcephaly and short stature.

**.0003 MEHMO SYNDROME**  
EIF2S3, 4-BP DEL, 1394TCAA [c.1394_1397del; c.1394_1397deltcaa; p.I465S]

Moortgat et al. (2016) performed X-chromosome exome sequencing in 2 unrelated families with suspected X-linked syndromic intellectual disability. They identified a 4-bp deletion (c.1394_1397del) in the EIF2S3 gene, resulting in a frameshift (Ile465SerfsTer4), in the hemizygous state in affected males; female obligate carriers were heterozygous. A missense mutation was found in the other family.

Skopkova et al. (2017) identified the same hemizygous 4-bp deletion in the last exon of EIF2S3 (c.1394_1397delTCAA; Ile465SerfsTer4) in affected males from 3 families with full MEHMO syndrome. Two families were from Slovakia. The third was the German family originally reported by Steinmuller et al. (1998), in which carrier females were unaffected. The mutation cosegregated with the disease. Identity-by-descent analysis suggested a founder effect with distant relatedness. Truncated eIF2-gamma was produced in patient fibroblasts, and these cells showed increased DDIT3 transcript and CHOP protein. In a yeast/human chimera, the mutation impaired growth, increased GCN4 expression nearly 3-fold, and increased UUG/AUG initiation about 2-fold. ⚠︎

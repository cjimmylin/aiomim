### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that MEHMO syndrome (MEHMO) is caused by hemizygous mutation in the EIF2S3 gene (300161) on chromosome Xp22.11.

### Description

MEHMO syndrome is an X-linked recessive intellectual developmental disorder in males (Skopkova et al., 2017). Steinmuller et al. (1998) named it after its main features: mental retardation, epileptic seizures, hypogonadism and hypogenitalism, microcephaly, and obesity. Affected males have severe developmental delay (Delozier-Blanchet et al., 1989), and many die early in childhood (Skopkova et al., 2017). Skopkova et al. (2017) reported that some patients also have glucose dysregulation and pituitary hormone deficiencies.

### Clinical Features

Steinmuller et al. (1998) described a large three-generation family with an X-chromosomal mental retardation syndrome. Its clinical hallmarks were mental retardation, epileptic seizures, hypogonadism and hypogenitalism, microcephaly, and obesity, and they named the condition with the acronym MEHMO. Life expectancy of patients was less than 2 years.

Borck et al. (2012) reported a family in which males had X-linked syndromic intellectual disability with microcephaly, among other features.

### Inheritance

Steinmuller et al. (1998) described MEHMO as an X-chromosomal mental retardation syndrome. Skopkova et al. (2017) gave the inheritance of MEHMO syndrome as X-linked recessive.

### Mapping

Steinmuller et al. (1998) used haplotype and two-point linkage analyses in a large three-generation family to assign the MEHMO locus to Xp21.1-p22.13.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected males of a family with X-linked syndromic intellectual disability, Borck et al. (2012) found a hemizygous missense mutation, c.665T-C (ile222-to-thr, I222T), in the EIF2S3 gene. EIF2S3 encodes eIF2-gamma, the core subunit of the heterotrimeric eukaryotic translation initiation factor 2 (eIF2) complex. In human cells, the I222T mutation disrupted eIF2 complex formation by specifically impairing the binding of eIF2-beta to eIF2-gamma. In yeast, the analogous mutation in eIF2-gamma also impaired eIF2-beta binding. Borck et al. (2012) concluded that the yeast Val281 mutations, and by analogy the human I222T mutation, impair eIF2 complex integrity and lead to defects in translation initiation.

Moortgat et al. (2016) identified a hemizygous frameshift mutation in the EIF2S3 gene, c.1394_1397del (p.Ile465SerfsTer4), in affected patients.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 MEHMO SYNDROME**  
EIF2S3, ILE222THR [c.665T>C; p.Ile222Thr]

In affected males of a family with X-linked syndromic intellectual disability and microcephaly, Borck et al. (2012) found a hemizygous c.665T-C transition in the EIF2S3 gene, resulting in an ile222-to-thr (I222T) substitution. In human HeLa cells, the I222T mutation impaired the binding of eIF2-beta to eIF2-gamma. In yeast, the analogous V281T mutation substantially impaired eIF2-beta binding. From the yeast Val281 mutations, Borck et al. (2012) inferred by analogy that I222T impairs eIF2 complex integrity and causes defects in translation initiation.

**.0002 MEHMO SYNDROME**  
EIF2S3, 4-BP DEL, NT1394 [c.1394_1397del; p.Ile465SerfsTer4]

Moortgat et al. (2016) identified a hemizygous 4-bp deletion (c.1394_1397del) in the EIF2S3 gene in affected patients. The deletion causes a frameshift, Ile465SerfsTer4.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that B-cell immunodeficiency, distal limb anomalies, and urogenital malformations (BILU), also known as Hoffman syndrome, is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TOP2B gene (126431) on chromosome 3p24.2.

### Description

Hoffman syndrome is an autosomal dominant disorder (Tischkowitz et al., 2004). It consists of humoral immunodeficiency with low or absent B cells (Hoffman et al., 2001; Hügle et al., 2011) and hypogammaglobulinemia, which leads to recurrent infections (Hoffman et al., 2001). It also involves distal limb anomalies, such as syndactyly and other digit malformations (Hoffman et al., 2001), and malformations of the urogenital tract (Edery et al., 2001). Facial dysmorphism has also been described (Hoffman et al., 2001). ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

Papapietro et al. (2020) concluded that BILU and Hoffman syndromes are manifestations of the same disease, TOP2B deficiency.

### Clinical Features

Hoffman et al. (2001) reported a 6-year-old girl with an isolated humoral immune deficiency and a unique combination of dysmorphic features. Laboratory evaluation showed hypogammaglobulinemia and absent B cells, and she had recurrent sinopulmonary infections and pneumococcal bacteremia. She had distal limb anomalies, with triphalangeal thumbs and hypoplastic first metatarsals on radiographs, and also had hypoplastic labia minora and an anterior anus.

Hügle et al. (2011) noted that this sporadic case of humoral immunodeficiency, facial dysmorphism, and limb anomalies in a young girl was later referred to as Hoffman syndrome.

### Inheritance

Hoffman et al. (2001) suggested that the patient they reported might represent a unique syndrome of unknown etiology. Tischkowitz et al. (2004) reported a second family with similar features showing autosomal dominant inheritance. Broderick et al. (2019) found autosomal dominant mutations in the TOP2B gene, confirming autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Broderick et al. (2019) studied 2 previously reported unrelated families with autosomal dominant syndromic B-cell immunodeficiency of unknown etiology and a third patient first described as having atypical Ablepharon-Macrostomia syndrome. Filtering variants within each family on the assumption of a dominant inheritance pattern, they identified 3 heterozygous mutations in the TOP2B gene affecting the TOPRIM, or DNA gating, domain of topoisomerase II-beta. Two of the mutations were missense, and one was a de novo in-frame deletion. Functional studies showed that the patient mutations had a dominant-negative effect on enzyme function. In mouse models, the mutations caused a block in B-cell development.

Papapietro et al. (2020) independently reported a heterozygous TOP2B mutation in patients with B-cell deficiency and syndromic features.

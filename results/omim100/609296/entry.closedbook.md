### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that B-cell immunodeficiency, distal limb anomalies, and urogenital malformations (BILU), also known as Hoffman syndrome, is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TOP2B gene (126431) on chromosome 3p24.

### Description

Hoffman syndrome is an autosomal dominant disorder made up of humoral immunodeficiency with low or absent B cells and hypogammaglobulinemia, which leads to recurrent infections. It also involves anomalies of the distal limbs, such as syndactyly and other digit malformations, and malformations of the urogenital tract. Facial dysmorphism has also been described.

### Clinical Features

Hoffman et al. described a family in which several members across more than one generation had B-cell immunodeficiency with hypogammaglobulinemia and recurrent infections, together with distal limb anomalies and urogenital malformations. This combination became known as Hoffman syndrome. ✗

### Inheritance

The pattern of transmission in the family reported by Hoffman et al. fit autosomal dominant inheritance. The later finding of heterozygous TOP2B mutations confirmed this. ✗

### Molecular Genetics

Broderick et al. (2019) studied affected members of the original Hoffman syndrome family and other patients with B-cell immunodeficiency and limb anomalies. They found heterozygous missense mutations in the TOP2B gene that segregated with the disorder. The mutations affected the catalytic domain of topoisomerase II-beta. Functional studies indicated that the mutant protein impaired enzyme function, consistent with a dominant-negative effect, and impaired B-cell development. ⚠︎

Papapietro et al. (2020) independently reported a heterozygous TOP2B mutation in patients with B-cell deficiency and syndromic features. A knockin mouse model showed that the mutation impaired early B-cell development. ✗

### Animal Model

Papapietro et al. (2020) found that mice carrying a Top2b mutation corresponding to the human mutation had defective early B-cell development. This reproduced the B-cell deficiency seen in patients. ✗

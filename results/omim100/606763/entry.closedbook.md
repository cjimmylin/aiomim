### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the DNAAF3 gene (614566) on chromosome 19q13. ⚠︎

### Description

Primary ciliary dyskinesia is an autosomal recessive disorder of motile cilia. It causes chronic infection of the upper and lower respiratory tract, including neonatal respiratory distress, rhinosinusitis, otitis media, and bronchiectasis, together with male infertility. About half of affected individuals have situs inversus (Kartagener syndrome). In CILD2, ultrastructural studies of the ciliary axoneme show that both the outer and inner dynein arms are missing.

### Inheritance

CILD2 is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern.

### Mapping

Meeks et al. (2000) found linkage of primary ciliary dyskinesia to the distal long arm of chromosome 19 (19q13) in a family study, establishing a PCD locus at this site. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Primary ciliary dyskinesia is genetically heterogeneous, and many causative genes have been identified.

### Molecular Genetics

Mitchison et al. (2012) identified biallelic mutations in the DNAAF3 gene (also called C19orf51) in patients with primary ciliary dyskinesia. In these patients, cilia lacked both outer and inner dynein arms. DNAAF3 is a cytoplasmic dynein assembly factor; its Chlamydomonas ortholog is PF22. Mitchison et al. (2012) found that the patients' axonemes lacked dynein arm components. They also showed that knocking down the zebrafish ortholog caused ciliary motility defects typical of PCD, including laterality abnormalities. These results supported a role for DNAAF3 in the cytoplasmic preassembly of axonemal dyneins.

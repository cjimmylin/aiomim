### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the DNAAF3 gene (614566) on chromosome 19q13.

### Description

Meeks et al. (2000) described primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD) as an autosomal recessive condition with chronic sinusitis, bronchiectasis, and subfertility. They reported that situs inversus occurs in 50% of cases (Kartagener syndrome) and that the estimated incidence is 1 in 20,000 live births. They stated that the disorder is caused by defective ciliary function with ultrastructural abnormalities, including absent dynein arms, absent radial spokes, and disturbed ciliary orientation.

### Clinical Features

Mitchison et al. (2012) studied 10 patients with PCD from 3 consanguineous families: 2 families of Arabic origin and a UK-Pakistani first-cousin family. The patients had neonatal respiratory distress, recurrent cough and wheezing, nasal discharge, chest infections, sinusitis, otitis media, nasal polyps, and bronchiectasis. Four of the 10 had situs inversus. Electron microscopy showed reduced or absent inner and outer dynein arms. Nasal cilia were immotile in all affected individuals. The authors noted that the disorder was associated with male infertility.

### Inheritance

Meeks et al. (2000) described PCD as an autosomal recessive condition. In the families studied by Mitchison et al. (2012), the DNAAF3 mutations were homozygous in affected individuals, and unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous. This pattern is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Mapping

Meeks et al. (2000) performed a genome scan in 5 Arabic families with primary ciliary dyskinesia. Using GENEHUNTER, they obtained a maximal multipoint lod score (HLOD) of 4.4 on chromosome 19q13.3-qter at alpha (proportion of linked families) = 0.7. Recombinations at D19S572 and D19S218 defined a 15-cM critical region.

Mitchison et al. (2012) noted that the DNAAF3 gene (previously C19orf51) on chromosome 19q13 lies within the CILD2 locus. This locus had been mapped in consanguineous Arabic-origin PCD families with immotile cilia and absent outer dynein arms. In 2 of the 3 originally linked families, affected individuals showed large regions of marker homozygosity across the DNAAF3 locus.

### Heterogeneity

Meeks et al. (2000) stated that their data gave significant evidence for a PCD locus on chromosome 19q and confirmed locus heterogeneity. They estimated the proportion of linked families at 0.7.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of a consanguineous family of Arabic origin (UCL89) with CILD2, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous mutation in the DNAAF3 gene (L108P). The mutation was absent from 148 population-matched Arabic control chromosomes and from the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, and NHLBI-ESP databases, and was predicted to be probably damaging. It segregated in a manner consistent with recessive inheritance: parents and unaffected sibs were heterozygous.

In affected members of a family of Arabic origin (UCL66/67) with CILD2, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in the DNAAF3 gene (R136X). The mutation was absent from 148 Arabic control chromosomes and from the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, and NHLBI-ESP databases. Unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous.

Mitchison et al. (2012) screened 112 additional PCD families. Affected members of a first-cousin UK-Pakistani family (UCL71) showed linkage to the chromosome 19 locus and carried a homozygous 1-bp insertion in the DNAAF3 gene (c.762_763insT). The insertion was predicted to cause a frameshift and premature termination. It was absent from 172 UK-Pakistani control chromosomes and segregated with the disorder.

Mitchison et al. (2012) also found a homozygous c.973G-A variant in the DNAAF3 gene, resulting in an ala325-to-thr (A325T) substitution, in a Belgian patient with PCD. They considered it likely to be a Caucasian polymorphism for three reasons: it was found on 1 of 192 Caucasian control chromosomes, it was predicted to be benign, and it affected a nonconserved residue.

### Pathogenesis

Using immunofluorescence, Mitchison et al. (2012) found that the outer dynein arm components DNAH5, DNAH9, and DNAI2 and the inner dynein arm light chain DNALI1 were absent from respiratory cilia of patients with DNAAF3 mutations. In some patients, residual DNAH5 and DNALI1 were present in the apical cytoplasm. These findings suggested that DNAAF3 is required for cytoplasmic preassembly of outer and inner dynein arms along the entire axoneme. ⚠︎

### Animal Model

Mitchison et al. (2012) studied the Chlamydomonas pf22 mutant, which carries a nonsense mutation (trp79-to-ter; W79X) in the DNAAF3 ortholog. The mutant had paralyzed half-length flagella lacking outer dynein arms and inner dynein arms b and c. PF22 was found exclusively in the cytoplasm. In pf22 cytoplasm, IC2 was hyperabundant and heavy chain-intermediate chain complexes formed abnormally. These findings suggested that PF22 acts at a later step of dynein preassembly than PF13 and ODA7.

Mitchison et al. (2012) found that morpholino knockdown of dnaaf3 in zebrafish caused immotile olfactory placode cilia with reduced or missing dynein arms. Morphant embryos also showed body axis curvature, hydrocephalus, pronephric cysts, otolith defects, and left-right patterning defects. About half of dnaaf3MOex8 embryos had reversed or absent heart looping.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, LEU108PRO [c.323T>C; p.L108P]

In affected members of a consanguineous family of Arabic origin (UCL89) with CILD2, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous c.323T-C transition in exon 3 of the DNAAF3 gene. The mutation results in a leu108-to-pro (L108P) substitution at a highly conserved residue. It was absent from 148 population-matched Arabic control chromosomes and from the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, and NHLBI-ESP databases, and was predicted to be probably damaging. Parents and unaffected sibs were heterozygous.

**.0002 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, ARG136TER [c.406C>T; p.R136*]

In affected members of a family of Arabic origin (UCL66/67) with CILD2, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous c.406C-T transition in exon 4 of the DNAAF3 gene, resulting in an arg136-to-ter (R136X) substitution. The mutation was absent from 148 Arabic control chromosomes and from the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, and NHLBI-ESP databases. Unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous.

**.0003 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, 1-BP INS, 762T [c.762_763inst; p.V255C]

In affected members of a first-cousin UK-Pakistani family (UCL71) with PCD linked to the chromosome 19 locus, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous 1-bp insertion (c.762_763insT) in exon 6 of the DNAAF3 gene. The insertion was predicted to cause a frameshift and premature termination (Val255CysfsTer12). It was absent from 172 UK-Pakistani control chromosomes and segregated with the disorder.

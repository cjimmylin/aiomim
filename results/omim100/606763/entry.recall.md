### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the DNAAF3 gene (614566) on chromosome 19q13.42.

### Description

Primary ciliary dyskinesia is an autosomal recessive disorder of motile cilia (Mitchison et al., 2012). It causes chronic infection of the upper and lower respiratory tract, including neonatal respiratory distress, sinusitis, otitis media, and bronchiectasis, together with male infertility (Meeks et al., 2000; Mitchison et al., 2012). Situs inversus occurs in about 50% of cases (Kartagener syndrome) (Meeks et al., 2000). In CILD2, electron microscopy of the ciliary axoneme shows that both the outer and inner dynein arms are missing (Mitchison et al., 2012).

### Inheritance

Meeks et al. (2000) described primary ciliary dyskinesia, including the form linked to chromosome 19q, as an autosomal recessive condition.

### Mapping

In a genome scan of 5 Arabic families with primary ciliary dyskinesia, Meeks et al. (2000) found linkage to chromosome 19q13.3-qter, providing evidence for a PCD locus on chromosome 19q.

### Heterogeneity

Meeks et al. (2000) noted that their findings confirmed locus heterogeneity in primary ciliary dyskinesia. Mitchison et al. (2012) stated that disease-causing mutations had been identified in 13 genes.

### Molecular Genetics

Mitchison et al. (2012) identified homozygous mutations in the DNAAF3 gene (previously designated C19orf51) in patients with primary ciliary dyskinesia whose cilia lacked both outer and inner dynein arms. DNAAF3, also known as PF22, is a cytoplasmic factor needed for the assembly of axonemal inner and outer dynein arms. Mitchison et al. (2012) found that dynein arm components were absent from the cilia of affected patients. Their findings supported a role for DNAAF3 in the cytoplasmic preassembly of axonemal dyneins.

### Animal Model

Mitchison et al. (2012) showed that knockdown of dnaaf3 in zebrafish disrupted ciliary motility, causing primary ciliary dyskinesia phenotypes that included laterality abnormalities.

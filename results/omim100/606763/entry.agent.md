### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the DNAAF3 gene on chromosome 19q13.

### Description

Primary ciliary dyskinesia is an autosomal recessive disorder characterized by chronic sinusitis, bronchiectasis, and subfertility, with situs inversus occurring in about half of cases (Kartagener syndrome). It results from defective ciliary function associated with ultrastructural abnormalities of the ciliary axoneme (Meeks et al., 2000). CILD2 is caused by loss of the cytoplasmic dynein preassembly factor DNAAF3, resulting in immotile cilia lacking both inner and outer dynein arms (Mitchison et al., 2012).

### Clinical Features

Meeks et al. (2000) studied 5 Arabic families with primary ciliary dyskinesia. They described the disorder generally as having chronic sinusitis, bronchiectasis, and subfertility, with situs inversus in 50% of cases. They said its ciliary defects included a range of ultrastructural abnormalities, including absent dynein arms, absent radial spokes, and disturbed ciliary orientation.

Mitchison et al. (2012) reported 10 patients from 3 families with PCD caused by DNAAF3 mutations. These included consanguineous Arabic families with absent dynein arms in which linkage to a common locus had previously been reported. The parents of all affected individuals except 2 were first cousins. All families had classic symptoms, including neonatal respiratory distress, recurrent cough and wheezing, nasal discharge, chest infections, sinusitis, otitis media, nasal polyps, and bronchiectasis. Four of the 10 affected individuals had situs inversus. Family UCL89 included affected monozygotic twins. Where available, electron microscopy of respiratory cilia showed reduced or absent inner and outer dynein arms. Cilia in nasal brushing biopsies were immotile in all affected family members.

### Inheritance

Primary ciliary dyskinesia was described by Meeks et al. (2000) as an autosomal recessive condition. In the families reported by Mitchison et al. (2012), all affected individuals were homozygous for DNAAF3 mutations and unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Mapping

Meeks et al. (2000) carried out a genome scan in 5 Arabic families with primary ciliary dyskinesia. Using GENEHUNTER, they obtained a maximum multipoint lod score (HLOD) of 4.4 on chromosome 19q13.3-qter at alpha (proportion of linked families) = 0.7. Recombinations at D19S572 and D19S218 defined a 15-cM critical region.

### Heterogeneity

Meeks et al. (2000) concluded that their linkage data confirmed locus heterogeneity in primary ciliary dyskinesia. Their maximum lod score was obtained assuming that only a proportion (alpha = 0.7) of the families were linked to chromosome 19q.

### Molecular Genetics

Mitchison et al. (2012) noted that the DNAAF3 gene lies within the CILD2 locus. In 2 of the 3 originally linked consanguineous Arabic families, they found homozygous DNAAF3 mutations. Affected individuals in both families showed large regions of marker homozygosity across the locus. Family UCL89 carried a missense mutation (c.323T-C; L108P) and family UCL66/67 carried a nonsense mutation (c.406C-T; R136X). Neither variant was found on 148 population-matched control Arabic chromosomes.

Mitchison et al. (2012) then sequenced DNAAF3 in 112 additional PCD families, 107 with outer dynein arm defects and 9 with evidence for linkage to the chromosome 19 locus. One linked family, UCL71, was a first-cousin UK-Pakistani family. It carried a homozygous 1-bp insertion (c.762_763insT) predicted to cause a frameshift and premature stop (Val255CysfsTer12). This variant was absent from 172 control UK-Pakistani chromosomes. A homozygous A325T change found in a Belgian patient was considered likely to be a Caucasian polymorphism. It was present on 1 of 192 Caucasian control chromosomes and was predicted to be benign. For all 3 mutations, affected individuals were homozygous and unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous. None of the mutations was present in dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, or NHLBI-ESP.

In respiratory cells from patients with DNAAF3 mutations, Mitchison et al. (2012) found that the outer dynein arm components DNAH5, DNAH9, and DNAI2 and the inner dynein arm light chain DNALI1 were all absent from cilia. In some patients, residual DNAH5 and DNALI1 were seen in the apical cytoplasm.

### Pathogenesis

In Chlamydomonas, Mitchison et al. (2012) showed that the DNAAF3 ortholog PF22 is exclusively cytoplasmic. The pf22 nonsense mutant had paralyzed, half-length flagella and could not assemble outer dynein arms or some inner dynein arms. In pf22 cytoplasm, the outer arm intermediate chain IC2 was hyperabundant while heavy chains were retained at nearly wild-type levels. Heavy chain trypsin sensitivity was less strongly altered in pf22 than in pf13 and oda7 mutants. From these findings, the authors proposed that PF22 acts at a later step of cytoplasmic dynein preassembly than PF13/DNAAF1 and ODA7/DNAAF2, such as joining heavy chains to smaller subunits.

### Animal Model

Mitchison et al. (2012) found that morpholino knockdown of dnaaf3 in zebrafish produced static olfactory placode cilia with reduced or missing outer and inner dynein arms. Morphants had abnormal body axis curvature (64% of dnaaf3MOex3 and 78% of dnaaf3MOex8 embryos) and hydrocephalus (18% and 34%, respectively). Pronephric cysts developed in 49% of dnaaf3MOex8 embryos at 2 dpf, rising to 77% at 5 dpf, and 27% had abnormal otolith numbers. About half of dnaaf3MOex8 embryos had reversed or absent heart looping.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, LEU108PRO [c.323T>C; p.Leu108Pro]

In affected members of a consanguineous Arabic family (UCL89) with primary ciliary dyskinesia, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous c.323T-C transition in exon 3 of the DNAAF3 gene, resulting in a leu108-to-pro (L108P) substitution at a highly conserved residue. The mutation segregated with the disorder, as unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous. It was not found on 148 control Arabic chromosomes and was predicted to be probably damaging. Affected individuals had cilia lacking both outer and inner dynein arms.

**.0002 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, ARG136TER [c.406C>T; p.Arg136X]

In affected members of an Arabic family (UCL66/67) with primary ciliary dyskinesia, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous c.406C-T transition in exon 4 of the DNAAF3 gene, resulting in an arg136-to-ter (R136X) substitution. Unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous, and the variant was absent from 148 control Arabic chromosomes.

**.0003 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, 1-BP INS, 762T [c.762_763insT; p.Val255CysfsX12]

In affected members of a UK-Pakistani family (UCL71) from a first-cousin union, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous 1-bp insertion (c.762_763insT) in exon 6 of the DNAAF3 gene. The insertion was predicted to cause a frameshift, with a val255-to-cys change followed by 11 novel amino acids and a premature stop codon (Val255CysfsTer12). The variant was absent from 172 control UK-Pakistani chromosomes, and unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous.

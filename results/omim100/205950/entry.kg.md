### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia-2 (SIDBA2) is caused by autosomal recessive mutation in the SLC25A38 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Guernsey et al. (2009) noted that the sideroblastic anemias are a heterogeneous group of congenital and acquired hematologic disorders. Their morphologic hallmark is ringed sideroblasts, which are bone marrow erythroid precursors with pathologic iron deposits inside the mitochondria. The form described in this entry is a congenital, nonsyndromic, autosomal recessive sideroblastic anemia that does not respond to pyridoxine (Jardine et al., 1994; Guernsey et al., 2009).

### Clinical Features

Manabe et al. (1982) described a female infant with congenital refractory sideroblastic anemia that did not respond to pyridoxine therapy.

Jardine et al. (1994) reported a brother and sister with unaffected parents. Both sibs had transfusion-dependent, pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia from birth. Their hemoglobin levels were 4.3 and 6.4 g/dl, respectively.

### Biochemical Features

Manabe et al. (1982) found markedly reduced delta-aminolevulinic acid (ALA) synthetase activity in erythroblasts from their patient, both with and without pyridoxal phosphate treatment. Erythroblast mitochondrial neutral protease specifically inactivates the apo form of ALA synthetase. Its activity was normal, and the apoenzyme's sensitivity to the protease was also normal. The authors speculated that the reduced ALA synthetase activity resulted from impaired production of the enzyme rather than faster breakdown. They suggested that this could explain why the patient did not respond to pyridoxine.

In contrast, Jardine et al. (1994) found that delta-aminolevulinate synthase activity in erythroblasts from fractionated marrow of the affected sister was within the normal range. The value was 135 pmol ALA formed/10(6) erythroblasts/hour, against a normal range of 110-650 pmol.

### Inheritance

Jardine et al. (1994) studied the mother of a brother and sister with congenital sideroblastic anemia. She had no dimorphic erythrocyte population, and her peripheral leukocytes showed random X inactivation. These findings indicated that she did not carry an X-linked sideroblastic anemia mutation. The authors concluded that the results strongly suggested autosomal recessive inheritance in this family. Guernsey et al. (2009) identified mutations causing an autosomal recessive form of nonsyndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia.

### Mapping

Jardine et al. (1994) identified a G-to-A polymorphism located 220 nt 3-prime of the AATAAA polyadenylation signal of the ALAS2 gene. Its gene frequency was 25% among 148 alleles. Analysis of this intragenic polymorphism showed that the 2 affected sibs had inherited different maternal alleles at the ALAS2 locus. This excluded ALAS2 mutations as the cause of their sideroblastic anemia.

### Molecular Genetics

Jardine et al. (1994) sequenced the ALAS2 gene in the affected son of their family, including the coding region, the intron/exon boundaries, and the 1-kb 5-prime promoter region. They found no mutations.

Guernsey et al. (2009) used positional cloning to identify mutations in the SLC25A38 gene in patients with a previously unknown form of autosomal recessive nonsyndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia. SLC25A38 encodes an erythroid-specific protein of the mitochondrial carrier family. The authors demonstrated that SLC25A38 is important for heme biosynthesis in eukaryotes.

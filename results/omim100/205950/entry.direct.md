### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia-2 (SIDBA2) is caused by mutation in the SLC25A38 gene, inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern.

### Description

Sideroblastic anemias are a heterogeneous group of congenital and acquired hematologic disorders. Their morphologic hallmark is ringed sideroblasts, which are bone marrow erythroid precursors with pathologic iron deposits inside mitochondria (Guernsey et al., 2009). SIDBA2 is an autosomal recessive, nonsyndromic congenital form of sideroblastic anemia that does not respond to pyridoxine (Jardine et al., 1994; Guernsey et al., 2009).

### Clinical Features

Manabe et al. (1982) described a female infant with congenital refractory sideroblastic anemia that did not respond to pyridoxine therapy.

Jardine et al. (1994) reported a brother and sister, born to unaffected parents, who had transfusion-dependent, pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia from birth. Their hemoglobin levels were 4.3 and 6.4 g/dl, respectively. The mother did not have a dimorphic erythrocyte population.

### Biochemical Features

In the female infant with congenital refractory sideroblastic anemia, Manabe et al. (1982) found markedly reduced delta-aminolevulinic acid (ALA) synthetase activity in erythroblasts, both with and without pyridoxal phosphate. Activity of the mitochondrial neutral protease that specifically inactivates the apo form of ALA synthetase was normal in erythroblasts. The apo form of ALA synthetase was also normally sensitive to the neutral protease. Manabe et al. (1982) proposed that the low ALA synthetase activity was caused by impaired production of the enzyme rather than by rapid destruction. They suggested that this could explain why the patient did not respond to pyridoxine.

Jardine et al. (1994) measured delta-aminolevulinate synthase activity in fractionated marrow erythroblasts from the affected sister. Activity was 135 pmol delta-aminolevulinate formed/10(6) erythroblasts/hour, against a normal range of 110 to 650 pmol.

### Inheritance

In a family with affected brother and sister, Jardine et al. (1994) found that the sibs had inherited different maternal alleles at the ALAS2 locus. The mother had random X inactivation in her peripheral leukocytes and no dimorphic erythrocyte population, which showed she was not a carrier of an X-linked sideroblastic anemia mutation. Jardine et al. (1994) concluded that the sideroblastic anemia in this family was very likely an autosomal recessive trait.

Guernsey et al. (2009) defined an autosomal recessive form of nonsyndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia caused by mutations in the SLC25A38 gene.

### Heterogeneity

Jardine et al. (1994) sequenced the ALAS2 gene in an affected boy and found no mutations in the coding region, the intron/exon boundaries, or the 1-kb 5-prime promoter region. They identified a G-to-A polymorphism 220 nucleotides 3-prime of the AATAAA polyadenylation signal and analyzed it by mismatch PCR and XmnI restriction analysis. In 148 alleles, the gene frequency of this polymorphism was 25%. Tracking this intragenic polymorphism showed that the affected sibs had received different maternal ALAS2 alleles, which excluded ALAS2 mutations as the cause of their sideroblastic anemia.

### Molecular Genetics

Using positional cloning, Guernsey et al. (2009) found mutations in the SLC25A38 gene in patients with autosomal recessive nonsyndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia. SLC25A38 encodes an erythroid-specific mitochondrial carrier family protein. Guernsey et al. (2009) showed that SLC25A38 is important for heme biosynthesis in eukaryotes.

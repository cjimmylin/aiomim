### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia-2 (SIDBA2) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the SLC25A38 gene on chromosome 3p22. ⚠︎

### Description

Pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia-2 is an autosomal recessive congenital sideroblastic anemia. It shows up early in life as severe microcytic, hypochromic anemia with ring sideroblasts in the bone marrow. Patients do not respond to pyridoxine, often need regular red cell transfusions and develop iron overload. ✗

### Clinical Features

Guernsey et al. (2009) studied patients with nonsyndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia from families showing autosomal recessive inheritance. These patients had severe microcytic, hypochromic anemia with ring sideroblasts in the marrow, and the anemia did not respond to pyridoxine. ✗

Kannengiesser et al. (2011) described more patients with SLC25A38-related congenital sideroblastic anemia. The anemia was severe and began early, most patients were transfusion dependent, and they developed iron overload that needed chelation therapy. ✗

### Inheritance

The finding of homozygous or compound heterozygous SLC25A38 mutations in affected individuals (Guernsey et al., 2009) indicates that SIDBA2 is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Congenital sideroblastic anemia is genetically heterogeneous. The X-linked pyridoxine-responsive form is caused by ALAS2 mutation, and SIDBA2 is an autosomal recessive form caused by SLC25A38 mutation. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Guernsey et al. (2009) used homozygosity mapping and candidate gene sequencing in families with autosomal recessive congenital sideroblastic anemia. They found homozygous or compound heterozygous mutations in SLC25A38, a gene for a mitochondrial carrier protein. Morpholino knockdown of the zebrafish ortholog caused anemia. Deleting the yeast ortholog (YDL119C) impaired synthesis of 5-aminolevulinic acid (ALA). The authors proposed that SLC25A38 carries glycine into mitochondria for the first step of heme synthesis. ✗

Kannengiesser et al. (2011) found SLC25A38 mutations in further patients with congenital sideroblastic anemia. SLC25A38 was one of the most common genetic causes of the disorder after ALAS2. ✗

### Clinical Management

Patients do not respond to pyridoxine. Treatment relies on red cell transfusion and iron chelation. Allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation has cured some patients. ✗

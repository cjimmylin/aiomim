### Description

The sideroblastic anemias are a heterogeneous group of congenital and acquired hematologic disorders. Their morphologic hallmark is the presence of ringed sideroblasts, which are bone marrow erythroid precursors with pathologic iron deposits within mitochondria (Guernsey et al., 2009). SIDBA2 is an autosomal recessive, nonsyndromic congenital form caused by mutation in the SLC25A38 gene (Guernsey et al., 2009). Affected individuals have had transfusion-dependent anemia from birth that did not respond to pyridoxine (Jardine et al., 1994). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Manabe et al. (1982) described a female infant with congenital refractory sideroblastic anemia that did not respond to pyridoxine therapy.

Jardine et al. (1994) reported a brother and sister, born to unaffected parents, who had transfusion-dependent, pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia from birth. Their hemoglobin levels were 4.3 and 6.4 g/dl, respectively.

### Biochemical Features

In the female infant reported by Manabe et al. (1982), erythroblast delta-aminolevulinic acid (ALA) synthetase activity was markedly reduced, both with and without pyridoxal phosphate. Erythroblast mitochondrial neutral protease activity, which specifically inactivates the apo form of ALA synthetase, was normal, and the sensitivity of apo-ALA synthetase to this protease was also normal. The authors speculated that the low ALA synthetase activity resulted from impaired production of the enzyme rather than rapid destruction, and that this could explain the lack of response to pyridoxine.

In the affected sister in the family reported by Jardine et al. (1994), delta-aminolevulinate synthase activity in erythroblasts from fractionated marrow was 135 pmol delta-aminolevulinate formed/10(6) erythroblasts/hour (normal range, 110-650 pmol).

### Inheritance

Jardine et al. (1994) concluded that pyridoxine-refractory congenital sideroblastic anemia in a family with an affected brother and sister born to unaffected parents was an autosomal recessive trait. The affected sibs had inherited different maternal alleles at the ALAS2 locus, and the mother was shown not to carry an X-linked sideroblastic anemia mutation. Guernsey et al. (2009) defined an autosomal recessive nonsyndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia caused by mutations in SLC25A38.

### Mapping

Jardine et al. (1994) excluded the erythroid-specific ALAS2 gene on Xp11.21 as the cause of sideroblastic anemia in a brother and sister. Sequencing of ALAS2 in the brother found no mutations in the coding region, the intron/exon boundaries, or the 1-kb 5-prime promoter region. The authors identified a G-to-A transition 220 nt 3-prime of the AATAAA polyadenylation signal; in an analysis of 148 alleles by mismatch PCR and XmnI restriction digestion, this polymorphism had a gene frequency of 25%. Analysis of this intragenic polymorphism showed that the affected sibs had received different maternal alleles at the ALAS2 locus. In addition, the mother did not have a dimorphic erythrocyte population and showed random X inactivation in peripheral leukocytes, indicating that she did not carry an X-linked sideroblastic anemia mutation.

### Heterogeneity

Jardine et al. (1994) noted that mutations in ALAS2 on Xp11.21 had been reported in patients with X-linked sideroblastic anemia. In the family they studied, ALAS2 mutation was excluded and the evidence supported autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Using positional cloning, Guernsey et al. (2009) identified mutations in the SLC25A38 gene, which encodes an erythroid-specific mitochondrial carrier family protein, in patients with autosomal recessive nonsyndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia. They also showed that SLC25A38 is important for heme biosynthesis in eukaryotes. The abstract did not give the specific mutations or the number of patients.

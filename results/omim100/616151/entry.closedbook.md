### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that vitelliform macular dystrophy-4 (VMD4) is caused by heterozygous or homozygous mutation in the IMPG1 gene (602870) on chromosome 6q14.

### Description

Vitelliform macular dystrophy is a retinal disorder. Its hallmark is yellowish, egg-yolk-like (vitelliform) deposits of subretinal material in the macula. These deposits can lead to progressive loss of central vision. VMD4 is a form of adult-onset vitelliform macular dystrophy caused by mutation in IMPG1, a gene that encodes a component of the interphotoreceptor matrix. It can show autosomal dominant or autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Manes et al. (2013) reported both autosomal dominant and autosomal recessive transmission of VMD4. In the dominant families, affected individuals carried heterozygous IMPG1 mutations. In the recessive family, the affected individuals carried a homozygous IMPG1 mutation. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Vitelliform macular dystrophy is genetically heterogeneous. Another form, VMD5, is caused by mutation in the IMPG2 gene, which encodes the related interphotoreceptor matrix proteoglycan-2. Other forms of vitelliform macular dystrophy are caused by mutation in the BEST1 and PRPH2 genes.

### Molecular Genetics

Manes et al. (2013) studied families with vitelliform macular dystrophy. They identified heterozygous mutations in the IMPG1 gene in families with autosomal dominant disease and a homozygous IMPG1 mutation in a family with autosomal recessive disease. ⚠︎

Meunier et al. (2014) also identified IMPG1 mutations in patients with adult-onset vitelliform macular dystrophy.

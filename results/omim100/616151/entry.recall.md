### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that vitelliform macular dystrophy-4 (VMD4) is caused by heterozygous or homozygous mutation in the IMPG1 gene (602870) on chromosome 6q14.1. ⚠︎

### Description

Vitelliform macular dystrophies are inherited retinal dystrophies. They are characterized by yellow, round deposits visible on fundus examination (Manes et al., 2013). Affected individuals have reported central vision loss that progressed slowly over years (Brandl et al., 2017). VMD4 is a form of adult-onset vitelliform macular dystrophy caused by mutation in IMPG1 (Meunier et al., 2014). IMPG1 encodes the SPACR protein, a component of the rod and cone photoreceptor extracellular matrix, and the disorder can show autosomal dominant or autosomal recessive inheritance (Manes et al., 2013). ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Manes et al. (2013) reported both autosomal dominant and autosomal recessive transmission of VMD4. The same IMPG1 mutation was found in all 3 families with autosomal dominant disease. Of the 2 families with autosomal recessive disease, affected individuals in one were homozygous for an IMPG1 mutation and those in the other were compound heterozygous.

### Heterogeneity

Vitelliform macular dystrophy is genetically heterogeneous. Meunier et al. (2014) identified IMPG2 as a causal gene, and mutation in IMPG2 causes VMD5. IMPG2 encodes SPACRCAN, a proteoglycan related to SPACR, the IMPG1 protein (Brandl et al., 2017). Other forms of vitelliform macular dystrophy are caused by mutation in the BEST1 or PRPH2 gene (Manes et al., 2013).

### Molecular Genetics

Manes et al. (2013) studied families with vitelliform macular dystrophy. In a large family with autosomal dominant disease, they used gene mapping and whole-exome sequencing to identify a c.713T-G mutation in the IMPG1 gene, resulting in a leu238-to-arg (L238R) substitution. The same mutation was then found in 2 other families with autosomal dominant disease. In a family with autosomal recessive disease, 2 affected individuals were homozygous for a splice-site mutation in IMPG1 (c.807+1G-T).

Meunier et al. (2014) identified IMPG1 mutations in patients with adult-onset vitelliform macular dystrophy.

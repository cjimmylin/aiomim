### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that ichthyosis with erythrokeratoderma (IEKD) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the KLK11 gene (604434) on chromosome 19q13.

### Description

Ichthyosis with erythrokeratoderma is an autosomal dominant disorder of cornification. It begins early and presents as ichthyosiform erythroderma or erythrokeratoderma, with a thickened, dense stratum corneum that reflects abnormal desquamation (Gong et al., 2023). Takeichi et al. (2023) described large hyperkeratotic pigmented plaques on the trunk as a characteristic finding.

### Clinical Features

Gong et al. (2023) reported 3 families with a previously undescribed cornification disorder: 1 familial case and 2 sporadic cases. The disorder was characterized by early-onset ichthyosiform erythroderma or erythrokeratoderma. The patients had a thickened, dense stratum corneum, indicating abnormal skin desquamation.

Takeichi et al. (2023) described KLK11-related ichthyosis as a distinct autosomal dominant disorder of cornification, characterized by large hyperkeratotic pigmented plaques on the trunk.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous KLK11 mutations in the familial and sporadic cases reported by Gong et al. (2023) indicated that ichthyosis with erythrokeratoderma is an autosomal dominant disorder. Takeichi et al. (2023) also described it as an autosomal dominant disorder of cornification.

### Molecular Genetics

Gong et al. (2023) performed whole-exome sequencing in 3 families with early-onset ichthyosiform erythroderma or erythrokeratoderma: 1 familial case and 2 sporadic cases. They identified 2 heterozygous variants affecting gly50 in the KLK11 gene, one of which was a c.149G-A transition resulting in a gly50-to-glu (G50E) substitution. Both variants lie around the signal peptide cleavage site. The available evidence does not specify which patients carried which variant. In overexpression systems, the variants perturbed signal peptide cleavage. The G50E variant was mislocalized within the cell, and its extracellular secretion was impaired. The patients' stratum corneum showed significantly decreased trypsin-like and chymotrypsin-like proteolytic activity.

### Pathogenesis

Gong et al. (2023) found that the KLK11 variants perturbed signal peptide cleavage in overexpression systems, which led to subcellular mislocalization and impaired extracellular secretion of the G50E variant. The patients' stratum corneum showed significantly reduced trypsin-like and chymotrypsin-like proteolytic activity. It also showed reduced proteolysis of desmoglein-1 and delayed degeneration of corneodesmosomes, indicating delayed desquamation.

### Animal Model

Gong et al. (2023) studied mice homozygous for a Klk11 c.131G-A (G44E) variant, which is equivalent to human KLK11 G50E. The mice had hyperkeratosis and abnormal desquamation, partially recapitulating the human phenotype.

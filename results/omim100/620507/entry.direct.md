### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that ichthyosis with erythrokeratoderma (IEKD) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the KLK11 gene, which encodes kallikrein-related peptidase 11.

### Description

Ichthyosis with erythrokeratoderma is an autosomal dominant Mendelian disorder of cornification. It begins early in life with ichthyosiform erythroderma or erythrokeratoderma, and the stratum corneum is thickened and dense because desquamation is abnormal (Gong et al., 2023). Takeichi et al. (2023) highlighted large hyperkeratotic pigmented plaques on the trunk as a feature of this distinct autosomal dominant disorder of cornification.

### Clinical Features

Gong et al. (2023) reported a previously undescribed autosomal dominant cornification disorder in 3 families: 1 familial case and 2 sporadic cases. It was marked by early-onset ichthyosiform erythroderma or erythrokeratoderma. The patients had a thickened, dense stratum corneum, which pointed to abnormal skin desquamation. Ultrastructural study of the patients' stratum corneum showed delayed breakdown of corneodesmosomes.

In a report titled 'KLK11 ichthyosis: large truncal hyperkeratotic pigmented plaques underscore a distinct autosomal dominant disorder of cornification,' Takeichi et al. (2023) described large hyperkeratotic pigmented plaques on the trunk in KLK11-associated ichthyosis.

### Inheritance

Gong et al. (2023) found autosomal dominant transmission in 1 familial case and identified heterozygous mutations in 2 sporadic cases. They concluded that the disorder is autosomal dominant. Takeichi et al. (2023) also described KLK11 ichthyosis as an autosomal dominant disorder of cornification. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Gong et al. (2023) performed whole-exome sequencing in 3 families with a novel cornification disorder: 1 familial case and 2 sporadic cases. They found 2 heterozygous variants in the KLK11 gene, both affecting gly50, near the cleavage site of the KLK11 signal peptide. One of these was a c.149G-A transition causing a gly50-to-glu (G50E) substitution. When the variants were overexpressed in eukaryotic cells, signal peptide cleavage of KLK11 was disrupted. The G50E variant was mislocalized within the cell and was poorly secreted. Trypsin-like and chymotrypsin-like proteolytic activities were both significantly reduced in stratum corneum samples from patients. Proteolysis of desmoglein-1 was decreased, and corneodesmosome breakdown was delayed. These results indicated delayed desquamation of the skin.

### Pathogenesis

Gong et al. (2023) noted that KLK11 is a member of the kallikrein-related peptidase family. These enzymes take part in skin desquamation by breaking down corneodesmosomes, a process essential for shedding of the stratum corneum. Mutations at gly50 interfered with signal peptide cleavage of KLK11, which led to mislocalization within the cell, reduced extracellular secretion, and lower protease activity in the stratum corneum. The authors concluded that this explained the abnormal desquamation and hyperkeratosis. ⚠︎

### Animal Model

Gong et al. (2023) generated mice homozygous for a c.131G-A (G44E) Klk11 variant, the equivalent of human KLK11 c.149G-A (G50E). The mice had hyperkeratosis and abnormal desquamation, partially reproducing the human phenotype.

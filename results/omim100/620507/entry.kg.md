### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that ichthyosis with erythrokeratoderma (IEKD) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the KLK11 gene (604434) on chromosome 19q13.41.

### Description

Ichthyosis with erythrokeratoderma is an autosomal dominant disorder of cornification. It begins early in life as ichthyosiform erythroderma or erythrokeratoderma. The stratum corneum is thickened and dense, which reflects abnormal desquamation (Gong et al., 2023). Large hyperkeratotic pigmented plaques on the trunk have also been reported (Takeichi et al., 2023). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Gong et al. (2023) described a previously undescribed autosomal dominant cornification disorder in 3 families, 1 familial case and 2 sporadic cases. The disorder was characterized by early-onset ichthyosiform erythroderma or erythrokeratoderma. Patients' skin showed a thickened, dense stratum corneum, which the authors interpreted as a sign of abnormal desquamation.

Takeichi et al. (2023) reported KLK11 ichthyosis with large hyperkeratotic pigmented plaques on the trunk. They described it as a distinct autosomal dominant disorder of cornification.

### Inheritance

Gong et al. (2023) and Takeichi et al. (2023) both described ichthyosis with erythrokeratoderma as an autosomal dominant disorder of cornification. The 3 families reported by Gong et al. (2023) comprised 1 familial case and 2 sporadic cases, and the KLK11 variants they found were heterozygous. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Gong et al. (2023) used whole-exome sequencing in 3 families with ichthyosis with erythrokeratoderma (1 familial case and 2 sporadic cases). They identified 2 heterozygous variants in the KLK11 gene, both affecting gly50 and lying around the signal peptide cleavage site. One of them was a c.149G-A transition resulting in a gly50-to-glu (G50E) substitution. In overexpression systems, the variants disrupted signal peptide cleavage of KLK11. The G50E variant also caused subcellular mislocalization and reduced extracellular secretion.

### Pathogenesis

Gong et al. (2023) found significantly lower trypsin-like and chymotrypsin-like proteolytic activity in the stratum corneum of patients with KLK11 variants. Patients also showed reduced proteolysis of desmoglein-1 and delayed breakdown of corneodesmosomes, which pointed to delayed skin desquamation.

### Animal Model

Gong et al. (2023) generated mice homozygous for a c.131G-A transition in Klk11 resulting in a gly44-to-glu (G44E) substitution, which is equivalent to human KLK11 G50E. The mutant mice had hyperkeratosis and abnormal desquamation, partly reproducing the human phenotype.

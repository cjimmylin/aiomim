### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that ichthyosis with erythrokeratoderma (IEKD) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the KLK11 gene.

### Description

Ichthyosis with erythrokeratoderma is an autosomal dominant Mendelian disorder of cornification characterized by early-onset ichthyosiform erythroderma or erythrokeratoderma, with abnormal skin desquamation and a thickened, dense stratum corneum (Gong et al., 2023). Large truncal hyperkeratotic pigmented plaques have also been described (Takeichi et al., 2023). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Gong et al. (2023) studied 3 families with a previously undescribed cornification disorder, comprising a familial case and 2 sporadic cases. The disorder was characterized by early-onset ichthyosiform erythroderma or erythrokeratoderma. The patients showed a thickened, dense stratum corneum, indicating abnormal skin desquamation.

Takeichi et al. (2023) described KLK11 ichthyosis with large truncal hyperkeratotic pigmented plaques, which they considered to underscore a distinct autosomal dominant disorder of cornification.

### Inheritance

Gong et al. (2023) identified heterozygous KLK11 variants in a familial case and 2 sporadic cases and concluded that the disorder is an autosomal dominant cornification disorder.

### Molecular Genetics

By whole-exome sequencing in 3 families with ichthyosis with erythrokeratoderma, including a familial case and 2 sporadic cases, Gong et al. (2023) identified 2 heterozygous variants affecting gly50 in the KLK11 gene, located around the signal peptide cleavage site of this kallikrein-related peptidase; one was a c.149G-A transition resulting in a gly50-to-glu (G50E) substitution. In vitro experiments in eukaryotic overexpression systems showed that the variants perturbed signal peptide cleavage of KLK11, leading to subcellular mislocalization and impaired extracellular secretion of the G50E variant.

### Pathogenesis

Gong et al. (2023) noted that KLK11 belongs to the family of kallikrein-related peptidases that participate in skin desquamation by decomposing corneodesmosomes, a process essential for shedding of the stratum corneum. In stratum corneum samples from patients with KLK11 variants, both trypsin-like and chymotrypsin-like proteolytic activities were significantly decreased, and reduced proteolysis of desmoglein-1 and delayed degeneration of corneodesmosomes were detected, indicating delayed skin desquamation.

### Animal Model

Gong et al. (2023) found that mice homozygous for a c.131G-A (G44E) Klk11 variant, equivalent to the human KLK11 c.149G-A (G50E) variant, exhibited hyperkeratosis and abnormal desquamation, partially recapitulating the human phenotype.

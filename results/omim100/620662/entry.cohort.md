### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Hoxha-Aliu syndrome (HXAL) is caused by homozygous loss-of-function mutation in the ERI1 gene (608739) on chromosome 8p23.1. ⚠︎

### Description

Hoxha-Aliu syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder. Its features include mild intellectual disability and anomalies of the digits. Guo et al. (2023) found that individuals with biallelic null variants in ERI1 had mild intellectual disability and digital anomalies, whereas individuals with ERI1 missense variants had severe spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia.

### Clinical Features

Choucair et al. (2017) reported a male child born to consanguineous parents. He had intellectual disability, short stature, dysmorphic facial features, synpolydactyly, and cardiac malformations.

Szot et al. (2021) reported a girl (F5.II.1). She had a perimembranous ventricular septal defect and pulmonary vein stenosis. Her limb findings were reduction defects, namely a transverse terminal limb reduction of the right hand with a rudimentary fifth digit, together with absent toes, absent or hypoplastic nails, and syndactyly. She also had microcephaly, global developmental delay, and autism. Her craniofacial features included brachycephaly, a flat occiput, bitemporal narrowing, a small nose with a normal philtrum, and cupped, prominent ears. She had bilateral inguinal hernias. She had no vertebral or renal anomalies.

Hoxha and Aliu (2023) reported a female patient with mild intellectual disability and eyelid ptosis. She had anomalies of the hands and feet: brachydactyly, clinodactyly, dysplastic or short nails of the halluces, brachytelephalangy, short metacarpals, and toe syndactyly.

Guo et al. (2023) reported 8 affected individuals from 7 unrelated families who had biallelic ERI1 variants. The individuals with biallelic null variants had mild intellectual disability and digital anomalies, and none of them had spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of Hoxha-Aliu syndrome is autosomal recessive. In the patient reported by Choucair et al. (2017), born to consanguineous parents, the 8p23.1 deletion was homozygous. Homozygous ERI1 nonsense variants were found in the patients reported by Szot et al. (2021) and by Hoxha and Aliu (2023), and Guo et al. (2023) found biallelic ERI1 null variants in their affected individuals.

### Molecular Genetics

Choucair et al. (2017) performed chromosomal microarray analysis in a boy with intellectual disability, synpolydactyly, and cardiac malformations who was born to consanguineous parents. They found a homozygous deletion at 8p23.1 that contained the microRNA miR-4660 and the ERI1 and MFHAS1 genes. The authors noted that ERI1 is a ribonuclease involved in RNA metabolism and is required for correct skeletal patterning because it defines HOXC8 expression. They discussed whether loss of ERI1 might contribute to the patient's limb abnormalities. ⚠︎

In a girl (F5.II.1) with limb reduction defects and cardiac anomalies, Szot et al. (2021) found a homozygous loss-of-function variant in the ERI1 gene (c.514C-T, gln172-to-ter, Q172X). The same girl was also homozygous for a missense variant in the KYNU gene (glu206-to-lys, E206K), which she had inherited from asymptomatic parents. This KYNU variant caused only mild NAD deficiency in a yeast assay. The authors noted that ERI1 had previously been found in a homozygous microdeletion, together with MFHAS1, in a patient with limb and cardiac abnormalities similar to hers. They suggested that the ERI1 variant may partly explain her limb and cardiac anomalies, but noted that the functional link between ERI1 mutation and these defects remained to be assessed. ⚠︎

Hoxha and Aliu (2023) found a homozygous nonsense variant in the ERI1 gene in a female patient with mild intellectual disability and anomalies of the hands and feet. They described ERI1 as an evolutionarily conserved 3-prime-to-5-prime exonuclease that functions in multiple RNA processing pathways. They noted that no human disease linked to ERI1 variants had been reported before.

Guo et al. (2023) reported 8 affected individuals from 7 unrelated families who had biallelic ERI1 variants. The individuals with biallelic null variants had mild intellectual disability and digital anomalies. The ERI1 missense variants were associated with a different phenotype, severe spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Guo et al. (2023) described a phenotypic dichotomy in individuals with biallelic ERI1 variants. Five affected individuals with missense variants had severe spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia. Individuals with biallelic null variants did not have it, and instead showed mild intellectual disability and digital anomalies. The missense variants abolished the exoribonuclease activity of ERI1. This led to defective trimming of the 3-prime end of 5.8S rRNA and reduced degradation of replication-dependent histone mRNAs. Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) derived from affected individuals showed impaired in vitro chondrogenesis and downregulation of genes that regulate skeletal patterning. The authors proposed a model in which, within recessive genotypes, missense alleles have a more severe effect than null alleles.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 HOXHA-ALIU SYNDROME**  
ERI1, GLN172TER [c.514C>T; p.Q172*]

Szot et al. (2021) reported a girl (F5.II.1) with limb reduction defects, a perimembranous ventricular septal defect, pulmonary vein stenosis, microcephaly, global developmental delay, and autism. She was homozygous for a c.514C-T transition (NM_153332.4) in the ERI1 gene, which results in a gln172-to-ter (Q172X) substitution and is predicted to cause loss of function. She was also homozygous for a KYNU missense variant (E206K), which caused only mild NAD deficiency in a yeast assay. The authors suggested that the ERI1 variant may partly explain her limb and cardiac anomalies, but noted that its functional link to these defects remained to be assessed. ⚠︎

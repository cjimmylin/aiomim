### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Hoxha-Aliu syndrome (HXAL) is caused by homozygous or other biallelic null mutation in the ERI1 gene on chromosome 8p23. ⚠︎

### Description

Hoxha-Aliu syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder associated with biallelic null variants in the ERI1 gene, which encodes a 3-prime-to-5-prime exoribonuclease. Affected individuals show mild intellectual disability or developmental delay and digital anomalies of the hands and feet, such as brachydactyly, clinodactyly, short nails of the halluces, short metacarpals, and toe syndactyly (Hoxha and Aliu, 2023; Guo et al., 2023). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Choucair et al. (2017) reported a boy, born to consanguineous parents, who had intellectual disability, short stature, dysmorphic facial features, synpolydactyly, and cardiac malformations.

Szot et al. (2021) reported a girl (F5.II.1) who was homozygous for a missense variant in the KYNU gene and was considered to have congenital NAD deficiency disorder; she was also homozygous for a nonsense variant in ERI1 (see MOLECULAR GENETICS). Her features included a perimembranous ventricular septal defect, pulmonary vein stenosis, and limb reduction defects (right hand transverse terminal limb reduction with a rudimentary fifth digit, hypoplastic deep-set or absent nails, absent toes, and syndactyly), as well as microcephaly, global developmental delay, autism, craniofacial dysmorphism, and bilateral inguinal hernias. Szot et al. (2021) noted that the ERI1 variant might confound the contribution of mild NAD deficiency to her phenotype.

Hoxha and Aliu (2023) reported a girl with mild intellectual disability, eyelid ptosis, and anomalies of the hands and feet, including brachydactyly, clinodactyly, dysplastic or short nails of the halluces, brachytelephalangy, short metacarpals, and toe syndactyly. ⚠︎

Guo et al. (2023) reported 8 affected individuals from 7 unrelated families with biallelic ERI1 variants. Those with biallelic null variants had mild intellectual disability and digital anomalies, whereas a severe spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia (SEMD) was found in 5 affected individuals with missense variants but not in those with null variants.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of Hoxha-Aliu syndrome was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Choucair et al. (2017) found a homozygous deletion including ERI1 in a child of consanguineous parents, Hoxha and Aliu (2023) described the condition as an autosomal recessive syndrome in a patient with a homozygous nonsense ERI1 variant, and Guo et al. (2023) found biallelic ERI1 variants in affected individuals.

### Cytogenetics

In a boy with intellectual disability, short stature, dysmorphic facial features, synpolydactyly, and cardiac malformations, Choucair et al. (2017) found by chromosomal microarray analysis a homozygous deletion at chromosome 8p23.1 that included the microRNA miR-4660 and the ERI1 and MFHAS1 genes. The authors discussed the possible contribution of the deleted genes to the phenotype, noting that ERI1 is a ribonuclease involved in RNA metabolism that is required for correct skeletal patterning through HOXC8 expression.

### Molecular Genetics

In a girl (F5.II.1) with limb reduction defects, ventricular septal defect, microcephaly, global developmental delay, and autism, who was also homozygous for a missense variant in the KYNU gene, Szot et al. (2021) identified a homozygous loss-of-function variant in the ERI1 gene, c.514C-T (gln172-to-ter; Q172X). Trio exome or genome sequencing was performed in the patients of that study. Szot et al. (2021) noted that ERI1 had previously been included in a homozygous microdeletion in a patient with similar limb and cardiac abnormalities, but that the functional link between ERI1 mutation and these defects remained to be assessed.

In a girl with mild intellectual disability, eyelid ptosis, and hand and foot anomalies, Hoxha and Aliu (2023) identified a homozygous nonsense variant in the ERI1 gene. ⚠︎

Guo et al. (2023) identified biallelic ERI1 variants in 8 affected individuals from 7 unrelated families. Individuals with biallelic null variants showed mild intellectual disability and digital anomalies, whereas 5 individuals with missense variants had severe SEMD. The missense variants caused loss of exoribonuclease activity, resulting in defective trimming of the 3-prime end of 5.8S rRNA and decreased degradation of replication-dependent histone mRNAs. Induced pluripotent stem cells derived from affected individuals showed impaired in vitro chondrogenesis with downregulation of genes regulating skeletal patterning.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Guo et al. (2023) described a phenotypic dichotomy associated with biallelic ERI1 variants: severe SEMD was found in 5 affected individuals with missense variants but not in individuals with biallelic null variants, who had mild intellectual disability and digital anomalies. The authors proposed a model in which missense alleles have a more severe effect than null alleles within recessive genotypes.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 HOXHA-ALIU SYNDROME**  
ERI1, GLN172TER [NM_153332.4:c.514C>T; p.Gln172Ter]

In a girl (F5.II.1) with limb reduction defects, ventricular septal defect, pulmonary vein stenosis, microcephaly, global developmental delay, and autism, Szot et al. (2021) identified a homozygous c.514C-T transition in the ERI1 gene, resulting in a gln172-to-ter (Q172X) substitution, which they considered a loss-of-function variant. The patient was also homozygous for a glu206-to-lys (E206K) variant in the KYNU gene, and the authors noted that the ERI1 variant might confound the contribution of mild NAD deficiency to her phenotype.

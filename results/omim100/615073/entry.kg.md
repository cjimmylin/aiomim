### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that dystonia-25 (DYT25) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GNAL gene (139312) on chromosome 18p11.21. ⚠︎

### Description

Dystonia-25 is an autosomal dominant primary torsion dystonia. It is mainly cervical, and age at onset ranges widely. Onset is usually in the neck. Dystonia often spreads to other sites, especially the cranial and facial muscles, and speech is commonly involved. Arm involvement is less frequent, and some patients develop generalized dystonia (Fuchs et al., 2013).

### Clinical Features

Bressman et al. (1994) examined 53 individuals from 4 generations of a non-Jewish North American family with adult-onset idiopathic torsion dystonia. Seven family members were affected. Mean age at onset was 28.4 years (range, 7 to 50 years). In 6 of the 7 affected individuals the neck was affected first. All 7 developed cervical dystonia, and 5 had dysarthria or dysphonia.

Fuchs et al. (2013) described 28 individuals with definite dystonia from 8 multiplex families with GNAL mutations. All were Caucasian of mixed European ancestry. Mean age at onset was 31.3 years (range, 7 to 54 years). Onset was in the neck in 82%, and 93% had neck involvement at final examination. However, most patients progressed to other sites, and only 46% had focal dystonia at last examination. Cranial involvement was present in 57% and speech involvement in 44%. Brachial onset was not observed, and only 32% had arm involvement. The authors noted that these features distinguished the disorder from DYT6. Fuchs et al. (2013) summarized the phenotype as mainly cervical dystonia with a relatively broad range of age at onset. In over half of subjects the dystonia spread to other muscles, especially facial muscles. Some individuals had generalized dystonia with onset in the leg, and other onset sites included the larynx, jaw, and tongue.

### Inheritance

Fuchs et al. (2013) noted that dystonia was inherited in an autosomal dominant manner in the families with GNAL mutations. Because GNAL may be imprinted, they examined the parent of origin. Dystonia was inherited equally from the maternal and paternal sides, and there were no apparent phenotypic differences between maternally and paternally inherited cases.

### Cytogenetics

Fuchs et al. (2013) noted that GNAL is located on chromosome 18p, centromeric to the DYT7 and DYT15 loci. They also noted that dystonia occurs in patients with 18p deletions, suggesting that absence of GNAL may contribute to dystonia in these cases.

### Heterogeneity

In a non-Jewish family with adult cervical-onset dystonia, Bressman et al. (1994) found that linkage data excluded the DYT1 region on chromosome 9q34. They concluded that a gene other than DYT1 is responsible for some cases of adult cervical-onset dystonia.

Fuchs et al. (2013) identified GNAL mutations in 6 of 39 (19%) additional multiplex primary torsion dystonia families of mixed European origin. All 39 families were negative for DYT1 and DYT6 mutations.

### Molecular Genetics

Fuchs et al. (2013) performed exome sequencing in 2 families with autosomal dominant primary torsion dystonia and found heterozygous GNAL mutations in both. Family P carried a nonsense mutation (S293X), which lay within 1 of 3 regions of potential linkage (lod scores 1.5 to 2.1). Family D1 carried a missense mutation (V137M). The authors then Sanger sequenced the GNAL coding region in 39 additional multiplex families of mixed European origin who were negative for DYT1 and DYT6 mutations. They found 6 additional novel mutations in 6 families (19%). These included 2 frameshift insertions, 1 nonsense mutation, 1 missense mutation, a 9-bp in-frame deletion, and a possible splice site mutation. In every family with DNA from multiple affected individuals, the mutation segregated with the disease. None of the variants was found in dbSNP135, about 3,500 European exomes, or 572 control chromosomes.

Fuchs et al. (2013) tested mutant proteins in a cell-based BRET assay in HEK293 cells. The assay measured Galpha-olf function through its interaction with Gbeta-gamma subunits during the D1 dopamine receptor (D1R)-initiated cycle. The S293X mutant failed to support any D1R-driven responses. The E155K and V137M mutants showed intermediate impairment, and a control variant (V16F) behaved like wildtype. The authors concluded that the assay suggested loss of function.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Fuchs et al. (2013) found no correlation between mutation type and phenotype among the 8 families with GNAL mutations. Phenotypes varied within families carrying the same GNAL mutation.

### Pathogenesis

Based on BRET assays showing loss or impairment of mutant Galpha-olf function in the D1R-driven cycle, Fuchs et al. (2013) concluded that GNAL mutations point to primary abnormalities of D1R and/or A2AR transmission in striatal neurons as a possible cause of dystonia.

**.0001 DYSTONIA 25**  
GNAL, SER293TER [c.878C>A; p.S293*]

Fuchs et al. (2013) studied affected members of a previously reported family (family P) with primary torsion dystonia (DYT25). The family was of mixed Russian and Swiss Mennonite heritage. Using exome sequencing, the authors identified a heterozygous c.878C-A transversion in the GNAL gene, resulting in a ser293-to-ter (S293X) substitution. The mutation co-segregated with dystonia in the family and was not present in about 3,500 European exomes. In a BRET assay, the S293X mutant failed to support any D1R-driven responses.

**.0002 DYSTONIA 25**  
GNAL, VAL137MET [c.409G>A; p.V137M]

Fuchs et al. (2013) studied affected members of a family (family D1) with primary torsion dystonia (DYT25). Using exome sequencing, they identified a heterozygous c.409G-A transition in the GNAL gene, resulting in a val137-to-met (V137M) substitution. The affected residue is completely conserved in GNAL orthologs. The mutation segregated with the disease. It was not found in 572 control chromosomes or about 3,500 European exomes. In BRET assays, the mutant showed an intermediate phenotype, largely consistent with impaired association with Gbeta-gamma subunits.

**.0008 DYSTONIA 25**  
GNAL, 274-5T-C [c.274-5T>C]

Fuchs et al. (2013) identified a heterozygous c.274-5T-C transition upstream of exon 4 of the GNAL gene in 2 affected members of a family (family B) with primary torsion dystonia (DYT25). The variant lies at chr18:11,753,820 (hg19) and is a possible splice site mutation. Bioinformatic analysis suggested that the wildtype sequence was 10 times more likely than the mutant sequence to be found near an acceptor splice site. However, the effect could not be proven because GNAL expression was undetectable in patient lymphoblasts.

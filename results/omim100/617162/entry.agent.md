### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-46 (DEE46) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GRIN2D gene.

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-46 (DEE46) is a severe neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by developmental delay and intractable epilepsy, caused by heterozygous missense variants in GRIN2D, which encodes the GluN2D subunit of the N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) receptor (Li et al., 2016; Tsuchida et al., 2018). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Li et al. (2016) reported 2 unrelated children with epileptic encephalopathy. In both, seizures had proven refractory to conventional antiepileptic medications. The older proband developed refractory status epilepticus.

Tsuchida et al. (2018) reported 3 unrelated patients with severe developmental delay and intractable epilepsy. Comparison of their clinical features with those of the 2 patients reported by Li et al. (2016) indicated that all showed similar clinical features.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous GRIN2D mutation in the patients reported by Li et al. (2016) occurred de novo, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

In 2 unrelated children with epileptic encephalopathy, Li et al. (2016) identified a recurrent de novo heterozygous missense mutation (c.1999G-A; V667I) in the GRIN2D gene, found by exome and candidate panel sequencing. The V667I substitution lies in the M3 transmembrane domain involved in channel gating. Functional studies showed a gain-of-function effect: the mutation increased glutamate and glycine potency 2-fold, increased channel open probability 6-fold, reduced receptor sensitivity to endogenous negative modulators such as extracellular protons, and prolonged the deactivation time course after glutamate removal. Transfection of cultured neurons with mutant human GRIN2D cDNA led to dendritic swelling and neuronal cell death, suggestive of excitotoxicity mediated by NMDA receptor overactivation.

By whole-exome sequencing in 3 unrelated patients with severe developmental delay and intractable epilepsy, Tsuchida et al. (2018) identified novel heterozygous missense variants in the GRIN2D gene. All altered residues were highly conserved across vertebrates and among the 4 GluN2 subunits. Structural consideration indicated that all 3 variants probably impair GluN2D function, either by affecting intersubunit interaction or by altering channel gating activity.

### Clinical Management

Li et al. (2016) evaluated the sensitivity of the mutant NMDA receptors to FDA-approved NMDA receptor antagonists and, based on the results, administered oral memantine to both patients, with mild to moderate improvement in seizure burden and development. The older proband subsequently developed refractory status epilepticus that showed dramatic electroclinical improvement upon treatment with ketamine and magnesium.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-46 (DEE46) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GRIN2D gene (602717) on chromosome 19q13.33.

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-46 is a severe neurologic disorder with seizures that are refractory to conventional antiepileptic medications (Li et al., 2016) and severe developmental delay (Tsuchida et al., 2018). The disorder is caused by de novo heterozygous mutation in GRIN2D, which encodes the GluN2D subunit of the NMDA-type glutamate receptor. Functional studies showed that the mutation increases receptor function (gain of function) (Li et al., 2016).

### Clinical Features

Li et al. (2016) reported 2 unrelated children with epileptic encephalopathy. In both children, seizures had proven refractory to conventional antiepileptic medications. Both children carried the same de novo mutation in the GRIN2D gene.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous GRIN2D mutation in the patients reported by Li et al. (2016) arose de novo, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. Li et al. (2016) described the mutation as dominant.

### Molecular Genetics

Li et al. (2016) used exome and candidate panel sequencing to study 2 unrelated children with epileptic encephalopathy. Both carried the same de novo heterozygous missense mutation in the GRIN2D gene, val667-to-ile (V667I). In functional studies, NMDA receptors containing the mutant GluN2D subunit showed a 2-fold increase in glutamate and glycine potency, and Li et al. (2016) classified the mutation as gain-of-function. Li et al. (2016) also tested the sensitivity of the mutant receptors to FDA-approved NMDA receptor antagonists in vitro.

### Clinical Management

Li et al. (2016) based treatment on in vitro results showing that the mutant GRIN2D receptors were sensitive to FDA-approved NMDA receptor antagonists. Both children received oral memantine, and Li et al. (2016) reported mild to moderate improvement in seizure burden and development. The older proband later developed refractory status epilepticus and was treated with ketamine and magnesium. ⚠︎

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DEVELOPMENTAL AND EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY 46**  
GRIN2D, VAL667ILE [c.1999G>A; p.Val667Ile]

Li et al. (2016) found a de novo heterozygous c.1999G-A transition in the GRIN2D gene in 2 unrelated children with epileptic encephalopathy (DEE46). The mutation results in a val667-to-ile (V667I) substitution in the M3 transmembrane domain of GluN2D. Functional studies showed that the mutant receptors gained function, including a 2-fold increase in sensitivity to glutamate and glycine.

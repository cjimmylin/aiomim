### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-46 (DEE46) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GRIN2D gene.

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-46 (DEE46) is a neurologic disorder with severe developmental delay and intractable epilepsy. Antiepileptic medications often do not control the seizures (Li et al., 2016; Tsuchida et al., 2018).

### Clinical Features

Li et al. (2016) reported 2 unrelated children with epileptic encephalopathy. Conventional antiepileptic medications did not control the seizures in either child. The older proband later developed refractory status epilepticus.

Tsuchida et al. (2018) reported 3 unrelated patients with severe developmental delay and intractable epilepsy. Their clinical features were similar to those of the 2 patients reported by Li et al. (2016).

### Inheritance

The heterozygous GRIN2D mutation found in the 2 patients reported by Li et al. (2016) arose de novo. This finding is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Li et al. (2016) used exome and candidate panel sequencing to study 2 unrelated children with epileptic encephalopathy. Both children had the same de novo heterozygous missense mutation in the GRIN2D gene, c.1999G-A (V667I). The mutation lies in the M3 transmembrane domain of GluN2D, a region involved in channel gating. Functional studies showed that the mutation caused a gain of function. It increased glutamate and glycine potency 2-fold and channel open probability 6-fold. It also reduced the receptor's sensitivity to endogenous negative modulators such as extracellular protons and prolonged the deactivation time course after glutamate removal. When cultured neurons were transfected with human GRIN2D cDNA carrying the mutation, they showed dendritic swelling and cell death, suggesting excitotoxicity from NMDA receptor overactivation.

Tsuchida et al. (2018) used whole-exome sequencing to study 3 unrelated patients with severe developmental delay and intractable epilepsy. Each patient had a different novel heterozygous missense variant in the GRIN2D gene. The altered residues were highly conserved across vertebrates and among the 4 GluN2 subunits. Based on structural analysis, the authors concluded that all 3 variants probably impair GluN2D function, either by affecting intersubunit interaction or by altering channel gating.

### Clinical Management

Li et al. (2016) tested FDA-approved NMDA receptor antagonists against the mutant receptors in vitro. Based on these results, both of their patients were given oral memantine, and seizure burden and development improved mildly to moderately. The older proband later developed refractory status epilepticus, which improved dramatically on electroclinical measures after treatment with ketamine and magnesium. The authors suggested that NMDA receptor antagonists may be useful as add-on epilepsy therapy in patients with GRIN2D gain-of-function mutations.

Tsuchida et al. (2018) suggested that a genetic diagnosis of a GluN2-related disorder may be clinically useful when considering drugs that target NMDA receptors.

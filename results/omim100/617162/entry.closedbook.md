### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-46 (DEE46) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GRIN2D gene (602717) on chromosome 19q13.

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-46 is a severe neurologic disorder that begins in infancy or early childhood. Affected individuals have refractory seizures and global developmental delay with intellectual disability. The disorder is caused by de novo heterozygous mutations in GRIN2D, which encodes the GluN2D subunit of the NMDA-type glutamate receptor. Functional studies show that the mutations increase receptor function (gain of function). ✗

### Clinical Features

Li et al. (2016) reported 2 unrelated children with an epileptic encephalopathy. Both had early-onset seizures that did not respond to treatment, and both had profound developmental delay. Each child carried the same de novo mutation in GRIN2D. ✗

### Inheritance

The heterozygous GRIN2D mutations in the patients reported by Li et al. (2016) arose de novo, which fits autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Li et al. (2016) used exome sequencing to study 2 unrelated patients with epileptic encephalopathy. Both carried the same de novo heterozygous missense mutation in the GRIN2D gene, V667I. In functional studies, NMDA receptors containing the mutant subunit were more sensitive to the agonists glutamate and glycine, which indicated a gain of function. In vitro, the FDA-approved NMDA receptor channel blockers memantine and ketamine inhibited the mutant receptors. Li et al. (2016) treated the patients with these drugs and reported some reduction in seizures. ⚠︎

### Clinical Management

Li et al. (2016) based treatment on the gain-of-function mechanism and gave NMDA receptor channel blockers, including memantine and ketamine and/or magnesium. They reported that seizure control improved in their patients with GRIN2D mutations. ⚠︎

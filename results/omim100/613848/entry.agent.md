### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that osteogenesis imperfecta type X (OI10) is caused by homozygous mutation in the SERPINH1 gene, which encodes the collagen chaperone protein HSP47.

### Description

Osteogenesis imperfecta type X is an autosomal recessive form of osteogenesis imperfecta (OI) caused by mutation in SERPINH1, which encodes the type I procollagen chaperone HSP47. OI is characterized by bone fragility and fractures that may be accompanied by bone deformity, dentinogenesis imperfecta, short stature, and shortened life span (Christiansen et al., 2010). Duran et al. (2015) noted that mutations in SERPINH1 and FKBP10 predominantly produce a moderately severe form of OI.

### Inheritance

Christiansen et al. (2010) reported a severe OI phenotype resulting from an autosomal recessive (homozygous) missense mutation in SERPINH1, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

In an individual with severe osteogenesis imperfecta, Christiansen et al. (2010) identified a homozygous c.233T-C missense mutation in the SERPINH1 gene, resulting in a leu78-to-pro (L78P) substitution. The mutation resulted in proteasomal degradation of the endoplasmic reticulum-resident HSP47. Type I procollagen accumulated in the Golgi of fibroblasts from the affected individual, and a population of the secreted type I procollagen was protease sensitive. The authors suggested that HSP47 monitors the integrity of the type I procollagen triple helix at the ER/cis-Golgi boundary and that, in its absence, the rate of transit from the ER to the Golgi is increased and helical structure is compromised.

Duran et al. (2015) identified a new OI mutation in SERPINH1 that resulted in destabilization and mislocalization of HSP47 and secondarily had similar effects on FKBP65. They found evidence that HSP47 and FKBP65 act cooperatively during posttranslational maturation of type I procollagen and that the 2 proteins fail to interact properly in cells with mutant HSP47, suggesting a common cellular pathway in OI caused by HSP47 and FKBP65 deficiency.

### Pathogenesis

Christiansen et al. (2010) found normal 3-hydroxylation of the prolyl residue at position 986 of the triple helical domain of proalpha1(I) chains in cells from a patient with HSP47 deficiency, placing the role of HSP47 downstream of the CRTAP/P3H1/CyPB complex involved in prolyl 3-hydroxylation.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that osteogenesis imperfecta type X (OI10) is caused by homozygous mutation in the SERPINH1 gene (600943) on chromosome 11q13.5.

### Description

Osteogenesis imperfecta (OI) is characterized by bone fragility and fractures that may be accompanied by bone deformity (Christiansen et al., 2010). Osteogenesis imperfecta type X is a severe autosomal recessive form of OI caused by defects in HSP47, the endoplasmic reticulum-resident collagen chaperone protein encoded by SERPINH1 (Christiansen et al., 2010).

### Clinical Features

Christiansen et al. (2010) reported an individual with a severe OI phenotype.

### Inheritance

Christiansen et al. (2010) found a homozygous mutation in their patient, which indicates autosomal recessive inheritance of OI10.

### Molecular Genetics

In an individual with severe osteogenesis imperfecta, Christiansen et al. (2010) found a homozygous missense mutation in the SERPINH1 gene, leu78-to-pro (L78P). The mutant HSP47 protein was unstable and was degraded via the proteasome. Type I procollagen accumulated in the Golgi of fibroblasts from the affected individual, indicating abnormal procollagen processing, and a population of the secreted type I procollagen was protease sensitive. Christiansen et al. (2010) suggested that HSP47 monitors the integrity of the triple helix of type I procollagen at the ER/cis-Golgi boundary and that, when it is absent, the rate of transit from the ER to the Golgi is increased.

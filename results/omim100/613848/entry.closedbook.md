### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that osteogenesis imperfecta type X (OI10) is caused by homozygous mutation in the SERPINH1 gene (600943) on chromosome 11q13.

### Description

Osteogenesis imperfecta type X is a severe autosomal recessive form of osteogenesis imperfecta, a connective tissue disorder marked by bone fragility. Patients have multiple fractures and bone deformities. The disorder is caused by defects in HSP47, an endoplasmic reticulum chaperone that binds procollagen. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Christiansen et al. (2010) reported a child of Saudi Arabian origin with severe osteogenesis imperfecta. Features included multiple fractures, bone deformities, blue sclerae, and dentinogenesis imperfecta. The child also had renal stones. ✗

### Inheritance

Christiansen et al. (2010) found homozygous mutation in their patient, which indicates autosomal recessive inheritance of OI10.

### Molecular Genetics

In a child with severe osteogenesis imperfecta, Christiansen et al. (2010) found a homozygous missense mutation in the SERPINH1 gene, leu78-to-pro (L78P). The candidate gene approach was prompted by the earlier finding of SERPINH1 mutation in Dachshunds with OI (Drogemuller et al., 2009). In the patient's cells, the mutant HSP47 protein was unstable and degraded by the proteasome. Type I procollagen processing and secretion were abnormal. ✗

### Animal Model

Drogemuller et al. (2009) studied Dachshunds with autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta and found a homozygous missense mutation in the canine SERPINH1 gene, leu326-to-pro (L326P). ✗

Nagai et al. (2000) found that mice lacking Hsp47 died during embryonic development and had abnormal collagen formation. ✗

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that osteogenesis imperfecta type X (OI10) is caused by homozygous mutation in the SERPINH1 gene (600943) on chromosome 11q13.5.

### Description

Osteogenesis imperfecta type X is a severe autosomal recessive form of osteogenesis imperfecta caused by deficiency of HSP47, the collagen chaperone-like protein encoded by SERPINH1 (Christiansen et al., 2010).

### Biochemical Features

In the patient with OI10, Christiansen et al. (2010) found normal 3-hydroxylation of the prolyl residue at position 986 of the proalpha1(I) triple helical domain. From this they concluded that HSP47 acts downstream of the CRTAP/P3H1/CyPB complex, which is involved in prolyl 3-hydroxylation.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of osteogenesis imperfecta type X in the patient reported by Christiansen et al. (2010) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. The patient was homozygous for a missense mutation in SERPINH1. Duran et al. (2015) also described OI caused by HSP47 deficiency as recessive.

### Molecular Genetics

In a patient with severe autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta, Christiansen et al. (2010) identified a homozygous missense mutation (c.233T-C; L78P) in the SERPINH1 gene, which encodes the collagen chaperone-like protein HSP47. The mutation caused the endoplasmic reticulum-resident HSP47 to be degraded through the proteasome. In fibroblasts from the patient, type I procollagen accumulated in the Golgi, and part of the secreted type I procollagen was protease sensitive.

Duran et al. (2015) identified a new osteogenesis imperfecta mutation in the SERPINH1 gene. The mutation destabilized HSP47 and caused it to be mislocalized. It also had similar secondary effects on FKBP65.

### Pathogenesis

Christiansen et al. (2010) found that the L78P mutation in HSP47 led to proteasomal degradation of the protein. In the patient's fibroblasts, type I procollagen accumulated in the Golgi, and part of the secreted type I procollagen was protease sensitive. The authors suggested that HSP47 monitors the integrity of the type I procollagen triple helix at the boundary between the endoplasmic reticulum and the cis-Golgi. Without HSP47, procollagen would move from the ER to the Golgi faster and its helical structure would be compromised.

Duran et al. (2015) found evidence that HSP47 and FKBP65 act cooperatively during the posttranslational maturation of type I procollagen. In cells with mutant HSP47, the two proteins did not interact properly. The authors concluded that OI caused by HSP47 deficiency and OI caused by FKBP65 deficiency share a common cellular pathway.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 OSTEOGENESIS IMPERFECTA, TYPE X**  
SERPINH1, LEU78PRO [c.233T>C; p.L78P]

In a patient with severe autosomal recessive osteogenesis imperfecta (OI10), Christiansen et al. (2010) identified a homozygous c.233T-C transition in the SERPINH1 gene, resulting in a leu78-to-pro (L78P) substitution in HSP47. The mutation caused the endoplasmic reticulum-resident HSP47 to be degraded through the proteasome. In the patient's fibroblasts, type I procollagen accumulated in the Golgi and part of the secreted type I procollagen was protease sensitive. 3-Hydroxylation of proalpha1(I) prolyl residue 986 was normal.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive dilated cardiomyopathy-2A (CMD2A) is caused by homozygous mutation in the gene encoding cardiac troponin I (TNNI3). Heterozygous mutations in TNNI3 have also been reported in families with autosomal dominant dilated cardiomyopathy. ⚠︎

### Description

Idiopathic dilated cardiomyopathy is a common cause of heart failure (Murphy et al., 2004). Familial isolated dilated cardiomyopathy is usually reported as autosomal dominant with variable penetrance and expressivity, but an autosomal recessive form has also been described (Goldblatt et al., 1987). In a study of infants of Arab descent, Seliem et al. (2000) found that onset was usually before 30 months of age and mortality was high.

### Clinical Features

Battersby and Glenner (1961) and Kariv et al. (1964) published reports of familial cardiomyopathy. The report by Kariv et al. (1964) focused on electrocardiographic and vectorcardiographic findings.

Goldblatt et al. (1987) described dilated cardiomyopathy in 3 members of a consanguineous Madeira Portuguese family.

Seliem et al. (2000) studied 55 consecutive patients younger than 10 years of age with dilated cardiomyopathy, seen over a 5-year period in the Eastern Province of Saudi Arabia. Echocardiography was the primary diagnostic method. The 55 patients came from 41 families of Arab descent and made up 20% of the offspring in those families. In 19 families (46%) the parents were first cousins; the other 22 families (54%) had no obvious consanguinity. Of the patients, 95% presented before 30 months of age (range, 1 to 100 months). Females made up 62% of patients and males 38%. Of the patients, 25 (46%) died, 15 (27%) improved, and 15 (27%) recovered. Left ventricular shortening fraction at diagnosis ranged from 5 to 28% and did not differ among those who died, improved, or recovered.

### Inheritance

Goldblatt et al. (1987) concluded that dilated cardiomyopathy was inherited as an autosomal recessive trait in a consanguineous Madeira Portuguese family with 3 affected members. After reviewing the literature, they suggested that the autosomal recessive form is more common than previously reported.

Seliem et al. (2000) performed complex segregation analysis under the mixed model of inheritance on 41 Saudi Arabian families with infantile dilated cardiomyopathy. Recessive inheritance gave the best fit to the data.

Murphy et al. (2004) identified a TNNI3 mutation in a family with recessive dilated cardiomyopathy. In contrast, Carballo et al. (2009) reported heterozygous TNNI3 mutations in families with autosomal dominant dilated cardiomyopathy. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Murphy et al. (2004) analyzed the TNNI3 gene, which encodes cardiac troponin I, in 235 patients with dilated cardiomyopathy. They used a method able to detect both dominant and recessive mutations. They found a novel TNNI3 mutation in a family with recessive disease. Functional studies showed impaired troponin interactions, which could reduce myocardial contractility. Murphy et al. (2004) stated that TNNI3 was the first gene identified for recessive dilated cardiomyopathy.

Carballo et al. (2009) screened TNNI3 exons by heteroduplex analysis in 96 probands with dilated cardiomyopathy, then sequenced exons with abnormal profiles. They identified 2 novel missense mutations, lys36-to-gln (K36Q) and asn185-to-lys (N185K). Each was associated with severe, early-onset familial dilated cardiomyopathy. Variants were evaluated by segregation analysis and by study of normal controls. Of the 5 mutation carriers, 3 needed cardiac transplantation, at ages 6, 15, and 24 years. Troponin reconstituted with either mutant troponin I gave a lower maximum actin-tropomyosin-activated myosin ATPase rate and lower calcium sensitivity than wildtype. Mutant thin filaments also had reduced calcium affinity. Carballo et al. (2009) stated that these were the first reported autosomal dominant mutations in TNNI3 causing dilated cardiomyopathy.

### Population Genetics

Seliem et al. (2000) found that, among 41 families of Arab descent from the Eastern Province of Saudi Arabia, the 55 children with dilated cardiomyopathy made up 20% of the offspring. They suggested that recessively inherited dilated cardiomyopathy may be hard to recognize in populations where consanguineous marriage is uncommon and families are small.

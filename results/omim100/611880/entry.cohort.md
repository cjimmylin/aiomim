### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive dilated cardiomyopathy-2A (CMD2A) is caused by mutation in the TNNI3 gene (191044) on chromosome 19q13.42.

### Description

Dilated cardiomyopathy-2A (CMD2A) is a form of idiopathic dilated cardiomyopathy that is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. It has been reported in consanguineous families, and in 1 population-based series it presented in infancy or early childhood with reduced left ventricular systolic function. Murphy et al. (2004) described a recessive form caused by mutation in the cardiac troponin I gene (TNNI3). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Kariv et al. (1964) reported familial cardiomyopathy and focused on the electrocardiographic and vectorcardiographic findings.

Goldblatt et al. (1987) described dilated cardiomyopathy in 3 members of a consanguineous Madeira Portuguese family and concluded that it was inherited as an autosomal recessive trait.

Seliem et al. (2000) studied 55 consecutive patients younger than 10 years who had dilated cardiomyopathy and were seen over a 5-year period in the Eastern Province of Saudi Arabia. The 55 patients came from 41 families of Arab descent and made up 20% of the offspring of these families. In 19 families (46%) the parents were first cousins, and in 22 families (54%) there was no obvious consanguinity. Age at presentation was under 30 months in 95% of patients (range, 1 to 100 months). Of the patients, 38% were male and 62% were female. The left ventricular shortening fraction at diagnosis ranged from 5 to 28% and did not differ between patients who died, improved, or recovered. Twenty-five patients (46%) died, 15 (27%) improved, and 15 (27%) recovered.

### Inheritance

Goldblatt et al. (1987) reported autosomal recessive inheritance of dilated cardiomyopathy in a consanguineous Madeira Portuguese family. They noted that familial isolated dilated cardiomyopathy is usually reported as autosomal dominant with variable penetrance and expressivity. However, from their review of the literature they concluded that the autosomal recessive form appeared to be more common than previously reported.

Seliem et al. (2000) performed complex segregation analysis under the mixed model of inheritance on data from 41 Arab families with infantile dilated cardiomyopathy. A model of recessive inheritance fit the data best. The authors suggested that recessively inherited dilated cardiomyopathy may have been reported rarely because it is hard to recognize in populations where consanguineous marriage is uncommon and families have few children.

Murphy et al. (2004) identified a TNNI3 mutation in a family with recessive dilated cardiomyopathy.

### Heterogeneity

Carballo et al. (2009) used heteroduplex analysis to screen TNNI3 in 96 probands with dilated cardiomyopathy. They identified 2 novel heterozygous missense mutations, lys36-to-gln (K36Q) and asn185-to-lys (N185K), in 2 families with severe, early-onset familial dilated cardiomyopathy that was inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern. Segregation analysis was used to evaluate the variants. Of the 5 mutation carriers, 3 needed cardiac transplantation, at ages 6, 15, and 24 years. Troponin reconstituted with either mutant troponin I had lower maximum actin-tropomyosin-activated myosin ATPase rates and lower Ca(2+) sensitivity than wildtype troponin. Mutant thin filaments also had reduced Ca(2+) affinity. The authors noted that these changes closely mirrored those of proven dilated cardiomyopathy mutations in other thin filament proteins. They stated that these were the first reported autosomal dominant mutations in TNNI3 causing dilated cardiomyopathy. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Murphy et al. (2004) screened the TNNI3 gene in 235 patients with dilated cardiomyopathy, using mutation analysis that could detect both dominant and recessive mutations. They identified a novel TNNI3 mutation in a family with recessive disease. In functional studies, the mutation impaired troponin interactions, which could lead to reduced myocardial contractility. The authors suggested that TNNI3 was the first recessive gene identified for dilated cardiomyopathy.

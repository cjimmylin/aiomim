### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive dilated cardiomyopathy-2A (CMD2A) is caused by homozygous mutation in the gene encoding cardiac troponin I (TNNI3; 191044) on chromosome 19q13. ✗

### Description

Dilated cardiomyopathy is a disorder of the heart muscle. The left ventricle, or both ventricles, become enlarged and pump poorly, which leads to congestive heart failure and arrhythmias and can cause early death. CMD2A is an autosomal recessive form of idiopathic dilated cardiomyopathy caused by mutation in the cardiac troponin I gene. ✗

### Inheritance

Murphy et al. (2004) established autosomal recessive inheritance of CMD2A. In their family, affected individuals were homozygous for a TNNI3 mutation, and heterozygous carriers were clinically unaffected. ✗

### Heterogeneity

Dilated cardiomyopathy is genetically heterogeneous, and most forms are inherited as autosomal dominant traits. In other reports, heterozygous mutations in TNNI3 have also been associated with dominantly inherited dilated cardiomyopathy and with hypertrophic and restrictive cardiomyopathy. ✗

### Molecular Genetics

Murphy et al. (2004) studied a family with recessively inherited idiopathic dilated cardiomyopathy. In affected members they identified a homozygous ala2-to-val (A2V) mutation in the TNNI3 gene. Heterozygous family members were unaffected. In functional studies, the mutation weakened the interaction of cardiac troponin I with cardiac troponin T. ✗

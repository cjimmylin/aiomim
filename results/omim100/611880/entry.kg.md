### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive dilated cardiomyopathy-2A (CMD2A) is caused by homozygous mutation in the cardiac troponin I gene (TNNI3). ⚠︎

### Description

Dilated cardiomyopathy-2A is a form of idiopathic, isolated dilated cardiomyopathy. Familial isolated dilated cardiomyopathy is usually reported as autosomal dominant with variable penetrance and expressivity, but autosomal recessive inheritance has been shown in consanguineous families (Goldblatt et al., 1987). In an Arab population with frequent consanguinity, the recessive form presented mainly in infancy (Seliem et al., 2000). Murphy et al. (2004) identified TNNI3 as the first recessive gene for idiopathic dilated cardiomyopathy. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Battersby and Glenner (1961) reported on familial cardiomyopathy.

Kariv et al. (1964) described familial cardiomyopathy and paid particular attention to the electrocardiographic and vectorcardiographic findings.

Goldblatt et al. (1987) described idiopathic, isolated dilated cardiomyopathy in 3 members of a consanguineous Madeira Portuguese family. ⚠︎

Seliem et al. (2000) evaluated 55 consecutive patients younger than 10 years of age in whom dilated cardiomyopathy was diagnosed by echocardiography over a 5-year period in the Eastern Province of Saudi Arabia. The patients came from 41 families of Arab descent and made up 20% of the offspring of those families. Age at presentation was under 30 months in 95% of cases (range, 1 to 100 months). Of the patients, 38% were male and 62% female. At diagnosis, the left ventricular shortening fraction ranged from 5 to 28%. Twenty-five patients (46%) died, 15 (27%) improved, and 15 (27%) recovered. The shortening fraction at diagnosis did not differ among the patients who died, improved, or recovered.

Carballo et al. (2009) described 5 carriers of heterozygous TNNI3 mutations from 2 families with severe, early-onset familial dilated cardiomyopathy. Three of the 5 needed cardiac transplantation, at ages 6, 15, and 24 years. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Goldblatt et al. (1987) concluded that dilated cardiomyopathy in a consanguineous Madeira Portuguese family was inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. They noted that familial isolated dilated cardiomyopathy is usually reported as autosomal dominant with variable penetrance and expressivity. After reviewing the literature, they suggested that the autosomal recessive form is more common than previously reported.

In their study of 41 Arab families from the Eastern Province of Saudi Arabia, Seliem et al. (2000) found that the parents were first cousins in 19 families (46%). There was no obvious consanguinity in the other 22 families (54%). Complex segregation analysis of the family data using the mixed model of inheritance showed that recessive inheritance fit the data best. The authors suggested that recessive dilated cardiomyopathy may be hard to recognize in populations where consanguineous marriage is uncommon and families have few children.

The TNNI3 mutation identified by Murphy et al. (2004) was found in a family with recessive disease.

### Heterogeneity

Goldblatt et al. (1987) noted that cardiomyopathy is a heterogeneous disorder with many inherited and acquired causes. Murphy et al. (2004) suggested that other recessive genes for idiopathic dilated cardiomyopathy could be identified by mutation analyses designed to detect homozygous mutations.

Carballo et al. (2009) reported the first autosomal dominant mutations in TNNI3 that cause dilated cardiomyopathy. This finding added TNNI3 to the genes in which different mutations can cause either hypertrophic or dilated cardiomyopathy.

### Molecular Genetics

Murphy et al. (2004) screened the TNNI3 gene in 235 patients with dilated cardiomyopathy. Their mutation analysis was suitable for identifying both dominant and recessive mutations. They identified a novel TNNI3 mutation in a family with recessive disease. Functional studies showed that the mutation impaired troponin interactions. The authors stated that TNNI3 was the first recessive gene identified for idiopathic dilated cardiomyopathy.

Carballo et al. (2009) screened 96 probands with dilated cardiomyopathy for TNNI3 mutations, using heteroduplex analysis and sequencing. In 2 families with severe, early-onset familial dilated cardiomyopathy, they identified 2 novel missense mutations in TNNI3, K36Q and N185K, which were inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern. The K36Q variant was evaluated by segregation analysis and in normal controls. In functional studies, troponin reconstituted with either mutant troponin I gave a lower maximum ATPase rate and lower Ca(2+) sensitivity than wildtype. Mutant thin filaments had reduced Ca(2+) affinity.

### Pathogenesis

Murphy et al. (2004) found that the recessive TNNI3 mutation impaired troponin interactions. They suggested that this could lead to diminished myocardial contractility.

Carballo et al. (2009) noted that the functional changes caused by their dominant TNNI3 mutations, lower ATPase rates and reduced Ca(2+) sensitivity, closely matched a consistent phenotype seen with proven dilated cardiomyopathy mutations in other thin filament proteins. They took this as support that the mutations are disease-causing.

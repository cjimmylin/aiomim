### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that myofibrillar myopathy-9 with early respiratory failure (MFM9), also known as hereditary myopathy with early respiratory failure (HMERF), is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding titin (TTN) on chromosome 2q. ⚠︎

### Description

Myofibrillar myopathy-9 with early respiratory failure is an autosomal dominant muscle disorder with onset in adulthood. It is characterized by proximal and/or distal muscle weakness, early weakness of the respiratory muscles, and myofibrillar abnormalities on muscle biopsy (Pfeffer et al., 2014). Respiratory failure can develop while patients are still ambulant (Birchall et al., 2005). Muscle MRI shows a characteristic pattern, with involvement of the semitendinosus muscle (Pfeffer et al., 2012).

### Nomenclature

Pfeffer et al. (2014) found that patients with HMERF caused by the C30071R mutation in TTN met criteria for myofibrillar myopathy (MFM). They proposed that HMERF due to TTN mutations be classified as 'MFM-titinopathy.'

### Clinical Features

Edström et al. (1990) reported 16 patients from 7 pedigrees with an unusual limb-girdle myopathy that began in adulthood. Most cases showed evidence of autosomal dominant inheritance. All patients had respiratory muscle weakness early in the disease, and in many of them acute respiratory incapacity was the reason for the first neurologic examination. Other early symptoms were weakness of the neck flexors and sometimes of the foot extensors. Electrophysiologic studies confirmed a myopathy, and serum muscle enzyme levels were normal or slightly raised. On light microscopy, muscle biopsies showed myofibrillar changes, including plaques that stained strongly with rhodamine-conjugated phalloidin, a marker for F-actin. On electron microscopy, the plaques consisted of moderately dense thin filaments and were associated with splitting of Z discs or extended from Z discs. The authors suggested that the findings reflected defective myofibrillogenesis.

Nicolao et al. (1999) studied 2 Swedish families with autosomal dominant myopathy. Affected members had proximal weakness, early respiratory failure, and characteristic cytoplasmic bodies on muscle biopsy.

Xiang et al. (1999) studied a French family whose clinical phenotype and muscle biopsy pathology were similar to those of the Swedish families reported by Nicolao et al. (1999).

Chinnery et al. (2001) reported an autosomal dominant myopathy that presented in mid-adult life with weakness of the tibialis anterior. They assessed 24 individuals over 3 generations of 1 family, 11 of whom were affected, and studied muscle pathology in 7 affected individuals, including 1 at autopsy. Affected individuals in the second generation presented at an earlier age, and their disease progressed faster, than those in the first generation. Lung function tests showed progressive global respiratory muscle weakness from the time of presentation, and some patients had preferential involvement of the diaphragm. Weakness of the hip girdle and shoulder girdle developed later in the disease. Clinical and pathologic features correlated closely. Clinically unaffected muscles showed minimal pathologic change. Moderately affected muscles showed fiber splitting, eosinophilic inclusions, and vacuoles with basophilic rims. In severely affected muscles, fat and fibrous connective tissue had replaced the muscle fibers. The inclusions were Congophilic and reacted with antibodies to desmin, beta-amyloid, and phosphorylated tau protein.

Pfeffer et al. (2012) characterized the neuromuscular, respiratory, and radiologic phenotype in 31 mutation carriers, followed for up to 31 years, from the family reported by Chinnery et al. (2001) and from other families. Patients presented with predominantly distal, predominantly proximal, or predominantly respiratory muscle weakness. Age at onset varied widely, from early adulthood to a mild phenotype in advanced age. In nearly all patients, weakness began earlier and was more severe in the lower limbs. Seven patients also had axial muscle weakness. Respiratory function declined gradually over time. No patient had cardiomyopathy. Muscle MRI in 21 affected patients showed characteristic abnormalities, with semitendinosus involvement in 20 of the 21, including 3 who were presymptomatic. The most frequent diagnostic findings on muscle histopathology were eosinophilic inclusions (inclusion bodies) and rimmed vacuoles, but a minority of patients had nonspecific pathology.

Pfeffer et al. (2014) reviewed 5 British probands with the C30071R mutation and their affected relatives. Onset was usually in the fourth or fifth decade, with tripping and falling, myalgia, and difficulty walking. CK levels ranged from normal to 1195 IU/L (average 730 IU/L). The disease progressed slowly and involved proximal, distal, and axial muscles. All patients except 1 remained ambulant; that patient lost ambulation after about 25 years of disease. Ten of 11 patients had pulmonary function tests indicating respiratory muscle weakness. Four patients needed nocturnal noninvasive ventilation about 20 to 30 years after onset. New findings were scapular winging, which was often asymmetric, contractures of the elbows and/or Achilles tendons, variable spinal rigidity, and muscle hypertrophy. Two patients had cardiac involvement: 1 had mild left ventricular impairment, possibly related to respiratory dysfunction, and 1 had supraventricular tachycardia. Muscle biopsies showed myopathic or dystrophic changes. Rimmed vacuoles were present in 3 biopsies and eosinophilic inclusions in 3. Six of 7 biopsies showed variable cytoplasmic accumulation of desmin, myotilin, p62, and ubiquitin. In 3 biopsies the aggregates had a 'cheetah skin' appearance, particularly on myotilin staining. Muscle MRI showed early involvement of the semitendinosus and peroneus longus muscles.

### Inheritance

Inheritance of the disorder is autosomal dominant. Edström et al. (1990) found evidence of autosomal dominant inheritance in most of their cases. Autosomal dominant inheritance was also seen in the families reported by Nicolao et al. (1999), Chinnery et al. (2001), and Pfeffer et al. (2012).

### Mapping

Nicolao et al. (1999) performed a genomewide linkage screen in 2 Swedish families with the disorder and mapped it to chromosome 2q24-q31. Most other chromosomal regions were excluded. The maximum combined 2-point lod score was 4.87 at theta = 0, with marker D2S1245. Haplotype analysis placed the disease gene in a 17-cM region between markers D2S2384 and D2S364. Affected members of both families shared an identical haplotype, suggesting a common origin.

Chinnery et al. (2001) excluded linkage to the known distal myopathy loci in the family they studied.

### Heterogeneity

In a French family with a phenotype similar to that of the Swedish families, Xiang et al. (1999) completely excluded the 2q24-q31 region. A genomewide scan gave a maximum 2-point lod score of 2.11, the highest attainable in this family, for markers in a 4-cM region between D2S1272 and D2S1260. This suggested a disease locus on chromosome 2q21.

### Molecular Genetics

Lange et al. (2005) identified a signaling complex in which the titin kinase domain interacts with NBR1. They reported that a human mutation in the titin protein kinase domain causes hereditary muscle disease by disrupting this pathway.

Pfeffer et al. (2012) combined genomewide linkage analysis with whole-exome sequencing in the family reported by Chinnery et al. (2001). They identified a likely causal variant in the TTN gene, g.274375T-C, predicting a cys30071-to-arg (C30071R) substitution, within a shared 2.93-Mb haplotype on chromosome 2. The variant segregated with the phenotype in 21 individuals from the original family and in 9 individuals from a second family with the same highly selective pattern of muscle involvement on MRI. It also segregated in a third familial case with a similar phenotype. ⚠︎

Pfeffer et al. (2014) noted that 2 TTN mutations, C30071R in the 119th fibronectin-3 (FN3) domain and R32450W in the kinase domain, had been identified in 8 HMERF families. They screened 127 undiagnosed probands whose presentation was compatible with MFM, using Sanger sequencing of these regions. Five unrelated subjects (3.9%) carried the heterozygous C30071R mutation, as did 4 of the subjects with a clinical diagnosis of MFM (5.5%). The mutation cosegregated with disease in the 3 families in which segregation could be studied. No patient had a mutation at position 32450. Two further subjects had novel heterozygous variants of uncertain pathogenicity in the same FN3 domain: P30091L and N30145K. P30091L was also found in a healthy 61-year-old brother of the proband. Segregation analysis was not possible for N30145K.

### Diagnosis

Birchall et al. (2005) performed muscle MRI on 7 patients with autosomal dominant myopathy with early respiratory failure, aged 37 to 66 years, 4 of whom were male. MRI showed selective early involvement of the semitendinosus and obturator externus muscles that could not be detected clinically. Closely related muscles progressed at different rates. The authors concluded that these findings were specific to the disorder and allowed early noninvasive diagnosis in individuals at risk.

Pfeffer et al. (2012) proposed that the disorder be considered in patients with adult-onset proximal or distal myopathy and early respiratory failure, even if muscle pathology is nonspecific. They recommended muscle MRI as an initial investigation and screening for TTN mutations if MRI is positive. Pfeffer et al. (2014) recommended sequencing the relevant FN3 domain of TTN as part of the diagnostic workup for MFM, particularly when there is early respiratory involvement and supportive muscle MRI.

### Clinical Management

Major cardiac involvement did not appear to be part of the disorder. However, because titin has a role in myocardial function, Pfeffer et al. (2014) recommended cardiac surveillance for all patients with TTN mutations.

### Population Genetics

Pfeffer et al. (2014) found a common core haplotype of 171 kb in probands from 5 new British families with the C30071R mutation and 3 previously reported families. They judged it highly improbable that the families had acquired the mutation independently. They concluded that a British founder effect likely explained the finding of the C30071R mutation in 3.9% of their study population.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 MYOPATHY, MYOFIBRILLAR, 9, WITH EARLY RESPIRATORY FAILURE**  
TTN, CYS30071ARG [g.274375T>C; p.Cys30071Arg; p.C30071R]

Pfeffer et al. (2012) studied a family with hereditary myopathy with early respiratory failure. Using genomewide linkage and whole-exome sequencing, they identified a g.274375T-C transition in the TTN gene, resulting in a cys30071-to-arg (C30071R) substitution. The variant segregated with the phenotype in 21 individuals from the original family, 9 individuals from a second family, and a third familial case.

Pfeffer et al. (2014) identified the heterozygous C30071R mutation, located in the 119th FN3 domain of titin, in 5 of 127 unrelated British probands with undiagnosed myopathy compatible with MFM. The mutation cosegregated with disease in 3 families. Haplotype analysis indicated a founder effect.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that myofibrillar myopathy-9 with early respiratory failure (MFM9), also known as hereditary myopathy with early respiratory failure (HMERF), is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding titin (TTN; 188840) on chromosome 2q31. ⚠︎

### Description

Myofibrillar myopathy-9 with early respiratory failure (MFM9), or hereditary myopathy with early respiratory failure (HMERF), is an autosomal dominant myopathy that usually begins in adulthood. Patients develop weakness that may be proximal or distal and involves the respiratory muscles early in the disease, so that respiratory failure may develop while the person can still walk. Muscle biopsies show myofibrillar changes with cytoplasmic bodies, eosinophilic inclusions, and rimmed vacuoles.

### Clinical Features

Edstrom et al. (1990) described a Swedish family with an autosomal dominant myopathy that began in adulthood. Affected members had proximal muscle weakness and early involvement of the respiratory muscles. Muscle biopsy showed cytoplasmic bodies and other myofibrillar abnormalities. ✗

Chinnery et al. (2001) reported a British family with an autosomal dominant myopathy in which distal weakness was accompanied by early respiratory failure. Muscle pathology showed eosinophilic cytoplasmic inclusions and rimmed vacuoles. Linkage to known candidate loci was excluded. ⚠︎

Pfeffer et al. (2012) and Ohlsson et al. (2012) reported further families from northern England and Sweden with HMERF. The degree of proximal versus distal weakness varied. Respiratory muscle weakness often appeared early, and many affected people needed noninvasive ventilation. Muscle MRI showed early involvement of the semitendinosus muscle. Biopsies showed cytoplasmic bodies, eosinophilic inclusions, and rimmed vacuoles. The heart was not usually affected. ✗

### Inheritance

The families reported by Edstrom et al. (1990) and Chinnery et al. (2001) showed autosomal dominant inheritance. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Nicolao et al. (1999) studied a Swedish family with autosomal dominant myopathy, proximal weakness, and early respiratory muscle involvement. Using linkage analysis, they mapped the disorder to chromosome 2q24-q31. ✗

### Molecular Genetics

Lange et al. (2005) studied the Swedish HMERF family and found a heterozygous arg279-to-trp (R279W) substitution in the kinase domain of TTN. They reported that this change altered binding of the kinase domain to its signaling partners. Later studies (Ohlsson et al., 2012; Pfeffer et al., 2012) found that this variant did not explain the disorder in the families studied. The causal mutation proved to lie in the A-band region of titin. ✗

Ohlsson et al. (2012) studied Swedish families with HMERF. They identified a heterozygous cys30071-to-arg (C30071R) mutation in the A-band region of TTN, within fibronectin type III domain 119. The mutation segregated with the disorder in the families. ✗

Pfeffer et al. (2012) studied British families with HMERF, including the family reported by Chinnery et al. (2001). They identified the same heterozygous C30071R mutation in TTN, which segregated with the disease. Haplotype analysis suggested a shared founder origin. ⚠︎

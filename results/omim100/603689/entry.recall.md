### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that myofibrillar myopathy-9 with early respiratory failure (MFM9) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding titin (TTN; 188840).

### Description

Myofibrillar myopathy-9 with early respiratory failure (MFM9), or hereditary myopathy with early respiratory failure (HMERF), is an autosomal dominant myopathy (Nicolao et al., 1999) that begins in mid-adult life (Chinnery et al., 2001). Weakness may be predominantly distal or proximal (Pfeffer et al., 2012). The respiratory muscles are involved early in the disease (Nicolao et al., 1999), and respiratory failure develops while affected individuals can still walk (Birchall et al., 2005). Muscle biopsies show myofibrillar changes with cytoplasmic bodies (Pfeffer et al., 2014), eosinophilic inclusions, and rimmed vacuoles (Chinnery et al., 2001). ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

Pfeffer et al. (2014) suggested that HMERF due to mutations in the TTN gene be classified as MFM-titinopathy.

### Clinical Features

Edstrom et al. (1990) described 16 patients from 7 pedigrees with an unusual limb-girdle myopathy that began in adulthood. A prominent early finding in all cases was respiratory muscle weakness. Muscle biopsy showed myofibrillar changes.

Chinnery et al. (2001) reported a family with an autosomal dominant distal myopathy with early respiratory failure that presented in mid-adult life. Moderately affected muscles showed eosinophilic inclusions and rimmed vacuoles. The disease was not linked to any of the known loci associated with distal myopathies.

Pfeffer et al. (2012) reported further families with HMERF. The predominant pattern of weakness varied between distal, proximal, and respiratory muscle weakness, and respiratory failure appeared early. Muscle MRI showed characteristic semitendinosus involvement, including in presymptomatic patients. Muscle histopathology most frequently showed eosinophilic inclusions and rimmed vacuoles. Cardiomyopathy was not present in any of the patients.

### Inheritance

In most of the cases described by Edstrom et al. (1990), there was evidence of autosomal dominant inheritance. The family reported by Chinnery et al. (2001) showed autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Mapping

Nicolao et al. (1999) studied 2 Swedish families with autosomal dominant myopathy, proximal weakness, and early respiratory failure. Using the combined linkage data from both families, they established linkage to chromosome 2q24-q31.

### Molecular Genetics

Lange et al. (2005) reported that a human mutation in the titin protein kinase domain caused hereditary muscle disease by disrupting the titin kinase-nbr1-p62-MuRF2 signaling pathway.

In the family originally reported by Chinnery et al. (2001) and in additional families, Pfeffer et al. (2012) identified a likely causal variant in the TTN gene, a g.274375T-C change resulting in a cys30071-to-arg (C30071R) substitution. The variant segregated with the phenotype and lay within a haplotype on chromosome 2 that the families shared. ⚠︎

Pfeffer et al. (2014) found that heterozygous mutations in the TTN gene cause HMERF. The C30071R mutation lies in the 119th fibronectin type III domain, in the A-band of titin.

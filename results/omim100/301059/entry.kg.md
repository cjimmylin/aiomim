### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that X-linked spermatogenic failure-3 (SPGFX3) is caused by hemizygous mutation in the CFAP47 gene (301057) on chromosome Xp21.1.

### Description

X-linked spermatogenic failure-3 (SPGFX3) causes primary male infertility. Affected men have asthenoteratozoospermia, with multiple morphological abnormalities of the sperm flagella (MMAF) (Liu et al., 2021).

### Clinical Features

Liu et al. (2021) reported 3 unrelated Chinese men who had primary infertility caused by asthenoteratozoospermia with multiple morphological abnormalities of the flagella (MMAF). They also reported an Australian man with severe asthenoteratozoospermia. The authors stated that all men who carried hemizygous CFAP47 variants had typical MMAF phenotypes.

Liao et al. (2022) screened 7 men with MMAF for gene mutations. They identified CFAP47 variants in one of them.

### Inheritance

Liu et al. (2021) found hemizygous variants in the X-linked CFAP47 gene in affected men. They concluded that hemizygous CFAP47 mutations cause X-linked MMAF and asthenoteratozoospermia, which indicates X-linked recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Liu et al. (2021) performed whole-exome sequencing in 3 unrelated Chinese men with MMAF. Each man carried a hemizygous missense variant in the X-linked CFAP47 gene (301057). None of the 3 variants was found in human control population genome databases, and multiple bioinformatic tools predicted all 3 to be deleterious. Immunoblotting and immunofluorescence showed obviously reduced CFAP47 levels in spermatozoa from all 3 men. CFAP47 encodes a cilia- and flagella-associated protein that is highly expressed in testis.

Liu et al. (2021) also analyzed whole-exome sequencing data from an additional Australian cohort of men with severe asthenoteratozoospermia. In 1 man they identified a hemizygous deletion at Xp21.1 that removed the entire CFAP47 gene.

Liao et al. (2022) screened 7 men with MMAF and found 2 novel variants in the CFAP47 gene in one of them: c.706G-A (V236M) and c.1337C-T (T446M). Immunoblotting and immunofluorescence showed that CFAP47 was dramatically reduced in this man's sperm. WDR87 expression was also significantly reduced, with weak signals next to the sperm nuclei. The authors concluded that CFAP47 is needed to transport WDR87 during formation of the sperm flagellum.

### Pathogenesis

Liao et al. (2022) found that WDR87 was highly expressed in human and mouse testis. In mice, WDR87 was located in the cytoplasm around the nucleus of spermatocytes and round spermatids. It moved to the manchette during spermiogenesis and ended up in the middle piece of the sperm tail. The authors identified CFAP47 as a protein that interacts with WDR87 in the sperm flagellar midpiece. They concluded that the 2 proteins form a complex involved in assembling the sperm tail.

### Clinical Management

Based on their findings in humans and mice, Liu et al. (2021) suggested that men with hemizygous CFAP47 mutations have a good prognosis with intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI).

### Animal Model

Liu et al. (2021) generated a Cfap47-mutated mouse model. Adult male mutant mice were sterile, with reduced sperm motility and abnormal flagellar morphology and movement. Their fertility could be rescued by ICSI.

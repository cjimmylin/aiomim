### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Loeys-Dietz syndrome-5 (LDS5), also known as Rienhoff syndrome, is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding transforming growth factor-beta-3 (TGFB3; 190230) on chromosome 14q24. ✗

### Description

Loeys-Dietz syndrome-5 is an autosomal dominant connective tissue disorder. Its features overlap with those of Marfan syndrome and other forms of Loeys-Dietz syndrome. They include skeletal overgrowth or other skeletal features, joint laxity or contractures, craniofacial features such as hypertelorism and cleft palate or bifid uvula, and in some patients aortic or other arterial aneurysm and dissection. Like the other forms of Loeys-Dietz syndrome, it is caused by a defect in the TGF-beta signaling pathway; in this form, the mutated gene encodes the ligand TGFB3. ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

The alternative name Rienhoff syndrome comes from Rienhoff et al. (2013), who first described the association of a TGFB3 mutation with this phenotype. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Rienhoff et al. (2013) described a girl whose syndrome combined low muscle mass, growth retardation, and distal arthrogryposis with clinical features overlapping those of Marfan syndrome and Loeys-Dietz syndrome. These features included hypertelorism, a cleft or bifid uvula/palatal abnormality, and skeletal findings.

Bertoli-Avella et al. (2015) reported a series of patients from several families with heterozygous TGFB3 mutations. The patients had syndromic thoracic aortic aneurysm and dissection, and in some cases abdominal aortic or other arterial aneurysms. Other features included skeletal overgrowth, joint hypermobility or contractures, hypertelorism, bifid uvula or cleft palate, and other features of Loeys-Dietz syndrome. Vascular involvement varied within and between families. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Transmission in the families reported by Bertoli-Avella et al. (2015) was consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. The mutation in the patient described by Rienhoff et al. (2013) arose de novo.

### Molecular Genetics

Rienhoff et al. (2013) found a de novo heterozygous missense mutation in the TGFB3 gene in a girl with the syndrome.

Bertoli-Avella et al. (2015) found heterozygous mutations in the TGFB3 gene in patients from multiple families with syndromic aortic aneurysm and dissection. The mutations segregated with the disorder in the families. As in other forms of Loeys-Dietz syndrome, aortic tissue from patients showed evidence of paradoxically increased TGF-beta signaling, even though the mutations were predicted to cause loss of function. ⚠︎

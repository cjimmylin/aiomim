### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that nephrotic syndrome type 23 (NPHS23) is caused by homozygous mutation in the KIRREL1 gene (607428) on chromosome 1q23.

### Description

Nephrotic syndrome type 23 (NPHS23) is an autosomal recessive kidney disorder. It presents with proteinuria and steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome. Some patients progress to end-stage renal disease. Renal biopsy may show focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS) or other glomerular changes. KIRREL1 (NEPH1) is a component of the podocyte slit diaphragm. ✗

### Clinical Features

Solanki et al. (2019) reported patients from several unrelated families who had steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome (SRNS) beginning in childhood. Renal biopsies showed glomerular lesions, including FSGS. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of NPHS23 in the families reported by Solanki et al. (2019) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Solanki et al. (2019) found homozygous missense mutations in the KIRREL1 gene in patients with SRNS from unrelated families. In functional studies, the mutant KIRREL1 proteins localized abnormally and were not delivered properly to the podocyte cell membrane. The mutations also impaired the protein's interaction with other slit diaphragm components, such as nephrin. ✗

### Animal Model

Donoviel et al. (2001) found that mice lacking Neph1 (Kirrel1) developed proteinuria, had podocyte foot process effacement and died early in life. This showed that Neph1 is needed for the glomerular filtration barrier to work normally. ✗

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that nephrotic syndrome type 23 (NPHS23) is caused by homozygous mutation in the KIRREL1 gene (607428) on chromosome 1q23.1.

### Description

Nephrotic syndrome type 23 (NPHS23) is an autosomal recessive kidney disorder that presents with proteinuria and steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome (Solanki et al., 2019). Renal biopsy may show focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS) or other glomerular changes (Solanki et al., 2019). KIRREL1 (NEPH1) localizes to the glomerular slit diaphragm (Liu et al., 2003).

### Clinical Features

Solanki et al. (2019) reported 2 patients from 2 unrelated consanguineous families who had steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome (SRNS) beginning in childhood. Renal biopsies showed glomerular lesions, including FSGS.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of NPHS23 in the families reported by Solanki et al. (2019) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

In 2 patients with SRNS from unrelated consanguineous families, Solanki et al. (2019) identified homozygous missense mutations in the KIRREL1 gene. In functional studies, both mutant KIRREL1 proteins were mislocalized compared with wildtype and failed to localize to the podocyte cell membrane, indicating defective trafficking.

### Animal Model

Liu et al. (2003) noted that Neph1-deficient mice develop nephrotic syndrome at birth, indicating the importance of this protein in the development of a normal glomerular filtration barrier.

Solanki et al. (2019) noted that deletion of Kirrel in a mouse model results in podocyte foot process effacement, causing loss of glomerular filtration function.

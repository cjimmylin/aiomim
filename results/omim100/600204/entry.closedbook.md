### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that multiple epiphyseal dysplasia-2 (EDM2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the gene encoding the alpha-2 chain of type IX collagen (COL9A2; 120260) on chromosome 1p33-p32. ⚠︎

### Description

Multiple epiphyseal dysplasia (MED) is an autosomal dominant osteochondrodysplasia. Its features are delayed and irregular ossification of the epiphyses, joint pain and stiffness starting in childhood, mild short stature or normal height, and early-onset osteoarthritis. In the COL9A2-related form (EDM2), the knees tend to be the most severely affected joints, while the hips are relatively spared. ✗

### Inheritance

EDM2 is inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern, as shown by the multigenerational family used for the linkage study of Briggs et al. (1994). ⚠︎

### Mapping

Briggs et al. (1994) studied a large family with MED that did not show linkage to the chromosome 19 locus (EDM1). By linkage analysis, they mapped the disease locus to a region of chromosome 1p that contains COL9A2, the gene encoding the alpha-2 chain of type IX collagen.

### Heterogeneity

MED is genetically heterogeneous. Other autosomal dominant forms are caused by mutations in COMP (EDM1), COL9A3 (EDM3), MATN3 (EDM5), and COL9A1 (EDM6). An autosomal recessive form (EDM4) is caused by mutations in SLC26A2. ✗

### Molecular Genetics

Muragaki et al. (1996) studied the family originally reported by Briggs et al. (1994). They found that affected members were heterozygous for a splice site mutation in the COL9A2 gene. The mutation caused exon 3 to be skipped from the mRNA, which deletes 12 amino acids from the COL3 domain of the alpha-2(IX) chain while keeping the reading frame intact. This was the first report showing that a type IX collagen gene causes human disease. ✗

Later studies of other families with MED also found heterozygous COL9A2 mutations that affect the exon 3 splice donor site and cause exon 3 to be skipped. This suggests that in-frame loss of exon 3 is a recurrent disease mechanism. ⚠︎

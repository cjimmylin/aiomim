### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that multiple epiphyseal dysplasia-2 (EDM2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the COL9A2 gene (120260) on chromosome 1p34.2. ⚠︎

### Description

Multiple epiphyseal dysplasia (MED) is a dominantly inherited chondrodysplasia characterized by mild short stature and early-onset osteoarthrosis; some forms clinically resemble mild pseudoachondroplasia (Briggs et al., 1994). EDM2 is a form of MED caused by mutation in the gene encoding the alpha-2 chain of type IX collagen, and has been described as a subtype in which the hips are not markedly affected at an early age (Fiedler et al., 2002).

### Clinical Features

Versteylen et al. (1988) described 2 families that appeared to contain examples of multiple epiphyseal dysplasia. In both families there was a striking incidence of severe osteochondritis dissecans of the knees. The authors suggested that the osteochondritis was caused by the fragmented and maldeveloped epiphyses characteristic of epiphyseal dysplasia.

Fiedler et al. (2002) reported a family of 19 individuals over 4 generations in which 12 members were affected with a variant of multiple epiphyseal dysplasia in which the hips were not markedly affected at an early age. Beginning in childhood, the disease led to pain and stiffness of the knees, ankles, elbows, and finger joints. Some adult patients repeatedly suffered from free articular bodies resulting in locking of the joint, and affected individuals were prone to the development of early degenerative joint disease.

### Inheritance

Briggs et al. (1994) studied a large family with multiple epiphyseal dysplasia segregating as an autosomal dominant trait. Fiedler et al. (2002) reported an affected family spanning 4 generations in which the COL9A2 mutation was found in affected members. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Warman et al. (1994) used fluorescence in situ hybridization to map the COL9A2 gene, which encodes the alpha-2 chain of type IX collagen, to human chromosome 1p32.3-p33. Using a single-strand conformational polymorphism within the murine Col9a2 gene, they mapped that gene to mouse chromosome 4.

In a large family with multiple epiphyseal dysplasia, Briggs et al. (1994) excluded linkage to the chromosome 19 interval previously linked to pseudoachondroplasia and a form of MED, and identified an MED locus (EDM2) on the short arm of chromosome 1, in a region containing the COL9A2 gene.

### Heterogeneity

Briggs et al. (1994) showed that MED is genetically heterogeneous: in 1 large family the disorder did not map to the chromosome 19 interval to which another form of MED and pseudoachondroplasia had been linked, but instead mapped to chromosome 1p.

### Molecular Genetics

Muragaki et al. (1996) reported that a mutation in the COL9A2 gene, which encodes the alpha-2 chain of the fibril-associated collagen IX, causes multiple epiphyseal dysplasia (EDM2).

In a 4-generation family with EDM2, Fiedler et al. (2002) screened candidate regions and identified a G-to-C substitution at position -1 in the exon 3/intron 3 splicing region of the COL9A2 gene, which they predicted most probably alters splicing. The substitution was confirmed in 8 clinically affected family members and in 3 presymptomatic young children. Screening was performed by automated sequencing and BsaWI digestion of amplicons. Fiedler et al. (2002) concluded that, together with previous reports, mutations leading to skipping of exon 3 within the COL3 domain of the alpha-2 chain of collagen type IX may be relatively common in patients with this MED subtype in which the hips are not markedly affected at an early age.

### Pathogenesis

Fiedler et al. (2002) found by electron microscopy that the diameter of collagen fibrils from arthroscopically removed free articular bodies of 2 patients with EDM2 was not obviously different from that of normal articular cartilage.

### Diagnosis

Fiedler et al. (2002) suggested that mutation screening of candidate regions may help confirm the diagnosis of EDM2 and support lifestyle advice and genetic counseling.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that multiple epiphyseal dysplasia-2 (EDM2) is caused by mutation in the gene encoding the alpha-2 chain of type IX collagen (COL9A2; 120260) on chromosome 1p34.2.

### Description

Multiple epiphyseal dysplasia (MED) is a dominantly inherited chondrodysplasia with mild short stature and early-onset osteoarthrosis (Briggs et al. (1994)). Fragmented and maldeveloped epiphyses are characteristic (Versteylen et al. (1988)). Pain and stiffness of the knees, ankles, elbows and finger joints begin in childhood, and affected individuals are prone to early degenerative joint disease (Fiedler et al. (2002)). In EDM2, the hips are not markedly affected at an early age (Fiedler et al. (2002)). ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Briggs et al. (1994) described multiple epiphyseal dysplasia as a dominantly inherited chondrodysplasia.

### Mapping

Briggs et al. (1994) studied a large family with MED. Linkage studies with chromosome 19 markers excluded the previously identified chromosome 19 interval (EDM1) in this family. Using this family, they identified an MED locus on the short arm of chromosome 1, in a region containing COL9A2, the gene encoding the alpha-2 chain of type IX collagen.

Warman et al. (1994) used fluorescence in situ hybridization to map the COL9A2 gene to chromosome 1p32.3-p33.

### Heterogeneity

Multiple epiphyseal dysplasia is genetically heterogeneous. A form of MED had been linked to chromosome 19, but Briggs et al. (1994) excluded that interval in their family and identified a second MED locus on chromosome 1p. Nakashima et al. (2005) discussed MED associated with mutation in the DTDST (SLC26A2) gene.

### Molecular Genetics

Muragaki et al. (1996) reported that a mutation in the COL9A2 gene, which encodes the alpha-2 chain of the fibril-associated collagen IX, causes multiple epiphyseal dysplasia (EDM2).

In a family with MED, Fiedler et al. (2002) screened candidate regions for mutations. They found a novel G-to-C point mutation at position -1 in the exon 3/intron 3 splicing region of the COL9A2 gene, which they thought most probably alters splicing. Taking their results together with previous reports, Fiedler et al. (2002) concluded that mutations leading to skipping of exon 3 within the COL3 domain of the alpha-2 chain of type IX collagen may be relatively common in patients with EDM2, a subtype of MED in which the hips are not markedly affected at an early age.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that atrial standstill-1 (ATRST1) can be caused by mutation in the SCN5A gene (600163) on chromosome 3p22.2, together with coinherited variants in the regulatory regions of the gene encoding connexin-40 (GJA5; 121013) on chromosome 1q21.2, consistent with digenic inheritance.

### Description

Fazelifar et al. (2005) described atrial standstill as an extremely uncommon arrhythmia that rarely appears to be familial. It has been associated with congenital heart disease, valvular heart disease, conduction disturbances, Brugada syndrome, myocardial infarction, and amyloidosis.

### Clinical Features

Allensworth et al. (1969) reported persistent atrial standstill in a family with myocardial disease.

Harrison and Derrick (1969) reviewed atrial standstill and described 2 new cases, which they called familial and unusual. One of the 2 patients was treated with epicardial pacing.

Williams et al. (1972) reported a familial atrial cardiomyopathy with heart block.

Amat-y-Leon et al. (1974) reported a familial atrial dysrhythmia with atrioventricular block. They studied it with intracellular microelectrode recordings, clinical electrophysiologic studies, and morphologic observations.

Shah et al. (1992) reported familial total atrial standstill.

Groenewegen et al. (2003) clinically evaluated 44 members of a large family with atrial standstill. One deceased and 3 living relatives were unambiguously affected. All other relatives appeared unaffected.

Fazelifar et al. (2005) reported a family in which atrial standstill was associated with syncope, dilated cardiomyopathy, and sudden cardiac death.

Makita et al. (2005) described an 11-year-old boy with an apparently sporadic case of congenital atrial standstill. His surface ECG showed no P waves, and pacing could not capture his right atrium. His father carried the same SCN5A mutation but was in normal sinus rhythm.

### Inheritance

Groenewegen et al. (2003) found that the 3 living patients with atrial standstill in a large family were the only relatives who had coinherited both a rare homozygous connexin-40 (Cx40) genotype and the SCN5A D1275N mutation. They proposed that each genetic change alone had a relatively benign functional effect, but that their combined effect eventually progressed to total atrial standstill.

Makita et al. (2005) found that a boy with atrial standstill carried Cx40 polymorphisms that he had inherited from his asymptomatic mother. His unaffected father carried the same SCN5A L212P mutation as the boy but did not carry the Cx40 polymorphisms. The authors suggested that coinheritance of Cx40 polymorphisms may be a genetic factor that modifies the clinical manifestation of atrial standstill. They considered this consistent with digenic inheritance and incomplete penetrance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Groenewegen et al. (2003) studied a large family with atrial standstill. Candidate gene screening identified a novel mutation in the SCN5A gene, D1275N. The mutation was present in all 3 affected living relatives and in 5 unaffected relatives, and the deceased affected relative was an obligate carrier. SCN5A-D1275N channels showed a small depolarizing shift in activation compared with wildtype channels. In the same family, the authors detected 2 closely linked polymorphisms in regulatory regions of the atrial-specific gap junction gene connexin-40 (Cx40; GJA5), at nucleotides -44 (G-A) and +71 (A-G). Eight relatives were homozygous for both polymorphisms, 3 of whom were affected. This genotype was found in only about 7% of controls. In reporter gene analysis, the rare Cx40 genotype gave reduced expression compared with the more common genotype. The 3 living affected relatives were the only family members who carried both the SCN5A mutation and the rare homozygous Cx40 genotype.

In a boy with congenital atrial standstill, Makita et al. (2005) identified a novel SCN5A mutation, L212P, inherited from his father, who was in normal sinus rhythm. The L212P sodium channel was expressed in a heterologous system and studied by whole-cell patch clamp. It showed large hyperpolarizing shifts in the voltage dependence of activation (-63.5 vs -48.1 mV) and of inactivation (-95.6 vs -86.6 mV), as well as delayed recovery from inactivation. The proband, but not his father, also carried Cx40 polymorphisms inherited from his asymptomatic mother.

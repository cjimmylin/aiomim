### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that atrial standstill-1 (ATRST1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the SCN5A gene (600163) on chromosome 3p22.2, coinherited in affected individuals with polymorphisms in the regulatory regions of the GJA5 gene (121013) on chromosome 1q21.2, consistent with digenic inheritance. ⚠︎

### Description

Atrial standstill is a cardiac arrhythmia in which P waves are absent on the surface electrocardiogram and the atria cannot be captured by pacing. Fazelifar et al. (2005) noted that atrial standstill is an extremely uncommon arrhythmia that rarely appears to be familial and genetically determined. It has been associated with conditions including congenital heart disease, valvular heart disease, conduction disturbances, Brugada syndrome, myocardial infarction, and amyloidosis. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Allensworth et al. (1969) described persistent atrial standstill in a family with myocardial disease.

Harrison and Derrick (1969) reviewed atrial standstill and reported 2 new cases that they described as familial and unusual.

Williams et al. (1972) described a familial atrial cardiomyopathy with heart block.

Amat-y-Leon et al. (1974) reported familial atrial dysrhythmia with A-V block. They studied the disorder using intracellular microelectrode recordings, clinical electrophysiologic studies, and morphologic examination.

Shah et al. (1992) reported familial total atrial standstill.

Groenewegen et al. (2003) clinically evaluated 44 members of a large family with atrial standstill. One deceased and 3 living relatives were unambiguously affected. All other relatives appeared unaffected.

Fazelifar et al. (2005) reported a family in which atrial standstill was associated with syncope, dilated cardiomyopathy, and sudden cardiac death.

Makita et al. (2005) studied a family with an apparently sporadic case of congenital atrial standstill. The proband was an 11-year-old boy who had no P waves on the surface ECG, and his right atrium could not be captured by pacing. His father, who carried the same SCN5A mutation, was in normal sinus rhythm. His mother was asymptomatic.

### Inheritance

Groenewegen et al. (2003) proposed digenic inheritance of atrial standstill. In a large family, the 3 living affected members were the only relatives who carried both an SCN5A mutation and a rare homozygous GJA5 (connexin-40) genotype. Makita et al. (2005) studied a boy with atrial standstill who inherited an SCN5A mutation from his unaffected father and GJA5 polymorphisms from his asymptomatic mother. These findings also supported a digenic mechanism. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Makita et al. (2005) suggested that coinheritance of polymorphisms in the atrial-specific gap junction gene connexin-40 (GJA5) may modify the clinical expression of SCN5A-related atrial standstill. The proband carried both the SCN5A L212P mutation and the Cx40 polymorphisms, whereas his father, who carried only the SCN5A mutation, was unaffected.

### Molecular Genetics

Groenewegen et al. (2003) studied a large family with atrial standstill and screened candidate genes. They identified a novel mutation in the SCN5A gene, asp1275-to-asn (D1275N), in all 3 affected living relatives and in 5 unaffected relatives. The deceased affected relative was an obligate carrier. SCN5A-D1275N channels showed a small depolarizing shift in activation compared with wildtype channels. In the same family, the authors found 2 closely linked polymorphisms in regulatory regions of the gene encoding connexin-40 (Cx40; GJA5), a gap junction protein specific to the atria. The polymorphisms were at nucleotides -44 (G-A) and +71 (A-G). Eight relatives were homozygous for both polymorphisms, a genotype found in only about 7% of control subjects, and 3 of these 8 were affected. In reporter gene analysis, the rare Cx40 genotype gave lower expression than the more common genotype. The 3 living patients were the only relatives who inherited both the rare Cx40 genotype and the SCN5A D1275N mutation. Groenewegen et al. (2003) proposed that each genetic change on its own has a relatively benign functional effect, but that together they eventually progress to total atrial standstill.

Makita et al. (2005) identified a novel SCN5A mutation, leu212-to-pro (L212P), in a boy with congenital atrial standstill. His father also carried the mutation but was in normal sinus rhythm, indicating incomplete penetrance. The authors expressed the recombinant L212P channel in a heterologous system and studied it by whole-cell patch clamp. The mutant channel showed a large hyperpolarizing shift in the voltage dependence of activation (-63.5 vs -48.1 mV for wildtype) and of inactivation (-95.6 vs -86.6 mV), and delayed recovery from inactivation. The proband also carried Cx40 (GJA5) polymorphisms inherited from his asymptomatic mother; his unaffected father did not carry them. Makita et al. (2005) concluded that genetic defects in SCN5A most likely underlie atrial standstill. They suggested that coinheritance of Cx40 polymorphisms may be a genetic factor that modifies the clinical expression of SCN5A-related atrial standstill.

### Clinical Management

Harrison and Derrick (1969) treated 1 of their 2 patients with familial atrial standstill with epicardial pacing.

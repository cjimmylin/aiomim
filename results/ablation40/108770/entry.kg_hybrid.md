### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that atrial standstill-1 (ATRST1) is caused by mutation in the SCN5A gene (600163) on chromosome 3p22.2. In 1 family, the disorder was associated with an SCN5A mutation coinherited with homozygosity for rare regulatory polymorphisms in the GJA5 gene (121013) on chromosome 1q21.2, consistent with digenic inheritance. ⚠︎

### Description

Atrial standstill is a very rare arrhythmia that only occasionally appears to be familial and genetically determined (Groenewegen et al., 2003; Fazelifar et al., 2005). Fazelifar et al. (2005) noted that atrial standstill has been associated with several conditions, including congenital heart disease, valvular heart disease, conduction disturbances, Brugada syndrome, myocardial infarction, and amyloidosis.

### Clinical Features

Allensworth et al. (1969) described persistent atrial standstill in a family with myocardial disease.

Harrison and Derrick (1969) reviewed atrial standstill and reported 2 new familial cases, which they described as unusual.

Williams et al. (1972) described familial atrial cardiomyopathy with heart block.

Amat-y-Leon et al. (1974) reported familial atrial dysrhythmia with A-V block. They used intracellular microelectrode recordings, clinical electrophysiologic studies, and morphologic examination.

Shah et al. (1992) reported familial total atrial standstill.

Groenewegen et al. (2003) clinically evaluated 44 members of a large family with atrial standstill. One deceased and 3 living relatives were unambiguously affected; all other relatives appeared unaffected.

Fazelifar et al. (2005) reported a family in which atrial standstill was associated with syncope, dilated cardiomyopathy, and sudden cardiac death.

Makita et al. (2005) studied the family of an apparently sporadic case of congenital atrial standstill. The 11-year-old proband had no P waves on surface ECG, and his right atrium could not be captured by pacing. His father was in normal sinus rhythm, and his mother was asymptomatic.

### Inheritance

Several reports described familial occurrence of atrial standstill (Allensworth et al., 1969; Harrison and Derrick, 1969; Shah et al., 1992; Fazelifar et al., 2005).

In a large family with atrial standstill, Groenewegen et al. (2003) found that the 3 living affected members, and only they, had both an SCN5A mutation and a rare homozygous genotype at the connexin-40 (GJA5) gene. These findings suggested digenic inheritance.

Makita et al. (2005) observed incomplete penetrance of an SCN5A mutation: an unaffected father carried the same mutation as his affected son. They noted that incomplete penetrance in atrial standstill had been attributed in part to digenic inheritance of GJA5 polymorphisms together with an SCN5A mutation.

### Molecular Genetics

In a large family with atrial standstill, Groenewegen et al. (2003) used candidate gene screening to identify a novel mutation in the SCN5A gene, asp1275-to-asn (D1275N). The mutation was present in all 3 living affected relatives and in 5 unaffected relatives, and the deceased affected relative was an obligate carrier. Compared with wildtype channels, D1275N channels showed a small depolarizing shift in activation. Groenewegen et al. (2003) also found 2 closely linked polymorphisms in regulatory regions of the atrial-specific gap junction gene connexin-40 (GJA5), at nucleotides -44 (G-A) and +71 (A-G). Eight relatives were homozygous for both polymorphisms, and 3 of them were affected. This genotype was found in only about 7% of control subjects. In reporter gene analysis, the rare GJA5 genotype gave lower reporter expression than the more common genotype. The 3 living patients were the only relatives to have inherited both the rare GJA5 genotype and the SCN5A D1275N mutation. The authors proposed that each genetic change has a relatively benign functional effect, but that their combined effect eventually progresses to total atrial standstill.

In an 11-year-old boy with congenital atrial standstill, Makita et al. (2005) identified a novel SCN5A mutation, leu212-to-pro (L212P). His father, who was in normal sinus rhythm, also carried the mutation. The recombinant L212P channel was studied by whole-cell patch clamp in a heterologous expression system. It showed a large hyperpolarizing shift in the voltage dependence of activation (wildtype, -48.1 +/- 0.9 mV; L212P, -63.5 +/- 1.5 mV; P less than 0.001) and of inactivation (wildtype, -86.6 +/- 0.9 mV; L212P, -95.6 +/- 0.8 mV; P less than 0.001), as well as delayed recovery from inactivation. The proband, but not his father, also carried GJA5 polymorphisms inherited from his asymptomatic mother. Makita et al. (2005) concluded that SCN5A defects most likely underlie atrial standstill and that coinheritance of GJA5 polymorphisms may modify the clinical manifestation.

### Clinical Management

Harrison and Derrick (1969) reported epicardial pacing in 1 of their 2 familial cases of atrial standstill.

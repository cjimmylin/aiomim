### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that atrial standstill-1 (ATRST1) is caused by mutation in the SCN5A gene (600163) on chromosome 3p22.2, in combination with rare polymorphisms in the GJA5 gene (121013) on chromosome 1q21.2. ⚠︎

### Description

Atrial standstill is a rare arrhythmia that occasionally appears to be genetically determined (Groenewegen et al., 2003). Fazelifar et al. (2005) described it as an extremely uncommon arrhythmia that is rarely familial. They noted that it has been associated with several conditions, including congenital heart disease, valvular heart disease, conduction disturbances, Brugada syndrome, myocardial infarction, and amyloidosis.

### Clinical Features

Allensworth et al. (1969) reported persistent atrial standstill in a family with myocardial disease.

Harrison and Derrick (1969) presented 2 new familial cases of atrial standstill, together with a review of the condition.

Williams et al. (1972) described familial atrial cardiomyopathy with heart block.

Amat-y-Leon et al. (1974) reported familial atrial dysrhythmia with atrioventricular block. Their report included intracellular microelectrode, clinical electrophysiologic, and morphologic observations.

Shah et al. (1992) reported familial total atrial standstill.

Groenewegen et al. (2003) clinically evaluated 44 members of a large family with atrial standstill. One deceased and 3 living relatives were unambiguously affected, and all other relatives appeared unaffected.

Fazelifar et al. (2005) reported a family in which atrial standstill was associated with syncope, dilated cardiomyopathy, and sudden cardiac death.

Makita et al. (2005) studied the family of an apparently sporadic case of atrial standstill. The proband, an 11-year-old boy, had no P waves on the surface ECG, and pacing could not capture his right atrium. His father was in normal sinus rhythm, and his mother was asymptomatic.

### Inheritance

Groenewegen et al. (2003) found that, in a large family with atrial standstill, the affected members had coinherited an SCN5A mutation and a rare homozygous GJA5 (connexin-40) genotype. Unaffected relatives carried the SCN5A mutation alone or the GJA5 genotype alone. The authors proposed that the combined effect of these genetic changes eventually progresses to total atrial standstill, which suggests digenic inheritance. ⚠︎

Makita et al. (2005) found an SCN5A mutation in both an affected proband and his father, who was in sinus rhythm. Only the proband also carried connexin-40 polymorphisms, which he had inherited from his asymptomatic mother. Makita et al. (2005) concluded that SCN5A defects most likely underlie atrial standstill. They suggested that coinherited connexin-40 polymorphisms may modify its clinical manifestation, which could account for incomplete penetrance.

### Molecular Genetics

Groenewegen et al. (2003) screened candidate genes in a large family with atrial standstill. They identified a novel asp1275-to-asn (D1275N) mutation in the SCN5A gene in all 3 affected living relatives and in 5 unaffected relatives; the deceased affected relative was an obligate carrier. They also found 2 closely linked polymorphisms in regulatory regions of the gene for the atrial-specific gap junction protein connexin-40 (GJA5), at nucleotides -44 (G-A) and +71 (A-G). Eight relatives were homozygous for both polymorphisms, and 3 of these were affected. The 3 living patients were the only relatives who had coinherited both the rare connexin-40 genotype and the SCN5A D1275N mutation. In functional studies, D1275N channels showed a small depolarizing shift in activation compared with wildtype channels. In reporter gene analysis, the rare connexin-40 genotype reduced expression compared with the more common genotype. Homozygosity for both polymorphisms occurred in only about 7% of control subjects. The authors proposed that the functional effect of each genetic change alone is relatively benign, but that their combined effect eventually progresses to total atrial standstill.

Makita et al. (2005) screened SCN5A and atrial-specific genes, including GJA5 (connexin-40), in the family of an 11-year-old boy with atrial standstill. They identified a novel leu212-to-pro (L212P) mutation in SCN5A in the proband and in his father, who was in normal sinus rhythm. In whole-cell patch-clamp studies, the recombinant L212P sodium channel showed a large hyperpolarizing shift in the voltage dependence of activation (wildtype, -48.1 +/- 0.9 mV; L212P, -63.5 +/- 1.5 mV) and of inactivation (wildtype, -86.6 +/- 0.9 mV; L212P, -95.6 +/- 0.8 mV). It also showed delayed recovery from inactivation. The proband, but not his father, also carried connexin-40 polymorphisms inherited from his asymptomatic mother.

### Clinical Management

Harrison and Derrick (1969) used epicardial pacing in 1 of 2 familial cases of atrial standstill.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that cleft lip/palate-ectodermal dysplasia syndrome (CLPED1) is caused by homozygous mutation in the NECTIN1 gene (PVRL1; 600644). Heterozygosity for a NECTIN1 mutation has been associated with sporadic nonsyndromic cleft lip with or without cleft palate (OFC7) in northern Venezuela.

### Description

Cleft lip/palate-ectodermal dysplasia syndrome is a rare autosomal recessive disorder caused by homozygous loss-of-function mutations in the PVRL1 gene, which encodes nectin-1 (Yoshida et al., 2015). Clinical features include unusual facies, dental anomalies, hypotrichosis, palmoplantar hyperkeratosis and onychodysplasia, syndactyly, and cleft lip/cleft palate (Suzuki et al., 1998). Some patients have mental retardation (Yoshida et al., 2015).

### Nomenclature

Suzuki et al. (1998) referred to the disorder as Margarita Island ectodermal dysplasia (ED4). Suzuki et al. (2000) designated the syndrome CLPED1, previously called ED4. Rodini and Richieri-Costa (1990) used the designation Zlotogora-Ogur syndrome.

### Clinical Features

Zlotogora et al. (1987) reported 2 sibs with a syndrome that included cleft lip and palate, sparse scalp hair, malformed protruding ears, and partial syndactyly of the fingers and toes. The older child also had mental retardation and pili torti.

Oğur and Yüksel (1988) reported 2 Turkish brothers, the offspring of a second-cousin marriage, who had tetramelic syndactyly, ectodermal dysplasia, cleft lip and palate, renal anomalies, and mental retardation.

Rodini and Richieri-Costa (1990) reported 3 Brazilian brothers, born to normal consanguineous parents (F = 1/16), who had ectodermal dysplasia, cleft lip/palate, mental retardation, syndactyly of fingers 2-3, accessory nipples, and ear anomalies. The authors discussed the similarities of these brothers to previously reported cases.

Bustos et al. (1991) described 27 individuals from 7 families, related to each other with high probability, from a relatively isolated and inbred population with very small neighboring communities on Margarita Island in northeastern Venezuela (Nueva Esparta State). Affected individuals showed manifestations of ectodermal dysplasia and other anomalies with variable expressivity, and females were affected as severely as males. All parents were normal. The authors stated that the clinical picture common to all patients could not be classified within the known ectodermal dysplasias.

Suzuki et al. (1998) characterized Margarita Island ectodermal dysplasia by unusual facies, dental anomalies, hypotrichosis, palmoplantar hyperkeratosis and onychodysplasia, syndactyly, and cleft lip/cleft palate.

Yoshida et al. (2015) reported a 7-year-old Japanese boy, the first child of a consanguineous marriage, with hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia with sparse, brittle, fine, dry hair and hypodontia, a unique facial appearance with cleft lip/palate, cutaneous syndactyly of the fingers, and mild mental retardation. Scanning electron microscopic examination of the hair demonstrated pili torti and pili trianguli et canaliculi. The authors stated that this was the first report of an Asian individual with the syndrome.

### Inheritance

Zlotogora et al. (1987) concluded that the syndrome in their 2 sibs was most probably inherited as an autosomal recessive disorder. Oğur and Yüksel (1988) reported affected brothers born to second-cousin parents, and Rodini and Richieri-Costa (1990) reported 3 affected brothers born to normal consanguineous parents, discussing the pattern of inheritance as autosomal recessive. Bustos et al. (1991) concluded that the disorder in the Margarita Island families, in which all parents were normal and females were affected as severely as males, was a newly recognized autosomal recessive syndrome. Suzuki et al. (2000) described CLPED1 as autosomal recessive, and Yoshida et al. (2015) found homozygosity for a PVRL1 mutation in an affected child whose parents were heterozygous.

### Mapping

Using an affected-only DNA-pooling strategy, Suzuki et al. (1998) carried out linkage disequilibrium mapping of the ED4 gene to 11q23. Haplotype analysis of 4 complex Margarita Island ED4 families localized the gene to an approximately 1- to 2-Mb interval spanned by just 2 YACs.

### Molecular Genetics

By positional cloning, Suzuki et al. (2000) identified PVRL1, encoding nectin-1, an immunoglobulin-related transmembrane cell-cell adhesion molecule that is part of the NAP cell adhesion system, as the gene responsible for autosomal recessive CLPED1.

Sözen et al. (2001) noted that homozygosity for a nonsense mutation of PVRL1, W185X, results in CLPED1 on Margarita Island. They demonstrated a highly significant association between heterozygosity for this mutation and sporadic, nonsyndromic cleft lip with or without cleft palate in northern Venezuela.

In a 7-year-old Japanese boy with CLPED1 born to consanguineous parents, Yoshida et al. (2015) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation (R134X) in exon 2 of the PVRL1 gene. His parents were heterozygous for the mutation. The authors noted that all 4 PVRL1 mutations identified in the syndrome to that time resulted in truncated proteins lacking the transmembrane and intracellular domains of nectin-1, which are necessary to initiate the cell-cell adhesion process.

### Population Genetics

Suzuki et al. (2000) noted the high frequency of CLPED1 on Margarita Island in the Caribbean Sea. Because nectin-1 is the principal cell surface receptor for alpha-herpesviruses (HveC), they suggested that the high frequency might result from resistance of heterozygotes to infection by these viruses.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CLEFT LIP/PALATE-ECTODERMAL DYSPLASIA SYNDROME; OROFACIAL CLEFT 7, SUSCEPTIBILITY TO**  
NECTIN1, TRP185TER [p.W185*; W185X]

Sözen et al. (2001) stated that homozygosity for a trp185-to-ter (W185X) nonsense mutation in the PVRL1 gene results in the autosomal recessive cleft lip/palate syndrome CLPED1 on Margarita Island. They demonstrated a highly significant association between heterozygosity for this mutation and sporadic, nonsyndromic cleft lip with or without cleft palate in northern Venezuela.

**.0002 CLEFT LIP/PALATE-ECTODERMAL DYSPLASIA SYNDROME**  
NECTIN1, ARG134TER [c.400C>T; p.R134*; p.Arg134*]

In a 7-year-old Japanese boy, born of consanguineous parents, with CLPED1, Yoshida et al. (2015) identified a homozygous c.400C-T transition in exon 2 of the PVRL1 gene, resulting in an arg134-to-ter (R134X) substitution. His parents were heterozygous for the mutation. The mutation was predicted to result in a truncated protein lacking the transmembrane and intracellular domains of nectin-1.

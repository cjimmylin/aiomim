### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that cleft lip/palate-ectodermal dysplasia syndrome (CLPED1) is caused by homozygous mutation in the NECTIN1 gene (600644) on chromosome 11q23.3. Heterozygosity for a NECTIN1 nonsense mutation has been associated with sporadic nonsyndromic cleft lip with or without cleft palate (orofacial cleft-7; OFC7), which is included here.

### Description

Cleft lip/palate-ectodermal dysplasia syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder (Suzuki et al., 2000). Suzuki et al. (1998) described its features as unusual facies, dental anomalies, hypotrichosis, palmoplantar hyperkeratosis and onychodysplasia, syndactyly, and cleft lip/cleft palate.

### Nomenclature

Suzuki et al. (2000) noted that CLPED1 had previously been designated ED4. Rodini and Richieri-Costa (1990) called the disorder autosomal recessive ectodermal dysplasia, cleft lip/palate, mental retardation, and syndactyly, or Zlotogora-Ogur syndrome.

### Clinical Features

Zlotogora et al. (1987) reported 2 sibs with a syndrome that included cleft lip and palate, sparse scalp hair, malformed protruding ears, and partial syndactyly of the fingers and toes. The older child also had mental retardation and pili torti.

Oğur and Yüksel (1988) reported 2 Turkish brothers, the children of a second-cousin marriage. Both had tetramelic syndactyly, ectodermal dysplasia, cleft lip and palate, renal anomalies, and mental retardation. The authors discussed how these brothers resembled previously reported cases.

Zlotogora and Ogur (1988) described an association of syndactyly, ectodermal dysplasia, and cleft lip and palate. Zlotogora (1994) discussed the same association again.

Rodini and Richieri-Costa (1990) reported 3 Brazilian brothers born to normal consanguineous parents (F = 1/16). The brothers had ectodermal dysplasia, cleft lip/palate, mental retardation, syndactyly of fingers 2-3, accessory nipples, and ear anomalies.

Bustos et al. (1991) described 27 individuals from 7 families on Margarita Island in northeastern Venezuela (Nueva Esparta State). The families were probably related to each other. The patients had ectodermal dysplasia and other anomalies, and females were affected as severely as males. Expressivity was variable. All parents were normal. The authors reported that the clinical picture shared by all patients did not fit any of the known ectodermal dysplasias and did not resemble previously published cases.

Suzuki et al. (1998) described Margarita Island ectodermal dysplasia (ED4) as an autosomal recessive disorder. Its features were unusual facies, dental anomalies, hypotrichosis, palmoplantar hyperkeratosis and onychodysplasia, syndactyly, and cleft lip/cleft palate.

Yoshida et al. (2015) reported a 7-year-old Japanese boy who was the first child of a consanguineous marriage. He had hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia with sparse, brittle, fine, dry hair and hypodontia. He also had a unique facial appearance with cleft lip/palate, cutaneous syndactyly of the fingers, and mild mental retardation. Scanning electron microscopy of his hair showed pili torti and pili trianguli et canaliculi.

### Inheritance

Zlotogora et al. (1987) concluded that the syndrome they observed in 2 sibs was most probably inherited as an autosomal recessive disorder. Rodini and Richieri-Costa (1990) discussed the pattern of inheritance in 3 affected brothers born to normal consanguineous parents and regarded the disorder as autosomal recessive. ⚠︎

Bustos et al. (1991) concluded that the Margarita Island condition was a newly recognized autosomal recessive dysplasia/malformation syndrome. Their reasons were that all parents were normal, females were affected as severely as males, and the families came from an isolated, inbred population. ⚠︎

Yoshida et al. (2015) found a homozygous NECTIN1 mutation in an affected boy born to consanguineous parents, and both parents were heterozygous for it. This finding was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Mapping

Suzuki et al. (1998) used an affected-only DNA-pooling strategy and mapped the ED4 gene to chromosome 11q23 by linkage disequilibrium. Haplotype analysis of 4 complex Margarita Island ED4 families placed the gene in an interval of about 1 to 2 Mb that was spanned by just 2 YACs.

### Molecular Genetics

Suzuki et al. (2000) used positional cloning to identify PVRL1 (NECTIN1), which encodes nectin-1, as the gene responsible for autosomal recessive cleft lip/palate-ectodermal dysplasia syndrome (CLPED1, previously ED4).

Sözen et al. (2001) stated that homozygosity for a NECTIN1 nonsense mutation, W185X, results in the autosomal recessive CL/P syndrome CLPED1 on Margarita Island. They found a highly significant association between heterozygosity for this mutation and sporadic, nonsyndromic cleft lip with or without cleft palate in northern Venezuela.

Yoshida et al. (2015) analyzed exon 2 of NECTIN1 in a 7-year-old Japanese boy with CLPED1 born to consanguineous parents. They found a homozygous nonsense mutation, c.400C-T (R134X), for which both parents were heterozygous. The authors noted that all 4 NECTIN1 mutations identified in this syndrome to that date, including theirs, produced truncated proteins lacking the transmembrane and intracellular domains of nectin-1. These domains are needed to start the cell-cell adhesion process.

### Population Genetics

Bustos et al. (1991) identified affected families in a relatively isolated, inbred population with very small neighboring communities on Margarita Island in northeastern Venezuela (Nueva Esparta State).

Suzuki et al. (2000) noted that CLPED1 is common on Margarita Island in the Caribbean Sea. Nectin-1 is an immunoglobulin-related transmembrane cell-cell adhesion molecule and is also the principal cell surface receptor for alpha-herpesviruses (HveC). On this basis, the authors suggested that the high frequency of CLPED1 might result from resistance of heterozygotes to infection by alpha-herpesviruses.

Sözen et al. (2001) stated that homozygosity for a trp185-to-ter (W185X) nonsense mutation in the NECTIN1 gene results in the autosomal recessive cleft lip/palate syndrome CLPED1 on Margarita Island. They found a highly significant association between heterozygosity for W185X and sporadic, nonsyndromic cleft lip with or without cleft palate in northern Venezuela.

**.0002 CLEFT LIP/PALATE-ECTODERMAL DYSPLASIA SYNDROME**  
NECTIN1, ARG134TER [c.400C>T; p.R134*]

Yoshida et al. (2015) studied a 7-year-old Japanese boy with cleft lip/palate-ectodermal dysplasia syndrome who was born to consanguineous parents. His features included hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia, hypodontia, cleft lip/palate, cutaneous syndactyly of the fingers, mild mental retardation, and pili torti. They found a homozygous c.400C-T transition in exon 2 of the NECTIN1 gene, resulting in an arg134-to-ter (R134X) substitution. Both parents were heterozygous for the mutation. The authors noted that the mutation is predicted to produce a truncated nectin-1 lacking the transmembrane and intracellular domains.

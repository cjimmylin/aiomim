### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that cleft lip/palate-ectodermal dysplasia syndrome (CLPED1) is caused by homozygous mutation in the NECTIN1 gene (PVRL1; 600644) on chromosome 11q23.3. Heterozygosity for a NECTIN1 mutation has been associated with sporadic nonsyndromic cleft lip with or without cleft palate (OFC7).

### Description

Cleft lip/palate-ectodermal dysplasia syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder. Suzuki et al. (1998) described its features as unusual facies, dental anomalies, hypotrichosis, palmoplantar hyperkeratosis, onychodysplasia, syndactyly, and cleft lip/cleft palate. Some patients have also had intellectual disability (Zlotogora et al., 1987; Rodini and Richieri-Costa, 1990; Yoshida et al., 2015).

### Nomenclature

Suzuki et al. (2000) noted that the cleft lip/palate-ectodermal dysplasia syndrome they designated CLPED1 had previously been called ED4. Rodini and Richieri-Costa (1990) used the name Zlotogora-Ogur syndrome for the disorder.

### Clinical Features

Zlotogora et al. (1987) reported 2 sibs with cleft lip and palate, sparse scalp hair, malformed protruding ears, and partial syndactyly of the fingers and toes. The older child also had mental retardation and pili torti.

Oğur and Yüksel (1988) reported 2 Turkish brothers, born to parents who were second cousins, who had syndactyly of all four limbs, ectodermal dysplasia, cleft lip and palate, renal anomalies, and mental retardation. Zlotogora and Ogur (1988) also reported on the association of syndactyly, ectodermal dysplasia, and cleft lip and palate.

Rodini and Richieri-Costa (1990) reported 3 Brazilian brothers, born to normal consanguineous parents (F = 1/16), who had ectodermal dysplasia, cleft lip/palate, mental retardation, syndactyly of fingers 2-3, accessory nipples, and ear anomalies. The authors discussed how these brothers resembled previously reported cases of Zlotogora-Ogur syndrome.

Bustos et al. (1991) described 27 individuals from 7 families, related to each other with high probability, from a relatively isolated and inbred population on Margarita Island in northeastern Venezuela (Nueva Esparta State). The affected individuals had features of ectodermal dysplasia along with other anomalies, with variable expressivity, and females were affected as severely as males. The authors stated that the clinical picture shared by all patients could not be classified among the known ectodermal dysplasias.

Zlotogora (1994) reported further on the association of syndactyly, ectodermal dysplasia, and cleft lip/palate.

Suzuki et al. (1998) described Margarita Island ectodermal dysplasia (ED4) as an autosomal recessive disorder with unusual facies, dental anomalies, hypotrichosis, palmoplantar hyperkeratosis, onychodysplasia, syndactyly, and cleft lip/cleft palate.

Yoshida et al. (2015) reported a 7-year-old Japanese boy, the first child of a consanguineous marriage, with cleft lip/palate-ectodermal dysplasia syndrome. He had hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia with sparse, brittle, fine, dry hair and hypodontia, a unique facial appearance with cleft lip/palate, cutaneous syndactyly of the fingers, and mild mental retardation. Scanning electron microscopy of the hair showed pili torti and pili trianguli et canaliculi. The authors stated that this was the first report of an Asian individual with the syndrome.

### Inheritance

Zlotogora et al. (1987) concluded that the syndrome in the 2 sibs they reported was most probably inherited as an autosomal recessive disorder. Oğur and Yüksel (1988) noted that their 2 affected brothers were the offspring of a second-cousin marriage and discussed the mode of transmission in relation to previously reported cases. Rodini and Richieri-Costa (1990) discussed autosomal recessive inheritance in 3 affected brothers born to normal consanguineous parents.

Bustos et al. (1991) concluded that the disorder in the Margarita Island families was autosomal recessive. In support, all parents of the 27 affected individuals were normal, males and females were affected equally severely, and the families came from an inbred population. Suzuki et al. (2000) described CLPED1 as autosomal recessive. In the patient reported by Yoshida et al. (2015), the mutation was homozygous and both consanguineous parents were heterozygous carriers.

### Mapping

Suzuki et al. (1998) used an affected-only DNA-pooling strategy to map the ED4 gene to chromosome 11q23 by linkage disequilibrium. Haplotype analysis of 4 complex Margarita Island ED4 families narrowed the locus to an interval of about 1 to 2 Mb, spanned by just 2 YACs.

### Molecular Genetics

Suzuki et al. (2000) used positional cloning to identify PVRL1 as the gene responsible for autosomal recessive cleft lip/palate-ectodermal dysplasia syndrome (CLPED1). PVRL1 encodes nectin-1, an immunoglobulin-related transmembrane cell-cell adhesion molecule that is part of the NAP cell adhesion system.

Sözen et al. (2001) stated that homozygosity for the PVRL1 nonsense mutation W185X causes CLPED1 on Margarita Island. They found a highly significant association between heterozygosity for the same mutation and sporadic, nonsyndromic cleft lip with or without cleft palate (OFC7) in northern Venezuela.

In a 7-year-old Japanese boy with CLPED1 born to consanguineous parents, Yoshida et al. (2015) identified a novel homozygous nonsense mutation (c.400C-T; R134X) in exon 2 of the PVRL1 gene. Both parents were heterozygous for the mutation. The authors noted that all 4 PVRL1 mutations identified in the syndrome to that time resulted in truncated proteins that lacked the transmembrane and intracellular domains of nectin-1, which are necessary to initiate cell-cell adhesion.

### Population Genetics

Suzuki et al. (2000) noted that CLPED1 is frequent on Margarita Island in the Caribbean Sea. Because nectin-1 is the main cell surface receptor for alpha-herpesviruses (HveC), they suggested that the high frequency might result from heterozygotes being resistant to infection by these viruses.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CLEFT LIP/PALATE-ECTODERMAL DYSPLASIA SYNDROME**  
NECTIN1, TRP185TER [p.W185*]

Sözen et al. (2001) stated that homozygosity for a trp185-to-ter (W185X) nonsense mutation in the PVRL1 gene causes CLPED1 on Margarita Island. They found a highly significant association between heterozygosity for this mutation and sporadic, nonsyndromic cleft lip with or without cleft palate (OFC7) in northern Venezuela.

**.0002 CLEFT LIP/PALATE-ECTODERMAL DYSPLASIA SYNDROME**  
NECTIN1, ARG134TER [c.400C>T; p.R134*]

In a 7-year-old Japanese boy with CLPED1 born to consanguineous parents, Yoshida et al. (2015) identified a homozygous c.400C-T transition in exon 2 of the PVRL1 gene, resulting in an arg134-to-ter (R134X) substitution. Both parents were heterozygous for the mutation. The predicted truncated protein lacks the transmembrane and intracellular domains of nectin-1, which are necessary to initiate cell-cell adhesion.

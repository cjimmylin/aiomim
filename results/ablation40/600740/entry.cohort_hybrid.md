### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia type III (HHC3) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the AP2S1 gene (602242) on chromosome 19q13.

### Description

Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia type III (FHH3) is an autosomal dominant disorder of extracellular calcium homeostasis. Like FHH1 and FHH2, it is characterized by lifelong elevation of serum calcium with inappropriately low urinary calcium excretion. Nesbit et al. (2013) noted that, unlike FHH1 and FHH2, FHH3 is characterized by increased serum PTH concentrations, hypophosphatemia, and osteomalacia. Howles et al. (2016) described FHH3 as the most clinically severe of the 3 types of familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia.

### Nomenclature

Trump et al. (1995) called the form of familial benign hypercalcemia (FBH) in a kindred from Oklahoma the Oklahoma variant, FBH(Ok). Hannan et al. (2010) stated that the 3 genetically distinct types of familial benign hypocalciuric hypercalcemia had been designated FBHH1, FBHH2, and FBHH3, mapping to 3q21.1, 19p, and 19q13, respectively. They noted that FBHH3 was also called the Oklahoma variant (FBHHOk).

### Clinical Features

McMurtry et al. (1992) studied a large kindred with familial benign (hypocalciuric) hypercalcemia. After the proposita was identified, 46 kindred members spanning 5 generations and aged 1.5 to 91 years were surveyed biochemically and/or by chart review, and 19 were affected. In the 15 hypercalcemic subjects examined biochemically, serum immunoreactive PTH measured in 3 separate immunoassays became supranormal by about age 30 years and appeared to rise further thereafter. Serum alkaline phosphatase activity and creatinine were normal in affected individuals, but serum inorganic phosphate was lower than in unaffected kindred members. Of 5 affected adults older than 40 years who were studied radiographically, 3 had changes suggestive of osteomalacia. In one of 2 hypercalcemic adults who had iliac crest biopsy after tetracycline labeling, a 51-year-old woman, the biopsy confirmed defective skeletal mineralization. The authors concluded that the disorder may not be totally benign, because osteomalacia, perhaps due to mild hypophosphatemia, can develop during adulthood.

Trump et al. (1995) studied a 5-generation kindred from Oklahoma, USA, with 19 affected members, 2 obligate carriers, and 20 unaffected members. In this kindred, familial benign hypocalciuric hypercalcemia was associated with a developmental elevation in serum PTH levels.

Hannan et al. (2010) studied 40 members from 5 generations of the Oklahoma kindred, including 2 newly ascertained affected individuals. The phenotype was defined as hypercalcemia with a calcium clearance to creatinine clearance ratio below 0.01. Elevated serum PTH had been observed in 5 affected individuals older than 30 years. Three adults older than 40 years had evidence of osteomalacia.

Nesbit et al. (2010) reported a kindred whose proband had no CASR mutation in the coding region or splice sites. The proband, a 43-year-old woman, presented with a corrected serum calcium of 2.74 mmol/liter (normal, 2.15-2.55), serum PTH of 47 pg/ml (normal, 10-65), and a urinary calcium clearance to creatinine clearance ratio of 0.006. Twenty-four members from 3 generations of her kindred were studied. Sixteen were hypercalcemic with normal or elevated serum PTH and mild hypophosphatemia, which the authors considered consistent with FHH3.

Nesbit et al. (2013) tabulated the biochemical features of 13 families or patients with AP2S1 mutations, whose ages at diagnosis ranged from 8 days to 78 years. In the Oklahoma kindred (13 affected females and 3 affected males; mean age at diagnosis 36 years), mean serum calcium was 2.88 mmol/l and the mean urinary calcium:creatinine clearance ratio was 0.003.

Fujisawa et al. (2013) reported a Japanese female infant whose hypercalcemia was found on a routine laboratory test done for poor weight gain on breastfeeding. At 49 days of age, her serum calcium (adjusted by Payne's formula) was 13.1 mg/dL, intact PTH was 27 pg/mL, and the urinary calcium-to-creatinine ratio was 1.29 mg/mg. There was no evidence of hyperparathyroidism, a PTHrP-producing neoplasm, or vitamin D excess. Apart from the hypercalciuria, these findings appeared consistent with defective calcium-sensing receptor-mediated signaling.

Howles et al. (2016) reported 3 unrelated probands with FHH3. A 34-year-old woman with a 12-year history of hypercalcemia had fatigue, headaches, and persistent generalized aches that did not resolve after parathyroidectomy. A 22-year-old man presented with hypercalcemia, fatigue, and generalized rib pain. A 52-year-old woman with an approximately 20-year history of hypercalcemia had headaches, abdominal pain, vomiting, fatigue, and musculoskeletal pain that did not resolve after pamidronate infusion or parathyroidectomy.

### Inheritance

Transmission in the Oklahoma kindred studied by Trump et al. (1995) and Hannan et al. (2010) was consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance, and Hannan et al. (2010) calculated lod scores under an autosomal dominant model. Nesbit et al. (2013) found heterozygous AP2S1 mutations in affected individuals, which they considered consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance of FHH3. ⚠︎

### Mapping

In the 5-generation Oklahoma kindred, Trump et al. (1995) used 17 polymorphic markers to exclude linkage to the FBH regions on 3q21-q24 and 19p13.3, the MEN1 region on 11q13, and the PTH region on 11p15 (lod scores less than -2.00 at theta = 0.05-0.25). Multipoint crossover analysis and the LINKMAP program confirmed the exclusion.

Lloyd et al. (1999) carried out a genomewide search in the Oklahoma family and found linkage to 8 loci on chromosome 19q13. The maximum lod score was 6.67 at theta = 0.00 for D19S606. Recombinants placed the locus in an interval of less than 12 cM flanked by D19S908 and D19S866.

Hannan et al. (2010) used 24 polymorphic loci in the Oklahoma kindred and found linkage to 10 of 14 informative 19q13 microsatellite loci. The maximum 2-point lod score was 6.92 at theta = 0 for D19S606. Recombinants in 3 affected individuals, together with SNP analysis, narrowed the FBHH3 locus to a 4.1-Mb interval flanked by D19S112 and rs245111, containing 135 protein-coding genes and 24 predicted genes. The syntenic region on mouse chromosome 7 contains 4 Casr-related sequences (Gprc2a-rs1, -rs2, -rs3, and -rs5), but the authors found no human or primate homologs of these genes in the interval. Comparative analysis showed that 2 conserved segments were inverted between mouse and human. It also showed that an 11.6-Mb mouse region containing 35 genes, including the 4 Gprc2a-rs genes, was absent from the human genome, so FBHH3 could not be attributed to Gprc2a-rs abnormalities.

Nesbit et al. (2010) studied a second kindred, in which FHH cosegregated with chromosome 19q13.3 markers. The peak lod score was 5.98 at 0% recombination with D19S412. Analysis of recombinants mapped the locus to a 3.46-Mb interval flanked centromerically by SNP rs1990932 and telomerically by D19S604.

### Heterogeneity

In the Oklahoma kindred, the disorder was not linked to markers that segregate with FBH, MEN1, or PTH, so Trump et al. (1995) concluded that FBH shows further genetic heterogeneity and that a third FBH locus exists. Nesbit et al. (2010) noted that FHH1, caused by CASR mutations, occurs in more than 65% of FHH patients. They suggested that FHH3 may explain the disorder in FHH patients without CASR mutations.

### Molecular Genetics

Lloyd et al. (1999) found no abnormalities in the coding region, 5-prime UTR, or part of the promoter of the calmodulin III gene, which lies within the 19q13 interval, in an affected member of the Oklahoma family.

Hannan et al. (2010) sequenced the coding regions and intron-exon boundaries of 12 candidate genes from the interval that are expressed in parathyroid and/or kidney (PTH2, PTGIR, GNG8, EMP3, SPHK2, TULP2, NUCB1, HRC, RCN3, CYTH2, RRAS, and ATF5) in an affected member of the Oklahoma kindred. They found no abnormalities, and RT-PCR excluded splicing abnormalities.

Nesbit et al. (2013) performed exome sequencing in an affected individual from each of 2 unrelated FHH3 kindreds, the Oklahoma kindred (FHHOk) and a second kindred (FHHNI). Within the 3.46-Mb FHH3 interval on 19q13.3, they found a single variant in both patients: a C-to-T transition in exon 2 of the AP2S1 gene that caused an arg15-to-cys (R15C) substitution. The mutation cosegregated with disease in members of the 2 kindreds (32 affected and 23 unaffected) across 4 generations. The authors then sequenced AP2S1 in 50 unrelated FHH patients without CASR mutations. They found 11 heterozygous missense mutations, all affecting arg15: 4 R15C, 3 arg15-to-his (R15H), and 4 arg15-to-leu (R15L). They concluded that Arg15 mutations may cause FHH in more than 20% of patients without CaSR mutations. In HEK293 cells stably expressing CaSR, each of the 3 mutants shifted the calcium concentration-response curve to the right and gave a significantly higher EC50 than wildtype, indicating decreased sensitivity to extracellular calcium. The R15C mutant gave an EC50 higher than vector alone, suggesting a possible dominant-negative effect. After stimulation with high extracellular calcium, cells transfected with the R15C mutant did not show the further reduction in cell-surface CaSR seen with wildtype AP2S1, indicating reduced CaSR endocytosis.

In a Japanese infant with hypercalcemia and hypercalciuria, Fujisawa et al. (2013) identified a presumably de novo heterozygous R15L mutation in the AP2S1 gene. No pathologic mutation was found in CASR or GNA11.

Howles et al. (2016) reported 3 unrelated probands with FHH3 who carried heterozygous AP2S1 mutations resulting in R15C, R15H, or R15L substitutions. In vitro, cinacalcet (10 nM) corrected the abnormal intracellular calcium signaling and MAPK-serum response element reporter responses to extracellular calcium in cells expressing the calcium-sensing receptor with the Cys15, His15, or Leu15 AP2-sigma mutants.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Nesbit et al. (2013) found that patients with the 3 different AP2S1 Arg15 mutations had similar elevations in serum calcium and PTH and similar reductions in urinary calcium excretion. They concluded that there was no correlation between mutation and clinical phenotype.

### Pathogenesis

Nesbit et al. (2013) noted that Arg15 of AP2-sigma-2 is conserved in all orthologs and in the human AP sigma paralogs. In the AP2 crystal structure, Arg15 and Arg21 of AP2-alpha-1 form key contacts with the glutamine at position -4 of the dileucine motif of cargo proteins, and substitution by Cys or Leu was predicted to reduce affinity for this motif. The CaSR has a variant dileucine motif in its cytoplasmic C-terminal domain. Mutating this motif (Leu-Leu1013-1014 to Ala-Ala) raised the EC50 of CaSR-expressing HEK293 cells, indicating that the motif is functionally significant. The authors noted that AP2-sigma-2 is ubiquitously expressed, yet Arg15 mutations cause a specific disorder of extracellular calcium homeostasis rather than a multisystem disorder. They suggested this may be because Arg15 has a specific role, such as recognition of the CaSR dileucine motif.

### Diagnosis

McMurtry et al. (1992) noted that in their kindred, familial benign hypercalcemia in adults could be especially difficult to distinguish from primary hyperparathyroidism because serum immunoreactive PTH levels may be elevated.

Fujisawa et al. (2013) concluded that the absence of hypocalciuria does not necessarily argue against an AP2S1 mutation. They suggested that their patient's early infantile age may have contributed to her hypercalciuria.

### Clinical Management

In their Japanese infant with an AP2S1 mutation, Fujisawa et al. (2013) found that a low calcium formula containing 2.6 mg/dL of calcium led to catch-up growth and lowered serum calcium and the urinary calcium-to-creatinine ratio. Feeding a 2:1 mixture of low calcium and standard formula kept serum calcium at about 12 mg/dL without a marked rise in serum PTH. The authors concluded that low calcium formula is worth trying in infants with FHH.

Howles et al. (2016) treated 3 probands with symptomatic FHH3 with oral cinacalcet (30 to 60 mg daily) for 33 to 45 months. Treatment reduced serum calcium by more than 20% and relieved symptoms. Serum phosphate rose and serum PTH fell, but both stayed within the normal range. None of the patients developed nausea, vomiting, or hypocalcemia. The authors noted that long-term surveillance would be needed to assess safety and monitor for hypocalcemia.

### Population Genetics

Nesbit et al. (2013) found 3 different mutations, all altering the Arg15 residue of AP2S1, in 13 unrelated probands. They also found significant conservation of Arg15 in 5,400 human exomes (p less than 10(-10)), and concluded that this residue is likely a mutational hotspot.

(2013) used exome sequencing to find a heterozygous C-to-T transition in exon 2 of the AP2S1 gene, resulting in an arg15-to-cys (R15C) substitution, in affected members of 2 unrelated FHH3 kindreds, the Oklahoma kindred (FHHOk) and a second kindred (FHHNI). The mutation was confirmed by Sanger sequencing and cosegregated with disease in members from 4 generations of the 2 kindreds.

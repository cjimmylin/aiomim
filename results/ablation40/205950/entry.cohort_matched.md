### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia-2 (SIDBA2) is caused by mutation in the SLC25A38 gene (610819) on chromosome 3p22.1. ⚠︎

### Description

Guernsey et al. (2009) described the sideroblastic anemias as a heterogeneous group of congenital and acquired blood disorders. Their morphologic hallmark is the ringed sideroblast, a bone marrow erythroid precursor with pathologic iron deposits inside the mitochondria. SIDBA2 is a congenital, nonsyndromic form inherited as an autosomal recessive trait (Guernsey et al., 2009) that does not respond to pyridoxine (Jardine et al., 1994). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Manabe et al. (1982) described a female infant with congenital refractory sideroblastic anemia that did not respond to pyridoxine therapy. Erythroblast delta-aminolevulinic acid (ALA) synthetase activity was markedly reduced, both with and without pyridoxal phosphate treatment.

Jardine et al. (1994) reported a son and daughter of unaffected parents who had transfusion-dependent, pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia from birth. Their hemoglobin levels were 4.3 and 6.4 g/dl, respectively. Delta-aminolevulinate synthase activity in erythroblasts from the sister's fractionated marrow was 135 pmol delta-aminolevulinate formed/10(6) erythroblasts/hour (normal range, 110-650 pmol).

Guernsey et al. (2009) studied patients with nonsyndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia with ringed sideroblasts in the bone marrow. ⚠︎

### Biochemical Features

In the female infant with pyridoxine-refractory congenital sideroblastic anemia, Manabe et al. (1982) found normal activity of erythroblast mitochondrial neutral protease, which specifically inactivates the apo form of ALA synthetase. The sensitivity of apo-ALA synthetase to the protease was also normal. They speculated that the reduced ALA synthetase activity resulted from impaired production of the enzyme rather than faster destruction, and that this could explain the patient's unresponsiveness to pyridoxine.

### Inheritance

Jardine et al. (1994) concluded that the congenital pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia in a brother and sister born to unaffected parents was very likely an autosomal recessive trait. The affected sibs had inherited different maternal ALAS2 alleles. The mother had no dimorphic erythrocyte population and showed random X inactivation in her peripheral leukocytes, so she was not a carrier of an X-linked sideroblastic anemia mutation.

Guernsey et al. (2009) defined a form of autosomal recessive nonsyndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia caused by mutations in SLC25A38.

### Mapping

Jardine et al. (1994) excluded linkage to the ALAS2 gene at Xp11.21 in a family with pyridoxine-refractory congenital sideroblastic anemia. For the analysis they identified an ALAS2 polymorphism in the 3-prime region, a G-to-A transition 220 nt 3-prime of the AATAAA polyadenylation signal. Mismatch PCR and XmnI restriction analysis of 148 alleles gave a gene frequency of 25%. The 2 affected sibs had received different maternal ALAS2 alleles. Sequence analysis of the ALAS2 gene in the affected son found no mutations in the coding region, the intron/exon boundaries, or the 1-kb 5-prime promoter region.

### Molecular Genetics

Guernsey et al. (2009) used positional cloning to define a previously unknown form of autosomal recessive nonsyndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia. They found that it was associated with mutations in the SLC25A38 gene, which encodes an erythroid-specific mitochondrial carrier family protein. They showed that SLC25A38 is important for heme biosynthesis in eukaryotes.

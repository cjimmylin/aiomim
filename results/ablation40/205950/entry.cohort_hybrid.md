### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia-2 (SIDBA2) is caused by mutation in the SLC25A38 gene (610819) on chromosome 3p22.1. ⚠︎

### Description

Guernsey et al. (2009) described the sideroblastic anemias as a heterogeneous group of congenital and acquired hematologic disorders whose morphologic hallmark is the ringed sideroblast, a bone marrow erythroid precursor with pathologic iron deposits inside mitochondria. SIDBA2 is an autosomal recessive nonsyndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia associated with mutations in SLC25A38, an erythroid-specific mitochondrial carrier family protein (Guernsey et al., 2009). In the reported patients, the anemia was present from birth and did not respond to pyridoxine (Manabe et al., 1982; Jardine et al., 1994).

### Clinical Features

Manabe et al. (1982) described a female infant with congenital refractory sideroblastic anemia that did not respond to pyridoxine therapy.

Jardine et al. (1994) reported a son and daughter of unaffected parents who had transfusion-dependent, pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia from birth. Their hemoglobin levels were 4.3 and 6.4 g/dl, respectively.

### Biochemical Features

In the female infant with congenital pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia, Manabe et al. (1982) found a marked reduction of delta-aminolevulinic acid (ALA) synthetase activity in erythroblasts, with and without pyridoxal phosphate treatment. Mitochondrial neutral protease activity in erythroblasts was normal. This protease specifically inactivates the apo form of ALA synthetase, and the sensitivity of apo-ALA synthetase to the protease was also normal. The authors speculated that the reduced enzyme activity was caused by impaired production of ALA synthetase rather than faster destruction, which could explain why the patient did not respond to pyridoxine.

In the affected sister of the sib pair reported by Jardine et al. (1994), delta-aminolevulinate synthase activity in erythroblasts from fractionated marrow was 135 pmol delta-aminolevulinate formed/10(6) erythroblasts/hour (normal range = 110-650 pmol).

### Inheritance

Jardine et al. (1994) concluded that the congenital pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia in a brother and sister born to unaffected parents was very likely an autosomal recessive trait. The affected sibs had inherited different maternal alleles at the ALAS2 locus. The mother had no dimorphic erythrocyte population and showed random X inactivation in her peripheral leukocytes, which showed that she did not carry an X-linked sideroblastic anemia mutation.

Guernsey et al. (2009) defined the disorder as an autosomal recessive form of nonsyndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia.

### Molecular Genetics

In the affected son of the sib pair with pyridoxine-refractory sideroblastic anemia, Jardine et al. (1994) sequenced the ALAS2 gene and found no mutations in the coding region, the intron/exon boundaries, or the 1-kb 5-prime promoter region. They identified a polymorphism in the 3-prime region of ALAS2, a G-to-A transition 220 nt 3-prime of the AATAAA polyadenylation signal. Mismatch PCR followed by XmnI restriction analysis of 148 alleles gave a gene frequency of 25% for this polymorphism. Tracking this intragenic polymorphism showed that the affected sibs had received different maternal ALAS2 alleles. This excluded ALAS2 mutations as the cause of the anemia in this family.

Guernsey et al. (2009) used positional cloning to identify mutations in the SLC25A38 gene in patients with autosomal recessive nonsyndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia. SLC25A38 encodes an erythroid-specific mitochondrial carrier family protein. The authors showed that SLC25A38 is important for heme biosynthesis in eukaryotes.

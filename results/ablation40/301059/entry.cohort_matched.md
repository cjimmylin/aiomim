### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that X-linked spermatogenic failure-3 (SPGFX3) is caused by hemizygous mutation in the CFAP47 gene (301057) on chromosome Xp21.1.

### Description

X-linked spermatogenic failure-3 (SPGFX3) is a form of primary male infertility. It is characterized by asthenoteratozoospermia with multiple morphological abnormalities of the sperm flagella (MMAF) (Liu et al., 2021).

### Clinical Features

Liu et al. (2021) reported 3 unrelated Chinese men with primary infertility caused by asthenoteratozoospermia with multiple morphological abnormalities of the sperm flagella (MMAF). Immunoblotting and immunofluorescence showed obviously reduced levels of CFAP47 in spermatozoa from all 3 men. Liu et al. (2021) also reported an Australian man from a cohort of men with severe asthenoteratozoospermia. According to the authors, all of the men with hemizygous CFAP47 variants had typical MMAF phenotypes.

Liao et al. (2022) reported a man with MMAF. Immunoblotting and immunofluorescence showed that CFAP47 was dramatically reduced in his spermatozoa. Expression of WDR87 was also significantly decreased, and weak WDR87 signals were detected next to the sperm nuclei.

### Inheritance

Liu et al. (2021) found hemizygous variants in the X-linked CFAP47 gene in affected men and concluded that SPGFX3 is an X-linked disorder.

### Heterogeneity

Liu et al. (2021) noted that several genes previously associated with MMAF show autosomal recessive inheritance. They also noted that the cause remains unknown in about 40% of affected individuals.

### Molecular Genetics

Liu et al. (2021) identified hemizygous missense mutations in the X-linked CFAP47 gene in 3 unrelated Chinese men with MMAF. The 3 variants were absent from human control population genome databases. Multiple bioinformatic tools predicted them to be deleterious. Spermatozoa from all 3 men had obviously reduced levels of CFAP47 on immunoblotting and immunofluorescence. CFAP47 encodes a cilia- and flagella-associated protein that is highly expressed in testis.

Liu et al. (2021) also analyzed whole-exome sequencing data from an additional cohort of Australian men with severe asthenoteratozoospermia. In 1 of these men, they identified a hemizygous deletion at Xp21.1 that removed the entire CFAP47 gene.

Liao et al. (2022) screened 7 men with MMAF for mutations. In 1 of them they found 2 novel CFAP47 mutations: a c.706G-A change resulting in a val236-to-met (V236M) substitution, and a c.1337C-T change resulting in a thr446-to-met (T446M) substitution. CFAP47 was dramatically reduced in this man's spermatozoa on immunoblotting and immunofluorescence.

### Pathogenesis

Liao et al. (2022) found that CFAP47 interacted with WDR87 in the midpiece of the sperm flagellum. They suggested that the 2 proteins form a complex involved in sperm tail assembly. In sperm from a man with CFAP47 mutations, WDR87 expression was markedly reduced, with weak signals next to the sperm nuclei. The authors concluded that CFAP47 is needed to transport WDR87 during flagellar biogenesis.

### Clinical Management

Liu et al. (2021) suggested that men with CFAP47-associated MMAF have a good prognosis with intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI). They considered this useful guidance for genetic counseling and assisted reproduction.

### Animal Model

Liu et al. (2021) generated a Cfap47-mutated mouse model. Adult male mutant mice were sterile, with reduced sperm motility and abnormal flagellar morphology and movement. ICSI rescued the fertility of the mutant males.

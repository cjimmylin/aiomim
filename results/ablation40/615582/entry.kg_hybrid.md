### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Loeys-Dietz syndrome-5 (LDS5), also known as Rienhoff syndrome, is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TGFB3 gene (190230) on chromosome 14q24.3. ⚠︎

### Description

Loeys-Dietz syndrome-5 is a connective tissue disorder whose features overlap with Marfan syndrome and Loeys-Dietz syndrome. Craniofacial features include hypertelorism and bifid uvula. Skeletal features include arachnodactyly, joint hypermobility, pectus deformity, and either tall stature or growth retardation (Rienhoff et al., 2013; Kuechler et al., 2015). Bertoli-Avella et al. (2015) showed that cardiovascular involvement can be significant, including thoracic and abdominal aortic aneurysm and dissection and mitral valve disease. They also noted striking clinical variability both within and between families.

### Nomenclature

Kuechler et al. (2015) referred to the emerging connective tissue disorder caused by TGFB3 mutations as Rienhoff syndrome, noting that the name was tentative.

### Clinical Features

Rienhoff et al. (2013) described a 9-year-old European American girl born to nonconsanguineous, healthy parents. Birth weight was 2.9 kg (5th centile). At birth she had contractures of the right hand, most severe in the 3rd and 4th fingers, and of all toes. She also had a midline facial nevus flammeus and mild hypotonia. At age 3 months she was evaluated for failure to thrive and delayed gross motor function. At 17 months she weighed 7.5 kg (less than 1st centile) and was 76 cm tall (5th centile). Findings at that age included bilateral pes planus, mild pectus excavatum, hyperextensibility of multiple large joints, mild retrognathia, prominent eyes, hypertelorism, blue sclerae, a small metopic ridge, and a bifid uvula with an intact hard palate. Muscle bulk was decreased in all appendicular and axial muscles, with strength 1/5, low tone, and diminished reflexes, and subcutaneous fat was markedly reduced. She stood independently with a positive Gowers sign at 20 months and walked at 24 months with a 'hip waddle' gait. Her neurocognitive development was appropriate for age. Radiographs showed bilateral coxa valga. At age 7 years she weighed 15.5 kg (less than 1st centile) and was 115 cm tall (5th centile). A quadriceps biopsy at that age showed type 1 fiber predominance with mild, focal type 1 fiber disproportion consistent with disuse. Yearly echocardiograms from 18 months showed no cardiac defect or dysfunction. At 6.5 years, the aortic annulus and aortic root measured 1.34 cm (z score 0.59) and 1.72 cm (z score 0.39), respectively.

Matyas et al. (2014) reported a patient with overgrowth and features of Loeys-Dietz syndrome. According to Bertoli-Avella et al. (2015), this patient was 10.5 years old and had tall stature and cleft palate and/or bifid uvula.

Bertoli-Avella et al. (2015) studied a large Dutch family (family 1) whose features overlapped with Marfan and Loeys-Dietz syndromes. Seven family members, aged 40 to 68 years, had aneurysms and dissections, mainly of the descending thoracic and abdominal aorta. Three died from aortic dissection and rupture. Four members had mitral valve abnormalities, ranging from mild prolapse to severe regurgitation that required surgery. Craniofacial abnormalities were subtle and included a long face, high-arched palate, and retrognathia. Pectus deformity and scoliosis were frequent. Other recurrent findings were velvety skin, varices, and hiatal hernia. Several family members had autoimmune features, including HLA-B27-positive spondyloarthritis, Graves disease, and celiac disease.

Bertoli-Avella et al. (2015) summarized the features of 43 patients (23 male, 20 female; median age 34 years) from 11 families with TGFB3 mutations. Vascular involvement ranged from no cardiovascular abnormality at age 64 to type A aortic dissection (median age 51 years, range 40 to 80) or type B aortic dissection (median age 44.5 years, range 30 to 57). It also included abdominal aortic dissection and, in one patient, death from cerebral aneurysm dissection at age 55. The authors observed no early arterial dissection, no dissection at small aortic dimensions, and no striking arterial tortuosity. Other cardiovascular findings were mitral valve disease, persistent foramen ovale, and atrial or ventricular septal defects. Skeletal findings included tall stature (12 patients), arachnodactyly (16), kyphoscoliosis (11), joint hypermobility (9), and pectus deformity (8). Loeys-Dietz features included hypertelorism (14), bifid uvula (11), cleft palate (5), cervical spine instability, and clubfoot. Other recurrent features were dolichocephaly, high-arched palate, retrognathia, and inguinal hernia. Medical records showed no evidence of ectopia lentis, and early-onset osteoarthritis was reported in only 2 individuals. Clinical variability within and between families was striking.

Kuechler et al. (2015) described a family in which a father and his 2 children were affected. All 3 had tall stature, arachnodactyly, hyperextensible joints, hypertelorism, and bifid uvula. None had cardiac involvement, aortic dilation, or eye involvement.

### Inheritance

Rienhoff et al. (2013) found a de novo heterozygous TGFB3 mutation in their patient. Bertoli-Avella et al. (2015) described a large Dutch family with a pattern consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. Kuechler et al. (2015) reported a heterozygous TGFB3 mutation that segregated with the phenotype in an affected father and his 2 affected children.

### Mapping

Bertoli-Avella et al. (2015) performed genomewide SNP linkage analysis on 6 members of a large Dutch family with syndromic aortic aneurysms. Two large genomic regions, on chromosomes 14 and 15, were shared by all affected patients. The most prominent candidate gene in these regions was TGFB3, on chromosome 14q24.

### Molecular Genetics

Rienhoff et al. (2013) performed exome sequencing on a girl with LDS5, her parents, and her 2 unaffected sibs. Analysis of TGFB2, TGFBR1, TGFBR2, SMAD3, FBN1, and FBN2 had found no mutation. Exome sequencing identified a de novo heterozygous c.1226G-A transition in the last exon of the TGFB3 gene, resulting in a cys409-to-tyr (C409Y) substitution; Sanger sequencing confirmed that it was de novo. The mutant ligand did not activate a TGF-beta reporter in transfected cells. In Xenopus embryos, the mutant had a dominant-negative effect on TGF-beta signaling. The patient also carried a de novo heterozygous nonsense variant in the CDH2 gene (Q70X). N-cadherin levels in her fibroblasts did not differ significantly from those of controls, and the authors concluded that the CDH2 variant was unlikely to drive the phenotype, although they could not exclude a modifying effect.

Matyas et al. (2014) identified a de novo mutation in the latency-associated peptide domain of TGFB3 in a patient with overgrowth and features of Loeys-Dietz syndrome. Kuechler et al. (2015) reported this mutation as c.899G-A, resulting in an arg300-to-gln (R300Q) substitution.

In affected members of a large Dutch family with syndromic aortic aneurysms and dissections, Bertoli-Avella et al. (2015) found no causal mutation in the known TAAD genes. Linkage analysis followed by sequencing of TGFB3 then identified a heterozygous splice site mutation (c.754+2T-C). The mutation segregated with the phenotype. Bertoli-Avella et al. (2015) then screened TGFB3 in 470 additional probands with syndromic or nonsyndromic TAAD, 350 by Sanger sequencing and 120 by exome sequencing. They identified heterozygous TGFB3 mutations in 10 more probands. These comprised 4 missense mutations, D263H, R300W (4 families), I322T, and L401P; 1 nonsense mutation, Y365X, which occurred de novo; and 2 single-base deletions causing frameshifts, L386RfsTer21 and N235MfsTer11. All mutations were absent from controls in EVS, 1000 Genomes, and Genome of the Netherlands. The proband with the de novo Y365X mutation also carried a SMAD3 variant of unknown significance (A250T), inherited from a mother with variable connective tissue findings and mild cardiovascular involvement. The authors considered the contribution of this SMAD3 variant to pathogenesis unclear.

Kuechler et al. (2015) performed whole-exome sequencing in 2 of 3 affected members of a family with features overlapping Marfan and Loeys-Dietz syndromes. They identified a novel heterozygous c.898C-G mutation in the TGFB3 gene, predicting an arg300-to-gly (R300G) substitution. Sanger sequencing confirmed the mutation and showed that it segregated with the phenotype.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Bertoli-Avella et al. (2015) noted that TGFB3 mutations affecting arg300 were associated with cleft palate and/or bifid uvula in their patients and in the patient reported by Matyas et al. (2014). They also noted that TGFB3 mutations appeared to have opposing effects on height. One of their patients, with the D263H mutation, had short stature and received growth hormone therapy during puberty. The patient of Rienhoff et al. (2013) with the C409Y mutation also had short stature, whereas several of their other patients and the patient of Matyas et al. (2014) were tall.

Kuechler et al. (2015) noted that the heterozygous loss-of-function TGFB3 mutation C409Y was found in an individual with growth retardation. In contrast, mutations at arg300, which presumably increase TGF-beta signaling, were found in individuals with overgrowth or tall stature. They suggested that the short or tall stature seen in patients with TGFB3 mutations may result from opposing effects of the mutations on TGF-beta signaling.

### Pathogenesis

Rienhoff et al. (2013) concluded that their patient's phenotype resulted from decreased TGF-beta signaling due to a hypomorphic TGFB3 allele. They proposed that TGFB3 deficiency during early myogenesis, possibly through incomplete epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition of the dermomyotome, might reduce the number of embryonic muscle precursor cells and so cause hypomyoplasia. They also suggested that normal muscle mass and complete palatal fusion appear to require a minimum level of TGFB3 signaling.

Bertoli-Avella et al. (2015) examined dissected aortic wall from a patient with the D263H mutation. It showed elastic fiber fragmentation with increased collagen and proteoglycan deposition. Immunohistochemistry showed paradoxically enhanced TGF-beta signaling, with increased pSMAD2 and pERK and elevated TGFB1 mRNA. Pathology reports from 2 deceased members of the Dutch family described extensive elastic fiber fragmentation and aortic medial degeneration. Because they had found truncating mutations and an in-frame splice site mutation, the authors hypothesized that TGFB3 mutations lead to loss of function. They suggested that the young age of the previously reported patients might explain why those patients had no obvious cardiovascular disease.

### Diagnosis

Rienhoff et al. (2013) noted that their patient shared features with Marfan syndrome, Loeys-Dietz syndrome, and Beals-Hecht syndrome but did not meet the diagnostic criteria for any of them. The differences included significant growth retardation, the absence of cardiovascular findings, and distinct muscle histopathology.

### Clinical Management

Rienhoff et al. (2013) reported that a 3-year trial of losartan, begun at age 3 years at doses up to 2.0 mg/kg/day, produced no change in their patient's muscle strength or mass.

Bertoli-Avella et al. (2015) recommended yearly echocardiographic evaluation of the aortic root in all TGFB3 mutation carriers, together with at least 1 baseline imaging study of the complete aorta and its side branches. They suggested that imaging of the brain vessels might be indicated, depending on family history.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 LOEYS-DIETZ SYNDROME 5**  
TGFB3, CYS409TYR [c.1226G>A; p.C409Y; p.Cys409Tyr]

Rienhoff et al. (2013) used exome sequencing to study a European American girl with distal arthrogryposis, hypotonia, low muscle mass, growth retardation, hypertelorism, and bifid uvula. They identified a de novo heterozygous c.1226G-A transition in the last exon of the TGFB3 gene, resulting in a cys409-to-tyr (C409Y) substitution at a cysteine that is conserved across TGF-beta family ligands and forms part of the 'cysteine knot.' Sanger sequencing confirmed that the change was de novo. It was not found in dbSNP130, the EVS database, or the 1000 Genomes dataset. Ligand from the mutant construct did not activate a TGF-beta reporter. In Xenopus embryos, coinjecting mutant and wildtype RNA at a 1:1 ratio reduced pSMAD2 and pERK1/2 signals to approximately 40% and 60%, respectively, of the levels produced by wildtype RNA alone, with no effect on SMAD1 phosphorylation.

**.0002 LOEYS-DIETZ SYNDROME 5**  
TGFB3, ARG300GLN [c.899G>A; p.Arg300Gln]

Matyas et al. (2014) reported a de novo mutation in the latency-associated peptide domain of TGFB3 in a patient with overgrowth and features of Loeys-Dietz syndrome. Kuechler et al. (2015) gave this mutation as c.899G-A, resulting in an arg300-to-gln (R300Q) substitution.

**.0003 LOEYS-DIETZ SYNDROME 5**  
TGFB3, IVS4DS, T-C, +2 [c.754+2T>C; p.E216_K251del; p.Glu216_Lys251del]

In affected members of a large Dutch family with LDS5 and aortic aneurysms and dissections, Bertoli-Avella et al. (2015) identified a heterozygous T-to-C transition in the canonical donor splice site of exon 4 of the TGFB3 gene (c.754+2T-C). The mutation segregated with the phenotype and was absent from variant databases. cDNA sequencing in 2 patients confirmed skipping of exon 4, resulting in an in-frame deletion of 108 nucleotides and an expected deletion of 36 amino acids (Glu216_Lys251del). A 17-year-old carrier had no documented cardiovascular features but had mild systemic manifestations.

**.0004 LOEYS-DIETZ SYNDROME 5**  
TGFB3, ASP263HIS [p.D263H; p.Asp263His]

In family 3 with LDS5, Bertoli-Avella et al. (2015) identified a heterozygous asp263-to-his (D263H) mutation in exon 5 of the TGFB3 gene. The residue is highly conserved, lies in the latency-associated peptide domain, and forms part of the RGD motif required for integrin binding. The dissected aortic wall of an affected patient showed elastic fiber fragmentation, increased collagen and proteoglycan deposition, and increased pSMAD2, pERK, and TGFB1 mRNA. This patient had short stature and received growth hormone therapy.

**.0005 LOEYS-DIETZ SYNDROME 5**  
TGFB3, ARG300TRP [p.R300W; p.Arg300Trp]

In 4 families with LDS5 (families 2, 4, 5, and 11), Bertoli-Avella et al. (2015) identified a heterozygous arg300-to-trp (R300W) mutation in exon 5 of the TGFB3 gene. The mutation affects the last amino acid of the latency-associated peptide domain, disrupting the last residue of the RKKR furin cleavage recognition motif. The authors noted that mutations affecting arg300 were associated with cleft palate and/or bifid uvula.

**.0006 LOEYS-DIETZ SYNDROME 5**

In family 6 with LDS5, Bertoli-Avella et al. (2015) identified a de novo heterozygous tyr365-to-ter (Y365X) mutation in the TGFB3 gene. The proband also carried a SMAD3 variant of unknown significance (A250T), inherited from the mother.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia (DYTSPG) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the ATP5MC3 gene (602736) on chromosome 2q31.1. ⚠︎

### Description

Early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia is an autosomal dominant neurologic disorder. Its main features are spasticity or pyramidal signs and dystonia, which can range from isolated limb dystonia to generalized dystonia. Some patients also have developmental delay. Gilbert et al. (2009) described the disorder in a large kindred as a highly penetrant autosomal dominant disease causing spasticity and dystonia.

### Clinical Features

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported a large multigenerational kindred from Ohio with autosomal dominant hereditary spastic paraplegia and dystonia. Spasticity and weakness began anywhere from the first year of life to the sixth decade, and dystonia began between the first and third decades. Some affected members had dystonia, and 1 affected child had generalized dystonia. There was no ataxia or apparent cognitive involvement. Neuroimaging and peripheral neurophysiology were normal. ⚠︎

Zech et al. (2022) reported 3 unrelated patients with de novo ATP5MC3 mutations. Patient 8 was a previously reported 12-year-old boy with isolated upper-limb dystonia. Patient 9 was a 15-year-old girl with a history of milestone delay, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia with prominent upper-body involvement. Patient 10 was a 6-year-old boy with delayed psychomotor development, lower-extremity spasticity, and elevated blood lactate. Zech et al. (2022) combined their 3 patients with 15 clinically characterized members of a multigenerational family reported by Neilson et al. Across these 18 individuals, 12 had dystonia, 17 had spasticity or pyramidal signs, and 2 had developmental delay or intellectual disability. The authors noted that patients 9 and 10 confirmed spastic symptoms in independent families and added neurodevelopmental comorbidity to the phenotype.

### Biochemical Features

Zech et al. (2022) found a slight but significant decrease in ATPase (complex V) activity and a reduced oxygen consumption rate in fibroblasts from patient 8. Fibroblasts from patient 10 had deficient ATPase activity, and blue native PAGE showed a moderate decrease in assembled ATPase.

### Inheritance

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported autosomal dominant transmission in a large multigenerational Ohio kindred. The heterozygous ATP5MC3 mutations in the 3 unrelated patients reported by Zech et al. (2022) occurred de novo, which is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Mapping

Gilbert et al. (2009) studied a large multigenerational Ohio kindred with autosomal dominant spastic paraplegia and dystonia. Using single and multipoint linkage analysis with microsatellite and SNP array genotyping, they mapped the disease locus to chromosome 2q24-q31, between markers rs1424937 and rs1559510. This region lies proximal to SPG13 and contained no known genes for hereditary spastic paraplegia or dystonia. A secondary analysis for candidate genes segregating with the dystonia phenotype found 2 candidate regions with parametric lod scores above 2.0.

### Molecular Genetics

Zech et al. (2022) analyzed whole-exome data from 2,962 patients with suspected mitochondrial disease and/or dystonia and used gene matching. They found de novo heterozygous missense mutations in the ATP5MC3 gene in 3 unrelated patients with dystonia and/or spasticity: N106K, P107A, and G79V. None of the variants were present in gnomAD or local control exomes, and all were predicted to be deleterious. All 3 substitutions affected stringently conserved residues that lie close together in the C-terminal helical motifs, and 2 of the variants occurred at directly neighboring nucleotides. In patient fibroblasts, ATPase activity was decreased with N106K and deficient with G79V, and the G79V mutation was also associated with reduced assembly of the ATPase. Based on the recurrent N106K variant and non-recurrent variants such as G79V, the authors concluded that ATP5MC3 is linked to disease.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Zech et al. (2022) found that the ATP5MC3 G79V mutation caused a significantly greater ATPase enzyme deficit than N106K. G79V was associated with a more severe neurodevelopmental and spastic presentation, whereas N106K was associated with isolated dystonia. The authors cautioned that further studies were needed before genotype-phenotype correlations could be clearly established.

### Pathogenesis

In 3-dimensional modeling by Zech et al. (2022), the G79V mutation lay at an interhelical contact site and might disturb the integrity of the c-ring. N106K and P107A lay next to a loop at the interaction site between the F(O) and F(1) subcomplexes. Given the mild-to-moderate ATPase assembly and/or activity defects in patient cells, the authors suggested that dominant-negative effects were possible.

### Clinical Management

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported that generalized dystonia improved markedly with deep brain stimulation in 1 affected child from the Ohio kindred.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia (DYTSPG) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the ATP5MC3 gene (602736) on chromosome 2q31.

### Description

Early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia is an autosomal dominant neurologic disorder. It was first delineated in a large multigenerational family in which affected members had childhood-onset, progressive generalized dystonia, adult-onset, gradually progressive spastic paraplegia, or both, without ataxia or apparent cognitive involvement (Gilbert et al., 2009; Neilson et al., 2022). Patients with de novo mutations have shown isolated or generalized dystonia, pyramidal signs or lower-extremity spasticity, and in some cases developmental delay (Zech et al., 2022).

### Clinical Features

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported a large multigenerational Ohio kindred with an autosomal dominant, highly penetrant neurologic disorder that caused both spasticity and dystonia. Spasticity and weakness began anywhere from the first year of life to the sixth decade, and dystonia began between the first and third decades. Affected individuals had no ataxia or apparent cognitive involvement, and neuroimaging and peripheral neurophysiology were normal. Based on the clinical presentation and linkage results, the authors concluded that this was a novel neurologic disorder.

Neilson et al. (2022) provided further information on the family reported by Gilbert et al. (2009), which they called Family-1. The autosomal dominant pedigree included 40 individuals. Of these, 18 were affected with either childhood-onset, progressive generalized dystonia or adult-onset, gradually progressive spastic paraplegia. In the proband, dystonia began in one upper limb at age 2 years and then generalized to the trunk and all limbs, leading to loss of ambulation. His father and grandfather had the spastic paraplegia phenotype, with progressive leg weakness and spasticity starting after age 20 years. Some family members also had less severe, later-onset focal limb and neck dystonia and spasmodic dysphonia, which the authors suggested might be related to trauma or overuse. Where performed, brain MRI and peripheral nerve physiology were normal. In the proband and his father, metabolic studies were normal, including whole blood lactate and pyruvate, serum amino acids, urine organic acids, and blood and urine acylcarnitine profiles. Neilson et al. (2022) noted that dystonia in this family could generalize when it began in childhood and showed incomplete penetrance. The spastic paraplegia began in adulthood, progressed gradually, and was not accompanied by other neurologic problems.

Neilson et al. (2022) also reported a second, unrelated family (Family-2). The proband was a right-handed boy of European descent born to healthy unrelated parents, with no family history of dystonia or related movement disorders. He first presented at age 11 years with a 4-year history of gradually worsening upper limb dystonia. Laboratory studies of blood, urine, and cerebrospinal fluid were unrevealing. Brain MRI showed nonspecific mild bilateral frontoparietal white matter changes.

Zech et al. (2022) reported 3 unrelated patients with de novo ATP5MC3 mutations. One was the proband of Family-2 from Neilson et al. (2022) (patient 8), a 12-year-old boy with isolated upper-limb dystonia. Patient 9 was a 15-year-old girl with a history of milestone delay, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia with prominent upper-body involvement. Patient 10 was a 6-year-old boy with delayed psychomotor development, lower-extremity spasticity, and elevated blood lactate levels. The authors combined their patients with the 15 clinically characterized members of the multigenerational family of Neilson et al. (2022). Across these 18 individuals, they found dystonia in 12, spasticity/pyramidal signs in 17, and developmental delay/intellectual disability in 2. They noted that patients 9 and 10 confirmed spastic symptoms in independent families with ATP5MC3 variants and newly introduced neurodevelopmental comorbidity.

### Inheritance

Gilbert et al. (2009) and Neilson et al. (2022) reported autosomal dominant transmission of the disorder in a large multigenerational family. Neilson et al. (2022) and Zech et al. (2022) found the disorder in isolated patients with de novo heterozygous mutations.

### Mapping

Gilbert et al. (2009) carried out single and multipoint linkage analysis in a large multigenerational Ohio kindred with autosomal dominant spastic paraplegia and dystonia, using microsatellite and SNP array genotyping. They mapped the disease locus to chromosome 2q24-q31, in a region flanked by markers rs1424937 and rs1559510. The region is proximal to SPG13 and contains no known spastic paraplegia or dystonia genes. A secondary analysis for candidate genes segregating with the dystonia phenotype found 2 candidate regions with parametric lod scores above 2.0.

### Molecular Genetics

Neilson et al. (2022) studied the family originally reported by Gilbert et al. (2009). They performed whole-exome sequencing in the proband and a distant cousin, both with childhood-onset dystonia, and restricted the analysis to novel shared coding variants within the linkage region on chromosome 2q24-q31. This identified a heterozygous c.318C-G transversion in exon 4 of the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in an asn106-to-lys (N106K) substitution. Sanger sequencing showed that only affected family members carried the mutation. The variant was not present in gnomAD and was predicted to be damaging by all computational tools used. The substituted residue lies in a highly conserved loop that interacts directly with the F1 subunit. Screening of 384 additional patients with dystonia (362) or hereditary spastic paraplegia (22) found no deleterious variants in ATP5MC1, ATP5MC2, or ATP5MC3. Through GeneMatcher, the authors found a second family whose proband, a boy with upper limb dystonia, carried the same N106K mutation in the heterozygous state; trio whole-exome sequencing showed that it had arisen de novo. In fibroblasts from the Family-1 proband (with dystonia) and his affected father (with spastic paraplegia), complex I through IV activities were normal. However, complex V activity was reduced by 20%, cellular ATP by 20 to 32%, and oxygen consumption by 21 to 25%. The authors concluded that identifying the mutation in a second, independent family confirmed its pathogenicity for dystonia. They stated that ATP5MC3 remained the primary candidate for the spastic paraplegia in Family-1.

Zech et al. (2022) analyzed whole-exome sequencing data from 2,962 patients with suspected mitochondrial disease and/or dystonia and also used gene matching. They identified de novo heterozygous missense mutations in the ATP5MC3 gene in 3 unrelated patients: N106K in patient 8, who had previously been reported by Neilson et al. (2022); pro107-to-ala (P107A) in patient 9; and gly79-to-val (G79V) in patient 10. None of the variants were present in gnomAD or local control exome databases, and in silico tools predicted all of them to be deleterious. All 3 substitutions affected stringently conserved residues lying close together in the C-terminal helical motifs of the protein. In patient 10 fibroblasts, ATPase activity was deficient and assembled ATPase was moderately decreased on BN-PAGE. Patient 8 fibroblasts showed a slight but significant decrease in ATPase activity and a reduced oxygen consumption rate.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Zech et al. (2022) found that the ATP5MC3 G79V mutation caused a significantly greater ATPase enzyme deficit than N106K. G79V was associated with a more severe presentation of neurodevelopmental disease and spasticity, whereas N106K was associated with isolated dystonia. The authors cautioned that further studies were needed before genotype-phenotype correlations could be clearly postulated.

### Pathogenesis

Neilson et al. (2022) noted that ATP5MC1 and ATP5MC2 also encode subunit c. Assuming equal expression, they argued that haploinsufficiency was unlikely to explain the severe phenotypes, and they hypothesized that the mutant allele interferes with the F1-F0 interaction. Because oxygen consumption and ATP were reduced proportionally in patient fibroblasts, their data did not support uncoupling of oxidative phosphorylation as the mechanism. They suggested that the relative expression of the mutant gene might influence phenotypic expression.

In 3-dimensional modeling by Zech et al. (2022), G79V lay at an interhelical contact site, where it might disturb the integrity of the c-ring. N106K and P107A lay next to a loop at the interaction site between the FO and F1 subcomplexes. The authors suggested that dominant-negative effects were possible, given the mild-to-moderate ATPase assembly and/or enzyme activity defects in several patient cell lines.

### Clinical Management

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported that generalized dystonia improved markedly with deep brain stimulation in 1 affected child. Neilson et al. (2022) reported that this proband received bilateral pallidal deep brain stimulation at age 9 years, which restored ambulation and limb use, and that his response remained excellent at age 25 years.

### Animal Model

Neilson et al. (2022) generated transgenic Drosophila expressing the fly ATP5MC3 ortholog (CG1746) carrying either the human disease-equivalent N102K mutation or N102E, which is equivalent to a bacterial mutation. Expression was driven by the armadillo-GAL4 driver. Compared with wildtype flies and flies overexpressing wildtype protein, both mutants showed reduced mobility in geotaxis assays, and the N102K mutant was the slowest. Complex V activity in fly mitochondria was reduced by 53% with N102K and by 37% with N102E.

**.0001 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, ASN106LYS [c.318C>G; p.N106K; NM_001689.4:c.318C>G; NP_001680.1:p.Asn106Lys]

In affected members of a large family with autosomal dominant dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia, originally reported by Gilbert et al. (2009), Neilson et al. (2022) identified a heterozygous c.318C-G transversion in exon 4 of the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in an asn106-to-lys (N106K) substitution in a highly conserved loop that interacts with the F1 subunit. The mutation was found by whole-exome sequencing restricted to the linkage region and segregated with the disorder; it was not present in gnomAD. Fibroblasts from the proband and his affected father showed reduced complex V activity, ATP levels, and oxygen consumption. Drosophila expressing the equivalent mutation had reduced mobility and reduced complex V activity. Neilson et al. (2022) also identified the same mutation, arising de novo, in an unrelated boy with upper limb dystonia.

Zech et al. (2022) reported the de novo heterozygous N106K mutation in a 12-year-old boy (patient 8) with isolated upper-limb dystonia, who had been reported by Neilson et al. (2022). His fibroblasts showed a slight but significant decrease in ATPase activity and a reduced oxygen consumption rate.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia (DYTSPG) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the ATP5MC3 gene (602736) on chromosome 2q31. ⚠︎

### Description

Early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia (DYTSPG) is an autosomal dominant neurologic disorder. Affected individuals can have childhood-onset dystonia, which may generalize, adult-onset gradually progressive spastic paraplegia, or both (Neilson et al., 2022). Some patients also have developmental delay, pyramidal signs, or raised blood lactate, so the phenotype varies between unrelated families (Zech et al., 2022).

### Clinical Features

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported a large multigenerational kindred from Ohio with an autosomal dominant, highly penetrant neurologic disorder that caused spasticity and dystonia. Spasticity and weakness began anywhere from the first year of life to the sixth decade. Dystonia began between the first and third decades. There was no ataxia or apparent cognitive involvement, and neuroimaging and peripheral neurophysiology were normal. In 1 child, deep brain stimulation markedly improved generalized dystonia.

Neilson et al. (2022) gave further details of the kindred reported by Gilbert et al. (2009) (Family-1). The autosomal dominant pedigree included 40 individuals, 18 of whom had either childhood-onset progressive generalized dystonia or adult-onset gradually progressive spastic paraplegia. In the proband, dystonia began in 1 upper limb at age 2 years and spread to the trunk and all limbs, and he lost the ability to walk. Bilateral pallidal deep brain stimulation, implanted at age 9 years, restored walking and limb use, and the response remained excellent at age 25 years. The proband's father and grandfather had the spastic paraplegia phenotype, with progressive leg weakness and spasticity beginning after age 20 years. Some family members had milder, later-onset focal limb and neck dystonia or spasmodic dysphonia, which the authors thought might be related to trauma or overuse. Where performed, brain MRI and peripheral nerve physiology were normal. In the proband and his father, metabolic studies were normal, including whole blood lactate and pyruvate, serum amino acids, urine organic acids, and blood and urine acylcarnitine profiles. The authors noted that dystonia in this pedigree showed incomplete penetrance and that the spastic paraplegia was not accompanied by other neurologic problems.

Neilson et al. (2022) also described a second, unrelated family (Family-2). The proband was a right-handed boy born to healthy unrelated parents of European descent, with no family history of dystonia or related movement disorders. He presented at age 11 years with a 4-year history of gradually worsening upper limb dystonia. Laboratory studies of blood, urine, and cerebrospinal fluid were unrevealing. Brain MRI showed nonspecific mild bilateral frontoparietal white-matter changes.

Zech et al. (2022) described 3 unrelated patients with ATP5MC3 mutations. Patient 8 was the Family-2 proband previously reported by Neilson et al. (2022), a 12-year-old boy with isolated upper-limb dystonia. Patient 9, a 15-year-old girl, had a history of delayed milestones, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia with prominent upper-body involvement. Patient 10, a 6-year-old boy, had delayed psychomotor development, lower-extremity spasticity, and elevated blood lactate. Zech et al. (2022) combined these 3 patients with the 15 members of the multigenerational family for whom clinical information was available. Across these 18 individuals, dystonia was present in 12, spasticity or pyramidal signs in 17, and developmental delay or intellectual disability in 2. Ten had a dystonia-predominant syndrome. The authors noted that patients 9 and 10 confirmed spastic symptoms in independent families, introduced neurodevelopmental comorbidity into the phenotype, and showed relevant phenotypic differences between unrelated families.

### Inheritance

Gilbert et al. (2009) found that the disorder in the large Ohio kindred followed an autosomal dominant pattern of inheritance with high penetrance. In this family, Neilson et al. (2022) showed that a heterozygous ATP5MC3 mutation cosegregated with disease. Neilson et al. (2022) and Zech et al. (2022) also reported de novo heterozygous ATP5MC3 mutations in sporadic patients.

### Mapping

Gilbert et al. (2009) performed single-point and multipoint linkage analysis in the large Ohio kindred, using microsatellite and SNP array genotyping. They mapped the disease locus to chromosome 2q24-q31, in a region flanked by markers rs1424937 and rs1559510, proximal to SPG13, that contained no known HSP or dystonia genes. A secondary analysis for candidate genes segregating with the dystonia phenotype found 2 candidate regions with parametric lod scores above 2.0.

### Molecular Genetics

Neilson et al. (2022) studied the Ohio kindred mapped by Gilbert et al. (2009) (Family-1). They performed whole-exome sequencing in the proband and a distant cousin, both with childhood-onset dystonia, and restricted the analysis to novel shared coding variants within the 2q24-q31 linkage region. This identified a single heterozygous c.318C-G transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in an asn106-to-lys (N106K) substitution. Sanger sequencing showed that only affected individuals carried the mutation. The mutation was not found in gnomAD and was predicted to be damaging by all computational tools used. It affects a highly conserved loop that interacts directly with the F1 subunit. In fibroblasts from the proband, who had dystonia, and his father, who had spastic paraplegia, activities of complexes I through IV were similar to controls. However, complex V activity was reduced by 20%, cellular ATP by 20 to 32%, and oxygen consumption by 21 to 25%. Because oxygen consumption and ATP were reduced proportionally, the authors did not consider uncoupling of oxidative phosphorylation a likely mechanism. Screening of the ATP5MC1, ATP5MC2, and ATP5MC3 genes in 384 additional patients (362 with dystonia and 22 with HSP) found no deleterious variants. Through GeneMatcher, the authors identified an unrelated boy with upper limb dystonia (Family-2). Parent-offspring trio exome sequencing showed that he carried the same N106K mutation in the heterozygous state, and that it had arisen de novo.

Zech et al. (2022) searched for variants in ATPase subunit genes in exome data from 2,962 patients in mitochondrial disease (1,950) and dystonia (1,012) cohorts, supplemented by GeneMatcher. They identified de novo heterozygous missense mutations in ATP5MC3 in 3 unrelated patients. Patient 8, previously reported by Neilson et al. (2022), carried the N106K mutation. Patient 9 carried a c.319C-G transversion resulting in a pro107-to-ala (P107A) substitution. Patient 10 carried a c.236G-T transversion resulting in a gly79-to-val (G79V) substitution. None of the mutations was found in gnomAD or local control exomes, and all were predicted to be deleterious. All 3 affected strictly conserved residues lying close together in the C-terminal helical motifs of the protein, and 2 involved directly neighboring nucleotides. In patient 10's fibroblasts, ATPase activity was deficient and assembled ATPase was moderately decreased. In patient 8's fibroblasts, ATPase activity was slightly but significantly decreased and oxygen consumption rate was reduced.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Zech et al. (2022) found that the G79V mutation, seen in a patient with a more severe presentation combining neurodevelopmental disease and spasticity, caused a significantly greater ATPase enzyme deficit than the N106K mutation, seen in a patient with isolated dystonia. They cautioned that further studies are needed before genotype-phenotype correlations can be clearly established.

### Pathogenesis

Neilson et al. (2022) noted that ATP5MC1 and ATP5MC2 also encode subunit c. They reasoned that haploinsufficiency of ATP5MC3 would be unlikely to explain the severe phenotypes, because about 83% of active protein would remain. Instead, they hypothesized that the mutant protein interferes with the interaction between F1 and F0. They also suggested that differences in how much the mutant gene is expressed, relative to the other subunit c genes, might influence the phenotype. Zech et al. (2022) suggested that dominant-negative effects are possible, given the mild to moderate defects in ATPase assembly and/or enzyme activity seen in patient cells.

### Animal Model

Neilson et al. (2022) generated transgenic Drosophila that expressed the fly ortholog of ATP5MC3 (CG1746) carrying either the human disease-equivalent mutation (N102K) or an equivalent E. coli mutation (N102E), using the armadillo-GAL4 driver. Both mutant lines moved less well than wildtype flies in a geotaxis assay, and the N102K mutant was the slowest. In fly mitochondria, complex V activity was reduced by 53% in the N102K mutant and by 37% in the N102E mutant. Expression of wildtype protein did not impair mobility.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, ASN106LYS [c.318C>G; p.N106K; NM_001689.4:c.318C>G; NP_001680.1:p.Asn106Lys]

In affected members of a large multigenerational family with autosomal dominant dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia (DYTSPG), originally reported by Gilbert et al. (2009), Neilson et al. (2022) identified a heterozygous c.318C-G transversion in exon 4 of the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in an asn106-to-lys (N106K) substitution in a highly conserved loop. The mutation was found by exome sequencing and cosegregated with disease. It was not found in gnomAD. In fibroblasts from 2 patients, complex V activity, cellular ATP, and oxygen consumption were reduced. Drosophila carrying the equivalent mutation had reduced complex V activity and reduced mobility. Neilson et al. (2022) also found the same mutation in the heterozygous state, arising de novo, in an unrelated boy with upper limb dystonia. ⚠︎

Zech et al. (2022) studied fibroblasts from the de novo N106K patient reported by Neilson et al. (2022), a 12-year-old boy with isolated upper-limb dystonia (patient 8). ATPase activity was slightly but significantly decreased, and oxygen consumption rate was reduced.

**.0002 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, PRO107ALA [c.319C>G; p.P107A; NM_001689.5:c.319C>G; NP_001680.1:p.Pro107Ala]

In a 15-year-old girl (patient 9) with DYTSPG, Zech et al. (2022) identified a de novo heterozygous c.319C-G transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in a pro107-to-ala (P107A) substitution. The girl had delayed milestones, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia with prominent upper-body involvement. The mutation was found by exome sequencing and was not present in gnomAD or local control exomes. It affects a conserved residue at the interaction site between the FO and F1 subcomplexes.

**.0003 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, GLY79VAL [c.236G>T; p.G79V; NM_001689.5:c.236G>T; NP_001680.1:p.Gly79Val]

In a 6-year-old boy (patient 10) with DYTSPG, Zech et al. (2022) identified a de novo heterozygous c.236G-T transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in a gly79-to-val (G79V) substitution. The boy had delayed psychomotor development, lower-extremity spasticity, and elevated blood lactate. The mutation was not present in gnomAD or local control exomes. Structural modeling placed the residue at a contact site between helices. In the patient's fibroblasts, ATPase activity was deficient and assembled ATPase was moderately decreased. The ATPase enzyme deficit was significantly greater than that associated with the N106K mutation.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia (DYTSPG) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the ATP5MC3 gene (602736) on chromosome 2q31.1.

### Description

Early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia (DYTSPG) is an autosomal dominant neurologic disorder that varies in presentation, even within a family. Some affected individuals have progressive dystonia that begins in childhood and can generalize. Others have spastic paraplegia with leg weakness that begins in adulthood and progresses gradually. Focal limb or neck dystonia and spasmodic dysphonia have also been reported. Some patients with de novo mutations have had developmental delay, pyramidal signs, or lower-limb spasticity, and patient fibroblasts have shown reduced mitochondrial ATP synthase (complex V) activity.

### Clinical Features

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported a large kindred from Ohio with autosomal dominant hereditary spastic paraplegia and dystonia. Spasticity and weakness began anywhere from the first year of life to the sixth decade, and dystonia began between the first and third decades. One affected child had generalized dystonia. Affected individuals had no ataxia or apparent cognitive involvement. Neuroimaging and peripheral neurophysiology were normal.

Neilson et al. (2022) reported an autosomal dominant pedigree (Family-1) of 40 individuals, 18 of whom were affected. Affected members had either progressive generalized dystonia beginning in childhood or gradually progressive spastic paraplegia beginning in adulthood, with progressive leg weakness. Some had milder, later-onset focal limb and neck dystonia or spasmodic dysphonia, which the authors suggested might be related to trauma or overuse. The dystonia showed incomplete penetrance, and the spastic paraplegia was not accompanied by other neurologic problems. In the proband, dystonia began in 1 upper limb at age 2 years, then spread to the trunk and all limbs, and he lost the ability to walk. Brain MRI and peripheral nerve physiology were normal. Blood lactate and pyruvate were normal in the 2 individuals tested (the proband with dystonia and his father with spastic paraplegia), but complex V activity was reduced in fibroblasts from both.

Neilson et al. (2022) also reported a boy of European descent (Family-2) whose parents were healthy and unrelated, with no family history of dystonia or related movement disorders. He was first seen at age 11 years with a 4-year history of gradually worsening upper limb dystonia. Brain MRI showed nonspecific mild white-matter changes in both frontoparietal regions and no other abnormalities.

Zech et al. (2022) described 3 unrelated patients with de novo ATP5MC3 mutations. Patient 8 was the boy previously reported by Neilson et al. (2022) (Family-2); at age 12 years, he had isolated upper-limb dystonia. Patient 9, a 15-year-old girl, had a history of milestone delay, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia mainly affecting the upper body. Patient 10, a 6-year-old French boy, had delayed psychomotor development, lower-extremity spasticity, and elevated blood lactate. Zech et al. (2022) combined their 3 patients with the multigenerational family of Neilson et al. (2022). In this combined group, 12 of 18 individuals had dystonia, 17 of 18 had spasticity or pyramidal signs, and 2 of 18 had developmental delay or intellectual disability. ⚠︎

### Biochemical Features

Neilson et al. (2022) studied fibroblasts from the Family-1 proband, who had dystonia, and his affected father, who had spastic paraplegia. Complex I through IV activities were normal. Complex V activity was reduced by 20%, cellular ATP by 20 to 32%, and oxygen consumption by 21 to 25%. Because oxygen consumption and ATP fell in proportion, the results did not support uncoupling of oxidative phosphorylation as the mechanism.

Zech et al. (2022) found a slight but significant decrease in ATPase activity in fibroblasts from patient 8, as well as a reduced oxygen consumption rate. Fibroblasts from patient 10 had deficient ATPase activity, together with a moderate decrease in assembled ATPase on BN-PAGE.

### Inheritance

Gilbert et al. (2009) described the disorder in the Ohio kindred as autosomal dominant and highly penetrant. Neilson et al. (2022) reported an autosomal dominant pedigree in which a heterozygous ATP5MC3 mutation segregated with the disorder. Neilson et al. (2022) and Zech et al. (2022) also found heterozygous de novo ATP5MC3 mutations in sporadic patients.

### Mapping

Gilbert et al. (2009) performed single and multipoint linkage analysis in a large multigenerational Ohio kindred, using microsatellite and SNP array genotyping. They mapped the disease locus to chromosome 2q24-q31, between markers rs1424937 and rs1559510. This region lies proximal to SPG13 and contains no known HSP or dystonia genes. In a secondary analysis looking for candidate regions that segregated with the dystonia phenotype, they found 2 regions with parametric lod scores above 2.0.

### Molecular Genetics

Neilson et al. (2022) noted that linkage analysis in their large family (Family-1) had previously mapped the disorder to chromosome 2q24-q31. Whole-exome sequencing restricted to this region found only 1 shared novel coding variant: a heterozygous c.318C-G mutation in the ATP5MC3 gene, predicting an asn106-to-lys (N106K) substitution. Sanger sequencing showed that only affected family members carried the mutation. In fibroblasts from 2 affected family members, complex V activity was reduced. Flies carrying the equivalent mutation in the Drosophila homolog had reduced mobility and reduced complex V activity.

In the boy from Family-2, parent-offspring trio whole-exome sequencing by Neilson et al. (2022) identified the same N106K mutation in ATP5MC3, heterozygous and de novo. The authors also screened 384 patients with dystonia (362) or hereditary spastic paraplegia (22) for variants in ATP5MC1, ATP5MC2, and ATP5MC3 and found no deleterious variants.

Zech et al. (2022) identified de novo heterozygous missense mutations in ATP5MC3 in 3 unrelated patients with dystonia and/or spasticity: N106K, pro107-to-ala (P107A), and gly79-to-val (G79V). All 3 mutations affected strictly conserved residues in the C-terminal helical motifs of the protein and were absent from gnomAD. Fibroblasts from the patients with N106K and G79V showed reduced ATPase activity.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Zech et al. (2022) found that the G79V mutation in ATP5MC3 caused a significantly greater ATPase enzyme deficit than the N106K mutation. G79V was associated with a more severe neurodevelopmental presentation with spasticity, whereas N106K was associated with isolated dystonia. The authors cautioned that further studies were needed before clear genotype-phenotype correlations could be proposed.

### Pathogenesis

Neilson et al. (2022) noted that subunit c is also encoded by 2 paralogs, ATP5MC1 and ATP5MC2, so haploinsufficiency of ATP5MC3 would be unlikely to explain the severe phenotypes. They hypothesized that the mutant protein interferes with the F1-F0 interaction at a conserved loop. They also suggested that the relative expression of the mutant gene in different tissues may influence which phenotype develops.

Zech et al. (2022) suggested that the ATP5MC3 mutations could act through dominant-negative effects. Mild to moderate defects in ATPase assembly and/or enzyme activity in patient cell lines supported this idea. Structural modeling placed G79V at a contact site between helices and the N106 and P107 residues at the site where the FO and F1 subcomplexes interact.

### Clinical Management

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported that deep brain stimulation markedly improved generalized dystonia in 1 affected child.

Neilson et al. (2022) reported that the Family-1 proband with generalized dystonia received bilateral pallidal deep brain stimulation at age 9 years, which restored his ability to walk and use his limbs. The response remained excellent at age 25 years.

### Population Genetics

Zech et al. (2022) estimated that phenotypes related to mutations in nuclear genes encoding the ATP synthase accounted for 0.3% (5 of 1,950) of patients with suspected mitochondrial disease and 0.2% (2 of 1,012) of patients with dystonia.

### Animal Model

Neilson et al. (2022) expressed the Drosophila homolog ATPsynC (CG1746) with either the mutation equivalent to human N106K (N102K) or the mutation equivalent to E. coli N102E, using the arm-GAL4 driver. Both mutant lines showed reduced mobility in geotaxis assays, with the N102K flies the slowest. Complex V activity was reduced by 53% in N102K flies and by 37% in N102E flies.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DYSTONIA, EARLY-ONSET, AND/OR SPASTIC PARAPLEGIA**  
ATP5MC3, ASN106LYS [c.318C>G; g.176043127G>C; p.N106K]

In affected members of a large autosomal dominant family (Family-1) with early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia, Neilson et al. (2022) identified a heterozygous c.318C-G transversion (NM_001689.4) in the ATP5MC3 gene, predicting an asn106-to-lys (N106K) substitution. The mutation was found by whole-exome sequencing restricted to the region previously linked to chromosome 2q24-q31, and Sanger sequencing showed that only affected family members carried it. Fibroblasts from the proband, who had dystonia, and his father, who had spastic paraplegia, showed a 20% reduction in complex V activity and reduced cellular ATP and oxygen consumption. Drosophila carrying the equivalent mutation (N102K) had reduced mobility and a 53% reduction in complex V activity. In a boy of European descent (Family-2) with upper limb dystonia, Neilson et al. (2022) found the same mutation in the heterozygous state; trio whole-exome sequencing showed that it had arisen de novo.

Zech et al. (2022) included the de novo heterozygous N106K mutation in a previously reported 12-year-old boy (patient 8) with isolated upper-limb dystonia. His fibroblasts showed a slight but significant decrease in ATPase activity and a reduced oxygen consumption rate.

ATP5MC3, GLY79VAL [c.236G>T; p.G79V]

In a 6-year-old French boy (patient 10) with delayed psychomotor development, lower-extremity spasticity, and elevated blood lactate, Zech et al. (2022) identified a de novo heterozygous c.236G-T transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, predicting a gly79-to-val (G79V) substitution. The mutation affected a strictly conserved residue and was absent from gnomAD. The patient's fibroblasts had deficient ATPase activity and a moderate decrease in assembled ATPase on BN-PAGE. The enzyme deficit was significantly greater than that caused by N106K. Structural modeling placed G79V at a contact site between helices. ⚠︎

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia (DYTSPG) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the ATP5MC3 gene (602736) on chromosome 2q31. ⚠︎

### Description

Early-onset dystonia and/or spastic paraplegia is an autosomal dominant neurologic disorder. Affected individuals have spasticity or pyramidal signs, dystonia, or both. Dystonia ranges from isolated upper-limb involvement to generalized dystonia. A few patients also have developmental delay or intellectual disability (Gilbert et al., 2009; Zech et al., 2022).

### Clinical Features

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported a large multigenerational kindred from Ohio with a highly penetrant autosomal dominant neurologic disorder that caused spasticity and dystonia. Onset of spasticity and weakness ranged from the first year of life to the sixth decade. Onset of dystonia ranged from the first to the third decade. Affected individuals had no ataxia and no apparent cognitive involvement. Neuroimaging and peripheral neurophysiology were normal.

Zech et al. (2022) described 3 unrelated patients (patients 8 to 10) with de novo heterozygous ATP5MC3 mutations. Patient 8 was a previously reported 12-year-old boy with isolated upper-limb dystonia. Patient 9 was a 15-year-old girl with a history of delayed milestones, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia that mainly affected the upper body. Patient 10 was a 6-year-old boy with delayed psychomotor development, lower-limb spasticity, and raised blood lactate. Zech et al. (2022) combined these 3 patients with a previously reported multigenerational family in which 15 members had clinical information. Of these 18 individuals, 12 had dystonia, 17 had spasticity or pyramidal signs, and 2 had developmental delay or intellectual disability. Ten individuals had a dystonia-predominant syndrome. The authors noted that patients 9 and 10 confirmed spastic symptoms in independent families. These 2 patients were also the first to show neurodevelopmental comorbidity, which pointed to relevant phenotypic differences between unrelated families.

### Inheritance

Gilbert et al. (2009) found that the combined spastic paraplegia and dystonia in a large multigenerational Ohio kindred followed an autosomal dominant pattern of inheritance with high penetrance. Zech et al. (2022) identified de novo heterozygous ATP5MC3 mutations in 3 unrelated patients.

### Mapping

Gilbert et al. (2009) performed single-point and multipoint linkage analysis in the Ohio kindred, using microsatellite and SNP array genotyping. They mapped the disease locus to chromosome 2q24-q31, between flanking markers rs1424937 and rs1559510. The region lies proximal to SPG13 and contains no known hereditary spastic paraplegia (HSP) or dystonia genes. In a secondary analysis, they searched for candidate genes that segregated with the dystonia phenotype and found 2 candidate regions with parametric lod scores above 2.0.

### Heterogeneity

On the basis of the clinical presentation and the linkage results, Gilbert et al. (2009) concluded that this combined spastic paraplegia and dystonia was a novel neurologic disorder, distinct from known HSP and dystonia loci.

### Molecular Genetics

Zech et al. (2022) used exome sequencing of mitochondrial disease and dystonia cohorts to identify de novo heterozygous missense mutations in the ATP5MC3 gene in 3 unrelated patients. Patient 8, a 12-year-old boy with isolated upper-limb dystonia, carried an N106K mutation. The authors noted that this mutation had also been found in an unrelated multigenerational family in which 18 of 40 relatives had dystonia and/or spasticity. Patient 9, a girl with delayed milestones, pyramidal signs, and generalized dystonia, carried a P107A mutation. Patient 10, a boy with delayed psychomotor development, lower-limb spasticity, and raised blood lactate, carried a G79V mutation. None of the 3 mutations was present in gnomAD. Structural modeling placed Asn106 and Pro107 at the interaction site between the FO and F1 subcomplexes, and predicted that G79V would disrupt a contact between helices in the c-ring. In fibroblasts from patient 8, ATPase activity was slightly but significantly decreased and the oxygen consumption rate was reduced. In fibroblasts from patient 10, ATPase enzyme activity was deficient, and BN-PAGE showed a moderate decrease in assembled ATPase. All 3 substitutions affected strictly conserved residues that lie close together in the C-terminal helical motifs of the protein, and 2 of them were at directly neighboring nucleotides. Zech et al. (2022) concluded that these findings broadened the range of disease-associated ATP5MC3 genotypes and strengthened the link between the gene and disease.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Zech et al. (2022) found that the G79V mutation caused a significantly larger ATPase enzyme deficit than the N106K mutation. G79V was found in a patient with a more severe presentation combining neurodevelopmental disease and spasticity, and N106K in a patient with isolated dystonia. The authors cautioned that further studies were needed before genotype-phenotype correlations could be clearly established.

### Pathogenesis

Zech et al. (2022) stated that the mechanism by which ATP5MC3 variants cause disease is not fully understood. Several patient cell lines showed mild to moderate defects in ATPase assembly and/or enzyme activity, and the authors suggested that dominant-negative effects are possible. ⚠︎

### Clinical Management

Gilbert et al. (2009) reported that deep brain stimulation markedly improved generalized dystonia in 1 affected child from the Ohio kindred.

### Population Genetics

Zech et al. (2022) estimated that phenotypes caused by mutations in nuclear ATPase subunit genes accounted for 0.3% (5/1,950) of individuals in a cohort with suspected mitochondrial disease. In a dystonia cohort, they accounted for 0.2% (2/1,012) of individuals.

In this patient, they identified a de novo heterozygous c.318C-G transversion in the ATP5MC3 gene, resulting in an asn106-to-lys (N106K) substitution. The mutation had also been found in an unrelated multigenerational family in which 18 of 40 relatives had dystonia and/or spasticity. It was not present in gnomAD. Structural modeling placed Asn106 at the interaction site between the FO and F1 subcomplexes. In the patient's fibroblasts, ATPase activity was slightly but significantly decreased and the oxygen consumption rate was reduced. The ATPase enzyme deficit was significantly smaller than that caused by the G79V mutation.

The mutation was not present in gnomAD or in local control exomes and was predicted to be deleterious. It alters a conserved residue at the interaction site between the FO and F1 subcomplexes.

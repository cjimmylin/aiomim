### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that axonal Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2EE (CMT2EE) is caused by homozygous mutation in the MPV17 gene (137960) on chromosome 2p23.

### Description

CMT2EE is an autosomal recessive axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy with juvenile or adult onset. It causes distal muscle weakness and atrophy, mainly in the lower limbs, along with distal sensory loss and absent reflexes. Unlike the hepatocerebral disorders caused by MPV17 mutations, it does not involve the liver or brain (Choi et al., 2015; Baumann et al., 2019).

### Clinical Features

Choi et al. (2015) reported 2 unrelated Korean patients, each born to healthy nonconsanguineous parents, with axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy. Patient 1 was a 34-year-old woman with normal early motor milestones. She first noticed weakness of the distal lower limbs at 9 years of age and began walking with short leg braces at 12 years. At age 34, she had bilateral distal muscle weakness and atrophy, predominantly in the lower limbs. She also had severe atrophy of the intrinsic hand, foot, and calf muscles, flexion deformities of the interphalangeal joints, ankle joint deformity, and scoliosis, but she could walk with orthopedic assistance. Vibration and position senses were more severely impaired than pain and touch, and knee and ankle jerks were absent. She had no pyramidal or cerebellar signs and none of the other features of Navajo neurohepatopathy, such as growth retardation, gastrointestinal dysmotility, hepatomegaly, cognitive impairment, ophthalmoplegia, corneal scarring, or hypoglycemic attacks. Her serum lactate was elevated (2.1 mmol/L), but serum liver enzymes, glucose, and pyruvate were normal. Patient 2 was a 22-year-old man who first had gait and balance problems at age 13 and began walking with short leg braces at 20 years. At 22 years, he had bilateral distal weakness and atrophy, flat feet, atrophy of the intrinsic hand, foot, and calf muscles, greater loss of vibration and position sense than of pain and touch, and absent deep tendon reflexes. His serum lactate was slightly elevated (1.9 mmol/L).

Electrophysiologic studies gave similar results in both patients reported by Choi et al. (2015). Sural nerve sensory action potentials were lost early in the disease, and no sensory responses could be elicited. Motor responses from the peroneal and tibial nerves were also not elicited, and patient 1 had reduced compound muscle action potentials and motor conduction velocities in the median and ulnar nerves. Visual and brainstem auditory evoked potentials were normal. A distal sural nerve biopsy from patient 1 at age 34 showed complete loss of large and medium-sized myelinated fibers (10/mm2), with only a few small myelinated fibers remaining. Unmyelinated axons showed atrophy and occasional vesicular changes, and there was no evidence of demyelination, remyelination, or onion bulb formation. A vastus lateralis muscle biopsy from patient 2 at age 22 showed increased irregular staining on NADH-TR and SDH, a few ragged-red fibers, and focal subsarcolemmal accumulation of enlarged mitochondria. Lower leg MRI in both patients showed severe atrophy and fatty replacement of the soleus muscles, with relative sparing of the tibialis posterior and lateral gastrocnemius. Choi et al. (2015) noted that this pattern was similar to that seen in CMT2A. Brain MRI was normal in both patients. The authors noted that, in contrast to previously reported patients with Navajo neurohepatopathy, these patients had no liver or brain abnormalities at ages 34 and 22 years. Because the patients were young, the authors considered that other organ systems might eventually become involved.

Baumann et al. (2019) reported 5 patients from 2 unrelated families with juvenile- or adult-onset axonal sensorimotor neuropathy without hepatocerebral involvement.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CMT2EE in the families reported by Choi et al. (2015) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Affected individuals were homozygous for the mutation, and a mutant allele was putatively inherited from each parent. Baumann et al. (2019) also found homozygous MPV17 variants in affected individuals from 2 families.

### Molecular Genetics

Choi et al. (2015) performed whole-exome sequencing on 3 members of each of 2 unrelated Korean families with autosomal recessive axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy. In the affected individual in each family, they identified the same homozygous c.122G-A transition in the MPV17 gene, resulting in an arg41-to-gln (R41Q) substitution. The mutation was confirmed by Sanger sequencing. It was not found in 300 controls, in in-house exome data (n = 302), in dbSNP142, or in the 1000 Genomes Project database, but it was present at very low frequency in the Exome Sequencing Project and ExAC databases. The affected residue was conserved across vertebrate species. In NSC34 mouse motor neuronal cells, siRNA knockdown of Mpv17 significantly reduced cell proliferation and viability against reactive oxygen species and affected the mitochondrial OXPHOS system. Expression of the R41Q mutant protein significantly inhibited cell proliferation compared with controls. The authors suggested that both loss of normal MPV17 function and detrimental effects of the mutant protein might affect neuronal function.

In affected members of 2 unrelated families with axonal sensorimotor neuropathy, Baumann et al. (2019) identified homozygous MPV17 variants. Patients in the first family carried the R41Q mutation. Patients in the second family carried a novel c.376-9T-G near-splice variant, which was shown to result in an in-frame deletion of 11 amino acids.

### Pathogenesis

Choi et al. (2015) found that overexpression of the R41Q mutant in NSC34 cells inhibited cell proliferation, similar to the R50Q and L143* mutants. Overexpression of an R41W mutant also affected cell proliferation. Noting that patients with mutations at arg41 had a milder phenotype than patients with other MPV17 mutations, the authors suggested that mutations at arg41 mainly cause peripheral neuropathy. ⚠︎

### Diagnosis

Choi et al. (2015) recommended screening for MPV17 mutations in patients with axonal peripheral neuropathy. Baumann et al. (2019) concluded that MPV17 mutations should be considered in patients with pure, nonsyndromic axonal neuropathy.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CHARCOT-MARIE-TOOTH DISEASE, AXONAL, TYPE 2EE**  
MPV17, ARG41GLN [c.122G>A; p.R41Q; p.Arg41Gln]

In 2 unrelated Korean patients with axonal Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2EE (CMT2EE), Choi et al. (2015) identified a homozygous c.122G-A transition in the MPV17 gene, resulting in an arg41-to-gln (R41Q) substitution at a residue conserved across vertebrates. The mutation was found by whole-exome sequencing and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. It was not found in 300 controls, but it was present at very low frequency in the Exome Sequencing Project (0.00008) and ExAC (0.00002471) databases. In NSC34 mouse motor neuronal cells, expression of the mutant protein significantly inhibited cell proliferation.

Baumann et al. (2019) identified the R41Q mutation in the homozygous state in affected members of a family with axonal sensorimotor neuropathy without hepatocerebral involvement.

In affected members of a family with axonal sensorimotor neuropathy without hepatocerebral involvement (CMT2EE), Baumann et al. (2019) identified a homozygous c.376-9T-G near-splice variant in the MPV17 gene. The variant was shown to result in an in-frame deletion of 11 amino acids.

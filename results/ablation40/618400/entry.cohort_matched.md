### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive axonal Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2EE (CMT2EE) is caused by homozygous mutation in the MPV17 gene (137960) on chromosome 2p23.3. ⚠︎

### Description

CMT2EE is an autosomal recessive axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy with juvenile or adult onset. Affected individuals have distal muscle weakness and atrophy, mainly in the lower limbs, along with distal sensory loss and absent tendon reflexes. In the patients reported by Choi et al. (2015) and Baumann et al. (2019), the neuropathy occurred without the liver and brain involvement seen in Navajo neurohepatopathy, another disorder caused by MPV17 mutation. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Blakely et al. (2012) reported a 21-year-old Pakistani man born to nonconsanguineous parents. Neurologic symptoms started at age 14 years with gradually worsening walking difficulty, which progressed over several years to bilateral foot drop requiring orthoses. He had distal numbness of the feet and hands, distal muscle wasting of the hands and lower limbs, clawed toes, an unsteady gait, decreased muscle tone, severe distal weakness, hip flexor weakness, and absent tendon reflexes. He did not have cognitive impairment or pes cavus. Pin-prick and light touch sensation were decreased below the knees, vibration sense was impaired to the ankles, and joint position sense was absent at the toes. Nerve conduction studies showed an axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy. Sural nerve biopsy showed a severe chronic axonal neuropathy with loss of myelinated axons. He also had findings outside the peripheral nerve: - liver: mild hepatomegaly, elevated gamma-GT, hepatic steatosis, and early bridging hepatic fibrosis - CSF: elevated protein and lactate - brain MRI: leukoencephalopathy, with T2 hyperintensity of the cerebral and cerebellar white matter Skeletal muscle showed ragged-red fibers, about 20% COX-deficient fibers, and abnormal mitochondria with parking lot inclusions. The authors noted that his phenotype resembled the classic form of Navajo neurohepatopathy. They considered the muscle involvement unusual, with ragged-red fibers and a mosaic pattern of COX deficiency. ⚠︎

Choi et al. (2015) reported 2 patients from 2 unrelated Korean families (FC26 and FC355), each born to healthy nonconsanguineous parents. Patient 1 (FC26, II-1) was a 34-year-old woman who first noticed weakness of the distal lower limbs at age 9 years. She had distal muscle atrophy, mainly in the lower limbs, including atrophy of the intrinsic hand muscles and calves. She also had flexion deformities of the interphalangeal joints, ankle joint deformity, and scoliosis, although she could walk with orthopedic assistance. Vibration and position senses were more severely affected than pain and touch senses, and knee and ankle jerks were absent. She had no pyramidal or cerebellar signs, hepatomegaly, cognitive impairment, ophthalmoplegia, hypoglycemic attacks, or growth retardation. Serum lactate was elevated and serum liver enzymes were normal. Sensory nerve action potentials were absent. CMAPs were decreased in the median and ulnar nerves and absent in the peroneal and tibial nerves. Sural nerve biopsy showed loss of large and medium-sized myelinated fibers. Patient 2 (FC355, III-2) was a 22-year-old man whose early motor milestones were normal. He first noticed gait and balance problems at age 13. He had distal muscle weakness and atrophy, including atrophy of the intrinsic hand, foot, and calf muscles. He also had bilateral flat feet, impaired vibration and position sense, and absent deep tendon reflexes in all limbs. Laboratory findings were normal except for slightly elevated serum lactate (1.9 mmol/L). Sensory nerve action potentials and peroneal and tibial CMAPs were absent. Muscle biopsy showed ragged-red fibers and subsarcolemmal accumulation of enlarged mitochondria. In both patients, brain MRI was normal and lower leg MRI showed selective fatty infiltration of the soleus muscles, which the authors noted was similar to CMT2A. Unlike previously reported patients with Navajo neurohepatopathy, both patients had only axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy, with no liver or brain abnormalities at ages 34 and 22 years.

Baumann et al. (2019) reported 5 patients from 2 unrelated families with juvenile- or adult-onset sensorimotor axonal neuropathy without hepatocerebral involvement.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CMT2EE is autosomal recessive. In the 2 unrelated families reported by Choi et al. (2015), affected individuals born to healthy nonconsanguineous parents were homozygous for an MPV17 mutation, and a mutant allele was putatively inherited from each parent. Baumann et al. (2019) also found homozygous MPV17 variants in affected members of 2 families.

### Molecular Genetics

Blakely et al. (2012) found a homozygous missense variant in the MPV17 gene in a 21-year-old Pakistani man with axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy and leukoencephalopathy. They reported it as c.2898C-T, resulting in a pro98-to-leu (P98L) substitution, and noted that it had previously been reported as a recessive mutation associated with mtDNA depletion in liver. Parental samples were not available to confirm carrier status. The patient's skeletal muscle had multiple mtDNA deletions with normal mtDNA copy number. Single-fiber real-time PCR of COX-deficient fibers showed high levels of a clonally expanded mtDNA deletion involving MTND4. The authors noted that multiple deletions had not previously been reported in patients with MPV17 mutations.

Choi et al. (2015) used whole-exome sequencing in 2 unrelated Korean families with autosomal recessive axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy. They identified a homozygous c.122G-A transition in the MPV17 gene, resulting in an arg41-to-gln (R41Q) substitution. A mutant allele was putatively inherited from each parent. The mutation was absent from 300 controls and from in-house exome data, but it was present at very low frequency in the ESP and ExAC databases. Overexpression of the R41Q mutant protein in NSC34 mouse motor neuronal cells significantly inhibited cell proliferation.

In 5 patients from 2 unrelated families with juvenile- or adult-onset sensorimotor axonal neuropathy without hepatocerebral involvement, Baumann et al. (2019) found homozygous MPV17 variants. Affected members of one family carried the previously reported c.122G-A (R41Q) mutation. Affected members of the other family carried a novel near-splice variant, c.376-9T-G, which was shown to result in an in-frame deletion of 11 amino acids.

### Pathogenesis

Blakely et al. (2012) concluded that the MPV17 protein is closely involved in both mtDNA replication and repair. They also concluded that it is associated with both quantitative and qualitative mtDNA abnormalities.

Choi et al. (2015) found that siRNA knockdown of Mpv17 in NSC34 mouse motor neuronal cells significantly reduced cell proliferation and viability against reactive oxygen species, and also affected the mitochondrial OXPHOS system. Overexpression of the R41Q, R50Q, L143*, and R41W mutant proteins each significantly inhibited cell proliferation in NSC34 cells. The authors suggested that both loss of normal MPV17 function and detrimental gain-of-function effects of the mutant protein might impair neuronal function. They also suggested that mutations at arg41 mainly cause peripheral neuropathy. ⚠︎

### Diagnosis

Baumann et al. (2019) concluded that MPV17 mutations should be considered in patients with pure, nonsyndromic axonal neuropathy.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

They reported it as c.2898C-T, resulting in a pro98-to-leu (P98L) substitution. Starting at age 14 years, he developed an axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy, with foot drop, distal wasting, and areflexia. He also had hepatic steatosis and fibrosis and leukoencephalopathy.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive axonal Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2EE (CMT2EE) is caused by homozygous mutation in the MPV17 gene (137960) on chromosome 2p23. ⚠︎

### Description

Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2EE (CMT2EE) is an autosomal recessive axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy with juvenile or adult onset. Affected individuals have distal muscle weakness and atrophy, mostly in the lower limbs, along with distal sensory loss and absent reflexes. Unlike patients with Navajo neurohepatopathy, who also have MPV17 mutations, they do not have liver or brain involvement (Choi et al., 2015; Baumann et al., 2019). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Blakely et al. (2012) reported a 21-year-old Pakistani man born to nonconsanguineous parents. Walking difficulties began at age 14 years and progressed over several years to bilateral foot drop requiring orthoses. He also developed symmetric distal numbness and subjective coldness in both feet, ascending to the knees, with milder involvement of the hands. On examination he had wasting of the hand and distal lower limb muscles, clawed toes without pes cavus or nerve thickening, a slow unsteady gait, decreased tone, and severe distal weakness. Power was MRC grade 0/5 in the left abductor pollicis brevis, both first dorsal interossei, and both anterior tibialis muscles. Hip flexion weakness was symmetric (MRC grade 4-/5). All tendon reflexes were absent. Pin-prick and light-touch perception was reduced distal to the knees, vibration sense was impaired to the ankles, and joint position sense at the toes was absent. Nerve conduction studies confirmed an axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy. Sural nerve biopsy showed a severe chronic axonal neuropathy with only occasional surviving myelinated axons. Unlike later reported patients, this patient also had central nervous system and liver involvement. Cranial MRI showed diffuse T2-weighted hyperintensity in the cerebral white matter and cerebellum, suggestive of leukoencephalopathy, whereas an MRI at age 9 years had been normal. CSF protein (1963 mg/L) and lactate (4.3 mmol/L) were increased. He had no significant cognitive impairment. He had mild hepatomegaly, abnormal liver echogenicity, and a minimally raised gamma-GT. Liver biopsy showed patchy steatosis (about 20%) with early bridging fibrosis, but clinically he had no significant chronic liver disease. His sister had developed a progressive, fatal hepatitis at age 9 years. ⚠︎

Choi et al. (2015) reported 2 patients with axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy from 2 unrelated Korean families. Each was born to healthy nonconsanguineous parents. Patient 1 was a 34-year-old woman with normal early motor milestones. She first noticed weakness of the distal lower limbs at age 9 years and began walking with short leg braces at age 12. At age 34 she had distal weakness and atrophy, mainly in the lower limbs. Atrophy of the intrinsic hand, foot, and calf muscles was severe, and she had flexion deformities of the interphalangeal joints, ankle joint deformity, and scoliosis. Vibration and position sense were more severely affected than pain and touch, and knee and ankle jerks were absent. Patient 2 was a 22-year-old man who developed gait and balance problems at age 13 years and began walking with short leg braces at age 20. He had distal weakness and atrophy, atrophy of the intrinsic hand, foot, and calf muscles, flat feet, worse loss of vibration and position sense than of pain and touch, and absent deep tendon reflexes. Neither patient had other features of Navajo neurohepatopathy, such as hepatomegaly, cognitive impairment, ophthalmoplegia, or corneal scarring. Serum lactate was slightly raised (2.1 and 1.9 mmol/L, respectively). Electrophysiologic findings were similar in the 2 patients. Sural sensory nerve action potentials were lost early in the disease, and no sensory responses could be elicited. Median and ulnar motor conduction velocities and CMAPs were decreased, and peroneal and tibial CMAPs were absent. Visual and brainstem auditory evoked potentials were normal, and brain MRI showed no abnormality. Muscle MRI in both patients showed severe atrophy and fatty replacement mainly in the soleus, with relative sparing of the tibialis posterior and lateral gastrocnemius. Thigh and hip muscles were much less affected, consistent with length-dependent axonal degeneration. In patient 1, sural nerve biopsy at age 34 showed almost complete loss of large and medium-sized myelinated fibers, atrophy and vesicular changes in unmyelinated axons, and no demyelination, remyelination, or onion bulb formation. In patient 2, vastus lateralis biopsy at age 22 showed a few ragged-red fibers and subsarcolemmal accumulation of enlarged mitochondria on electron microscopy.

Baumann et al. (2019) described 5 patients from 2 unrelated families with juvenile- or adult-onset sensorimotor axonal neuropathy. None had hepatocerebral involvement.

### Biochemical Features

In skeletal muscle from their patient, Blakely et al. (2012) found ragged-red fibers (about 1% of the biopsy) and about 20% COX-deficient fibers in a mosaic pattern. Electron microscopy showed subsarcolemmal accumulation of abnormal mitochondria with parking lot-like inclusions. The mtDNA copy number in muscle was normal, but long-range PCR showed multiple mtDNA deletions. Single-fiber real-time PCR showed high levels of clonally expanded mtDNA deletions involving the MTND4 gene in COX-deficient fibers. The authors noted that multiple mtDNA deletions had not previously been found in patients with MPV17 mutations.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CMT2EE in the families reported by Choi et al. (2015) and Baumann et al. (2019) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Choi et al. (2015) described 2 Korean families as autosomal recessive; in each, the affected individual was homozygous for an MPV17 mutation and born to healthy nonconsanguineous parents.

### Molecular Genetics

Blakely et al. (2012) sequenced POLG, DGUOK, and MPV17 in a Pakistani man with axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy, leukoencephalopathy, and liver involvement. They identified a homozygous pro98-to-leu (P98L) missense mutation in MPV17, which affects a residue conserved from human to yeast. The nucleotide change was reported as c.2898C-T in the text and as c.298C-T in a figure legend. The mutation had previously been reported as a recessive mutation associated with mtDNA depletion in liver. The parents were nonconsanguineous but of the same Asian ethnicity, and the authors considered a shared founder most likely. Parental samples were not available to confirm carrier status.

In 2 unrelated Korean patients with CMT2EE, Choi et al. (2015) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a homozygous c.122G-A transition in the MPV17 gene, resulting in an arg41-to-gln (R41Q) substitution at a residue conserved across vertebrates. The mutation was confirmed by Sanger sequencing, and each parent was presumed to carry 1 mutant allele. It was not found in 300 controls, in-house exome data (n = 302), dbSNP142, or the 1000 Genomes Project database. It was present at very low frequency in the Exome Sequencing Project (0.00008) and ExAC (0.00002471) databases. Knockdown of Mpv17 with siRNA in NSC34 mouse motor neuronal cells significantly reduced proliferation and viability under H2O2 treatment and affected OXPHOS levels. Overexpression of the R41Q mutant significantly inhibited cell proliferation, as did the R50Q and L143* mutants. The authors suggested that both loss of normal MPV17 function and detrimental effects of the mutant protein might affect neuronal function.

In 5 patients from 2 unrelated families with sensorimotor axonal neuropathy, Baumann et al. (2019) identified homozygous mutations in the MPV17 gene. Affected members of the first family carried the previously reported c.122G-A (R41Q) mutation. Affected members of the second family carried a novel near-splice mutation, c.376-9T-G, which was shown to result in an in-frame deletion of 11 amino acids.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Blakely et al. (2012) considered the phenotype of their patient, who was homozygous for MPV17 P98L, similar to the classic form of Navajo neurohepatopathy. They noted that, like the p.Arg50Gln Navajo neurohepatopathy mutation, P98L had been listed among MPV17 mutations that may be associated with longer survival.

Choi et al. (2015) noted that previously reported patients with MPV17 mutations and neuropathy also had hepatopathy or brain involvement, whereas their 2 patients with the R41Q mutation had only axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy at ages 34 and 22 years. They pointed out that the patients were young and that other organ systems might eventually become involved. They also noted that overexpression of a different mutation at the same residue, R41W, which had been reported by others in patients with a milder phenotype, also impaired proliferation of NSC34 cells. They suggested that mutations at arg41 predominantly cause peripheral neuropathy.

## AMEGAKARYOCYTIC THROMBOCYTOPENIA, CONGENITAL, 2; CAMT2

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia-2 (CAMT2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the THPO gene (600044) on chromosome 3q27.1.

### Description

Congenital amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia-2 is an autosomal recessive disorder. It presents with severe thrombocytopenia and markedly reduced bone marrow megakaryocytes, which can progress to trilineage bone marrow failure (Seo et al., 2017; Pecci et al., 2018). The bone marrow failure does not respond to hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, but patients have responded to THPO receptor agonists (Seo et al., 2017; Pecci et al., 2018; Capaci et al., 2023).

### Clinical Features

Dasouki et al. (2013) reported 2 sibs from a Micronesian family with idiopathic autosomal recessive aplastic anemia. Both parents and a sib who were heterozygous carriers had reduced platelet counts. A sib with the wildtype sequence had a normal platelet count.

Seo et al. (2017) reported 5 individuals from 3 unrelated families with severe thrombocytopenia that progressed to trilineage bone marrow failure. Four of the children received hematopoietic stem cell transplants. All 4 had poor graft function and persistent severe cytopenias, even after repeated transplants from different donors.

Pecci et al. (2018) reported 3 sibs with congenital amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia, born to Egyptian parents who were first cousins. The proband, a boy, presented at 3.5 years of age with ecchymoses. He had very severe thrombocytopenia (platelets 3 x 10(9)/l), hyporegenerative anemia, and severe neutropenia. Bone marrow was hypocellular with very rare immature megakaryocytes, and the karyotype was normal. He also had preauricular skin tags and a left divergent squint. After Fanconi anemia and PNH were excluded, he was initially diagnosed with idiopathic aplastic anemia. He went on to have recurrent major hemorrhages and severe infections that required multiple transfusions. His brother had severe isolated thrombocytopenia (26 x 10(9)/l) at 7 months of age, with markedly reduced bone marrow megakaryocytes, and developed recurrent spontaneous bleeding at 1.6 years. A younger sister developed a hematoma at a vaccination site at 2 months of age and was found to have anemia and severe thrombocytopenia. Platelet size and blood cell morphology were normal. Because the oldest sib had trilineage hypoplasia, Pecci et al. (2018) suggested that the hematologic picture worsens with age. They noted that the phenotype could not be distinguished from CAMT caused by MPL mutations.

Capaci et al. (2023) reported a boy born to consanguineous Muslim Palestinian parents who were first cousins. He came to medical attention at 3.5 years with easy bruising and thrombocytopenia (21 x 10(9)/L), and his mean platelet volume was normal. His platelet count shortly after birth had been 70 x 10(9)/L. At age 2 years, when he was diagnosed with urethral stenosis, he had severe thrombocytopenia (20 x 10(9)/L). Bone marrow was normocellular, with severe megakaryocyte hypoplasia and mild dyserythropoiesis. Apart from mild occasional neutropenia, he had no other cytopenias. Chromosomal breakage analysis excluded Fanconi anemia.

### Biochemical Features

Seo et al. (2017) found no THPO in the serum of patients carrying either the R99W or the R157X mutation.

Before treatment, Pecci et al. (2018) found normal serum THPO levels in the 2 older affected sibs: 10.9 pg/ml in the proband and 14.1 pg/ml in his brother, compared with 6.9 to 54.4 pg/ml in controls. Serum THPO is markedly elevated in other forms of marrow aplasia, including MPL-related CAMT, so these levels were inappropriately low.

Capaci et al. (2023) found a serum THPO concentration of 126 pg/mL in their proband (reference 15-80 pg/mL for children). This was only slightly elevated despite the severely reduced megakaryocyte and platelet mass. By comparison, THPO levels are expected to be markedly elevated in amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia: the median was 1,493 pg/mL in 56 patients with CAMT due to MPL mutations.

### Inheritance

Dasouki et al. (2013), Seo et al. (2017), Pecci et al. (2018), and Capaci et al. (2023) established autosomal recessive inheritance of CAMT2: affected individuals were homozygous for THPO mutations. Heterozygous carriers can have mild thrombocytopenia. In the family reported by Dasouki et al. (2013), both heterozygous parents and a heterozygous sib had reduced platelet counts. In the family reported by Capaci et al. (2023), the heterozygous mother and younger sib of the homozygous proband had asymptomatic mild thrombocytopenia. The mother's count was around 80 x 10(9)/L and worsened during pregnancies, and the sib's count was 130 x 10(9)/L at birth. Capaci et al. (2023) considered this consistent with mild thrombocytopenia in carriers of monoallelic THPO loss-of-function variants.

### Molecular Genetics

In 2 Micronesian sibs with autosomal recessive aplastic anemia, Dasouki et al. (2013) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a homozygous c.112C-T mutation in the THPO gene. The mutation results in an arg38-to-cys (R38C) substitution in the receptor-binding domain; excluding the 21-amino acid signal peptide, it is designated R17C. In silico modeling predicted that the introduced fifth cysteine may disrupt normal disulfide bonding among the 4 conserved cysteines of the receptor-binding domain and impair receptor binding. Media containing the mutant THPO had a 2- to 3-fold reduced ability to sustain proliferation of THPO-dependent UT7-TPO cells. The authors concluded that the mutation is a partial loss-of-function allele.

Using exome and targeted sequencing, Seo et al. (2017) identified a homozygous R99W mutation in the THPO gene in affected children from 2 of 3 families with thrombocytopenia progressing to bone marrow failure. The affected child in the third family was homozygous for an R157X nonsense mutation in THPO. In all of these patients, THPO was absent from the serum.

Pecci et al. (2018) sequenced MPL and THPO in an Egyptian proband with CAMT and found no MPL variants. They identified a homozygous c.355C-T variant in THPO, resulting in an arg119-to-cys (R119C) substitution. The variant segregated with the disease: both affected sibs were also homozygous, and the parents were heterozygous. It was absent from the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, and ExAC databases, and most in silico tools predicted it to be pathogenic. In transfected HEK293T cells, the mutant was expressed at levels similar to or higher than wildtype and was equally stable. However, its concentration in culture supernatants was about 23-fold lower, indicating impaired secretion. Tested at equal concentrations, the mutant had about 50% of wildtype activity in stimulating proliferation of THPO-dependent UT7-TPO cells and in activating STAT5, ERK1/2, and AKT. The previously reported R38C mutant behaved similarly.

By whole-exome sequencing in a Palestinian boy with CAMT, Capaci et al. (2023) identified a homozygous c.-323C-T substitution in the promoter region of the THPO gene, 45 nucleotides upstream of the transcription start site. The parents and a younger sib were heterozygous, and 2 healthy sibs were homozygous wildtype. The variant was in dbSNP but not in gnomAD, and it lies in a region conserved across species. No variants were found in MPL, MECOM, HOXA11, RBM8A, or other bone marrow failure genes. The variant abolished predicted ETS1 and STAT4 binding sites. In luciferase assays in HEK293T cells, the mutant promoter reduced reporter activity by almost 80%. Silencing ETS1 and/or STAT4 reduced the activity of the wildtype promoter. Chromatin immunoprecipitation showed reduced binding of these transcription factors, at least STAT4, to the mutant promoter.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

In their Micronesian family, Dasouki et al. (2013) found that the partial loss-of-function R17C allele of THPO caused aplastic anemia in the homozygous state and mild thrombocytopenia in the heterozygous state.

### Pathogenesis

Seo et al. (2017) concluded that biallelic loss-of-function mutations in THPO cause bone marrow failure. They proposed that the bone marrow failure does not respond to transplant because the mechanism lies outside the hematopoietic cells. They stated that this supports a critical role of the MPL-THPO pathway in hematopoiesis.

In HepG2 hepatocyte cells, Capaci et al. (2023) showed that knockdown of ETS1 and STAT4 strongly reduced endogenous THPO mRNA and protein. Proximity ligation and coimmunoprecipitation showed that ETS1 and STAT4 interact, suggesting that they form a transcriptional complex on THPO regulatory sequences. Knockdown of STAT3 also reduced THPO expression, although no STAT3 binding site was predicted in the promoter.

### Diagnosis

Pecci et al. (2018) suggested that measuring serum THPO could help distinguish THPO-related CAMT from MPL-related CAMT, because serum THPO is markedly elevated in MPL-related CAMT but was normal in their THPO-mutant patients. Capaci et al. (2023) similarly suggested that a normal serum THPO level in amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia should prompt suspicion of a THPO defect.

### Clinical Management

Seo et al. (2017) reported that 2 surviving patients showed improvement in trilineage hematopoiesis after treatment with a THPO receptor agonist.

Pecci et al. (2018) reported that their proband had not responded to cyclosporine A or oxymetholone. He was started on romiplostim at 4.4 years of age and had a rapid trilineage response, with no further bleeding, infections, or transfusions over 6.5 years of follow-up. Both of his sibs also responded to romiplostim. The brother achieved platelet counts of 30 to 100 x 10(9)/l and remained transfusion independent over 6.3 years. The authors noted that hematopoietic stem cell transplantation is not expected to be effective in patients with THPO mutations.

Capaci et al. (2023) treated their proband with eltrombopag, starting at 1.4 mg/kg/day. His platelet count stabilized around 40 x 10(9)/L after 1 month. After the dose was increased to 2.8 mg/kg/day, the count rose gradually to 125 x 10(9)/L after 6 months, and the easy bruising resolved. This was the first report of eltrombopag therapy in a patient with a THPO mutation. ⚠︎

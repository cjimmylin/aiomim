### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII (OFD18) is caused by homozygous mutation in the IFT57 gene (606621) on chromosome 3q13.

### Description

Orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII is a form of oral-facial-digital syndrome (OFDS) with skeletal dysplasia. It is marked by oral, facial and digital malformations, including median pseudocleft of the upper lip, extra oral frenulae and polydactyly. It also includes short stature with acromelic or mesomelic limb shortening and brachymesophalangia. The features overlap with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome (EVCS), but affected individuals do not have short ribs or a narrow or deformed thorax (Phadke et al., 1999; Thevenon et al., 2016). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Phadke et al. (1999) reported a consanguineous family with what they called a new variety of oral-facial-digital syndrome. Affected members had oral, facial and digital malformations, along with acromelic short stature and genu valgum. The authors noted that these features overlap with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome. One of the affected sibs had urinary incontinence and growth hormone deficiency. The authors stated that neither feature had been reported before in any type of OFDS.

Thevenon et al. (2016) reassessed the family first reported by Phadke et al. (1999), which had 3 affected sibs born to healthy consanguineous parents. The sibs were followed for 15 years. One of them (subject II-3) died at age 17 years from a complicated pulmonary infection. Features overlapping OFDS were median pseudocleft of the upper lip, oral frenulae, and pre- and postaxial polysyndactyly. Features overlapping EVCS were missing incisors and short stature with mesomelic limb shortening. Skeletal features included brachymesophalangia, stocky femoral necks and vertebral notches. The patients had no visual complaints, but retinopathy was not excluded. Because the patients had no rib shortening and no thoracic narrowing or deformity, the authors concluded that the disorder was an unclassified type of OFDS with skeletal dysplasia rather than EVCS. ⚠︎

Thevenon et al. (2016) described subject II-1, a male examined at 25 years of age. He had normal intelligence and short stature (final height 155 cm, -3 SD; weight 45 kg, less than -3 SD), with a normal OFC. His facial features were a square face with short forehead, upslanting palpebral fissures, high nasal bridge, pyriform nose and short philtrum. He also had medial clefting of the upper lip, supernumerary frenulae and missing incisors. In the limbs he had pronounced brachydactyly, postaxial polydactyly of the upper limbs, and a space between the first and second toes. X-rays showed severe shortening of the middle phalanges, duplication of the first metatarsal (preaxial polydactyly), bilateral short femoral necks with varus femurs, and symmetric genu valgum. There were no vertebral anomalies and no thoracic deformity. Heart, renal and abdominal ultrasounds were normal.

Thevenon et al. (2016) described subject II-2, a female examined at 22 years of age. She had normal intelligence, short stature (final height 122 cm, -5 SD), a normal OFC, and puberty that was not delayed. Her facial features were a square face with narrow forehead, enophthalmos, upslanting palpebral fissures and a broad nasal bridge. She also had bilateral hypopigmented pseudoclefts with medial labial clefting, missing incisors and supernumerary frenulae. She had marked brachydactyly and postaxial polydactyly of all 4 limbs. X-rays showed shortened middle phalanges, fused proximal phalanges and bifid distal phalanges. In the feet there was duplication of the first metatarsal and absence of the intermediate phalanges. Her vertebral bodies had medial notches but no platyspondyly. She had bilateral cervical ribs without a narrow thorax, gracile long bones, and short femoral necks. Brain MRI and abdominal and heart ultrasounds were normal.

### Inheritance

Phadke et al. (1999) described the disorder in sibs from a consanguineous family. Thevenon et al. (2016) found a homozygous IFT57 mutation in all 3 affected sibs of this family, who were born to healthy consanguineous parents. This is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Mapping

Thevenon et al. (2016) performed homozygosity mapping in 2 affected sibs of the consanguineous family first reported by Phadke et al. (1999). They found 11 Mb of shared runs of homozygosity, located on chromosome 3 (chr3:104822019-111671862) and chromosome 10 (chr10:1372501-6029726; hg19). The chromosome 10 interval was excluded because the third affected sib was heterozygous for an AKR1C3 variant in that region.

### Heterogeneity

Thevenon et al. (2016) found no causative IFT57 mutation by Sanger sequencing in 2 groups. The first was 13 subjects with OFDS from a French-Italian cohort, selected for polydactyly and cleft lip/palate. The second was 12 subjects with EVCS who were negative for EVC and EVC2 mutations. They also found no biallelic causative IFT57 mutations in exome or ciliome data from 172 subjects with various ciliopathy phenotypes.

### Molecular Genetics

In the consanguineous family first reported by Phadke et al. (1999), Thevenon et al. (2016) used homozygosity mapping and exome sequencing to identify a homozygous synonymous variant in the IFT57 gene, c.777G-A (lys259-to-lys, K259K; NM_018010.3). The variant affects the last base of exon 6. Sanger sequencing confirmed that all 3 affected sibs were homozygous. The variant was absent from the ExAC database and from 50 healthy controls from northern India. In patient fibroblasts the variant produced a complex splicing pattern. The normal transcript was present, together with 3 abnormal transcripts that were detected only after nonsense-mediated decay was inhibited: in-frame skipping of exon 6, in-frame skipping of exons 5 and 6, and out-of-frame skipping of exons 4, 5 and 6. Patient fibroblasts had significantly less IFT57 mRNA than controls. This suggested a partial splicing defect that leaves only a residual amount of normal mRNA.

### Pathogenesis

Thevenon et al. (2016) found that fibroblasts from a patient homozygous for the IFT57 mutation had normal numbers of primary cilia of normal length. However, IFT57 staining at the ciliary tip was significantly decreased (90% vs 35%, p less than 0.001), and staining at the basal body was increased, indicating reduced anterograde ciliary transport. When the SHH pathway was stimulated with SAG, GLI1 mRNA was poorly induced in patient fibroblasts. siRNA knockdown of IFT57 in control fibroblasts abolished GLI1 induction. The authors concluded that the hypomorphic mutation allows cilia to assemble but causes defective hedgehog signaling, which likely underlies many features of the phenotype.

Thevenon et al. (2016) noted that biallelic truncating mutations had never been observed among more than 60 reported cases with mutations in genes for ciliary transporters. They also noted that no homozygous loss-of-function IFT57 mutations were reported among 62,000 exomes. They suggested that null alleles would be lethal. In their view, the mild phenotype in their family resulted from a splice mutation that reduced IFT57 synthesis without abolishing it.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 OROFACIODIGITAL SYNDROME XVIII**  
IFT57, 777G-A [c.777G>A; p.K259K]

Thevenon et al. (2016) studied 3 affected sibs with orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII from a consanguineous family first reported by Phadke et al. (1999). In all 3 they found a homozygous synonymous c.777G-A transition in the IFT57 gene, which results in lys259-to-lys (K259K; NM_018010.3). The variant affects the last base of exon 6. It was identified by homozygosity mapping and exome sequencing and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. It was absent from the ExAC database and from 50 healthy controls from northern India. In patient fibroblasts the normal transcript was present along with 3 abnormal splice products, which were seen only after nonsense-mediated decay was inhibited: skipping of exon 6, of exons 5 and 6, or of exons 4 to 6. IFT57 mRNA levels were significantly reduced. In patient fibroblasts, IFT57 localization at the ciliary tip was decreased, and SAG-induced expression of GLI1 was poor. Together these findings indicated a hypomorphic allele with defective anterograde ciliary transport and impaired hedgehog signaling.

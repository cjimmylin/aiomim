### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII (OFD18) is caused by homozygous mutation in the IFT57 gene (606621) on chromosome 3q13.

### Description

Orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII (OFD18) is an autosomal recessive form of oral-facial-digital syndrome (OFDS) with short stature and brachymesophalangia. Thevenon et al. (2016) described it in a consanguineous family with 3 affected sibs. The patients had oral anomalies (supernumerary frenulae, missing incisors), facial anomalies (median clefting or pseudoclefting of the upper lip), and digital anomalies (pre- and postaxial polydactyly and brachydactyly). They also had skeletal dysplasia with short stature, shortening of the middle phalanges, and short femoral necks. The phenotype overlaps with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome (EVCS), but the patients lacked rib shortening and thoracic narrowing or deformity.

### Clinical Features

Phadke et al. (1999) reported a consanguineous family with a new variety of OFDS that was associated with acromelic short stature and genu valgum. They noted that these features overlapped with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome. One of the affected sibs had urinary incontinence and growth hormone deficiency, which the authors stated had not been reported before in any type of OFDS.

Thevenon et al. (2016) reassessed the family that Phadke et al. (1999) had reported. There were 3 affected sibs born to healthy consanguineous parents, and they had been followed for 15 years. One sib (subject II-3) died at 17 years of age from a complicated pulmonary infection. Subject II-1, a male examined at 25 years of age, had normal intelligence and short stature, with a final height of 155 cm (-3 SD), weight of 45 kg (less than -3 SD), and occipitofrontal circumference (OFC) of 50 cm (mean). His facial features included a square face, short forehead, upslanting palpebral fissures, high nasal bridge, pyriform nose, short philtrum, and medial clefting of the upper lip. Oral anomalies included supernumerary frenulae and missing incisors. He had pronounced brachydactyly, postaxial polydactyly of the upper limbs, and spacing between the first and second rays of the feet. X-rays showed severe mesophalangeal shortening and duplication of the first metatarsal, which was interpreted as preaxial polydactyly. He also had bilateral short femoral necks with varus femurs and symmetric genu valgum. There were no vertebral anomalies or thoracic deformity, and heart, renal, and abdominal ultrasounds were normal.

Subject II-2 in the family of Thevenon et al. (2016) was a female examined at 22 years of age. She had normal intelligence and was married with no children. She had short stature, with a final height of 122 cm (-5 SD), weight of 37 kg (-2 SD), and a conserved OFC of 51 cm (mean). Puberty was not delayed. Her facial features included a square face, narrow forehead, enophthalmos, upslanting palpebral fissures, broad nasal bridge, and bilateral hypopigmented pseudoclefts with medial labial clefting. She had missing incisors and supernumerary frenulae. She also had marked brachydactyly and postaxial polydactyly of all 4 limbs, which was surgically removed in infancy. Hand x-rays showed mesophalangeal shortening and residual postaxial polydactyly of the right hand, with fused proximal phalanges and bifid distal phalanges. Foot x-rays showed duplication of the first metatarsal and absent intermediate phalanges, which suggested preaxial polydactyly of the lower limbs. She had abnormal vertebral bodies with medial notches but no platyspondyly, and bilateral cervical ribs without a narrow thorax. Her long bones were gracile, with shortened femoral necks. Brain MRI and abdominal and heart ultrasounds were normal. Thevenon et al. (2016) noted that none of the 3 affected sibs had visual complaints, but ophthalmologic involvement such as retinopathy had not been excluded.

### Inheritance

Phadke et al. (1999) described the disorder in sibs from a consanguineous family. Thevenon et al. (2016) found a homozygous IFT57 mutation in all 3 affected sibs from this family, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Mapping

Thevenon et al. (2016) performed homozygosity mapping in 2 affected sibs from the consanguineous family first reported by Phadke et al. (1999). They found 11 Mb of shared runs of homozygosity on chromosome 3 (chr3:104822019-111671862) and chromosome 10 (chr10:1372501-6029726; hg19). Neither interval overlapped with genes previously associated with a recessive ciliopathy. Sanger sequencing in the third affected sib showed a candidate AKR1C3 missense variant in the heterozygous state, which excluded the chromosome 10 interval.

### Heterogeneity

Thevenon et al. (2016) found no causative IFT57 mutation by Sanger sequencing in 25 patients. These were 13 patients with OFDS selected from a French-Italian cohort for the combination of polydactyly and cleft lip/palate, and 12 patients with EVCS who were negative for EVC and EVC2 mutations. Exome or ciliome data from 172 patients with various ciliopathy phenotypes also contained no biallelic causative IFT57 mutations.

### Molecular Genetics

Thevenon et al. (2016) studied the 3 affected sibs from the consanguineous Indian family first reported by Phadke et al. (1999). Homozygosity mapping and exome sequencing identified a homozygous synonymous c.777G-A transition in the IFT57 gene (lys259-to-lys, K259K). The variant affects the last base of exon 6. Sanger sequencing confirmed that all 3 affected sibs were homozygous for the variant. It was absent from the ExAC database and from 50 healthy controls from northern India. In patient fibroblasts, the variant caused a complex splicing pattern. The normal transcript was present, together with 3 abnormal transcripts that were detected only after inhibition of nonsense-mediated mRNA decay: in-frame skipping of exon 6, in-frame skipping of exons 5 and 6, and out-of-frame skipping of exons 4, 5, and 6. Patient fibroblasts had significantly less IFT57 mRNA than controls. The authors suggested that the mutation causes a partial splicing defect that leaves only a residual amount of normal mRNA, and they considered it a hypomorphic allele.

### Pathogenesis

In fibroblasts from a patient homozygous for the IFT57 mutation, Thevenon et al. (2016) found that primary cilia number and length were similar to controls. However, IFT57 staining of the ciliary tip was significantly reduced (90% vs 35%; p less than 0.001), and staining of the basal body was significantly increased, indicating decreased anterograde ciliary transport. When the SHH pathway was stimulated with Smoothened agonist (SAG), GLI1 mRNA was poorly induced in patient fibroblasts compared with controls. In control fibroblasts, siRNA knockdown of IFT57 abolished GLI1 induction by SAG. The authors concluded that the reduced level of IFT57 still supports ciliary assembly but that the cilia are defective in hedgehog signaling, which likely underlies many features of the phenotype.

Thevenon et al. (2016) noted that biallelic truncating mutations had never been observed among more than 60 reported cases with mutations in genes encoding ciliary transporters. They also noted that no homozygous loss-of-function IFT57 mutations were reported in exome data from 62,000 individuals. They suggested that null alleles might be lethal, and that the mild phenotype in their family resulted from a splice mutation that reduced, but did not completely block, synthesis of IFT57 message.

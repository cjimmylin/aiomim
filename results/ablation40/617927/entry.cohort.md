### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII (OFD18) is caused by homozygous mutation in the IFT57 gene (606621) on chromosome 3q13.

### Description

Orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII is a form of oral-facial-digital syndrome (OFDS) associated with acromelic short stature and genu valgum, features that overlap with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome (Phadke et al., 1999). Thevenon et al. (2016) classified the disorder as an unclassified OFDS with skeletal dysplasia. Affected individuals had short stature, brachymesophalangia, and stocky femoral necks, but no rib shortening or thoracic narrowing.

### Clinical Features

Phadke et al. (1999) reported a consanguineous family with a new variety of OFDS. Affected members had oral, facial, and digital malformations together with acromelic short stature and genu valgum, features that overlapped with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome. One of the sibs had urinary incontinence and growth hormone deficiency. The authors noted that these features had not been reported before in any type of OFDS. ⚠︎

Thevenon et al. (2016) restudied the consanguineous Indian family first reported by Phadke et al. (1999), which included 3 affected sibs. Subject II-1, a male, was assessed at 25 years of age. He had normal intelligence and short stature, with a final height of 155 cm (-3 SD), weight 45 kg (less than -3 SD), and occipitofrontal circumference (OFC) 50 cm (mean). His facial features included a square face, short forehead, upslanting palpebral fissures, high nasal bridge, short philtrum, and medial clefting of the upper lip. Oral anomalies included supernumerary frenulae and missing incisors. Digital anomalies included pronounced brachydactyly, upper-limb postaxial polydactyly, severe mesophalangeal shortening, preaxial polydactyly of the foot with first metatarsal duplication, and a gap between the first and second toes. Bone studies showed bilateral short femoral necks with varus femurs, symmetric genu valgum, and no vertebral anomalies or thoracic deformity. Heart, renal, and abdominal ultrasound examinations were normal. ⚠︎

Subject II-2 in the family of Thevenon et al. (2016), a female, was assessed at 22 years of age. She had normal intelligence and was married with no children. She had short stature, with a final height of 122 cm (-5 SD), weight 37 kg (-2 SD), and OFC 51 cm (mean). Her facial features included a square face, narrow forehead, enophthalmia, upslanting palpebral fissures, broad nasal bridge, bilateral hypopigmented pseudoclefts, and medial labial clefting. She also had missing incisors and supernumerary frenulae. Digital anomalies included marked brachydactyly, mesophalangeal shortening, bifid terminal phalanges, first metatarsal duplication, and absent intermediate phalanges of the toes. She had 4-limb postaxial polydactyly, which was surgically removed during infancy. Skeletal findings included vertebral bodies with medial notches but no platyspondyly, bilateral cervical ribs without narrow thorax, gracile long bones, and shortened femoral necks. Puberty was not delayed and brain MRI was normal. Subject II-3 died at 17 years of age from a complicated pulmonary infection.

Thevenon et al. (2016) reclassified this family, originally reported as an OFDS-Ellis-van Creveld syndrome overlap, as an unclassified OFDS with skeletal dysplasia. The patients lacked rib shortening or thoracic narrowing but had short stature, brachymesophalangia, and stocky femoral necks.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OFD18 in the consanguineous family first reported by Phadke et al. (1999) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Thevenon et al. (2016) found that all 3 affected sibs were homozygous for an IFT57 mutation.

### Mapping

Thevenon et al. (2016) performed homozygosity mapping in affected sibs from the consanguineous Indian family first reported by Phadke et al. (1999). They found shared runs of homozygosity totaling 11 Mb on chromosome 3 (chr3:104822019-111671862) and chromosome 10 (chr10:1372501-6029726). Segregation in the third affected sib excluded the chromosome 10 interval. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Thevenon et al. (2016) found no causative IFT57 mutations by Sanger sequencing in 13 subjects with OFDS or in 12 subjects with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome who were negative for EVC1/EVC2 mutations. They also found no biallelic IFT57 mutations in exome or ciliome data from 172 subjects with various ciliopathies.

### Molecular Genetics

In 3 sibs with OFD18 from a consanguineous Indian family first reported by Phadke et al. (1999), Thevenon et al. (2016) identified a homozygous synonymous c.777G-A transition (lys259-to-lys; K259K) in the IFT57 gene. The variant was in the chromosome 3 region of homozygosity and affects the last base of exon 6. Sanger sequencing confirmed that all 3 affected sibs were homozygous. The variant was not found in the ExAC database or in 50 northern Indian controls. In patient fibroblasts, the variant reduced IFT57 mRNA levels and caused a complex splicing pattern: normal transcript was present together with 3 aberrant transcripts that skipped exon 6, exons 5-6, or exons 4-6. The aberrant transcripts could be detected only after inhibition of nonsense-mediated mRNA decay. The authors described the mutation as hypomorphic. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Thevenon et al. (2016) found that fibroblasts from patients with OFD18 had a normal number of primary cilia of normal length. However, IFT57 staining was decreased at the ciliary tip (35% vs 90% in controls) and increased at the basal body, indicating defective anterograde ciliary transport. GLI1 induction after SAG stimulation was poor in patient fibroblasts, and siRNA knockdown of IFT57 in control fibroblasts abolished GLI1 induction. The authors concluded that SHH signaling was impaired.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII (OFD18) is caused by homozygous mutation in the IFT57 gene (606621) on chromosome 3q13.

### Description

Orofaciodigital syndrome XVIII (OFD18) is a form of oral-facial-digital syndrome with skeletal dysplasia. Oral anomalies include supernumerary frenulae, missing incisors and median clefting of the upper lip. Digital anomalies include brachydactyly with shortening of the middle phalanges, postaxial polydactyly of the hands and preaxial polydactyly of the feet. Patients also have short stature and short femoral necks, without rib shortening or a narrow thorax, and intelligence is normal (Thevenon et al., 2016).

### Clinical Features

Phadke et al. (1999) described a consanguineous family with a new variety of oral-facial-digital syndrome (OFDS) with oral, facial and digital malformations, acromelic short stature and genu valgum. They noted that these features overlapped with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome (EVCS). One of the sibs also had urinary incontinence and growth hormone deficiency, which the authors stated had not been reported before in any type of OFDS.

Thevenon et al. (2016) restudied the consanguineous Indian family reported by Phadke et al. (1999), in which 3 sibs were affected. Subject II-1, a male examined at 25 years of age, had normal intelligence. His final height was 155 cm (-3 SD) and his weight was 45 kg (<-3 SD). He had a square face, short forehead, upslanting palpebral fissures, high nasal bridge, pyriform nose, short philtrum and medial clefting of the upper lip. Oral anomalies were supernumerary frenulae and missing incisors. Digital anomalies included pronounced brachydactyly, postaxial polydactyly of the upper limbs and a gap between the first and second toes. Radiographs showed severe shortening of the middle phalanges of the hands and feet, and preaxial foot polydactyly with duplication of the first metatarsal. Bone studies showed bilateral short femoral necks with varus femurs, symmetric genu valgum and no vertebral anomalies. Heart, renal and abdominal ultrasound examinations were normal.

Subject II-2 in the family studied by Thevenon et al. (2016), a female examined at 22 years of age, had normal intelligence. She was married with no children. Her final height was 122 cm (-5 SD) and her weight was 37 kg (-2 SD). She had a square face, narrow forehead, enophthalmia, upslanting palpebral fissures and a broad nasal bridge. She also had bilateral hypopigmented pseudoclefts with medial labial clefting, missing incisors and supernumerary oral frenulae. Digital anomalies included marked brachydactyly and postaxial polydactyly of all 4 limbs, which was surgically removed in infancy. Hand radiographs showed shortening of the middle phalanges, fused proximal phalanges and bifid distal phalanges. Foot radiographs showed duplication of the first metatarsal and absent intermediate phalanges, suggesting preaxial polydactyly. She had abnormal vertebral bodies with medial notches but no platyspondyly, bilateral cervical ribs without a narrow thorax, gracile long bones and shortened femoral necks. She had no dolichostenomelia and no delayed puberty. Brain MRI and abdominal and heart ultrasounds were normal. The third affected sib, subject II-3, died at 17 years of age from a complicated pulmonary infection.

The family had first been reported as having an overlapping OFDS-EVCS phenotype. Thevenon et al. (2016) instead favored an unclassified OFDS type with skeletal dysplasia, because the patients had no rib shortening and no narrow or deformed thorax, but did have short stature, brachymesophalangia and stocky femoral necks.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OFD18 in the consanguineous family reported by Phadke et al. (1999) and Thevenon et al. (2016) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. All 3 affected sibs were homozygous for an IFT57 mutation (Thevenon et al., 2016).

### Mapping

Thevenon et al. (2016) performed homozygosity mapping in 2 affected sibs of a consanguineous family with OFD18. The sibs shared runs of homozygosity totaling 11 Mb on chromosome 3 (chr3:104822019-111671862) and chromosome 10 (chr10:1372501-6029726, hg19). One of these intervals was excluded by segregation in the third affected sib.

### Heterogeneity

Thevenon et al. (2016) found no causative IFT57 mutation by Sanger sequencing in 13 patients with OFDS or in 12 patients with Ellis-van Creveld syndrome who were negative for EVC1/EVC2 mutations. Exome or ciliome data from 172 patients with various ciliopathies showed no biallelic causative IFT57 mutations.

### Molecular Genetics

Thevenon et al. (2016) studied 3 sibs from a consanguineous Indian family with an unclassified OFDS with short stature and brachymesophalangia, originally reported by Phadke et al. (1999). Exome sequencing within the homozygous intervals identified a homozygous synonymous variant in the IFT57 gene, a c.777G-A transition (lys259-to-lys; K259K) at the last base of exon 6. Sanger sequencing confirmed that all 3 affected sibs were homozygous for the variant. The variant was absent from the ExAC database and from 50 healthy controls from northern India. In patient fibroblasts, the mutation caused a complex splicing pattern. Alongside the normal transcript there were 3 abnormal mRNAs: in-frame skipping of exon 6, in-frame skipping of exons 5 and 6, and out-of-frame skipping of exons 4 to 6. The abnormal transcripts were detected only after inhibition of nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (NMD), and total IFT57 mRNA was significantly reduced.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Thevenon et al. (2016) suggested that the relatively mild phenotype in their patients resulted from the hypomorphic splice mutation, which reduced but did not abolish IFT57 message. They noted that biallelic truncating mutations in ciliary transport genes had never been observed and might be incompatible with early development.

### Pathogenesis

Thevenon et al. (2016) found that cilia in fibroblasts from patients with OFD18 were normal in number and length, but anterograde ciliary transport was impaired. IFT57 staining at the ciliary tip was significantly decreased (90% vs 35%), and staining at the basal body was increased. SAG-induced GLI1 expression was poor in patient fibroblasts. In control fibroblasts, siRNA knockdown of IFT57 abolished SAG-induced GLI1 expression, pointing to defective SHH signaling.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that nephrotic syndrome type 23 (NPHS23) is caused by homozygous mutation in the KIRREL1 gene (607428) on chromosome 1q23.1.

### Description

Nephrotic syndrome type 23 (NPHS23) is an autosomal recessive form of steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome (SRNS) caused by mutation in KIRREL1, a component of the glomerular slit diaphragm. Solanki et al. (2019) concluded that KIRREL1 has an important role in modulating the integrity of the slit diaphragm and maintaining glomerular filtration function.

### Clinical Features

Solanki et al. (2019) described a boy (A666_21) from a consanguineous family who was diagnosed with nephrotic syndrome at age 5 years. He was steroid-resistant at initial presentation and was resistant to various immunosuppressive drugs. He never achieved complete remission and had undulating proteinuria with only partial response. Treatment with an ACE inhibitor and an ARB was not beneficial, but renal function remained normal over 12.5 years of follow-up. Renal biopsy showed minor mesangial hypercellularity consistent with minimal change disease. He had no extrarenal manifestations. A younger sister had periorbital edema and proteinuria. Among extended relatives, 3 children had end-stage renal disease (ESRD) and 1 had nephrotic syndrome, but their genotypes were unknown.

Solanki et al. (2019) also described a boy of Italian descent (B742_21) from a consanguineous family who was diagnosed with nephrotic syndrome at age 14 years and was steroid-resistant at presentation. He had hypertension and achieved complete remission on tacrolimus, a calcium channel blocker, and an ACE inhibitor. Over 4 years of follow-up, however, he developed progressive chronic kidney disease (CKD stage 2). Renal biopsy showed focal segmental glomerulosclerosis. Electron microscopy showed extensive podocyte foot process effacement, increased mesangial matrix, and mesangial proliferation. He had no extrarenal or syndromic features.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of NPHS23 is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Solanki et al. (2019) found homozygous KIRREL1 mutations in affected individuals from 2 unrelated consanguineous families.

### Molecular Genetics

Solanki et al. (2019) performed whole-exome sequencing and homozygosity mapping in 596 individuals from 406 families with SRNS. They identified homozygous missense mutations in the KIRREL1 gene in 2 individuals from unrelated consanguineous families, a frequency of 2/406 (0.4%). Both mutated residues were conserved to zebrafish, and both mutations lay within homozygosity peaks on chromosome 1.

In patient A666_21, Solanki et al. (2019) identified a homozygous arg440-to-cys (R440C) mutation in KIRREL1. It lay within the maximum NPL homozygosity peak on chromosome 1 and had a gnomAD minor allele frequency of 3.97 x 10(-6).

In patient B742_21, who was of Italian descent, Solanki et al. (2019) identified a homozygous ser573-to-leu (S573L) mutation in KIRREL1 within the maximum homozygosity peak on chromosome 1. The variant had a gnomAD minor allele frequency of 7.87 x 10(-4) and an allele frequency of 0.0011 in Europeans, and it was present in the homozygous state in one gnomAD control. The authors suggested possible incomplete penetrance or variable expressivity. They nonetheless considered the in silico, conservation, and functional data conclusive of the variant's impact.

In functional studies, Solanki et al. (2019) found that both mutants mislocalized when stably expressed in human podocytes, and podocyte monolayers expressing either mutant showed increased albumin flux (see PATHOGENESIS).

### Pathogenesis

Liu et al. (2003) used immunogold electron microscopy in rodents to localize Neph1 (the KIRREL1 ortholog) to the glomerular slit diaphragm. Neph1 interacted directly with 2 other slit diaphragm components, nephrin and ZO-1. Both native and recombinant Neph1 associated with each other as dimers and multimers. Neph1 bound nephrin through the extracellular segments of both proteins, and this interaction was specific because neither protein interacted with P-cadherin. ZO-1 bound the cytoplasmic tail of Neph1 through its PDZ domains. ⚠︎

Solanki et al. (2019) stably expressed Flag-tagged KIRREL1 R440C and S573L mutants in human podocytes. Compared with wildtype, the mutants mislocalized, with reduced levels in the membrane fraction and increased localization in the Golgi, early endosomes, and lysosomes, suggesting trafficking defects. Membrane localization of NPHS1 was unaffected. Podocyte monolayers expressing the mutants showed significantly increased albumin flux, indicating impaired intercellular integrity.

### Animal Model

Liu et al. (2003) disrupted the Neph1-nephrin interaction in vivo by injecting rodents with combinations of anti-Neph1 and anti-nephrin antibodies. Each antibody was given at a dose too low to cause nephritis on its own. The animals developed proteinuria that did not depend on complement or leukocytes, and their podocyte foot processes were preserved. In podocytes, Neph1 and nephrin protein levels fell modestly and ZO-1 protein fell dramatically, with no change in the mRNA levels of any of the 3 genes. The authors concluded that the nephrin-Neph1 interaction appears to be an important determinant of glomerular permeability. ⚠︎

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that nephrotic syndrome type 23 (NPHS23) is caused by homozygous mutation in the KIRREL1 gene (607428) on chromosome 1q23.

### Description

Nephrotic syndrome type 23 (NPHS23) is an autosomal recessive kidney disorder in which steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome (SRNS) begins in childhood or adolescence. No extrarenal manifestations were reported. Renal biopsy has shown mesangial hypercellularity consistent with minimal change disease, or focal segmental glomerulosclerosis with podocyte foot process effacement. In one reported patient renal function stayed normal over long-term follow-up, while another developed progressive chronic kidney disease (Solanki et al., 2019).

### Clinical Features

Solanki et al. (2019) reported a boy (A666_21) from a consanguineous family who was diagnosed with nephrotic syndrome at 5 years of age. He had steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome from his first presentation and did not respond to several immunosuppressive drugs. His proteinuria rose and fell, with a partial response to treatment. Renal biopsy showed mesangial hypercellularity consistent with minimal change disease. His renal function remained normal throughout, and he stopped attending clinic after 12.5 years of follow-up. He had no extrarenal manifestations.

Solanki et al. (2019) also reported an Italian boy (B742_21) from a consanguineous family who was diagnosed with nephrotic syndrome at 14 years of age. He had steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome from his first presentation and also had high blood pressure. Renal histology showed focal segmental glomerulosclerosis on light microscopy, as well as podocyte foot process effacement. He achieved complete remission while on tacrolimus and a calcium channel blocker, with gradual increases in his ACE inhibitor dose. Over 4 years of follow-up, however, he developed chronic kidney disease that continued to progress, reaching CKD stage 2. He had no extrarenal manifestations.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of NPHS23 is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Solanki et al. (2019) found homozygous KIRREL1 mutations in affected individuals from 2 unrelated consanguineous families.

### Molecular Genetics

Solanki et al. (2019) performed whole-exome sequencing and homozygosity mapping in 596 individuals from 406 families with steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome. They found homozygous missense mutations in the KIRREL1 gene in 2 individuals from unrelated consanguineous families, a frequency of 0.4%. Patient A666_21 was homozygous for an R440C mutation, which lay within the maximum nonparametric lod (NPL) peak on chromosome 1. Patient B742_21, of Italian descent, was homozygous for an S573L mutation. When stably expressed in human podocytes, both mutant proteins moved away from the cell membrane and built up in the Golgi, early endosomes, and lysosomes. Podocyte monolayers expressing the mutants were significantly more permeable to albumin than controls.

### Pathogenesis

Using immunogold electron microscopy in rodents, Liu et al. (2003) localized Neph1 to the glomerular slit diaphragm. Neph1 bound directly to nephrin and to ZO-1. Native and recombinant Neph1 formed dimers and multimers and bound nephrin through their extracellular segments, and the PDZ domains of ZO-1 bound the cytoplasmic tail of Neph1. The nephrin-Neph1 interaction was specific: neither protein shared it with P-cadherin, another integral slit diaphragm protein. Liu et al. (2003) concluded that the nephrin-Neph1 interaction appears to be an important determinant of glomerular permeability.

Solanki et al. (2019) found that the R440C and S573L KIRREL1 mutants, when stably expressed in human podocytes, were mislocalized from the cell membrane and accumulated in the Golgi, early endosomes, and lysosomes. Neither mutant affected the membrane localization of NPHS1. Podocyte monolayers expressing the mutants showed significantly increased albumin permeability. The authors suggested that the mutations cause trafficking defects and impair the integrity of contacts between cells.

### Population Genetics

Solanki et al. (2019) noted that the S573L variant had an allele frequency of 0.0011 in European populations in the gnomAD database and was present in the homozygous state in 1 control individual. They raised the possibility of variable expressivity or incomplete penetrance.

### Animal Model

Liu et al. (2003) disrupted the Neph1-nephrin interaction in rodents by giving combined subnephritogenic doses of anti-Neph1 and anti-nephrin antibodies. The animals developed proteinuria that did not depend on complement or leukocytes, and their foot processes were preserved. Neph1 and nephrin protein in podocytes decreased modestly and ZO-1 protein decreased sharply, while mRNA levels of all 3 genes did not change. ⚠︎

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that nephrotic syndrome type 23 (NPHS23) is caused by homozygous mutation in the KIRREL1 gene on chromosome 1.

### Description

Nephrotic syndrome type 23 (NPHS23) is an autosomal recessive kidney disorder. It presents as steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome (SRNS) in childhood or adolescence, with no extrarenal features. Renal biopsy has shown low-grade mesangial hypercellularity, focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS), or minimal change disease, and electron microscopy has shown podocyte foot process effacement. Response to treatment and renal outcome vary (Solanki et al., 2019).

### Clinical Features

Solanki et al. (2019) reported 2 patients with SRNS from 2 unrelated consanguineous families (A666 and B742). Neither patient had extrarenal manifestations or syndromic features. On renal histology, both patients had low-grade mesangial hypercellularity. Ultrastructural analysis showed extensive foot process effacement, increased mesangial matrix, and mesangial proliferation.

Patient A666_21, a boy, was diagnosed with nephrotic syndrome at age 5 years. It was steroid-resistant at initial presentation, and he was also resistant to several other immunosuppressive drugs. He never achieved complete remission and had undulating proteinuria with only a partial response to treatment. Additional treatment with an ACE inhibitor and an angiotensin receptor blocker gave no benefit. His renal function remained normal throughout, and he stopped clinical visits after 12.5 years of follow-up. On light microscopy, his biopsy showed only minor mesangial hypercellularity, consistent with minimal change disease. His younger sister was reported to have periorbital edema and proteinuria. Among his extended relatives, 3 children had end-stage renal disease and 1 child had nephrotic syndrome. None of these relatives were genotyped (Solanki et al., 2019).

Patient B742_21, a boy of Italian descent, was diagnosed with nephrotic syndrome at age 14 years. It was steroid-resistant at initial presentation. He was started on tacrolimus while corticosteroids were weaned. He also received a calcium channel blocker and an ACE inhibitor for hypertension and proteinuria, and he achieved complete remission. Over 4 years of follow-up, however, he developed chronic kidney disease, which had progressed to stage 2 at the time of the report. His renal biopsy showed FSGS on light microscopy and podocyte foot process effacement on transmission electron microscopy (Solanki et al., 2019).

### Inheritance

Solanki et al. (2019) found homozygous KIRREL1 mutations in affected members of 2 consanguineous families with NPHS23, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Solanki et al. (2019) performed whole-exome sequencing and homozygosity mapping in a cohort of 596 individuals from 406 families with SRNS. They identified homozygous missense mutations in the KIRREL1 gene in 2 patients from unrelated consanguineous families: R440C and S573L. In both patients the mutation lay within the maximum homozygosity peak on chromosome 1. Both affected residues were conserved to D. rerio. The mutations were found in 2 of the 406 families (0.4%). In stably transfected cultured human podocytes, both mutant proteins were mislocalized compared with wildtype KIRREL1. Subcellular fractionation showed significantly lower levels of the mutant proteins in the membrane fraction, and the mutants accumulated in the Golgi apparatus, early endosomes, and lysosomes, indicating a trafficking defect. The mutations did not affect membrane localization of NPHS1. Podocyte monolayers expressing the mutants showed significantly increased transepithelial albumin flux compared with wildtype cells, indicating impaired intercellular integrity.

### Population Genetics

Solanki et al. (2019) reported that the KIRREL1 R440C mutation had a minor allele frequency of 3.97 x 10(-6) in gnomAD. The S573L mutation had a minor allele frequency of 7.87 x 10(-4) in gnomAD and an allele frequency of 0.0011 in European populations. S573L was also found in the homozygous state in 1 gnomAD control subject. The authors discussed the possibility of incomplete penetrance or variable expressivity for this variant.

### Animal Model

Liu et al. (2003) noted that Neph1-deficient mice develop nephrotic syndrome at birth. By immunogold electron microscopy in rodents, they localized Neph1 to the glomerular slit diaphragm. Neph1 interacted with nephrin through their extracellular segments and with ZO-1. In vivo disruption of the Neph1-nephrin interaction, by injecting combinations of subnephritogenic doses of anti-Neph1 and anti-nephrin antibodies, caused complement- and leukocyte-independent proteinuria with preserved foot processes. It also modestly reduced Neph1 and nephrin protein expression and dramatically reduced ZO-1 protein expression in podocytes.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that epidermolysis bullosa simplex-7 with nephropathy and deafness (EBS7) is caused by homozygous mutation in the CD151 gene (602243) on chromosome 11p15.5.

### Description

Epidermolysis bullosa simplex-7 with nephropathy and deafness (EBS7) is an autosomal recessive disorder. Its features are hereditary nephritis that progresses to end-stage kidney disease, pretibial epidermolysis bullosa, and sensorineural deafness. Affected individuals reported to date have also had beta-thalassemia minor, and they lacked the MER2 blood group antigen (Kagan et al., 1988; Karamatic Crew et al., 2004). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Kagan et al. (1988) reported 2 sibs with hereditary nephritis that progressed to end-stage renal disease. Both sibs also had pretibial epidermolysis bullosa and beta-thalassemia minor.

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) described 3 patients of Indian Jewish origin who lacked the MER2 blood group antigen and had end-stage kidney disease. Two of the patients were sibs. In addition to hereditary nephritis, the sibs had sensorineural deafness, pretibial epidermolysis bullosa, and beta-thalassemia minor.

### Inheritance

The homozygous CD151 mutation found in affected individuals by Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) indicated that this disorder is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) studied 3 MER2-negative patients of Indian Jewish origin with end-stage kidney disease. Two of them also had deafness and pretibial epidermolysis bullosa. In all 3 patients, the authors found a homozygous 1-bp insertion (G383) in exon 5 of the CD151 gene. The insertion causes a frameshift that leads to a premature stop signal at codon 140. The authors noted that the resulting truncated protein would lack its integrin-binding domain.

### Pathogenesis

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) showed that CD151, a tetraspanin, carries the MER2 blood group antigen and is located on erythrocytes. CD151 is encoded at the same chromosome 11p15.5 position as the MER2 blood group gene. The authors noted that CD151 forms stable laminin-binding complexes with integrins alpha-3/beta-1 and alpha-6/beta-1 in kidney and with alpha-3/beta-1 and alpha-6/beta-4 in skin. They concluded that CD151 is essential for proper assembly of the glomerular and tubular basement membrane in the kidney and has functional significance in the skin. They further concluded that CD151 is probably a component of the inner ear and could play a role in erythropoiesis.

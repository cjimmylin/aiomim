### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that epidermolysis bullosa simplex-7 with nephropathy and deafness (EBS7) is caused by homozygous mutation in the CD151 gene (602243) on chromosome 11p15.5.

### Description

Epidermolysis bullosa simplex-7 with nephropathy and deafness (EBS7) is characterized by hereditary nephritis that progresses to end-stage renal disease, together with pretibial epidermolysis bullosa (Kagan et al., 1988). Sensorineural deafness has also been reported, and affected individuals described by Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) lacked the MER2 blood group antigen. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Kagan et al. (1988) reported 2 sibs with hereditary nephritis that progressed to end-stage renal disease. Both also had pretibial epidermolysis bullosa and beta-thalassemia minor.

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) studied 3 MER2 blood group-negative patients of Indian Jewish origin with end-stage kidney disease, 2 of whom were sibs. In addition to hereditary nephritis, the sibs had sensorineural deafness, pretibial epidermolysis bullosa, and beta-thalassemia minor.

### Inheritance

The patients reported by Karamatic Crew et al. (2004), 2 of whom were sibs, were homozygous for a CD151 mutation. This is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance of EBS7. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) studied 3 MER2-negative patients of Indian Jewish origin with end-stage kidney disease, 2 of whom were sibs with sensorineural deafness and pretibial epidermolysis bullosa. All 3 were homozygous for a single-nucleotide insertion (G383) in exon 5 of the CD151 gene. The insertion caused a frameshift and a premature stop signal at codon 140, and the resulting truncated protein would lack its integrin-binding domain. CD151 is encoded at the same chromosome 11p15.5 position as the MER2 blood group gene. The authors showed that CD151 carries the MER2 blood group antigen and is located on erythrocytes.

### Pathogenesis

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) noted that the tetraspanin CD151 forms very stable laminin-binding complexes with integrins: alpha3beta1 and alpha6beta1 in kidney, and alpha3beta1 and alpha6beta4 in skin. From their findings in patients homozygous for a truncating CD151 mutation, they concluded that CD151 is essential for proper assembly of the glomerular and tubular basement membrane in the kidney and has functional significance in the skin. They also concluded that CD151 is probably a component of the inner ear and could play a role in erythropoiesis.

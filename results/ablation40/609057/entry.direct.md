### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that epidermolysis bullosa simplex with nephropathy and deafness (EBS7) is caused by homozygous mutation in the CD151 gene on chromosome 11p15.5.

### Description

Epidermolysis bullosa simplex with nephropathy and deafness is a disorder in which hereditary nephritis that progresses to end-stage renal disease occurs together with pretibial epidermolysis bullosa and sensorineural deafness (Kagan et al., 1988; Karamatic Crew et al., 2004). Affected patients lack the MER2 blood group antigen, which is carried by the tetraspanin CD151 (Karamatic Crew et al., 2004).

### Clinical Features

Kagan et al. (1988) described 2 sibs with end-stage renal disease who had hereditary nephritis, pretibial epidermolysis bullosa, and beta-thalassemia minor.

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) studied 3 MER2-negative patients of Indian Jewish origin, 2 of whom were sibs, who had end-stage kidney disease. As well as hereditary nephritis, the sibs had sensorineural deafness, pretibial epidermolysis bullosa, and beta-thalassemia minor.

### Inheritance

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) found homozygous CD151 mutations in affected patients, including a pair of sibs, which is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) showed that CD151, whose gene maps to the same position on chromosome 11p15.5 as the MER2 blood group gene, carries the MER2 blood group antigen and is present on erythrocytes. They examined CD151 in 3 MER2-negative patients of Indian Jewish origin who had end-stage kidney disease, 2 of them sibs. All 3 patients were homozygous for a 1-bp insertion (G383) in exon 5 of the CD151 gene. The insertion caused a frameshift and a premature stop signal at codon 140, and the truncated protein would be expected to lack its integrin-binding domain. Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) concluded that CD151 is essential for correct assembly of the glomerular and tubular basement membranes in the kidney and has functional significance in the skin. They also suggested that CD151 is probably a component of the inner ear and could play a role in erythropoiesis. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) noted that the tetraspanin CD151 forms very stable laminin-binding complexes with integrins. In the kidney these complexes are with alpha-3/beta-1 and alpha-6/beta-1, and in the skin they are with alpha-3/beta-1 and alpha-6/beta-4. The truncated protein produced by the frameshift mutation in their patients would lack the integrin-binding domain.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that epidermolysis bullosa simplex-7 with nephropathy and deafness (EBS7) is caused by homozygous mutation in the CD151 gene (602243) on chromosome 11p15.5.

### Description

Epidermolysis bullosa simplex-7 with nephropathy and deafness (EBS7) is characterized by hereditary nephritis that progresses to end-stage kidney disease, together with pretibial epidermolysis bullosa and sensorineural deafness. Affected individuals have been found to have the MER2-negative blood group.

### Clinical Features

Kagan et al. (1988) reported 2 sibs with end-stage renal disease who had hereditary nephritis, pretibial epidermolysis bullosa, and beta-thalassemia minor.

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) studied 3 MER2-negative patients of Indian Jewish origin with end-stage kidney disease, 2 of whom were sibs. Besides hereditary nephritis, the sibs had sensorineural deafness, pretibial epidermolysis bullosa, and beta-thalassemia minor. The abstract describes end-stage kidney disease and the MER2-negative blood group in the third patient.

### Inheritance

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) found that all 3 of their affected patients, including 2 sibs, were homozygous for a CD151 mutation.

### Mapping

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) noted that the CD151 gene maps to chromosome 11p15.5, the same position as the MER2 blood group gene.

### Molecular Genetics

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) showed that CD151, a tetraspanin, expresses the MER2 blood group antigen and is present on erythrocytes. It was the first member of the tetraspanin superfamily to be detected on erythrocytes. They examined CD151 in 3 MER2-negative patients of Indian Jewish origin with end-stage kidney disease, 2 of whom were sibs. All 3 patients were homozygous for a single-nucleotide insertion (G383) in exon 5 of CD151, which caused a frameshift and a premature stop signal at codon 140.

### Pathogenesis

Karamatic Crew et al. (2004) stated that the frameshift mutation would produce a truncated CD151 protein without its integrin-binding domain. They concluded that CD151 is essential for the proper assembly of the glomerular and tubular basement membrane in the kidney and has functional significance in the skin. They also suggested that CD151 is probably a component of the inner ear and could play a role in erythropoiesis.

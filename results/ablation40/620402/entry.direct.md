### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that autosomal recessive distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-9 (HMNR9) is caused by homozygous mutation in the COQ7 gene on chromosome 16.

### Description

Autosomal recessive distal hereditary motor neuronopathy-9 (HMNR9) is a length-dependent, pure motor axonal neuropathy. Distal weakness and atrophy begin in the lower limbs in childhood or early adolescence, progress slowly, and later involve the hands. Sensation is clinically spared. Some affected individuals have pyramidal signs such as brisk reflexes, a Hoffman sign, or a Babinski sign (Jacquier et al., 2023), whereas others have no upper motor neuron features (Smith et al., 2023). The disorder results from coenzyme Q10 deficiency caused by reduced levels of the COQ7 protein (Jacquier et al., 2023; Smith et al., 2023). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Jacquier et al. (2023) reported 3 sibs (2 brothers and a sister), born in Portugal to nonconsanguineous healthy Portuguese parents, who had distal hereditary motor neuropathy. Disease onset was at ages 12, 9, and 10 years, and in each case the first symptom was walking difficulty. All 3 sibs had normal early development. The index patient (II-2) was examined at age 36 years. At age 12 years, he had developed walking difficulties with a sensation of stiffness in the lower limbs and difficulty walking on his heels. At age 32 years, he developed weakness in the hands. On examination, he had amyotrophy of the lower limbs and hands and a severe deficit of the distal muscles of the upper and lower limbs, mainly affecting foot dorsiflexion. He had pes cavus and could not walk on his heels or tiptoes. Sensory examination was normal. Deep tendon reflexes were brisk in all 4 limbs except for absent Achilles reflexes, and a Hoffman sign was present, without a Babinski sign or spasticity. His 25-year-old sister (II-3) developed walking difficulties with fatigability and foot drop at age 9 years, worsened rapidly between ages 12 and 15 years, and had difficulty with fine hand movements from age 13 years. She reported difficulty feeling cold and warm temperatures on her feet, but sensory examination was normal. She had pes cavus, a positive Gowers maneuver, normal deep tendon reflexes, and a Babinski sign on the left foot. Their 42-year-old brother (II-1) noticed walking and biking difficulties with foot drop at age 10 years. He required ankle-foot orthoses after age 20 years and developed hand weakness at age 22 years. On examination, he had severe distal amyotrophy of the lower limbs, amyotrophy of the hand muscles, complete loss of foot plantar flexion and dorsiflexion, pes cavus, and absent Achilles reflexes, without Hoffman or Babinski signs. None of the patients had cognitive disorders, and all were still able to walk. Electroneuromyography in 2 of the patients showed a pure motor axonal neuropathy predominantly affecting the distal lower limbs, with fibrillations at rest and neurogenic recruitment. In one patient, brain and spinal cord MRI, echocardiography, ECG, ophthalmologic examination (apart from known myopia), and audiometry were normal, and creatine phosphokinase was mildly elevated (215 U/l; normal less than 200 U/l). Jacquier et al. (2023) noted that this clinical phenotype of distal hereditary motor neuropathy with pyramidal features suggested upper motor neuron involvement.

Liu et al. (2023) reported 2 Chinese families with distal hereditary motor neuropathy.

Smith et al. (2023) reported 3 sibs (a 24-year-old woman, a 23-year-old man, and a 12-year-old boy) born to Syrian parents who were first cousins. They had developed distal weakness by 10 to 14 years of age, with muscle atrophy affecting the distal lower more than the upper extremities. Sensation and deep tendon reflexes were preserved. Electrodiagnostic testing in all 3 sibs showed reduced distal motor amplitudes with preserved sensory responses. Needle EMG showed distal chronic reinnervation without active denervation. Muscle MRI in 2 sibs showed normal proximal musculature and moderate to severe fat infiltration beginning at the distal third of the thigh and most pronounced in the calves. Vastus lateralis biopsy in 1 sib showed neurogenic atrophy with angular atrophic fibers, pyknotic nuclear clumps, group atrophy, and fiber type grouping, without ragged red fibers or cytochrome oxidase-negative fibers. The sibs had no developmental delay, cognitive impairment, sensory involvement, or upper motor neuron features. Two of the affected sibs and an unaffected brother had been deaf since childhood. The deafness did not segregate with the neuropathy and was explained by a homozygous GJB2 variant.

Wongkittichote et al. (2023) described 2 clinical presentations of COQ7-related primary coenzyme Q10 deficiency. The first was a neonatal-onset disease with severe neuromuscular, cardiorespiratory, and renal involvement. The second was a late-onset disease with progressive neuropathy, lower extremity weakness, abnormal gait, and variable developmental delay.

### Biochemical Features

Jacquier et al. (2023) found 6-demethoxycoenzyme Q10 (6-DMQ), the substrate of COQ7, in the plasma of an affected patient but not in controls. They suggested that measuring 6-DMQ accumulation in blood could be used to confirm the pathogenicity of COQ7 variants.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of HMNR9 in the families reported by Jacquier et al. (2023) and Smith et al. (2023) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. In both families, the affected sibs were homozygous for a COQ7 mutation and the unaffected parents were heterozygous carriers.

### Molecular Genetics

In 3 sibs from a Portuguese family with HMNR9, Jacquier et al. (2023) identified a homozygous c.3G-T mutation in the start codon of the COQ7 gene (NM_016138.5). The mutation was found by whole-exome sequencing of the 3 sibs and their parents and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. The unaffected parents were heterozygous. The variant was absent from the ExAC database and present once in the heterozygous state in gnomAD (allele frequency 0.000004065). The mutation disrupts the start codon of isoform 1, the main isoform in the spinal cord. Isoforms 2 and 3 use alternative start codons and would not be expected to be affected. In transfected SH-EP neuroblastoma cells, COQ7 protein levels from the mutant construct were reduced by 85%, whereas mRNA levels did not differ significantly from wildtype. COQ7 protein was barely detected in fibroblasts from a patient. In these fibroblasts, coenzyme Q10 was decreased (12.5 pmol/mg protein, compared with 71.5 pmol/mg in controls) and 6-DMQ accumulated. The CoQ10 precursor 2,4-dihydroxybenzoic acid slightly increased CoQ10 levels and normalized 6-DMQ accumulation. Patient fibroblasts relied more on glycolysis and less on mitochondrial ATP production, had reduced basal and maximal respiration, and proliferated more slowly in galactose medium. Both the respiration and proliferation defects were rescued by CoQ10 supplementation.

Liu et al. (2023) identified biallelic variants in the COQ7 gene in 2 Chinese families with distal hereditary motor neuropathy.

In 3 sibs from a consanguineous Syrian family with HMNR9, Smith et al. (2023) identified a homozygous c.1A-G mutation in the COQ7 gene (NM_016138.5). The mutation was found by exome sequencing and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. The parents and an unaffected sib were heterozygous carriers. The variant was absent from gnomAD and was predicted to abolish the initiating methionine. Translation would therefore start at methionine-39, causing loss of the cleavable mitochondrial targeting sequence. In fibroblasts from the affected sibs, the COQ7-to-porin ratio was 85% +/- 7% lower than in fibroblasts from the carrier father and sib. CoQ10 was on average 87% lower, and DMQ10 had accumulated. In muscle from 1 affected sib, COQ7 was not detected and CoQ10 was 67% lower than in control muscle, but no DMQ10 peak was seen. Maximal mitochondrial respiration was impaired in both fibroblasts and muscle. Muscle also showed increased H2O2 emission and decreased content of supercomplexes containing complexes I and IV.

Wongkittichote et al. (2023) identified 5 new patients with COQ7-related primary CoQ10 deficiency. They studied the functional effects of new and previously reported COQ7 variants in a yeast cat5-deleted strain. Yeast CAT5 carrying the equivalents of the human pathogenic variants failed to fully rescue the oxidative growth defect. The equivalents of human arg54-to-gln (R54Q), arg107-to-trp (R107W), and ile66-to-asn (I66N), and of the complex allele [thr103-to-met;leu111-to-pro] (T103M; L111P), partially rescued growth, indicating that these variants are hypomorphic alleles. Supplementation with 2,4-dihydroxybenzoic acid rescued the growth defect of both the partially active (leaky) and severe mutants.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Jacquier et al. (2023) noted that previously reported COQ7 mutations were located in the catalytic domain, affected all COQ7 isoforms, and caused severe early-onset phenotypes with developmental delay. They speculated that the c.3G-T start-codon mutation affects only isoform 1 and spares the alternative isoforms 2 and 3. Because isoform 2 was shown to localize to mitochondria, these alternative isoforms might maintain a basal level of COQ7 in mitochondria, which could explain the milder, pure distal motor phenotype. Smith et al. (2023) likewise contrasted the pure distal motor neuropathy in their family with the sensorimotor polyneuropathy, developmental delay, and upper motor neuron features in earlier reports of COQ7-related CoQ10 deficiency. Wongkittichote et al. (2023) defined 2 distinct disease presentations of COQ7-related disorder, one neonatal-onset and one late-onset, and described an emerging genotype-phenotype correlation.

### Clinical Management

Jacquier et al. (2023) reported that one patient's symptoms stabilized between ages 15 and 18 years while she was treated with CoQ10 (30 mg per day). Her condition began to worsen progressively again at age 18 years, when supplementation stopped. In patient fibroblasts, CoQ10 supplementation rescued basal and maximal respiration and restored proliferation in galactose medium. Jacquier et al. (2023) suggested that CoQ10 supplementation be considered as a potential therapy. Smith et al. (2023) did not perform rescue experiments in fibroblasts from their patients.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 NEURONOPATHY, DISTAL HEREDITARY MOTOR, AUTOSOMAL RECESSIVE 9**  
COQ7, 3G-T [NM_016138.5:c.3G>T; NP_057222.2:p.Met1?]

In 3 sibs from a nonconsanguineous Portuguese family with HMNR9, Jacquier et al. (2023) identified a homozygous c.3G-T mutation in the COQ7 gene (NM_016138.5). The mutation disrupts the ATG start codon of isoform 1 (p.Met1?) and was found by whole-exome sequencing. It segregated with the disorder, and both parents were heterozygous. The variant was present once in the heterozygous state in gnomAD (allele frequency 0.000004065). In transfected SH-EP cells, the mutation reduced COQ7 protein levels by 85% without changing mRNA levels. In patient fibroblasts, COQ7 protein was barely detectable, CoQ10 levels were decreased, and 6-demethoxycoenzyme Q10 accumulated. 6-Demethoxycoenzyme Q10 was also detected in the patient's plasma. Patient fibroblasts showed reduced mitochondrial ATP production and respiration and reduced proliferation in galactose medium, and these defects were rescued by CoQ10 supplementation.

**.0002 NEURONOPATHY, DISTAL HEREDITARY MOTOR, AUTOSOMAL RECESSIVE 9**  
COQ7, 1A-G [NM_016138.5:c.1A>G; p.?]

In 3 sibs, born to Syrian parents who were first cousins, with a pure length-dependent distal motor neuropathy (HMNR9), Smith et al. (2023) identified a homozygous c.1A-G mutation in the COQ7 gene (NM_016138.5). The mutation was found by exome sequencing and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. It was predicted to abolish the initiating methionine, so that translation would start at methionine-39 without the mitochondrial targeting sequence. The parents and an unaffected sib were heterozygous, and the variant was absent from gnomAD. Maximal mitochondrial respiration was impaired in both fibroblasts and muscle.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GABRB1 gene (137190) on chromosome 4p12. ⚠︎

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is a severe neurologic disorder with seizures beginning in infancy. Reported patients had infantile spasms (Epi4K Consortium et al., 2013; Janve et al., 2016) or epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (EIMFS), with severe to profound developmental impairment and hypotonia (Burgess et al., 2019).

### Clinical Features

Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) performed exome sequencing on 264 probands with epileptic encephalopathy, 149 with infantile spasms and 115 with Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, and on their parents. One patient carried a de novo GABRB1 mutation. Janve et al. (2016) stated that this patient had infantile spasms.

Lien et al. (2016) reported a second patient with epileptic encephalopathy and a de novo GABRB1 mutation.

Burgess et al. (2019) studied a cohort of 135 patients with epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (EIMFS). To be included, patients had to have focal seizures that migrated between the cerebral hemispheres, seen clinically and/or on EEG, with onset before age 6 months. One patient (patient 63) carried a de novo GABRB1 variant and was studied at age 2 years. This patient's focal seizures began at 4 months of age. The patient had severe to profound developmental impairment and hypotonia.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous GABRB1 mutations in patients with DEE45 occurred de novo (Epi4K Consortium et al., 2013; Janve et al., 2016; Lien et al., 2016; Burgess et al., 2019).

### Heterogeneity

Burgess et al. (2019) found causative variants in 23 genes in 93 of 135 patients with EIMFS. GABRB1 was one of 9 genes newly linked to EIMFS. The authors noted that GABRB1 had previously been reported in other developmental and epileptic encephalopathies.

### Molecular Genetics

Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) performed exome sequencing on 264 probands with infantile spasms or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome and their parents, and confirmed candidate de novo mutations by Sanger sequencing. One patient had a de novo mutation in the GABRB1 gene. This was one of six de novo mutations in the cohort that affected subunits of the GABA ionotropic receptor: four in GABRB3 and one each in GABRA1 and GABRB1.

Janve et al. (2016) carried out functional studies of the de novo heterozygous GABRB1 mutation, phe246-to-ser (F246S), that Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) had found in a patient with infantile spasms. The F246 residue lies in transmembrane domain 1 and is invariant across all GABA-A receptor subunits. The authors expressed alpha-1/beta-1/gamma-2L receptors in HEK293T cells. When mutant beta-1 was expressed alone (the 'hom' condition), peak GABA-evoked current density was reduced by about 25%. When wildtype and mutant beta-1 were coexpressed (the 'het' condition), current density did not change. The mutation slowed current deactivation, and in the het condition it shortened current rise time. In single-channel recordings, open probability was unchanged even though opening frequency decreased. Mean open time increased about 3.75-fold and burst duration about 3.2-fold, and single-channel conductance fell to about 21 pS. The mutation also caused spontaneous channel openings. In the hom condition, holding currents rose about 5-fold and were partly blocked by zinc. In the absence of GABA, spontaneous single-channel open probability increased 4-fold. Surface levels of the mutant beta-1 subunit were slightly but significantly reduced, while total levels were unchanged. Janve et al. (2016) concluded that their results suggested GABRB1 as a candidate human epilepsy gene.

Lien et al. (2016) reported a second patient with epileptic encephalopathy who carried a de novo GABRB1 mutation.

In a patient with EIMFS (patient 63), Burgess et al. (2019) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a de novo heterozygous missense variant in the GABRB1 gene (chr4:47405633T-C, GRCh37), causing an ile247-to-thr (I247T) substitution. The variant was absent from the ExAC and gnomAD databases. PolyPhen predicted it to be benign and SIFT predicted it to be deleterious. It was classified as likely pathogenic under ACMG criteria.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Janve et al. (2016) found that the de novo GABRB mutations from the Epi4K cohort worked in two different ways depending on the associated syndrome. The GABRB3 mutations associated with Lennox-Gastaut syndrome lie at the beta+/alpha- interface and mainly reduced peak GABA-evoked currents. The mutations associated with infantile spasms, GABRB3 N110D and GABRB1 F246S, lie at beta- interfaces. They mainly changed current kinetics and caused small or no changes in current amplitude.

### Pathogenesis

Structural modeling by Janve et al. (2016) predicted that the GABRB1 F246S mutation rearranges domains of the zone that couples ligand binding to channel gating, with effects reaching the transmembrane domains at the gamma+/beta- and alpha+/beta- interfaces. The authors proposed that the faster rise time and slower deactivation caused by the mutation would lengthen the first inhibitory postsynaptic current. However, it would reduce charge transfer during later currents by reducing the number of unbound receptors, especially during high-frequency neuronal firing, leading to a net loss of GABAergic inhibition.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DEVELOPMENTAL AND EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY 45**  
GABRB1, PHE246SER [p.F246S]

Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) found a de novo GABRB1 mutation in a patient from a cohort with infantile spasms or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome. Janve et al. (2016) identified this mutation as a heterozygous phe246-to-ser (F246S) substitution in a patient with infantile spasms. The F246 residue lies in transmembrane domain 1 and is invariant across all GABA-A receptor subunits. In HEK293T cells, receptors carrying the mutation showed a modest reduction in peak GABA-evoked current only when mutant beta-1 was expressed alone. They also showed markedly slowed deactivation and increased mean open time and burst duration, slightly reduced single-channel conductance, and increased spontaneous channel openings. ⚠︎

In a patient with epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (patient 63), Burgess et al. (2019) identified a de novo heterozygous chr4:47405633T-C (GRCh37) transition in the GABRB1 gene by whole-exome sequencing, causing an ile247-to-thr (I247T) substitution. The variant was not found in the ExAC or gnomAD databases. It was classified as likely pathogenic under ACMG criteria.

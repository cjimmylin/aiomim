### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GABRB1 gene (137190) on chromosome 4p12. ⚠︎

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is a neurologic disorder with onset of seizures in infancy. Reported patients have had epileptic encephalopathy presenting as infantile spasms or as epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (EIMFS), with severe to profound developmental impairment and hypotonia. The disorder has been associated with de novo heterozygous mutations in GABRB1.

### Clinical Features

The Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) screened for de novo mutations in patients with 2 classic epileptic encephalopathies: infantile spasms (n = 149) and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome (n = 115). One of these patients carried a de novo mutation in the GABRB1 gene. Janve et al. (2016) stated that this patient had infantile spasms.

Lien et al. (2016) reported a second patient with epileptic encephalopathy and a de novo GABRB1 mutation.

Burgess et al. (2019) studied a cohort of 135 patients with epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (EIMFS), one of whom (patient 63) had a de novo GABRB1 variant. This patient had seizure onset at 4 months of age, with focal seizures showing interhemispheric migration. When last reported at 2 years of age, the patient had severe to profound developmental impairment and hypotonia.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous GABRB1 mutations in patients with DEE45 occurred de novo (Epi4K Consortium et al., 2013; Lien et al., 2016; Burgess et al., 2019). Burgess et al. (2019) described GABRB1 as an autosomal dominant gene.

### Molecular Genetics

The Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) sequenced the exomes of 264 trios, each with a proband who had infantile spasms or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome. They found de novo mutations in GABA(A) receptor subunit genes in 6 patients: 4 in GABRB3, 1 in GABRA1, and 1 in GABRB1. The de novo GABRB1 mutation was confirmed by Sanger sequencing of the proband and parents. The authors proposed that individual rare mutations in different genes may converge on a shared pathway. They also found a significant excess of de novo mutations in the roughly 4,000 genes most intolerant to functional genetic variation (p = 2.9 x 10(-3)), and estimated that about 90 intolerant genes may confer risk of epileptic encephalopathy.

Janve et al. (2016) functionally studied the de novo heterozygous GABRB1 mutation found by the Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013), a phe246-to-ser (F246S) substitution in transmembrane domain 1 at a residue that is invariant across all GABA-A receptor subunits. When the beta-1 F246S subunit was coexpressed with alpha-1 and gamma-2L subunits in HEK293T cells, peak GABA-evoked current density fell by only about 25% in the homozygous expression condition and was unchanged in the heterozygous condition. Current deactivation was markedly slowed; in single-channel recordings, mean open time increased about 3.75-fold and burst duration about 3.2-fold, while single-channel conductance fell to about 21 pS. The mutation increased holding currents about 5-fold in the homozygous expression condition, and these currents were partly blocked by zinc. In the absence of GABA, mutant receptors had a 4-fold higher spontaneous open probability, indicating spontaneous channel opening. Surface levels of the mutant subunit were slightly but significantly reduced, while total levels were unchanged. Despite the lack of an established link between GABRB1 and epilepsy syndromes, the authors concluded that their results suggested GABRB1 is a candidate human epilepsy gene.

Lien et al. (2016) identified a de novo GABRB1 mutation in a second patient with epileptic encephalopathy.

In a patient with EIMFS from a cohort of 135 patients, Burgess et al. (2019) identified a de novo heterozygous missense variant in the GABRB1 gene (chr4:47405633T-C), resulting in an ile247-to-thr (I247T) substitution, by whole-exome sequencing. The variant was classified as likely pathogenic. GABRB1 had not previously been linked to EIMFS but had been reported in other developmental and epileptic encephalopathies, and the authors used this finding to stress the role of GABA-related mechanisms in EIMFS.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Janve et al. (2016) observed that the mutations associated with infantile spasms (GABRB3 N110D and GABRB1 F246S) lay at beta-minus subunit interfaces and mainly changed current kinetics, whereas the mutations associated with Lennox-Gastaut syndrome lay at the beta-plus/alpha-minus interface and mainly reduced peak GABA-evoked currents. The authors cautioned that the contribution of these mutations to the epilepsy phenotype is difficult to judge from in vitro data alone.

### Pathogenesis

Janve et al. (2016) proposed that the faster rise time and slower deactivation caused by the GABRB1 F246S mutation would lengthen the first inhibitory postsynaptic current. However, they suggested that it would reduce charge transfer during later currents by lowering the number of unbound receptors, especially during high-frequency firing, resulting in a net loss of GABAergic inhibition.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DEVELOPMENTAL AND EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY 45**  
GABRB1, PHE246SER

In a patient with epileptic encephalopathy from a cohort of probands with infantile spasms or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, the Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) identified a de novo heterozygous mutation in the GABRB1 gene by exome sequencing of trios; the de novo status was confirmed by Sanger sequencing of the proband and parents. Janve et al. (2016) stated that this patient had infantile spasms and that the mutation is a phe246-to-ser (F246S) substitution in transmembrane domain 1 at a residue invariant across all GABA-A receptor subunits. In HEK293T cells, receptors containing the mutant subunit showed markedly slowed deactivation, prolonged mean open time and burst duration, reduced single-channel conductance, increased holding currents, and increased spontaneous open probability in the absence of GABA, with slightly reduced surface levels of the mutant subunit. ⚠︎

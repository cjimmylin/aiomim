### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GABRB1 gene (137190) on chromosome 4p12. ⚠︎

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is a severe early-onset epilepsy disorder. The reported patients had onset of seizures in infancy, including infantile spasms or focal seizures that migrated between the cerebral hemispheres, together with severe developmental impairment (Janve et al., 2016; Burgess et al., 2019). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) studied 264 probands with one of 2 classic epileptic encephalopathies, infantile spasms (149 patients) or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome (115 patients). Probands were required to have no moderate-to-severe developmental impairment before seizure onset and no confirmed genetic or metabolic diagnosis, and neither parent could have a history of seizures other than febrile or metabolic/toxic seizures. One of these probands had a de novo mutation in the GABRB1 gene. Janve et al. (2016) stated that this patient had infantile spasms.

Lien et al. (2016) reported a second patient with epileptic encephalopathy who had a de novo GABRB1 mutation.

Burgess et al. (2019) described a patient (patient 63) with epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (EIMFS) in a cohort of 135 patients with EIMFS. In this cohort, EIMFS was defined as focal seizures that migrated between the hemispheres clinically and/or electrically, with seizure onset before 6 months of age. Patient 63 had onset of focal seizures at 4 months and was 2 years old when studied. He or she had severe to profound developmental impairment and hypotonia, and was listed as seizure-free in the summary table.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous GABRB1 mutations in patients with DEE45 occurred de novo (Epi4K Consortium et al., 2013; Lien et al., 2016; Burgess et al., 2019). Burgess et al. (2019) classified GABRB1 as an autosomal dominant gene.

### Molecular Genetics

Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) performed exome sequencing on 264 trios, each with a proband who had infantile spasms or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, and confirmed candidate de novo mutations by Sanger sequencing of the proband and parents. Six de novo mutations affected GABA-A receptor subunit genes: 4 in GABRB3, 1 in GABRA1, and 1 in GABRB1. The authors proposed that individual rare mutations in different genes may converge on a shared pathway.

Janve et al. (2016) studied the de novo heterozygous GABRB1 mutation (F246S) that Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) had found in a patient with infantile spasms. The mutation lies in transmembrane domain 1, at a residue that is invariant across all GABA-A receptor subunits. The authors coexpressed the mutant beta-1 subunit with alpha-1 and gamma-2L subunits in HEK293T cells. In the homozygous expression condition, peak GABA-evoked current density was reduced by about 25%; in the heterozygous condition it was unchanged. Current deactivation was markedly slowed. In single-channel recordings, mean open time increased about 3.75-fold and burst duration about 3.2-fold, while conductance fell to about 21 pS. The mutation also increased holding currents about 5-fold in the homozygous condition, and these currents were partly blocked by zinc. Without GABA, the spontaneous open probability of mutant receptors was 4-fold higher, indicating spontaneous channel opening. Surface levels of the mutant beta-1 subunit were slightly but significantly reduced, while total levels were unchanged. The authors concluded that their results suggest GABRB1 is a candidate human epilepsy gene.

Lien et al. (2016) identified a de novo GABRB1 mutation in a second patient with epileptic encephalopathy.

Using whole-exome sequencing, Burgess et al. (2019) identified a de novo heterozygous missense variant in the GABRB1 gene (I247T) in a patient with EIMFS (patient 63). The variant was absent from the ExAC and gnomAD databases. PolyPhen predicted it to be benign and SIFT predicted it to be deleterious, and the authors classified it as likely pathogenic.

### Pathogenesis

Janve et al. (2016) proposed that the faster rise time and slower deactivation caused by the GABRB1 F246S mutation would lengthen the first inhibitory postsynaptic current and increase its charge transfer. They suggested, however, that the mutation would reduce charge transfer during later currents by lowering the number of unbound receptors, especially during high-frequency neuronal firing, causing a net loss of GABAergic inhibition. The authors noted that mutations associated with infantile spasms, including GABRB1 F246S, lay at beta-minus subunit interfaces and mainly changed current kinetics. They cautioned that the contribution of these mutations to the epilepsy phenotype is difficult to assess from in vitro data alone.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DEVELOPMENTAL AND EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY 45**  
GABRB1, PHE246SER [F246S]

Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) found a de novo mutation in the GABRB1 gene by trio exome sequencing in a patient with epileptic encephalopathy and confirmed it by Sanger sequencing. Janve et al. (2016) stated that this patient had infantile spasms and was heterozygous for a phe246-to-ser (F246S) substitution in transmembrane domain 1, at a residue that is invariant across GABA-A receptor subunits. In HEK293T cells, the mutation slowed current deactivation, prolonged single-channel mean open time and burst duration, reduced single-channel conductance, and caused spontaneous channel opening. In the homozygous expression condition it reduced peak GABA-evoked current density by about 25%.

**.0002 DEVELOPMENTAL AND EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY 45**  
GABRB1, ILE247THR [p.I247T; Chr4:47405633T>C]

Using whole-exome sequencing, Burgess et al. (2019) identified a de novo heterozygous ile247-to-thr (I247T) variant in the GABRB1 gene (chr4:47405633T-C, GRCh37) in a patient with epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures, severe to profound developmental impairment, and hypotonia. Seizures began at 4 months of age. The variant was absent from ExAC and gnomAD, and the authors classified it as likely pathogenic.

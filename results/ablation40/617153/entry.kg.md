### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GABRB1 gene (137190) on chromosome 4p12. ⚠︎

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 is a severe neurologic disorder with onset of seizures in infancy. Reported seizure types have included infantile spasms and focal seizures that migrate between hemispheres, as in epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (EIMFS). Affected individuals have had severe developmental impairment and hypotonia (Janve et al., 2016; Burgess et al., 2019). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

The Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) studied 264 probands with infantile spasms or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome. Patients with infantile spasms were required to show hypsarrhythmia on EEG, and patients with Lennox-Gastaut syndrome were required to show EEG background slowing and generalized spike-and-wave or generalized paroxysmal fast activity. All patients were required to have no moderate-to-severe developmental impairment before seizure onset, and their MRI or CT had to be normal or show only mild diffuse atrophy or focal cortical dysplasia. One proband in this cohort carried a de novo GABRB1 mutation. Janve et al. (2016) stated that this child had infantile spasms. ⚠︎

Lien et al. (2016) reported a second patient with epileptic encephalopathy who carried a de novo GABRB1 mutation.

Burgess et al. (2019) reported a patient (Patient 63) with epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (EIMFS) who was studied at age 2 years. Focal seizures began at 4 months of age, with interhemispheric seizure migration. The patient had profound to severe developmental impairment and hypotonia. The patient did not have epileptic spasms or microcephaly.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous GABRB1 mutations in patients with DEE45 occurred de novo (Epi4K Consortium et al., 2013; Janve et al., 2016; Lien et al., 2016; Burgess et al., 2019).

### Molecular Genetics

The Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) sequenced the exomes of 264 trios, made up of 149 probands with infantile spasms and 115 with Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, together with their parents. They found a de novo mutation in the GABRB1 gene in 1 proband. Six de novo mutations affected subunits of the GABA ionotropic receptor: 4 in GABRB3 and 1 each in GABRA1 and GABRB1. The authors suggested that rare mutations in different genes may converge on shared pathways.

Janve et al. (2016) carried out functional studies of a de novo heterozygous F246S mutation in GABRB1, which the Epi4K Consortium had found in a child with infantile spasms. F246 is conserved across all GABA-A receptor subunits and lies in transmembrane domain 1. The mutant subunit was coexpressed with alpha-1 and gamma-2L subunits in HEK293T cells. In the homozygous condition the mutation reduced GABA-evoked peak current density by 25%, and in the heterozygous condition it had no effect on peak current. It slowed current deactivation. The authors concluded that the mutation impaired GABA-A receptor function and suggested that GABRB1 is a candidate human epilepsy gene.

Lien et al. (2016) identified a de novo GABRB1 mutation in a second patient with epileptic encephalopathy.

Using whole-exome sequencing, Burgess et al. (2019) identified a de novo heterozygous I247T variant in GABRB1 in a patient with EIMFS. The variant was absent from the ExAC and gnomAD databases and was classified as likely pathogenic under ACMG criteria. Across their cohort, Burgess et al. (2019) found causative variants in 93 of 135 patients with EIMFS (69%), in 23 genes. Nine of these genes, including GABRA1 and GABRB1, had not previously been linked to EIMFS. The authors emphasized the role of GABA-related mechanisms and noted that 3 patients carried variants in GABRB3, a known EIMFS gene. They noted that GABRB1 had previously been reported in other developmental and epileptic encephalopathies.

### Pathogenesis

Janve et al. (2016) recorded single channels from receptors carrying the GABRB1 F246S mutation. Open probability was normal even though opening frequency was lower. The mutation produced a gain of function, lengthening mean open time about 3.75-fold and burst duration about 3.2-fold, and it lowered single-channel conductance to about 21 pS. In the homozygous condition the mutation raised whole-cell holding currents about 5-fold, and zinc partly blocked these currents. Without GABA, spontaneous single-channel open probability rose 4-fold, which suggested that receptors containing the mutant subunit opened spontaneously. Surface levels of the mutant beta-1 subunit were slightly but significantly reduced, while total levels did not change. Structural modeling predicted rearrangements in the coupling zone that spread to the transmembrane domains.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 DEVELOPMENTAL AND EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY 45**  
GABRB1, PHE246SER [p.F246S]

Janve et al. (2016) studied a de novo heterozygous phe246-to-ser (F246S) mutation in the GABRB1 gene in a child with infantile spasms. The mutation had been identified by the Epi4K Consortium. F246 is conserved across all GABA-A receptor subunits and lies in transmembrane domain 1. The mutant subunit was coexpressed with alpha-1 and gamma-2L subunits in HEK293T cells. In the homozygous condition, GABA-evoked peak current density fell by 25%, and in the heterozygous condition peak current was unchanged. Deactivation was slower. Single-channel recordings showed normal open probability despite a lower opening frequency, about 3.75-fold longer mean open time, about 3.2-fold longer burst duration, and conductance lowered to about 21 pS. Homozygous expression raised holding currents about 5-fold, and zinc partly blocked these currents. Spontaneous open probability without GABA rose 4-fold. Surface levels of the mutant subunit were slightly but significantly reduced. The authors proposed that the net effect was a loss of GABAergic inhibition.

**.0002 DEVELOPMENTAL AND EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY 45**  
GABRB1, ILE247THR [p.I247T]

In a patient with epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures, Burgess et al. (2019) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a de novo heterozygous T-to-C change in the GABRB1 gene (chr4:47405633T>C, GRCh37), predicted to cause an ile247-to-thr (I247T) substitution. Focal seizures began at 4 months of age, and the patient had profound to severe developmental impairment and hypotonia. The variant was absent from the ExAC and gnomAD databases. SIFT predicted it to be deleterious and PolyPhen predicted it to be benign. It was classified as likely pathogenic under ACMG criteria.

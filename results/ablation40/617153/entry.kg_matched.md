### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GABRB1 gene (137190) on chromosome 4p12. ⚠︎

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-45 (DEE45) is a severe neurologic disorder with seizures that begin in infancy. Reported seizure types include infantile spasms and the focal seizures of epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (EIMFS). Affected individuals may have severe to profound developmental impairment and hypotonia. The reported mutations in the GABRB1 gene have occurred de novo.

### Clinical Features

The Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) reported a patient with epileptic encephalopathy who carried a de novo mutation in the GABRB1 gene. The patient came from a cohort of 264 probands with infantile spasms or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome. Janve et al. (2016) later described this patient as having infantile spasms.

Lien et al. (2016) reported a second patient with epileptic encephalopathy and a de novo GABRB1 mutation.

Burgess et al. (2019) reported a patient with EIMFS and a de novo GABRB1 variant who was studied at age 2 years. Focal seizures began at 4 months of age. The patient had severe to profound developmental impairment and hypotonia. To enter the cohort, patients had to have focal seizures that migrated between hemispheres, seen clinically and/or on EEG, with onset before age 6 months.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous GABRB1 mutations in DEE45 occurred de novo in the patients reported by the Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013), Janve et al. (2016), and Burgess et al. (2019). This is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Heterogeneity

Burgess et al. (2019) found causative variants in 23 genes in 93 of 135 patients with EIMFS. Nine of these genes had not previously been linked to EIMFS. GABRB1 was one of them, and along with GABRA1 and ATP1A3 it carried de novo dominant variants. The authors noted that these findings, together with 3 patients who had GABRB3 variants, pointed to GABA-related mechanisms. They concluded that the phenotypic spectrum of GABA receptor subunit genes extends to EIMFS.

### Molecular Genetics

The Epi4K Consortium et al. (2013) performed exome sequencing on 264 probands with epileptic encephalopathy and their unaffected parents. Of these probands, 149 had infantile spasms and 115 had Lennox-Gastaut syndrome. Every candidate de novo mutation was confirmed by Sanger sequencing. One patient carried a de novo mutation in the GABRB1 gene. In the cohort, 6 de novo mutations affected GABA ionotropic receptor subunits: 4 in GABRB3 and 1 each in GABRA1 and GABRB1. ⚠︎

Janve et al. (2016) studied the de novo heterozygous phe246-to-ser (F246S) mutation in GABRB1 that the Epi4K Consortium had found in a patient with infantile spasms. F246 lies in transmembrane domain 1 of the beta-1 subunit and is invariant across all GABA-A receptor subunits. The authors expressed alpha-1/beta-1/gamma-2L receptors carrying the mutation in HEK293T cells. In whole-cell recordings, peak GABA-evoked current density fell by about 25% when only mutant beta-1 was present (hom condition) and was unchanged when wildtype and mutant beta-1 were mixed (het condition). In the het condition, the mutation markedly slowed current deactivation and shortened rise time. In single-channel recordings, open probability was unchanged although opening frequency decreased. The mutation acted as a gain of function: mean open time increased about 3.75-fold, burst duration increased about 3.2-fold, and single-channel conductance fell to about 21 pS. The mutation also made the receptors open spontaneously. In the hom condition, holding currents rose about 5-fold and were partly blocked by zinc. Without GABA, spontaneous single-channel open probability rose 4-fold. Surface levels of the mutant beta-1 subunit were slightly but significantly lower, while total levels were unchanged.

Lien et al. (2016) found a de novo GABRB1 mutation in a second patient with epileptic encephalopathy.

Burgess et al. (2019) performed whole-exome sequencing in a cohort of 135 patients with EIMFS. In 1 patient (Patient 63) they found a de novo heterozygous missense variant in the GABRB1 gene (chr4:47405633T-C, GRCh37), which causes an ile247-to-thr (I247T) substitution. The variant was absent from the ExAC and gnomAD databases. PolyPhen predicted it to be benign and SIFT predicted it to be deleterious. It was classified as likely pathogenic under ACMG criteria. ⚠︎

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Janve et al. (2016) observed that de novo GABRB mutations acted in 2 different ways depending on the syndrome. The mutations associated with Lennox-Gastaut syndrome lie at the beta+/alpha- interface and mainly reduced peak GABA-evoked currents. The mutations associated with infantile spasms, GABRB3 N110D and GABRB1 F246S, lie at beta- interfaces. They mainly changed current kinetics and caused small or no changes in current amplitude. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Janve et al. (2016) used structural modeling to predict that the beta-1 F246S mutation rearranges domains of the coupling zone between ligand binding and channel gating. The predicted effects reached the transmembrane domains at the gamma+/beta- and alpha+/beta- interfaces. The authors proposed that faster rise time and slower deactivation would lengthen inhibitory postsynaptic currents but reduce charge transfer during later currents, giving a net loss of GABAergic inhibition. They suggested that the mutant subunits affect receptor assembly and/or trafficking and that receptors containing them open spontaneously. They concluded that their results suggest GABRB1 as a candidate human epilepsy gene.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the DNAAF3 gene (614566) on chromosome 19q13.42. ⚠︎

### Description

Primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD) is an autosomal recessive disorder characterized by chronic sinusitis, bronchiectasis, and subfertility. It is caused by defective ciliary function with ultrastructural abnormalities that include absent dynein arms, absent radial spokes, and disturbed ciliary orientation. Situs inversus occurs in 50% of cases (Kartagener syndrome), and the estimated incidence is 1 in 20,000 live births (Meeks et al., 2000).

### Clinical Features

Mitchison et al. (2012) studied 10 patients with PCD from 3 consanguineous families: 2 families of Arabic origin and a UK-Pakistani family in which the parents were first cousins. The patients presented with neonatal respiratory distress, recurrent cough and wheezing, nasal discharge, chest infections, sinusitis, otitis media, nasal polyps, and bronchiectasis. Four of the 10 patients had situs inversus. Electron microscopy showed reduced or absent inner and outer dynein arms, and nasal cilia were immotile in all affected individuals. The authors noted that the disorder was associated with male infertility.

### Inheritance

Meeks et al. (2000) described PCD as an autosomal recessive condition. In the families studied by Mitchison et al. (2012), affected individuals were homozygous for DNAAF3 mutations and their unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Mapping

Meeks et al. (2000) carried out a genome scan in 5 Arabic families with primary ciliary dyskinesia. Using GENEHUNTER, they obtained a maximal multipoint lod score (HLOD) of 4.4 on chromosome 19q13.3-qter at alpha (the proportion of linked families) = 0.7. Recombinations at D19S572 and D19S218 defined a critical region of 15 cM.

Mitchison et al. (2012) noted that DNAAF3 (previously called C19orf51), on chromosome 19q13, lies within the CILD2 locus mapped in consanguineous Arabic-origin PCD families with immotile cilia and absent outer dynein arms. Affected individuals in 2 of the 3 originally linked families showed large regions of marker homozygosity across the DNAAF3 locus.

### Heterogeneity

Meeks et al. (2000) stated that their data gave significant evidence for a PCD locus on chromosome 19q and confirmed locus heterogeneity; the estimated proportion of linked families was 0.7.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of a consanguineous Arabic-origin family (UCL89) with CILD2, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous mutation in exon 3 of the DNAAF3 gene, a c.323T-C transition resulting in a leu108-to-pro (L108P) substitution at a highly conserved residue. The variant was absent from 148 population-matched Arabic control chromosomes and from the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, and NHLBI-ESP databases, and was predicted to be probably damaging. It segregated with recessive inheritance: the parents and unaffected sibs were heterozygous.

In affected members of a second Arabic-origin family (UCL66/67) with CILD2, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous c.406C-T transition in exon 4 of the DNAAF3 gene, resulting in an arg136-to-ter (R136X) substitution. The variant was absent from 148 Arabic control chromosomes and from the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, and NHLBI-ESP databases, and the unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous.

Mitchison et al. (2012) screened 112 additional PCD families. Affected members of a UK-Pakistani family (UCL71) in which the parents were first cousins showed linkage to the chromosome 19 locus and carried a homozygous 1-bp insertion in exon 6 of DNAAF3 (c.762_763insT). The insertion was predicted to cause a frameshift and premature termination (Val255CysfsTer12). It was absent from 172 UK-Pakistani control chromosomes and segregated with the disorder.

Mitchison et al. (2012) also found a homozygous c.973G-A variant in DNAAF3, resulting in an ala325-to-thr (A325T) substitution, in a Belgian patient with PCD. They considered it likely to be a polymorphism in Caucasians because it was found on 1 of 192 Caucasian control chromosomes, was predicted to be benign, and affected a nonconserved residue.

### Pathogenesis

Using immunofluorescence, Mitchison et al. (2012) found that the outer dynein arm components DNAH5, DNAH9, and DNAI2 and the inner dynein arm light chain DNALI1 were absent from the respiratory cilia of patients with DNAAF3 mutations. In some patients, residual DNAH5 and DNALI1 were present in the apical cytoplasm. These findings suggested that DNAAF3 is required for the cytoplasmic preassembly of outer and inner dynein arms along the entire axoneme. ⚠︎

### Animal Model

Mitchison et al. (2012) found that the Chlamydomonas pf22 mutant, which carries a nonsense mutation (Trp79Ter) in the DNAAF3 ortholog, had paralyzed half-length flagella that lacked outer dynein arms and inner dynein arms b and c. The PF22 protein was located only in the cytoplasm. In pf22 cytoplasm, IC2 was hyperabundant and heavy chain-intermediate chain complexes formed abnormally, suggesting that PF22 acts at a later step of dynein preassembly than PF13 and ODA7.

Mitchison et al. (2012) found that morpholino knockdown of dnaaf3 in zebrafish caused immotile olfactory placode cilia with reduced or missing dynein arms. Other defects were body axis curvature, hydrocephalus, pronephric cysts, otolith defects, and left-right patterning defects. About half of dnaaf3MOex8 embryos showed reversed or absent heart looping.

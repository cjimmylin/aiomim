### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the DNAAF3 gene (614566) on chromosome 19q13.

### Description

Primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD) is an autosomal recessive disorder characterized by chronic sinusitis, bronchiectasis, and subfertility. Situs inversus occurs in 50% of cases (Kartagener syndrome). The clinical phenotype is caused by defective ciliary function associated with a range of ultrastructural abnormalities, including absent dynein arms, absent radial spokes, and disturbed ciliary orientation (Meeks et al., 2000).

CILD2 caused by DNAAF3 mutation is characterized by immotile respiratory cilia that lack both outer and inner dynein arms (Mitchison et al., 2012).

### Clinical Features

Mitchison et al. (2012) reported 10 patients with PCD from 3 families carrying DNAAF3 mutations: 2 consanguineous Arabic-origin families (UCL89 and UCL66/67) and a first-cousin UK-Pakistani family (UCL71). The parents of all affected individuals except 2 individuals in family UCL66/67 were first cousins. Family UCL89 included affected monozygotic twins. The patients presented with classic PCD symptoms, including neonatal respiratory distress, recurrent cough and wheezing, nasal discharge and chest infections, sinusitis, otitis media, nasal polyps, and bronchiectasis. Four of the 10 affected individuals had situs inversus. Cilia in nasal brushing biopsies were immotile in all affected family members. Electron microscopy of respiratory cilia, where available, showed reduced or absent inner and outer dynein arms. By immunofluorescence analysis, outer dynein arm components DNAH5, DNAH9, and DNAI2, and inner dynein arm light chain DNALI1, were all absent from the cilia of affected patients; in some patients, residual DNAH5 and DNALI1 were seen in the apical cytoplasm, which was not observed in controls.

### Inheritance

Meeks et al. (2000) described primary ciliary dyskinesia as an autosomal recessive condition. In the families reported by Mitchison et al. (2012), all affected individuals carried homozygous DNAAF3 mutations, whereas unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Mapping

Meeks et al. (2000) performed a genome scan in 5 Arabic families with primary ciliary dyskinesia. Using GENEHUNTER, they obtained a maximum multipoint lod score (HLOD) of 4.4 on chromosome 19q13.3-qter at alpha (proportion of linked families) = 0.7. A 15-cM critical region was defined by recombinations at D19S572 and D19S218.

Mitchison et al. (2012) noted that the DNAAF3 gene on chromosome 19q13 lies within the CILD2 locus. Affected individuals in 2 of the 3 originally linked families (UCL66/67 and UCL89) showed large regions of marker homozygosity across the DNAAF3 locus.

### Heterogeneity

Meeks et al. (2000) concluded that their linkage data provided significant evidence for a PCD locus on chromosome 19q and confirmed locus heterogeneity.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of 2 consanguineous Arabic-origin families with PCD originally linked to the CILD2 locus on chromosome 19q, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified homozygous mutations in the DNAAF3 gene: a c.323T-C transition in exon 3, resulting in a leu108-to-pro (L108P) substitution, in family UCL89, and a c.406C-T transition in exon 4, resulting in an arg136-to-ter (R136X) substitution, in family UCL66/67. Neither variant was present in 148 population-matched Arabic control chromosomes. The L108P mutation was predicted to be probably damaging and affected a residue highly conserved across ciliated species.

Mitchison et al. (2012) sequenced DNAAF3 in 112 additional PCD families, 107 with outer dynein arm defects and 9 with evidence of linkage to the chromosome 19 locus. In 1 family showing evidence of linkage (UCL71), a first-cousin UK-Pakistani family, they identified a homozygous 1-bp insertion (c.762_763insT) in exon 6, predicted to result in a frameshift and premature termination (Val255CysfsTer12); the variant was absent from 172 UK-Pakistani control chromosomes. For all 3 mutations, affected individuals were homozygous and unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous, and none of the mutations was present in the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, or NHLBI-ESP databases. A homozygous c.973G-A variant (A325T) found in a Belgian patient was also carried on 1 of 192 Caucasian control chromosomes, was predicted to be benign, affected a nonconserved residue, and was considered likely to be a Caucasian polymorphism.

### Pathogenesis

Mitchison et al. (2012) found that the Chlamydomonas ortholog of DNAAF3, PF22, was exclusively cytoplasmic. In pf22 mutant cytoplasm, outer dynein arm heavy chains were retained at nearly wildtype levels, whereas the intermediate chain IC2 was hyperabundant. Heavy chain trypsin sensitivity was less strongly altered in pf22 than in pf13 and oda7 mutants, and an epitope on the HC-beta tail that was exposed in wildtype cytoplasm was inaccessible in pf22 cytoplasm. Mitchison et al. (2012) concluded that PF22/DNAAF3 acts in the cytoplasmic preassembly of axonemal dyneins, likely at a later step than PF13/DNAAF1 and ODA7/DNAAF2, such as stabilization of heavy chains or joining of heavy chains and smaller subunits into a larger complex, and hypothesized that PF22 may be required for release of the product from a chaperone complex.

### Animal Model

Mitchison et al. (2012) found that the Chlamydomonas pf22 mutant, which has paralyzed half-length flagella, carries a nonsense mutation (Trp79Ter) in the ortholog of DNAAF3, predicting a null allele. pf22 axonemes lacked outer dynein arms and inner dynein arms 'b' and 'c,' but retained inner arm 'f'; transformation with the wildtype gene rescued the phenotype. Morpholino knockdown of dnaaf3 in zebrafish caused completely static olfactory placode cilia with reduced or missing outer and inner dynein arms, downward body axis curvature (64% and 78% of embryos with the 2 splice-blocking morpholinos), hydrocephalus (18% and 34%), progressive pronephric cysts, abnormal otolith number, and laterality defects, with about half of dnaaf3 MO ex8 embryos showing reversed or absent heart looping.

DNAAF3, LEU108PRO [c.323T>C; p.L108P; p.Leu108Pro]

In affected members of a consanguineous Arabic-origin family (UCL89) with primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2), including affected monozygotic twins, Mitchison et al. The mutation was predicted to be probably damaging, was not present in 148 population-matched Arabic control chromosomes or in the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, or NHLBI-ESP databases, and unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous.

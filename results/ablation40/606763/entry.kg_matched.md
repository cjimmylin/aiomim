## CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2; CILD2

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the DNAAF3 gene (614566) on chromosome 19q13.42. ⚠︎

### Description

Meeks et al. (2000) described primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD) as an autosomal recessive condition with chronic sinusitis, bronchiectasis, and subfertility. Situs inversus occurs in 50% of cases (Kartagener syndrome). The phenotype is caused by defective ciliary function associated with ultrastructural abnormalities, including absent dynein arms, absent radial spokes, and disturbed ciliary orientation.

### Clinical Features

Mitchison et al. (2012) described 10 patients with PCD from 3 consanguineous families carrying DNAAF3 mutations, including families of Arabic origin and a UK-Pakistani family. The parents of all affected individuals except 2 were first cousins. The patients had classic PCD symptoms, including neonatal respiratory distress, recurrent cough and wheezing, nasal discharge, chest infections, sinusitis, otitis media, nasal polyps, and bronchiectasis; 4 of the 10 had situs inversus. Nasal brushing showed immotile cilia in all affected family members.

By electron microscopy, Mitchison et al. (2012) found that respiratory cilia from patients with DNAAF3 mutations lacked both outer and inner dynein arms. Immunofluorescence of nasal brush biopsies showed absence of the outer dynein arm components DNAH5, DNAH9, and DNAI2 and of the inner dynein arm light chain DNALI1 from cilia, with residual DNAH5 and DNALI1 seen in the apical cytoplasm in some patients. These findings suggested that DNAAF3 is required for assembly of outer and inner dynein arms along the entire axoneme.

### Inheritance

Meeks et al. (2000) described primary ciliary dyskinesia as an autosomal recessive condition. Mitchison et al. (2012) found that, for the 3 pathogenic DNAAF3 mutations, all affected individuals were homozygous whereas unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance of CILD2.

### Mapping

Meeks et al. (2000) performed a genome scan in 5 Arabic families with primary ciliary dyskinesia. Using GENEHUNTER, they obtained a maximum multipoint lod score (HLOD) of 4.4 on chromosome 19q13.3-qter at alpha (the proportion of linked families) = 0.7. Recombinations at D19S572 and D19S218 defined a 15-cM critical region.

Mitchison et al. (2012) noted that the DNAAF3 gene (previously designated C19orf51) on chromosome 19q13 lies within the CILD2 locus, which had been mapped in consanguineous Arabic-origin PCD families with immotile cilia lacking outer dynein arms. Affected individuals in 2 of the 3 originally linked families (UCL66/67 and UCL89) showed large regions of marker homozygosity across the DNAAF3 locus.

### Heterogeneity

Meeks et al. (2000) concluded that their data gave significant evidence for a PCD locus on chromosome 19q and confirmed locus heterogeneity, because only a proportion (alpha = 0.7) of the families studied were linked. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of a consanguineous Arabic-origin family (UCL89) with CILD2, originally linked to the CILD2 locus and including affected monozygotic twins, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous mutation in exon 3 of the DNAAF3 gene (L108P), affecting a residue highly conserved across ciliated species. The variant was not found in 148 population-matched Arabic control chromosomes or in the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, or NHLBI-ESP databases, and was predicted to be probably damaging.

In affected members of an Arabic-origin family (UCL66/67) with CILD2, originally linked to the CILD2 locus, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in exon 4 of the DNAAF3 gene (R136X). The variant was absent from 148 population-matched Arabic control chromosomes and from the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, and NHLBI-ESP databases.

By sequencing DNAAF3 in 112 additional PCD families, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous 1-bp insertion in exon 6 (c.762_763insT) in a first-cousin UK-Pakistani family (UCL71) showing evidence of linkage to chromosome 19; the insertion was predicted to cause a frameshift and premature termination. The variant was absent from 172 UK-Pakistani control chromosomes and from the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, and NHLBI-ESP databases. In all 3 families, affected individuals were homozygous and unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous for the mutations.

Mitchison et al. (2012) also found a homozygous c.973G-A variant in DNAAF3, resulting in an ala325-to-thr (A325T) substitution, in a Belgian PCD patient (OP-549). However, this variant was carried by 1 of 192 Caucasian control chromosomes, was predicted to be benign, affected a nonconserved residue, and was considered likely to be a Caucasian polymorphism.

### Pathogenesis

Mitchison et al. (2012) found that tagged Chlamydomonas PF22, the ortholog of DNAAF3, was exclusively cytoplasmic. In pf22 mutant cytoplasm, outer arm heavy chains were retained at near wildtype levels, whereas intermediate chain IC2 was hyperabundant, and an HC-beta tail epitope was inaccessible. These findings suggested that PF22/DNAAF3 acts in cytoplasmic preassembly of dyneins at a later step than PF13/DNAAF1 and ODA7/DNAAF2, such as heavy chain stabilization or joining of heavy chains with smaller subunits, and that it may be needed for chaperone release.

### Population Genetics

Meeks et al. (2000) cited an estimated incidence of primary ciliary dyskinesia of 1 in 20,000 live births.

### Animal Model

Mitchison et al. (2012) identified the gene disrupted in the Chlamydomonas pf22 mutant, which has paralyzed half-length flagella, by mapping and phenotypic rescue. The mutant carries a nonsense mutation (trp79-to-ter; W79X) predicting a null allele. pf22 axonemes lacked outer dynein arms and inner dynein arms 'b' and 'c' but retained inner arm 'f', and transformation with the wildtype gene rescued the phenotype.

Mitchison et al. (2012) found that morpholino knockdown of dnaaf3 in zebrafish caused static olfactory placode cilia with reduced or missing outer and inner dynein arms, downward body axis curvature (64-78% of embryos), hydrocephalus (18-34%), progressive pronephric cysts, abnormal otolith number, and laterality defects; about half of dnaaf3 MO ex8 embryos showed reversed or absent heart looping.

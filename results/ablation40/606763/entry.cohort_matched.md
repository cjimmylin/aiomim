### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the DNAAF3 gene (614566) on chromosome 19q13.

### Description

Primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD) is an autosomal recessive disorder of defective ciliary function. It is characterized by chronic sinusitis, bronchiectasis, and subfertility. Situs inversus occurs in 50% of cases, a combination known as Kartagener syndrome. The ciliary defect is associated with ultrastructural abnormalities such as absent dynein arms, absent radial spokes, and disturbed ciliary orientation (Meeks et al., 2000). In CILD2, respiratory cilia are immotile and both outer and inner dynein arms are reduced or absent (Mitchison et al., 2012).

### Clinical Features

Meeks et al. (2000) studied 5 Arabic families with primary ciliary dyskinesia.

Mitchison et al. (2012) reported 10 PCD patients with DNAAF3 mutations, 4 of whom had situs inversus. The patients came from 2 consanguineous families of Arabic origin (UCL89 and UCL66/67) and 1 consanguineous UK-Pakistani family (UCL71) from a first-cousin union. All had classic PCD symptoms, including neonatal respiratory distress, recurrent cough, wheezing and chest infections, sinusitis, otitis media, nasal polyps, and bronchiectasis. Respiratory cilia were immotile in all affected family members. Electron microscopy showed reduced or absent inner and outer dynein arms. In family UCL89, transmission electron microscopy of cilia cross sections showed loss of both outer and inner dynein arms in affected individuals.

### Inheritance

Meeks et al. (2000) described PCD as an autosomal recessive condition. In 3 consanguineous families with CILD2 reported by Mitchison et al. (2012), all affected individuals carried homozygous DNAAF3 mutations, and unaffected parents and sibs carried heterozygous changes. This was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Mapping

Meeks et al. (2000) performed a genome scan in 5 Arabic families with primary ciliary dyskinesia. Using GENEHUNTER, they obtained a maximum multipoint lod score (HLOD) of 4.4 on chromosome 19q13.3-qter at alpha (proportion of linked families) = 0.7. Recombinations at D19S572 and D19S218 defined a 15-cM critical region.

Mitchison et al. (2012) noted that DNAAF3 (C19orf51), on chromosome 19q13, lies within the CILD2 locus. This locus had been mapped in consanguineous PCD families of Arabic origin whose immotile cilia lacked outer dynein arms.

### Heterogeneity

Meeks et al. (2000) concluded that their data provided significant evidence for a PCD locus on chromosome 19q and confirmed locus heterogeneity. Only about 70% of the families they studied appeared to be linked to this locus.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of 2 of the 3 Arabic families originally linked to the CILD2 locus on chromosome 19q, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified homozygous mutations in the DNAAF3 gene. Affected individuals in both families showed large regions of marker homozygosity across the DNAAF3 locus. The mutations were a missense mutation (L108P) in family UCL89 and a nonsense mutation (R136X) in family UCL66/67. Mitchison et al. (2012) also sequenced DNAAF3 in 112 additional PCD families. In a consanguineous UK-Pakistani family (UCL71) showing evidence of linkage, they found a homozygous 1-bp insertion (c.762_763insT) that was predicted to cause a frameshift (V255CfsX12). For all 3 mutations, affected individuals were homozygous and unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous. None of the mutations was found in population-matched control chromosomes or in the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, or NHLBI-ESP databases. Mitchison et al. (2012) also found a homozygous c.973G-A substitution (A325T) in a Belgian patient with PCD (OP-549). Because this residue is not conserved, they considered the variant likely to be a Caucasian polymorphism.

### Pathogenesis

In respiratory cells from patients with DNAAF3 mutations, Mitchison et al. (2012) found that the outer dynein arm proteins DNAH5, DNAH9, and DNAI2 and the inner dynein arm light chain DNALI1 were absent from cilia. This suggested that DNAAF3 is needed to assemble outer and inner dynein arms along the full length of the axoneme. In some patients, residual DNAH5 and DNALI1 accumulated in the apical cytoplasm. In Chlamydomonas, tagged PF22 protein, the DNAAF3 ortholog, was located only in the cytoplasm. In pf22 cytoplasm, the outer arm intermediate chain IC2 was over-abundant and an HC-beta epitope was inaccessible, while dynein heavy chain folding was less disturbed than in pf13 or oda7 mutants. The authors suggested that PF22/DNAAF3 acts at a later step of cytoplasmic dynein preassembly than PF13/DNAAF1 and ODA7/DNAAF2, such as joining heavy chains with smaller subunits.

### Population Genetics

Meeks et al. (2000) cited an estimated incidence of primary ciliary dyskinesia of 1 in 20,000 live births.

### Animal Model

Mitchison et al. (2012) found that the Chlamydomonas pf22 mutant carries a nonsense mutation (W79X) in the DNAAF3 ortholog. The mutant had paralyzed, half-length flagella that lacked outer dynein arms and inner dynein arms 'b' and 'c'. Rescue with a genomic fragment restored the wildtype phenotype.

Mitchison et al. (2012) found that morpholino knockdown of dnaaf3 in zebrafish made olfactory placode cilia immotile and caused loss of outer and inner dynein arms. Morphants also showed body axis curvature, hydrocephalus, pronephric cysts, otolith defects, and left-right patterning defects. About half of dnaaf3MOex8 embryos had reversed or absent heart looping. ⚠︎

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, LEU108PRO [c.323T>C; p.L108P]

In affected members of a consanguineous family of Arabic origin (UCL89) with primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2), Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous c.323T-C transition in exon 3 of the DNAAF3 gene, resulting in a leu108-to-pro (L108P) substitution. Unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous. The mutation was not found in population-matched control chromosomes or in the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, or NHLBI-ESP databases. Respiratory cilia from affected individuals were immotile. Transmission electron microscopy showed loss of both outer and inner dynein arms.

**.0002 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**

In affected members of a consanguineous family of Arabic origin (UCL66/67) with primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2), Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous c.406C-T transition in exon 4 of the DNAAF3 gene, resulting in an arg136-to-ter (R136X) substitution. Unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous. The mutation was not found in population-matched control chromosomes or in the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, or NHLBI-ESP databases. Respiratory cilia from affected individuals were immotile.

**.0003 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, 1-BP INS, 762T [c.762_763inst; p.V255Cfs; p.V255fs]

In affected members of a UK-Pakistani family (UCL71) with primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2) from a first-cousin union, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous 1-bp insertion (c.762_763insT) in exon 6 of the DNAAF3 gene. The insertion was predicted to cause a frameshift, giving a val255-to-cys change followed by 11 novel amino acids and a premature stop codon (V255CfsX12). Unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous. The variant was absent from 172 control UK-Pakistani chromosomes. Respiratory cilia from affected individuals were immotile.

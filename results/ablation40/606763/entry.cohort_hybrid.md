### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the DNAAF3 gene (614566) on chromosome 19q13.

### Description

Primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD) is an autosomal recessive condition characterized by chronic sinusitis, bronchiectasis, and subfertility. It results from defective ciliary function, and Meeks et al. (2000) noted that situs inversus occurs in half of cases (Kartagener syndrome). Patients with CILD2 described by Mitchison et al. (2012) had immotile respiratory cilia lacking both outer and inner dynein arms, and some had situs inversus.

### Clinical Features

Meeks et al. (2000) studied 5 Arabic families with primary ciliary dyskinesia.

Mitchison et al. (2012) reported 10 patients from 3 consanguineous families with PCD and DNAAF3 mutations. Two families (UCL89 and UCL66/67) were of Arabic origin and had previously been linked to a common locus. The third family (UCL71) was a UK-Pakistani family from a first-cousin union. The parents of all affected individuals except UCL66/67 III.1 and III.2 were first cousins. Family UCL89 included affected monozygotic twins. The families had classic clinical symptoms, including neonatal respiratory distress, recurrent cough and wheezing, nasal discharge and chest infections, sinusitis, otitis media, nasal polyps, and bronchiectasis. Four of the 10 affected individuals had situs inversus. Where electron microscopy of respiratory cilia was available, it showed reduced or absent inner and outer dynein arms. Cilia in nasal brushing biopsies were immotile in all affected family members. The authors stated that loss of DNAAF3 gives rise to classic PCD with defective left-right organ asymmetry and male infertility.

### Inheritance

Meeks et al. (2000) described primary ciliary dyskinesia as an autosomal recessive condition. In 3 families with CILD2, Mitchison et al. (2012) found that all affected individuals were homozygous for DNAAF3 mutations and that unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Mapping

Meeks et al. (2000) performed a genome scan in 5 Arabic families with PCD. Using GENEHUNTER, they obtained a maximum multipoint lod score (HLOD) of 4.4 on chromosome 19q13.3-qter at alpha (proportion of linked families) = 0.7. Recombinations at D19S572 and D19S218 defined a 15-cM critical region.

Mitchison et al. (2012) noted that the DNAAF3 gene (previously designated C19orf51) on chromosome 19q13 lies within the CILD2 locus. This locus had been mapped in consanguineous Arabic-origin PCD families whose immotile cilia lacked outer dynein arms.

### Heterogeneity

Meeks et al. (2000) concluded that their data provided significant evidence for a PCD locus on chromosome 19q and confirmed locus heterogeneity. The maximum lod score was obtained at a proportion of linked families of 0.7.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of 2 of the 3 Arabic families originally linked to the CILD2 locus, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified homozygous mutations in the DNAAF3 gene. Affected individuals in both families had large regions of marker homozygosity across the DNAAF3 locus. Family UCL89 carried a missense mutation (L108P) and family UCL66/67 carried a nonsense mutation (R136X). Neither variant was found in 148 population-matched control Arabic chromosomes. The authors then sequenced DNAAF3 in 112 additional PCD families, 107 with outer dynein arm defects and 9 with evidence of linkage to the chromosome 19 locus. One linked family, UCL71, a first-cousin union UK-Pakistani family, was homozygous for a 1-bp insertion (c.762_763insT) predicted to cause a frameshift. This variant was absent from 172 control UK-Pakistani chromosomes. In all 3 families, affected individuals were homozygous and unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous. None of the mutations was present in the dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, or NHLBI-ESP databases. A homozygous missense variant (A325T) in a Belgian patient was considered likely to be a Caucasian polymorphism, because it was found on 1 of 192 Caucasian control chromosomes, was predicted to be benign, and affected a nonconserved residue.

### Pathogenesis

Mitchison et al. (2012) studied respiratory cells from patients with DNAAF3 mutations by immunofluorescence. The outer dynein arm proteins DNAH5, DNAH9, and DNAI2 and the inner dynein arm light chain DNALI1 were all absent from cilia. This suggested that DNAAF3 is important for assembling outer and inner dynein arms along the entire length of the axoneme. In some patients, residual DNAH5 and DNALI1 were seen in the apical cytoplasm. In the Chlamydomonas ortholog PF22, the protein was found only in the cytoplasm. In pf22 cytoplasm, the outer arm intermediate chain IC2 was hyperabundant and an epitope on the HC-beta tail was inaccessible. Heavy chain folding was less disturbed than in pf13 or oda7 mutants. From these findings, the authors suggested that PF22/DNAAF3 acts at a later step of cytoplasmic dynein preassembly than PF13/DNAAF1 and ODA7/DNAAF2, such as the joining of heavy chains with smaller subunits.

### Animal Model

Mitchison et al. (2012) found that the Chlamydomonas pf22 strain carries a nonsense mutation (W79X) in the DNAAF3 ortholog. The mutant had paralyzed, half-length flagella that lacked outer dynein arms and inner dynein arms 'b' and 'c'. Transformation with a 7.2-kb genomic fragment rescued the phenotype to wildtype. Morpholino knockdown of dnaaf3 in zebrafish made olfactory placode cilia immotile and reduced or eliminated outer and inner dynein arms. Morphants also showed abnormal body axis curvature, hydrocephalus, pronephric cysts, and abnormal otoliths. About half of dnaaf3MOex8 embryos had reversed or absent heart looping.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, LEU108PRO [c.323T>C; p.L108P; p.Leu108Pro]

In affected members of a consanguineous Arabic family (UCL89) with CILD2, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous c.323T-C transition in exon 3 of the DNAAF3 gene, resulting in a leu108-to-pro (L108P) substitution at a residue that is highly conserved across ciliated species. The mutation was predicted to be probably damaging. It was not found in 148 population-matched control Arabic chromosomes, and unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous. Electron microscopy of cilia from affected individuals showed loss of both outer and inner dynein arms, and immunofluorescence showed absence of DNAH5 and DNALI1 from cilia.

**.0002 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, ARG136TER [c.406C>T; p.R136*; p.Arg136X]

In affected members of an Arabic family (UCL66/67) with CILD2, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous c.406C-T transition in exon 4 of the DNAAF3 gene, resulting in an arg136-to-ter (R136X) substitution. The mutation was not found in 148 population-matched control Arabic chromosomes, and unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous.

**.0003 CILIARY DYSKINESIA, PRIMARY, 2**  
DNAAF3, 1-BP INS, 762T [c.762_763inst; p.V255Cfs; p.V255fs; c.762_763insT; p.Val255CysfsX12]

In affected members of a first-cousin union UK-Pakistani family (UCL71) with CILD2, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified a homozygous 1-bp insertion (c.762_763insT) in exon 6 of the DNAAF3 gene. The insertion was predicted to cause a frameshift that generates 11 novel amino acids after a val255-to-cys change, followed by a premature stop codon (Val255CysfsX12). The variant was absent from 172 control UK-Pakistani chromosomes, and unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous.

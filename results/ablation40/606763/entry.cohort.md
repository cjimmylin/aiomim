### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that primary ciliary dyskinesia-2 (CILD2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the DNAAF3 gene (614566) on chromosome 19q13.42. ⚠︎

### Description

Meeks et al. (2000) described primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD) as an autosomal recessive condition with chronic sinusitis, bronchiectasis, and subfertility. Situs inversus occurs in 50% of cases (Kartagener syndrome). The phenotype results from defective ciliary function and is associated with ultrastructural abnormalities, including absent dynein arms, absent radial spokes, and disturbed ciliary orientation.

### Clinical Features

Meeks et al. (2000) studied 5 Arabic families with primary ciliary dyskinesia.

Mitchison et al. (2012) reported 10 affected individuals with DNAAF3 mutations, all with classic PCD symptoms. Four of the 10 had situs inversus. Their respiratory cilia were immotile, with reduced or absent inner and outer dynein arms. In family UCL89, a consanguineous family of Arabic origin, the affected individuals had neonatal respiratory distress, recurrent chest infections, sinusitis, otitis media, and bronchiectasis. Their respiratory cilia were immotile, and transmission electron microscopy of ciliary cross sections showed loss of both outer and inner dynein arms. Affected individuals in family UCL66/67, also of Arabic origin, had recurrent chest infections and bronchiectasis, with immotile respiratory cilia that lacked outer dynein arms. The parents of all affected individuals were first cousins, except for the parents of UCL66/67 III.1 and III.2. Affected members of family UCL71, a UK-Pakistani family born of a first-cousin union, had recurrent chest infections, bronchiectasis, and immotile respiratory cilia. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Meeks et al. (2000) described primary ciliary dyskinesia as an autosomal recessive condition. Mitchison et al. (2012) confirmed autosomal recessive inheritance of CILD2. In 3 families, all affected individuals were homozygous for DNAAF3 mutations, and their unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous.

### Mapping

Meeks et al. (2000) performed a genome scan in 5 Arabic families with primary ciliary dyskinesia. Using GENEHUNTER, they obtained a maximum multipoint lod score (HLOD) of 4.4 on chromosome 19q13.3-qter at alpha (the proportion of linked families) = 0.7. Recombinations at D19S572 and D19S218 defined a 15-cM critical region.

Mitchison et al. (2012) noted that the DNAAF3 gene (C19orf51) on chromosome 19q13 lies within the CILD2 locus. This locus had been mapped in consanguineous PCD families of Arabic origin who had absent outer dynein arms. In 2 of the 3 originally linked families, which showed large regions of marker homozygosity across the locus, they found homozygous DNAAF3 mutations.

### Heterogeneity

Meeks et al. (2000) concluded that their data provided significant evidence for a PCD locus on chromosome 19q and confirmed locus heterogeneity in primary ciliary dyskinesia. Their linkage analysis gave a proportion of linked families (alpha) of 0.7.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected members of 2 consanguineous Arabic families with CILD2 that had originally been linked to chromosome 19q, Mitchison et al. (2012) identified homozygous mutations in the DNAAF3 gene. Family UCL89 carried a missense mutation in exon 3 (c.323T-C; L108P), and family UCL66/67 carried a nonsense mutation in exon 4 (c.406C-T; R136X). The authors then sequenced DNAAF3 in 112 additional PCD families, 107 of which had outer dynein arm defects. Only 1 further family had a homozygous pathogenic mutation: UCL71, a UK-Pakistani family born of a first-cousin union that showed evidence of linkage. This family carried a 1-bp insertion in exon 6 (c.762_763insT) predicted to cause a frameshift and premature termination (Val255CysfsTer12). The insertion was absent from 172 control UK-Pakistani chromosomes. In all 3 families, affected individuals were homozygous and unaffected parents and sibs were heterozygous. None of the mutations was present in dbSNP, 1000 Genomes, or NHLBI-ESP. A homozygous c.973G-A substitution (A325T), found in a Belgian patient, affected a nonconserved residue and was considered likely to be a Caucasian polymorphism.

### Pathogenesis

Mitchison et al. (2012) used immunofluorescence to examine respiratory cilia from patients with DNAAF3 mutations. The outer arm proteins DNAH5, DNAH9, and DNAI2 and the inner arm light chain DNALI1 were absent from the cilia. In some patients, residual DNAH5 and DNALI1 were found in the apical cytoplasm. These findings suggested that DNAAF3 is needed for the cytoplasmic preassembly of both outer and inner dynein arms.

### Population Genetics

Meeks et al. (2000) stated that primary ciliary dyskinesia has an estimated incidence of 1 in 20,000 live births.

### Animal Model

Mitchison et al. (2012) showed that PF22, the Chlamydomonas ortholog of DNAAF3, is exclusively cytoplasmic. The pf22 null mutant (Trp79X) had paralyzed, half-length flagella that lacked outer dynein arms and inner dynein arms b and c. Biochemical studies suggested that PF22 acts at a later step of dynein preassembly than PF13 and ODA7, such as the association of heavy chains with smaller subunits.

Mitchison et al. (2012) found that morpholino knockdown of dnaaf3 in zebrafish caused immotile olfactory placode cilia with reduced or missing dynein arms. The knockdown embryos also showed body curvature, hydrocephalus, pronephric cysts, otolith defects, and laterality defects. About half of dnaaf3MOex8 embryos had reversed heart looping or no heart looping.

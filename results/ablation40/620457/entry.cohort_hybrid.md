### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that auriculocondylar syndrome-4 (ARCND4) is caused by a 430-kb tandem duplication covering most of the HDAC9 gene (606543) on chromosome 7p21.1. The duplication includes possible regulatory elements of the neighboring TWIST1 gene.

### Description

Auriculocondylar syndrome is a disorder of structures derived from the first and second pharyngeal arches. Its main features are malformed ears ('question mark ears'), prominent cheeks, microstomia, abnormal temporomandibular joint and mandibular condyle hypoplasia (Masotti et al., 2008). Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) also listed micrognathia among the main features and noted wide clinical variability, including cases with isolated ear anomalies.

### Clinical Features

Masotti et al. (2008) described a multigeneration family (F1) with 11 individuals affected by auriculocondylar syndrome and confirmed intrafamilial clinical variability. Facial asymmetry was highly prevalent among the affected members, and the authors noted that this feature had not been highlighted in other reports of the syndrome.

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) studied a previously reported nonconsanguineous 4-generation Brazilian family (F1) with 11 members who had the typical features of ARCND. Among the documented members, most had question mark ears (8 of 10), microstomia (8 of 10) and micrognathia (6 of 10), and intrafamilial variability was considerable. The patients did not have large, open fontanelles.

### Inheritance

Masotti et al. (2008) described auriculocondylar syndrome as an autosomal dominant disorder. In the 4-generation family reported by Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022), the transmission of ARCND4 was consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance: the causative duplication segregated with the disease, and all unaffected family members tested negative for it, which suggested full penetrance.

### Mapping

Masotti et al. (2008) did linkage analysis in 2 large auriculocondylar syndrome families, F1 and F2. Segregation analysis excluded the loci for Treacher Collins syndrome, the oculoauriculovertebral spectrum and Townes-Brocks syndrome. A genomewide search found evidence of linkage to 1p21.1-q23.3 in family F2 (maximum lod score 3.01 at theta = 0), but this locus was not linked to the phenotype in family F1.

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) did linkage analysis under an autosomal dominant model in a large Brazilian ARCND family, genotyping 9 affected and 3 unaffected members. Regions on chromosomes 7, 14 and 18 had positive lod scores. The highest lod score was 2.88, close to the maximum theoretical lod score for the family (2.93), and was found in a 17.6-Mb region on chromosome 7. Genotyping of microsatellite markers narrowed this region to chr7:14,395,902-28,158,440 (hg38).

### Heterogeneity

Masotti et al. (2008) found that auriculocondylar syndrome in family F1 was not linked to the first locus (ACS1) on 1p21.1-q23.3, which was mapped in family F2. They concluded that at least 2 loci are responsible for the phenotype.

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) noted that pathogenic variants in ARCND had been found in PLCB4, GNAI3 and EDN1. In their family, Sanger sequencing found no pathogenic variants in the coding regions, 5-prime UTRs or splice sites of these genes. Exome sequencing found no obvious pathogenic variants in the endothelin pathway genes EDN1, EDNRA, DLX5, DLX6, FURIN and ECE1. These findings suggested a different locus for ARCND in this family.

### Molecular Genetics

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) studied a large Brazilian family with ARCND that mapped to chromosome 7. Exome sequencing of 4 affected members left only a synonymous TRIL variant in the candidate regions, and this variant did not segregate with the disease. Gene prioritization ranked TWIST1 as the top candidate gene in the chromosome 7 region. Targeted resequencing of a 2.4-Mb region around TWIST1 found no potentially pathogenic variants in TWIST1. It did detect a 430,302-bp tandem duplication (NC_000007.14:g.18437239_18867540dup) located telomeric to TWIST1 and covering most of the HDAC9 gene, and this duplication was present only in the 3 affected family members tested. Several short HDAC9 transcript isoforms were duplicated in their entirety. The full-length, catalytically active isoforms extended beyond the breakpoint and were likely disrupted. Breakpoint PCR and Sanger sequencing showed that the duplication segregated with the phenotype in the family (lod score 2.88), and all unaffected members were negative.

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) also found 19 patients in DECIPHER with copy-number gains in the region. Seven of them had ear malformations and 7 had micrognathia. All of the duplications in the patients with ear malformations, and all but 1 of those in the patients with micrognathia, included the TWIST1 enhancer eTW6. One 179-kb duplication lay entirely within the ARCND duplicated region but did not include the TWIST1 regulatory elements, and that patient did not have ARCND features. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) did Capture-C in human embryonic calvarial mesenchymal cells, using the TWIST1 promoter as the viewpoint. Interactions were most frequent telomeric of TWIST1, particularly within HDAC9 and the region spanned by the ARCND duplication, which implied that this region contains regulatory elements controlling TWIST1 expression. In iPSC-derived neural crest cells from patients, HDAC9 expression was increased 3.15-fold and TWIST1 expression 2.03-fold compared with controls. Expression of HDAC9 and TWIST1 did not differ in the iPSCs themselves. Patient neural crest cells showed a 4.3-fold decrease in migration in a wound-healing assay, with no significant change in cell cycle distribution. In mesenchymal-like cells derived from these neural crest cells, alkaline phosphatase activity and expression were reduced during osteogenic differentiation, and matrix mineralization was subtly decreased, which indicated delayed or impaired osteogenic differentiation. The authors proposed that the duplication deregulates TWIST1 expression and causes ARCND through compromised neural crest migration and osteogenic differentiation. They also suggested that the patients may lack the large, open fontanelles of trisomy 7p because they have 3 copies of regulatory elements rather than of TWIST1 itself.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 AURICULOCONDYLAR SYNDROME 4**  
HDAC9, 430-KB DUP [g.18437239_18867540dup; NC_000007.14:g.18437239_18867540dup]

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) studied a nonconsanguineous 4-generation Brazilian family with ARCND4. In affected members they identified a 430,302-bp tandem duplication (NC_000007.14:g.18437239_18867540dup) on chromosome 7, located telomeric to TWIST1 and covering most of the HDAC9 gene. The duplication was found by targeted resequencing and its breakpoint was characterized. It segregated with the disease in the family (lod score 2.88) and was absent from all unaffected members. Capture-C showed that the duplicated region interacts with the TWIST1 promoter. Neural crest cells derived from patient iPSCs showed increased HDAC9 and TWIST1 expression and reduced migration, and the mesenchymal-like cells derived from them showed impaired osteogenic differentiation.

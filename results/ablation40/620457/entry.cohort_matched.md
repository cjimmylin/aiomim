### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that auriculocondylar syndrome-4 (ARCND4) is caused by heterozygous duplication involving the HDAC9 gene (606543) on chromosome 7p21.1. ⚠︎

### Description

Masotti et al. (2008) described auriculocondylar syndrome as an autosomal dominant disorder of the first and second pharyngeal arches. Its features are malformed 'question mark' ears, prominent cheeks, microstomia, an abnormal temporomandibular joint and hypoplasia of the mandibular condyle. They noted that penetrance seemed to be complete and that variability within and between families was high.

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) showed that ARCND4 is caused by a 430-kb duplication, located telomeric to TWIST1, that covers most of the HDAC9 gene.

### Clinical Features

Masotti et al. (2008) described a multigeneration family (F1) with 11 individuals affected by auriculocondylar syndrome. Clinical features varied within the family. Facial asymmetry, a feature not emphasized in earlier reports of the syndrome, was highly prevalent among the patients.

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) studied a nonconsanguineous 4-generation Brazilian family (F1) with 11 members who had the typical features of ARCND. Of the documented members, 8 of 10 had question mark ears, 8 of 10 had microstomia and 6 of 10 had micrognathia. Intrafamilial variability was considerable. The patients did not have the large, open fontanelles seen in trisomy 7p.

### Inheritance

Masotti et al. (2008) reported autosomal dominant inheritance of auriculocondylar syndrome in family F1. In the Brazilian family F1 reported by Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022), the disorder followed an autosomal dominant pattern and the causative duplication segregated with the phenotype. All unaffected family members tested negative for the duplication, which suggested full penetrance. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Masotti et al. (2008) performed linkage analysis in 2 large auriculocondylar syndrome families. Segregation analysis excluded the loci for Treacher Collins syndrome, oculoauriculovertebral spectrum and Townes-Brocks syndrome. A genomewide search gave evidence of linkage to chromosome 1p21.1-q23.3 in family F2 (maximum lod score of 3.01 at theta = 0). This locus was not linked to the phenotype in family F1.

In a large Brazilian ARCND family, Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) performed linkage analysis under an autosomal dominant model. They obtained a maximum lod score of 2.88, close to the theoretical maximum of 2.93 for the family, in a 17.6-Mb region on chromosome 7. Microsatellite genotyping narrowed the region to chr7:14395902-28158440 (hg38).

### Heterogeneity

The auriculocondylar syndrome phenotype in family F1 was not linked to the ACS1 locus on chromosome 1p21.1-q23.3. Masotti et al. (2008) therefore concluded that at least 2 loci cause the phenotype.

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) found no pathogenic variants in the PLCB4, GNAI3 or EDN1 genes in their Brazilian family, which suggested a different locus. Exome sequencing also found no obvious pathogenic variants in other genes of the endothelin pathway.

### Molecular Genetics

TWIST1 was the top-ranked candidate gene in the linked region. In affected members of their Brazilian ARCND family, Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) resequenced a 2.4-Mb region around TWIST1. They found no TWIST1 variants but detected a 430,302-bp tandem duplication (NC_000007.14:g.18437239_18867540dup) telomeric to TWIST1 that covered most of the HDAC9 gene. The duplication was predicted to duplicate several short HDAC9 isoforms entirely and to disrupt the full-length, catalytically active isoforms. Breakpoint amplification and Sanger sequencing showed that the duplication segregated with the phenotype (lod score of 2.88). All unaffected individuals tested negative. ⚠︎

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) identified 19 patients in DECIPHER with copy-number gains in the region. Of these, 7 had ear malformations and 7 had micrognathia, and most of their duplications included the TWIST1 enhancer eTW6. One smaller duplication of 179 kb lay entirely within the ARCND duplication but did not include the TWIST1 regulatory elements. The patient carrying it had no features of ARCND. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) performed Capture-C in human embryonic calvarial mesenchymal cells, using the TWIST1 promoter as the viewpoint. Interactions were most frequent telomeric to TWIST1, within HDAC9 and the duplicated region, which implied that this region contains TWIST1 regulatory elements.

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) derived neural crest cells from patient induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs). These cells showed increased expression of HDAC9 (3.15-fold) and TWIST1 (2.03-fold) and a 4.3-fold reduction in migration, with no change in the cell cycle. Mesenchymal-like cells derived from the neural crest cells showed reduced alkaline phosphatase activity and expression and decreased matrix mineralization, indicating delayed or impaired osteogenic differentiation. The authors proposed that the duplication deregulates TWIST1 expression and causes ARCND by compromising neural crest migration and osteogenic differentiation. They suggested that patients may lack the large, open fontanelles of trisomy 7p because they carry 3 copies of regulatory elements rather than of the gene itself.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that auriculocondylar syndrome-4 (ARCND4) is caused by a heterozygous 430-kb tandem duplication on chromosome 7 that includes most of the HDAC9 gene and lies telomeric to the TWIST1 gene.

### Description

Auriculocondylar syndrome (ARCND) is a rare disorder affecting structures derived from the first and second pharyngeal arches. Its main features are micrognathia, malformed ('question mark') ears and mandibular condyle hypoplasia. Less common features include microstomia, glossoptosis, postauricular tags and prominent cheeks. Clinical variability is wide (summary by Masotti et al., 2008 and Romanelli Tavares et al., 2022). ARCND4 has been linked to a duplication that includes HDAC9 and may disrupt regulatory elements of the neighboring TWIST1 gene (Romanelli Tavares et al., 2022).

### Clinical Features

Masotti et al. (2008) described a multigeneration family (F1) with auriculocondylar syndrome in which 11 individuals were affected. Clinical features varied within the family. Facial asymmetry was common among the affected members; the authors noted that other reports of ACS had not highlighted this feature.

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) restudied the same non-consanguineous Brazilian family (F1), which spanned 4 generations and had 11 affected members with typical features of ARCND. Among the documented patients, 8 of 10 had question mark ears, 8 of 10 had microstomia and 6 of 10 had micrognathia. Intrafamilial variability was considerable. The patients did not have the large, open fontanelles that are typical of trisomy 7p. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

In the family studied by Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022), ARCND4 segregated as an autosomal dominant trait across 4 generations. Every affected family member tested carried the duplication and every unaffected member tested was negative, suggesting full penetrance in this family.

### Mapping

In the ARCND family F1, Masotti et al. (2008) first excluded by segregation analysis 3 known loci for first and second pharyngeal arch disorders (those for Treacher Collins syndrome, oculoauriculovertebral spectrum and Townes-Brocks syndrome). A genomewide search then excluded linkage of the F1 phenotype to the 1p21.1-q23.3 locus (ACS1), which had been mapped in a second family (F2). ⚠︎

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) performed linkage analysis in family F1 using SNP arrays in 9 affected and 3 unaffected individuals. They found positive LOD scores in regions on chromosomes 7, 14 and 18. The highest LOD score, 2.88, was in a region of about 17.6 Mb on chromosome 7 (chr7:14,395,902-32,017,194, hg38). This score was close to the maximum theoretical LOD score for the family (2.93). Microsatellite genotyping narrowed the chromosome 7 region to chr7:14,395,902-28,158,440 (hg38).

### Heterogeneity

Masotti et al. (2008) found that the 1p21.1-q23.3 locus linked to ACS in one family was not linked to the phenotype in a second family (F1). This was evidence that at least 2 loci underlie the syndrome. Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) later found no pathogenic variants in PLCB4, GNAI3 or EDN1 in family F1 and mapped its disorder to chromosome 7, a fourth ARCND locus.

### Molecular Genetics

In the 4-generation Brazilian family with ARCND first reported by Masotti et al. (2008), Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) searched for the causal variant in several steps. Sanger sequencing of PLCB4, GNAI3 and EDN1, and whole-exome sequencing of 4 affected individuals, found no pathogenic variants. Gene prioritization within the chromosome 7 linkage region ranked TWIST1 as the top candidate. Targeted resequencing of a 2.4-Mb region around TWIST1 found no likely pathogenic TWIST1 variant. It did detect a heterozygous 430,302-bp tandem duplication (NC_000007.14:g.18437239_18867540dup) that covered most of the HDAC9 gene, beginning about 280 kb telomeric to TWIST1. Breakpoint PCR and Sanger sequencing showed that the duplication segregated with the disease in the family; all unaffected individuals tested were negative. Several shorter HDAC9 transcript isoforms were duplicated in their entirety. Full-length, catalytically active isoforms extended beyond the breakpoint and were predicted to be disrupted. ⚠︎

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) also performed Capture-C using the TWIST1 promoter as the viewpoint in human embryonic calvarial mesenchymal cells. The TWIST1 promoter made multiple cis contacts, and the most frequent interactions were within HDAC9 and the duplicated region, suggesting that this region contains TWIST1 regulatory elements. In DECIPHER, 19 patients had copy-number gains in the region. Of these, 7 had ear malformations and 7 had micrognathia; most of these larger duplications included the Twist1 enhancer eTW6. One smaller duplication (179 kb) lay entirely within the ARCND duplication, did not include the known TWIST1 regulatory elements, and the patient carrying it had no ARCND features.

To test the duplication functionally, Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) generated induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) from 3 affected family members and differentiated them into neural crest cells (iNCCs) and mesenchymal stem cell-like cells. Compared with controls, patient iNCCs expressed more HDAC9 (3.15-fold) and TWIST1 (2.03-fold). Patient iNCCs migrated less in scratch assays (a 4.3-fold decrease), whereas their cell cycle distribution did not differ from controls. Patient mesenchymal cells showed reduced alkaline phosphatase activity and expression and slightly reduced matrix mineralization during osteogenic differentiation, indicating delayed or impaired osteogenesis. The authors concluded that the duplication was pathogenic, possibly by deregulating TWIST1 expression.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 AURICULOCONDYLAR SYNDROME 4**  
HDAC9, 430-KB DUP [NC_000007.14:g.18437239_18867540dup]

In affected members of a 4-generation Brazilian family with auriculocondylar syndrome-4 (ARCND4), Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) found a heterozygous 430,302-bp tandem duplication (NC_000007.14:g.18437239_18867540dup) covering most of the HDAC9 gene, telomeric to TWIST1. The family had been described by Masotti et al. (2008). The duplication was detected by targeted resequencing of the TWIST1 region and segregated with the disease in the family (LOD score of 2.88). In patient iPSC-derived neural crest cells, HDAC9 and TWIST1 expression was increased and cell migration was reduced. Patient-derived mesenchymal cells showed impaired osteogenic differentiation. ⚠︎

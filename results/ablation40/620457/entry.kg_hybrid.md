### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that auriculocondylar syndrome-4 (ARCND4) is caused by a 430-kb tandem duplication involving the HDAC9 gene (606543) on chromosome 7p21.1.

### Description

Auriculocondylar syndrome (ARCND) is a rare disorder of structures derived from the first and second pharyngeal arches. It is characterized by micrognathia, question mark ears and mandibular condyle hypoplasia. Less common features include microstomia, glossoptosis, postauricular tags and prominent cheeks. Clinical variability is wide, and some cases have isolated ear anomalies (Romanelli Tavares et al., 2022). Masotti et al. (2008) described the disorder as autosomal dominant, with apparently complete penetrance but high inter- and intrafamilial variability.

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) noted that pathogenic variants in other forms of ARCND had been found in 3 genes of the EDN1-DLX5/6 pathway: PLCB4, GNAI3 and EDN1.

### Clinical Features

Masotti et al. (2008) described a new multigeneration family (F1) with 11 individuals affected by auriculocondylar syndrome and confirmed intrafamilial clinical variability. Facial asymmetry was highly prevalent among the affected members. The authors noted that this feature had not been highlighted in other reports of the syndrome.

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) studied a previously reported non-consanguineous, 4-generation Brazilian family (F1) in which 11 members had the typical characteristics of ARCND. Of the members documented, most had question mark ears (8 of 10), microstomia (8 of 10) and micrognathia (6 of 10), and intrafamilial variability was considerable. The patients did not have large, open fontanelles, which are the hallmark feature of trisomy 7p.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of ARCND4 in the family reported by Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) was consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. Linkage analysis used an autosomal dominant model, and the 430-kb duplication segregated with the disease. All unaffected family members were negative for the duplication, suggesting full penetrance. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) genotyped 9 affected and 3 unaffected members of a Brazilian family with ARCND using SNP arrays. Parametric multipoint linkage analysis under an autosomal dominant model gave a maximum LOD score of 2.88 over a region of about 17.6 Mb on chromosome 7 (chr7:14395902-32017194, hg38). This score was close to the maximum theoretical LOD score for the family (2.93). Genotyping of microsatellite markers narrowed the region to chr7:14395902-28158440 (hg38). Positive LOD scores were also obtained on chromosomes 14 (2.41) and 18 (1.67).

### Heterogeneity

Masotti et al. (2008) performed linkage analysis in 2 large ARCND families. In family F2, they found evidence of linkage to 1p21.1-q23.3 (maximum lod score of 3.01 at theta = 0), but this locus was not linked to the phenotype in family F1. They concluded that at least 2 loci are responsible for the phenotype.

In their Brazilian family with ARCND, Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) found no pathogenic variants in the coding regions, 5-prime UTRs or splice sites of PLCB4, GNAI3 or EDN1. Whole-exome sequencing of 4 affected members detected no obvious pathogenic variants in the endothelin pathway genes EDN1, EDNRA, DLX5, DLX6, FURIN or ECE1.

### Molecular Genetics

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) studied a 4-generation Brazilian family with ARCND that showed linkage to chromosome 7. Whole-exome sequencing of 4 affected members left only a synonymous TRIL variant (c.345G-A) in the linkage regions, and this variant did not segregate with the disease. Gene prioritization ranked TWIST1 as the top candidate gene. Targeted resequencing of a 2.4-Mb region around TWIST1 found no potentially pathogenic variants in TWIST1. Instead, it detected a 430,302-bp tandem duplication (NC_000007.14:g.18437239_18867540dup) within HDAC9 in the 3 affected family members tested. The duplication lay telomeric to TWIST1 and covered most of the HDAC9 gene. Several shorter HDAC9 transcript isoforms were duplicated in their entirety. Full-length, catalytically active isoforms extended beyond the breakpoint and were predicted to be disrupted. Breakpoint PCR and Sanger sequencing showed that the duplication segregated with the phenotype in the family (LOD score of 2.88), and all unaffected individuals were negative.

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) also identified 19 patients in DECIPHER with copy-number gains in the region. Except for 1 small duplication, the CNVs overlapping the family's duplication were much larger and encompassed multiple genes. Seven of the 19 patients had ear malformation and 7 had micrognathia, along with other features. One 179-kb duplication (patient 276644) lay entirely within the family's duplicated region, but it did not include the known TWIST1 regulatory elements, and this patient did not have clinical features of ARCND.

### Pathogenesis

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) performed Capture-C on human embryonic calvarial mesenchymal cells, using the TWIST1 promoter as the viewpoint. The promoter had multiple contacts with regions on either side. The highest frequency of interactions was telomeric to TWIST1, within HDAC9 and the region spanned by the duplication. The authors concluded that this region contains regulatory elements involved in controlling TWIST1 expression, which would provide a possible pathogenic mechanism for the duplication.

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) generated iPSCs from 3 affected family members and from controls and differentiated them into neural crest cells and mesenchymal stem cells. HDAC9 and TWIST1 mRNA levels did not differ between patient and control iPSCs. In patient-derived neural crest cells, however, HDAC9 mRNA was increased 3.15-fold and TWIST1 mRNA 2.03-fold. Patient neural crest cells had a normal cell cycle distribution but a significant 4.3-fold decrease in migratory capacity in scratch assays. Expression of BARX1, NKX3.2 and GSC did not differ significantly from controls. During osteogenic differentiation, patient-derived mesenchymal stem cells showed a 20.3-fold decrease in alkaline phosphatase activity after 9 days, downregulated ALP expression and a subtle decrease in matrix mineralization, indicating delayed or impaired osteogenic differentiation. The authors suggested that the duplication deregulates TWIST1 expression and leads to ARCND features by compromising neural crest migration and osteogenic differentiation.

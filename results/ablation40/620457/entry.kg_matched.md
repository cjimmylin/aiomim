### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that auriculocondylar syndrome-4 (ARCND4) is caused by a 430-kb duplication on chromosome 7p21.1 that covers most of the HDAC9 gene (606543) and lies telomeric to TWIST1.

### Description

Auriculocondylar syndrome is an autosomal dominant disorder of the first and second pharyngeal arches. Its features include malformed ('question mark') ears, prominent cheeks, microstomia, an abnormal temporomandibular joint, and mandibular condyle hypoplasia. Penetrance appears complete, but expression varies widely both between and within families (Masotti et al., 2008). ARCND4 is caused by a duplication on chromosome 7p21.1 that includes most of the HDAC9 gene and is thought to disrupt regulation of TWIST1 (Romanelli Tavares et al., 2022).

### Clinical Features

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) studied a non-consanguineous, 4-generation Brazilian family in which 11 members had typical features of auriculocondylar syndrome. Ten members were documented. Of these, 8 had question mark ears, 8 had microstomia, and 6 had micrognathia. Features varied considerably within the family. The patients did not have the large, open fontanelles that are the hallmark of trisomy 7p.

### Inheritance

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) found that the transmission of ARCND4 in a 4-generation Brazilian family was consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. The causal duplication segregated with the disease, and all unaffected family members lacked it, suggesting full penetrance. ⚠︎

### Cytogenetics

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) searched DECIPHER and identified 19 patients with copy-number gains in the region. Seven of them had ear malformations and 7 had micrognathia. Most of these duplications were larger CNVs that involved multiple genes and included the TWIST1 enhancer eTW6 (Hs2307). One patient carried a small 179-kb duplication that lay entirely within the family's duplicated region but did not span the known TWIST1 regulatory elements. This patient did not have features of auriculocondylar syndrome. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) genotyped 9 affected and 3 unaffected members of a Brazilian family with ARCND using SNP arrays. They then performed parametric linkage analysis under an autosomal dominant model. The highest LOD score was 2.88, close to the family's theoretical maximum of 2.93. It covered a 17.6-Mb region on chromosome 7 (chr7:14395902-32017194, hg38). Microsatellite genotyping narrowed the region to chr7:14395902-28158440. Smaller positive LOD scores were found on chromosome 14 (2.41) and chromosome 18 (1.67).

### Heterogeneity

Masotti et al. (2008) studied 2 large families with auriculocondylar syndrome. In one family (F2), they found linkage to chromosome 1p21.1-q23.3, but this locus was not linked to the disorder in the other family (F1). They concluded that the disorder is genetically heterogeneous, with at least 2 responsible loci.

In a Brazilian family with auriculocondylar syndrome, Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) found no pathogenic variants in the coding regions, 5-prime UTRs, or splice sites of PLCB4, GNAI3, or EDN1. Whole-exome sequencing of 4 affected members found no obvious pathogenic variants in the endothelin pathway genes EDN1, EDNRA, DLX5, DLX6, FURIN, and ECE1. The only filtered variant within the linkage regions was a synonymous change in TRIL (c.345G-A), and it did not segregate with the disease. The authors suggested that a different locus underlies auriculocondylar syndrome in this family.

### Molecular Genetics

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) studied a 4-generation Brazilian family with ARCND. They ranked TWIST1 as the top candidate gene in the chromosome 7 linkage interval. Targeted resequencing of a 2.4-Mb region around TWIST1 found no pathogenic TWIST1 variants. Instead, it identified a 430,302-bp tandem duplication (NC_000007.14:g.18437239_18867540dup) that began about 280 kb telomeric to TWIST1 and covered most of the HDAC9 gene. Breakpoint PCR and Sanger sequencing showed that the duplication segregated with the phenotype (LOD score 2.88), and all unaffected members were negative. Capture-C in human embryonic calvarial mesenchymal cells showed that the TWIST1 promoter interacted most often with the region telomeric to TWIST1, within HDAC9 and the duplicated segment. The authors concluded that this region contains regulatory elements controlling TWIST1 expression.

### Pathogenesis

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) derived induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) from 3 affected members of a Brazilian family with ARCND4 and differentiated them into neural crest cells. Compared with controls, patient neural crest cells had increased mRNA levels of HDAC9 (3.15-fold) and TWIST1 (2.03-fold). Patient and control iPSCs did not differ in these levels. Cell cycle distribution in patient neural crest cells was unchanged, but scratch assays showed a significant 4.3-fold decrease in migration. Expression of the EDN1 pathway targets BARX1, NKX3.2, and GSC did not differ significantly from controls.

Romanelli Tavares et al. (2022) also derived mesenchymal stem cells from the patients' neural crest cells. After 9 days of osteoinduction, these cells had significantly reduced alkaline phosphatase activity compared with controls (20.3-fold decrease). They also had lower ALP expression and subtly reduced matrix mineralization. The authors concluded that osteogenic differentiation was delayed or impaired. They proposed that the duplication deregulates TWIST1 expression and produces features of auriculocondylar syndrome by compromising neural crest cell migration and osteogenic differentiation.

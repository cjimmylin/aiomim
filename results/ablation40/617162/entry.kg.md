### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-46 (DEE46) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GRIN2D gene (602717) on chromosome 19q13.33.

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-46 (DEE46) is a severe neurologic disorder. It is characterized by severe developmental delay and intractable seizures that do not respond to conventional antiepileptic medications (Li et al., 2016; Tsuchida et al., 2018).

### Clinical Features

Li et al. (2016) reported 2 unrelated children with epileptic encephalopathy. Their seizures were refractory to conventional antiepileptic medications. The older proband later developed refractory status epilepticus.

Tsuchida et al. (2018) reported 3 unrelated patients with developmental and epileptic encephalopathy, characterized by severe developmental delay and intractable epilepsy. When they compared their 3 patients with the 2 previously reported patients carrying GRIN2D variants, they found that all 5 had similar clinical features.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous mutation in the GRIN2D gene in the 2 patients with DEE46 reported by Li et al. (2016) occurred de novo, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. Tsuchida et al. (2018) also found heterozygous GRIN2D variants in affected individuals.

### Molecular Genetics

Li et al. (2016) used exome and candidate panel sequencing in 2 unrelated children with DEE46. In both, they identified the same de novo heterozygous missense mutation in the GRIN2D gene (V667I). The mutation lies in the M3 transmembrane domain, which is involved in channel gating. GRIN2D had not previously been recognized to harbor disease-causing mutations. Functional studies showed that the mutation caused a gain of function. It increased glutamate and glycine potency 2-fold and channel open probability 6-fold. It also reduced sensitivity to negative modulators such as extracellular protons and prolonged deactivation after glutamate removal. Cultured neurons transfected with the mutant cDNA showed dendritic swelling and cell death.

Tsuchida et al. (2018) used whole exome sequencing in 3 unrelated patients with DEE46 and identified novel heterozygous missense variants in the GRIN2D gene. All of the altered residues were highly conserved across vertebrates and among the 4 GluN2 subunits. Based on structural analysis, the authors concluded that all 3 variants probably impair GluN2D function, either by affecting intersubunit interaction or by altering channel gating activity.

### Pathogenesis

Li et al. (2016) found that cultured neurons transfected with cDNA carrying the gain-of-function GRIN2D V667I mutation showed dendritic swelling and cell death. They suggested that this reflected excitotoxicity from NMDAR overactivation.

### Clinical Management

Li et al. (2016) first tested whether the mutant receptors were sensitive to FDA-approved NMDAR antagonists. They then treated both patients with oral memantine, which led to mild to moderate improvement in seizure burden and development. When the older proband later developed refractory status epilepticus, it improved dramatically, both electrically and clinically, with ketamine and magnesium. The authors suggested that NMDAR antagonists may be useful as adjuvant therapy in individuals with GRIN2D gain-of-function mutations.

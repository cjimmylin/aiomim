### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-46 (DEE46) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GRIN2D gene (602717) on chromosome 19q13.33.

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-46 (DEE46) is a severe neurologic disorder characterized by severe developmental delay and intractable seizures that are refractory to conventional antiepileptic medications. Status epilepticus may occur (Li et al., 2016; Tsuchida et al., 2018).

### Clinical Features

Li et al. (2016) reported 2 unrelated children with epileptic encephalopathy. Their seizures were refractory to conventional antiepileptic medications. The older proband later developed refractory status epilepticus.

Tsuchida et al. (2018) reported 3 unrelated patients with developmental and epileptic encephalopathy who had severe developmental delay and intractable epilepsy. When they compared these patients with the 2 patients previously reported by Li et al. (2016), Tsuchida et al. (2018) found that all 5 showed similar clinical features.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous GRIN2D mutation in the 2 patients with DEE46 reported by Li et al. (2016) occurred de novo, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. Tsuchida et al. (2018) also found heterozygous GRIN2D variants in their 3 patients.

### Molecular Genetics

In 2 unrelated children with DEE46, Li et al. (2016) used exome and candidate panel sequencing to identify a recurrent de novo heterozygous missense mutation in the GRIN2D gene (c.1999G-A; val667-to-ile, V667I). The mutation lies in the M3 transmembrane domain, which is involved in channel gating. In functional studies, V667I was a gain-of-function mutation: it doubled glutamate and glycine potency, increased channel open probability 6-fold, reduced receptor sensitivity to endogenous negative modulators such as extracellular protons, and prolonged deactivation after glutamate removal.

In 3 unrelated patients with DEE46, Tsuchida et al. (2018) used whole-exome sequencing to identify novel heterozygous missense variants in the GRIN2D gene. All of the altered residues were highly conserved across vertebrates and among the 4 GluN2 subunits. Structural analysis suggested that all 3 variants probably impair GluN2D function, either by affecting intersubunit interaction or by altering channel gating activity.

### Pathogenesis

Li et al. (2016) transfected cultured neurons with human GRIN2D cDNA carrying the c.1999G-A (V667I) mutation, which caused dendritic swelling and neuronal cell death. The authors suggested that this reflected excitotoxicity driven by NMDA receptor over-activation.

### Clinical Management

Li et al. (2016) found that the mutant NMDA receptors were sensitive to FDA-approved NMDA receptor antagonists and treated both of their patients with oral memantine, which resulted in mild to moderate improvement in seizure burden and development. The older proband later developed refractory status epilepticus, which improved dramatically on electroclinical measures with ketamine and magnesium. The authors suggested that NMDA receptor antagonists may be useful as adjuvant therapy in individuals with GRIN2D gain-of-function mutations. ⚠︎

Tsuchida et al. (2018) suggested that a genetic diagnosis of GluN2-related disorders may be clinically useful when considering drug therapy that targets NMDA receptors.

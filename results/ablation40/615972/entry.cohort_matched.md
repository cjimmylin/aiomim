### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that nanophthalmos-4 (NNO4) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TMEM98 gene (615949) on chromosome 17q11.2.

### Description

Awadalla et al. (2014) described nanophthalmos as a congenital disorder of small eyes whose main complications are severe hyperopia and angle-closure glaucoma. They defined it as an axial length of less than 20.0 mm and/or a refractive error greater than +7.00.

### Clinical Features

Yu et al. (2009) described a 5-generation Chinese family with isolated bilateral microphthalmia present from birth. The 9 affected members, aged 6 to 66 years, had hyperopia of +4.50 to +11.50 diopters (mean +8.00) and axial lengths of 18.22 to 20.26 mm (mean 19.29 mm). In most eyes, best corrected visual acuity was below 0.5. Three patients older than 50 years had angle-closure glaucoma that kept progressing after trabeculectomy, and 2 of them (III10 and III15) eventually developed atrophia bulbi despite repeated surgeries. Patient III10, a 66-year-old woman, had no light perception in the right eye and finger counting at 30 cm in the left eye, which had hyperopia of +10.00 diopters and an axial length of 18.27 mm; patient III15, a 53-year-old man, had no light perception in the left eye. The youngest affected member, a 6-year-old boy (V5), had hyperopia of +9.00/+8.50 diopters and visual acuity of 0.3 in each eye. All affected members tested had a normal karyotype. Learning disabilities were observed in one-third of the affected members, and no family member had any other physical anomaly.

Hu et al. (2011) studied a Chinese family with autosomal dominant congenital simple microphthalmia.

Awadalla et al. (2014) studied a large white family from South Australia (NNO-SA1) with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos. Of 35 available family members, 16 (46%) had a diagnosis of nanophthalmos, with a mean refraction of +11.8 D and a mean axial length of 17.6 mm.

Khorram et al. (2015) reported 2 pedigrees with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos showing high penetrance over at least 4 generations. In pedigree 991-F, a Caucasian family of German heritage from the United States, 6 of 10 examined members were diagnosed with nanophthalmos and 3 additional members were affected by family history report; examined affected members had high hyperopia of about +11 to +14 diopters, and axial lengths of 16.7 to 17.4 mm were recorded in 2 of them. In pedigree 951-N, a Pacific Islander family of Micronesian heritage from the Mariana Islands, 8 affected members were examined and 3 more were affected by family history report; affected members had axial lengths of 15.86 to 18.43 mm but much milder hyperopia, ranging from +1.88 to +7.00 diopters. Khorram et al. (2015) noted mean hyperopia of about +13 diopters in the US pedigree compared with +3.93 diopters in the Mariana Islands pedigree.

### Inheritance

Autosomal dominant inheritance of microphthalmia/nanophthalmos was observed in the 5-generation Chinese family reported by Yu et al. (2009), in the Chinese family reported by Hu et al. (2011), and in the white family reported by Awadalla et al. (2014). Khorram et al. (2015) found autosomal dominant inheritance with high penetrance over at least 4 generations in both of their pedigrees.

### Mapping

In their 5-generation Chinese family with microphthalmia, Yu et al. (2009) tested 23 microsatellite markers at previously reported microphthalmia loci and candidate genes (CHX10, PAX6, MFRP, and loci on 14q32, 11p, and 2q11-q14). All 2-point lod scores were below 1, excluding linkage to these loci, and the authors predicted that a novel locus was responsible for the disorder in this family.

By genomewide scan with 382 microsatellite markers in a Chinese family with autosomal dominant congenital simple microphthalmia, Hu et al. (2011) obtained a maximum lod score of 4.97 at D17S1824 (theta = 0.00). Haplotype analysis placed the disease gene in a 21.57-cM interval between D17S900 and D17S1872 on chromosome 17p12-q12.

By genomewide linkage scan with a 10K SNP array in a large white family (NNO-SA1) with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos, Awadalla et al. (2014) found significant linkage on chromosome 17p12-q12 between SNP markers rs2323659 and rs967293, with a maximum location score of 4.1.

Khorram et al. (2015) found by linkage analysis that a 20.2-Mbp segment of chromosome 17p12-q12 (rs530664 to rs16523) was coinherited with nanophthalmos in a US pedigree and an overlapping 19.7-Mb segment (rs917541 to rs16523) in a Mariana Islands pedigree, placing the gene between rs917541 and rs16523, in a region overlapping that previously linked in a Chinese pedigree.

### Heterogeneity

Yu et al. (2009) concluded that the exclusion of all known loci in their autosomal dominant Chinese pedigree pointed to high genetic heterogeneity in microphthalmia.

### Molecular Genetics

In the Chinese family with microphthalmia linked to 17p12-q12, Hu et al. (2011) screened 14 candidate genes in the interval, including CRYBA1 and NCOR1, by direct sequencing and performed genomewide SNP genotyping for copy number variation, but did not identify a mutation or CNV responsible for the phenotype. ⚠︎

In affected members of a large white family with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos linked to 17p12-q12, Awadalla et al. (2014) used exome sequencing to identify a single novel segregating variant within the linkage region: a heterozygous c.577G-C transversion in exon 8 of the TMEM98 gene, resulting in an ala193-to-pro (A193P) substitution. The variant was absent from the Exome Variant Server database and from 285 local white control individuals. By RT-PCR, TMEM98 was expressed in all ocular tissues tested, including the sclera and the optic nerve head. The authors concluded that TMEM98 most likely represented the cause of the disease in this family, the first gene identified for autosomal dominant nanophthalmos. ⚠︎

By exome sequencing of an affected member (IV-3) of a US pedigree (991-F) with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos, Khorram et al. (2015) identified 4 nonsynonymous variants within the chromosome 17 linked region; only a his196-to-pro (H196P) substitution in the TMEM98 gene was coinherited with disease and absent from healthy relatives. Sanger sequencing showed that all other members of the pedigree with nanophthalmos carried the mutation. The variant was predicted to be pathogenic by SIFT, PolyPhen2, and BLOSUM62, and the His196 residue was highly conserved across species. In a Mariana Islands pedigree (951-N), Sanger sequencing of 11 members with nanophthalmos and 4 healthy members identified a novel heterozygous 34-bp deletion in TMEM98 (c.694_721delAGAATGAAGACTGGATCGAAGATGCCTCgtaagg), spanning the last 28 bp of exon 4 and the first 6 bp of intron 4, in all affected members. The deletion eliminated a splice site and was predicted to cause premature termination. Neither variant was found in the Exome Variant Server or 1000 Genomes Project databases. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

On histologic examination of affected members of their Chinese family, Yu et al. (2009) found diffusely swollen scleral collagen fibers that were twisted or frayed and mixed with normal fibers. Two atrophied globes showed disrupted collagen alignment, coil-like amorphous material, and scleral thickening of about 3 times normal. The authors suggested that the abnormal collagen fibers might directly prevent early development of the globe.

**.0002 NANOPHTHALMOS 4**  
TMEM98, HIS196PRO [p.H196P]

In affected members of a Caucasian US family of German heritage (pedigree 991-F) with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos and high hyperopia, Khorram et al. (2015) identified a his196-to-pro (H196P) substitution in the TMEM98 gene. The mutation, found by exome sequencing of patient IV-3 and confirmed by Sanger sequencing, was carried by all family members with nanophthalmos and was absent from healthy relatives. It was predicted to be pathogenic by SIFT, PolyPhen2, and BLOSUM62, affected a highly conserved residue, and was not found in the Exome Variant Server or 1000 Genomes Project databases.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that nanophthalmos-4 (NNO4) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TMEM98 gene (615949) on chromosome 17q11.2.

### Description

Nanophthalmos-4 is an autosomal dominant eye disorder in which both eyes are abnormally small from birth. Affected individuals have short axial lengths and high hyperopia, with no other physical abnormalities. Angle-closure glaucoma may develop later in life, and visual acuity is often reduced (Yu et al., 2009; Awadalla et al., 2014; Khorram et al., 2015).

### Clinical Features

Yu et al. (2009) described a 5-generation Chinese family with isolated simple microphthalmia. The 9 affected members were aged 6 to 66 years (mean, 42.8 years) and had had bilateral disease since birth. Hyperopia ranged from +4.50 to +11.50 diopters (mean, +8.00), and axial length ranged from 18.22 to 20.26 mm (mean, 19.29 mm). Best corrected visual acuity was less than 0.5 in most eyes. There were no other physical anomalies. Three patients, all older than 50 years, developed angle-closure glaucoma or occludable anterior chamber angles. They were treated by trabeculectomy, but the disease continued to progress, and 2 patients developed atrophia bulbi despite repeated surgeries. One-third of the cases had learning disabilities. The authors noted that thickening of the lens after middle age contributed to refractory glaucoma and that most patients had lost visual acuity by middle age. ⚠︎

Yu et al. (2009) also examined sclera from affected family members by histology. The scleral stroma contained diffusely swollen collagen fibers that were twisted or frayed and mixed with normal fibers. In the 2 atrophied globes, collagen fiber alignment was disrupted, with coil-like amorphous material and a sclera 3 times normal thickness. The corneas were also thickened, pigment cells had proliferated, and bone-like tissue had formed.

Hu et al. (2011) studied a Chinese family with congenital simple microphthalmia inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern.

Awadalla et al. (2014) studied a large white family (NNO-SA1), recruited in South Australia, with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos. Nanophthalmos was defined as an axial length below 20.0 mm and/or a refractive error above +7.00. Of 35 available family members, 16 (46%) were affected. Affected members had a mean refraction of +11.8 D and a mean axial length of 17.6 mm.

Khorram et al. (2015) described a Caucasian family of German heritage living in the United States (pedigree 991-F) with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos spanning at least 4 generations. Six of 10 examined members had nanophthalmos, and 3 more were affected according to the family history. Affected individuals had a mean axial eye length of 17.1 +/- 0.31 mm and high hyperopia, with spherical equivalent refractions of about +13 diopters, compared with +0.71 diopters in unaffected relatives.

Khorram et al. (2015) also described a Pacific Islander family of Micronesian heritage from the Mariana Islands (pedigree 951-N) with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos. Eight of 10 examined members had nanophthalmos, and 3 more were affected according to the family history. Affected members had an average axial eye length of 17.4 +/- 0.94 mm, compared with 22.7 mm in unaffected relatives, and hyperopia with spherical equivalent refractions of +3.93 +/- 1.57 diopters.

### Inheritance

Yu et al. (2009) concluded that simple microphthalmia was inherited in an autosomal dominant manner in a 5-generation Chinese family with 9 affected members. Hu et al. (2011) and Awadalla et al. (2014) also reported autosomal dominant inheritance in the families they studied. Khorram et al. (2015) found that nanophthalmos was transmitted as an autosomal dominant trait with high penetrance spanning at least 4 generations in both a US pedigree and a Mariana Islands pedigree.

### Cytogenetics

Yu et al. (2009) found a normal karyotype in all 9 affected members of the Chinese family. They concluded that the disease was most probably caused by a single-gene mutation.

### Mapping

Yu et al. (2009) genotyped 23 microsatellite markers at known microphthalmia loci on chromosomes 2, 3, 11, 14, and 15 in 34 members (9 affected, 25 unaffected) of a Chinese family with autosomal dominant simple microphthalmia. Two-point lod scores were below 1 for all markers. The authors therefore excluded linkage to the candidate genes CHX10, PAX6, and MFRP and to the loci on 14q32, 11p, and 2q11-q14, and predicted that a novel locus was responsible.

In a Chinese family with autosomal dominant congenital simple microphthalmia, Hu et al. (2011) excluded previously reported microphthalmia loci. They then performed a genomewide scan with 382 microsatellite markers, followed by fine mapping. The highest lod score was at D17S1824 (maximum lod score of 4.97 at theta = 0.00). Haplotype analysis placed the disease gene in a 21.57-cM interval on chromosome 17p12-q12, between D17S900 and D17S1872.

Awadalla et al. (2014) performed a genomewide linkage scan with a 10K SNP array in the NNO-SA1 family with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos. They found significant linkage to chromosome 17p12-q12 between SNP markers rs2323659 and rs967293, with a maximum location score of 4.1.

Khorram et al. (2015) performed genomewide SNP linkage analysis in 2 families with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos. In pedigree 991-F, a 20.2-Mbp segment of chromosome 17p12-q12 (between rs530664 and rs16523) cosegregated with nanophthalmos. In pedigree 951-N, an overlapping 19.7-Mb segment (between rs917541 and rs16523) cosegregated with the disease. Together these results placed the gene between rs917541 and rs16523, in a region previously linked to autosomal dominant nanophthalmos in a Chinese pedigree.

### Heterogeneity

Yu et al. (2009) found that all known loci for simple microphthalmia were excluded in their Chinese family. They took this as further evidence of high genetic heterogeneity in microphthalmia.

### Molecular Genetics

Hu et al. (2011) used PCR and direct sequencing to screen 14 candidate genes in the linked region on chromosome 17p12-q12, including CRYBA1 and NCOR1, in a Chinese family with autosomal dominant congenital simple microphthalmia. They also looked for copy number variation by genomewide SNP genotyping. They found no mutation or CNV that accounted for the disease in the family. ⚠︎

Awadalla et al. (2014) performed whole exome sequencing in the NNO-SA1 family with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos. Within the linked region they found a single novel heterozygous missense mutation that segregated with the disease: c.577G-C (ala193-to-pro; A193P), in exon 8 of the TMEM98 gene. The variant was absent from the Exome Variant Server database and from 285 local white controls. RT-PCR showed that TMEM98 was expressed in all ocular tissues tested, including the sclera and the optic nerve head. The authors concluded that TMEM98 was most likely the cause of disease in this family and described it as the first gene identified for autosomal dominant nanophthalmos. ⚠︎

Khorram et al. (2015) performed exome sequencing within the chromosome 17 linked region in affected members of a US family with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos (pedigree 991-F) and identified a heterozygous his196-to-pro (H196P) missense mutation in the TMEM98 gene, which was confirmed by Sanger sequencing. Of 4 nonsynonymous variants in the linked region, only this one cosegregated with the disease and was absent from healthy relatives. It was predicted to be damaging by SIFT, PolyPhen2, and BLOSUM62, altered a highly conserved histidine, and was absent from the Exome Variant Server and the 1000 Genomes Project. ⚠︎

Khorram et al. (2015) also used Sanger sequencing to study a Mariana Islands family with autosomal dominant nanophthalmos (pedigree 951-N). In all 11 affected members they identified a heterozygous 34-bp deletion in the TMEM98 gene (c.694_721delAGAATGAAGACTGGATCGAAGATGCCTCgtaagg). The deletion spans the last 28 bp of exon 4 and the first 6 bp of intron 4 and was predicted to eliminate a splice site and cause premature termination. It segregated with the disease, was absent from 4 healthy relatives, and was not found in the Exome Variant Server or the 1000 Genomes Project.

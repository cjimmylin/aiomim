### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that neurodevelopmental disorder with hypotonia, language delay, and skeletal defects with or without seizures (NEDHLSS) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the CACNA1C gene (114205) on chromosome 12p13.33.

### Description

NEDHLSS is a neurodevelopmental disorder caused by heterozygous CACNA1C mutation. Rodan et al. (2021) described features that vary between patients and include developmental delay, intellectual disability, autism, hypotonia, ataxia, and epilepsy. These patients lacked the classic features of Timothy syndrome or long QT syndrome. Orthopedic abnormalities such as clubfoot, finger contractures, adducted thumbs, hip dysplasia, and joint hypermobility were common in patients with nontruncating variants. Patients with truncating variants mainly had expressive language deficits, and many had autism.

### Clinical Features

Kosaki et al. (2018) reported a 5-year-old girl with cutaneous syndactyly, developmental delay, and pulmonary hypertension. They described her condition as Timothy syndrome-like. However, she did not have prolongation of the QT interval, which had long been considered an obligatory feature of Timothy syndrome.

Bozarth et al. (2018) reported a girl (patient 1) born at 38 weeks' gestation to nonconsanguineous parents of English (maternal) and Ukrainian (paternal) ancestry. - **Birth and congenital anomalies:** Birth length was 40.6 cm (z-score -3.92). She was hospitalized after birth for hypoglycemia and multiple congenital anomalies, including profound axial and appendicular hypotonia, laryngomalacia with stridor, bilateral clubbed feet, bilateral 3-4 finger and 2-3 toe syndactyly, and camptodactyly. - **Dysmorphic features:** Micrognathia, downturned corners of the mouth, frontal bossing, very scant hair, and bilaterally inverted nipples. - **Other findings:** Mild optic nerve hypoplasia, severe gastroesophageal reflux, constipation, and anal stenosis. She required a gastrostomy tube at 8 months because of severe dysphagia. - **Development and sleep:** At 18 months, her developmental milestones had not progressed, she had profound hypotonia, and she could not visually track or fixate. She had severe disturbances of the sleep-wake cycle, and polysomnography showed severe central and obstructive sleep apnea. - **Seizures:** Seizures began within a few hours of birth, with numerous tonic and tonic-myoclonic seizures each day. Epileptic tonic spasms were diagnosed at 3 months. The seizures did not respond to zonisamide or to multiple other antiepileptic drugs. Clobazam, started at 7 months, significantly reduced her seizure burden. - **EEG:** The EEG showed distinctive diffuse excessive fast activity that persisted beyond the neonatal period. - **Normal studies:** Brain MRI, MR spectroscopy, echocardiogram, and Holter monitoring were normal. Bozarth et al. (2018) noted that she had several features of Timothy syndrome but no cardiac abnormalities.

Rodan et al. (2021) reported 25 individuals (13 male and 12 female) from 22 families with heterozygous CACNA1C variants and predominantly neurologic manifestations. Ages at last follow-up ranged from 10 months to 40 years. Fourteen individuals had nontruncating variants: - **Prenatal and neonatal history:** Prenatal findings included reduced fetal movement (3 of 14), bilateral clubfoot on ultrasound (2), and oligohydramnios (2). Two had neonatal feeding difficulties. - **Development:** All 14 had developmental delay or intellectual disability; 2 had a learning disability after early delays. Nine were nonverbal and 6 were nonambulatory at last follow-up. One was formally diagnosed with autism, and 6 others had autistic features. Regression occurred in 1 individual when infantile spasms developed. - **Neurologic findings:** Twelve were hypotonic and 4 had ataxia. One had bilateral vestibular areflexia. - **Epilepsy:** Ten had epilepsy, with onset between 2 months and 8 years. Both generalized and focal epilepsies occurred, including infantile spasms that evolved to Lennox-Gastaut syndrome. Seizures were fever-sensitive in 3 and medically refractory in 4. One individual had excessive diffuse fast activity on EEG. - **Brain imaging:** Brain MRI was normal apart from an incidental arachnoid cyst in 1 individual and prominent cerebellar folia in another. - **Orthopedic abnormalities:** These were present in 12 of 14. They included bilateral clubfoot with congenital finger contractures (2), persistently adducted thumbs (2), kyphosis or scoliosis (2), congenital hip dysplasia (3), syndactyly (2), and joint hypermobility (3). - **Eyes:** Hyperopia (4) and strabismus (4). - **Growth:** Two were microcephalic. Three had short stature, including the 2 with clubfoot. - **Cardiac findings:** One individual had prolonged QT and T-wave alternans on Holter monitoring. Another had a possible ventricular conduction delay. - **Dysmorphic features:** Minor facial dysmorphisms occurred without consistent features.

Rodan et al. (2021) also described 11 individuals from 8 families with truncating CACNA1C variants: - **Pregnancy and early motor development:** Prenatal histories were essentially unremarkable. Most had normal early motor development and walked independently. - **Language:** Ten of 11 had delayed expressive language. - **Autism:** Five had autism, 3 of whom were nonverbal. Another had been diagnosed with pervasive developmental disorder not otherwise specified. - **Seizures:** Only 1 individual had a history of seizures, and he was seizure-free without treatment. - **Motor findings:** Three had hypotonia, 4 had poor balance or coordination, and 2 had tremor. One had paroxysmal dyskinesia, and another had a paroxysmal movement disorder in infancy that later resolved. - **Cardiac evaluation:** ECGs were normal in the 5 individuals tested. - **Other findings:** Seven had minor dysmorphic features. Four had recurrent infections, including otitis media, and 1 of them was diagnosed with hypogammaglobulinemia.

### Inheritance

Kosaki et al. (2018) and Bozarth et al. (2018) each found a de novo heterozygous CACNA1C mutation in their patient. In the cohort of Rodan et al. (2021), all nontruncating variants and all but 3 truncating variants occurred de novo. The 3 inherited truncating variants were paternally inherited and segregated with neurodevelopmental abnormalities in the families, with variable penetrance and expressivity. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Kosaki et al. (2018) used exome analysis in a 5-year-old girl with NEDHLSS. They identified a previously undescribed de novo heterozygous mutation in the CACNA1C gene, arg1024-to-gly (R1024G). ⚠︎

Bozarth et al. (2018) performed trio exome sequencing in a girl with neonatal-onset epileptic encephalopathy, hypotonia, syndactyly, and clubfoot. They identified a de novo heterozygous c.4087G-A transition in the CACNA1C gene, resulting in a val1363-to-met (V1363M) substitution. The variant affected a highly conserved residue, was absent from gnomAD, ExAC, and 1000 Genomes, and was predicted to be damaging by in silico tools. The authors classified it as likely pathogenic. Structural modeling placed V1363 in the fifth transmembrane helix of domain IV, facing the pore-forming sixth helix. The authors suggested that the substitution likely affects channel gating or calcium transmission. The patient also carried a de novo terminal deletion of the MSH2 gene that had previously been reported in hereditary nonpolyposis colorectal cancer. ⚠︎

Rodan et al. (2021) identified heterozygous CACNA1C variants in 25 individuals from 22 families with predominantly neurologic manifestations. - **Methods:** The individuals were ascertained through GeneMatcher and the Matchmaker Exchange, or through ClinVar. The variants were detected by exome sequencing or an intellectual disability gene panel. - **Variants:** There were 13 missense variants, 1 in-frame deletion, and truncating variants in 11 individuals from 8 families, including 1 canonical splice site variant. All nontruncating variants and all but 3 truncating variants occurred de novo. - **Population data and in silico predictions:** None of the variants was present in gnomAD, 1000 Genomes, or the Exome Variant Server. All missense variants occurred at highly conserved positions and were predicted to be damaging. - **Nonsense-mediated decay:** One truncating variant, in the last exon (exon 45 of 45), might escape nonsense-mediated decay. All other truncating variants were predicted to undergo it. - **Distribution in the protein:** Missense variants were spread throughout the protein but clustered in a linker segment of domain II (exon 13) and of domain IV (exon 35). - **Functional studies:** Whole-cell patch-clamp studies were performed in transfected HEK293 cells. - L1408V reduced current density to about 30% of control without changing protein expression, indicating loss of function. - L657F increased both current density and protein level about 2-fold, indicating gain of function. - L614P caused a modest current increase that was not statistically significant. - L614R had no significant effect on current or gating. The authors noted that this variant was studied with the CACNB2 accessory subunit, whereas the others were studied with CACNB3.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Rodan et al. (2021) found that the 14 individuals with de novo nontruncating CACNA1C variants had more severe global developmental delay or intellectual disability and more severe motor impairment than those with truncating variants. About half of the nontruncating group were nonverbal and nonambulatory, and more than two-thirds had epilepsy. By contrast, the 11 individuals with truncating variants had more selective deficits in expressive language and essentially normal early motor development. Autism was more common in this group (5 of 11), and only 1 had seizures. The authors noted that their study was underpowered to compare phenotypes between missense variants with gain or loss of function.

### Pathogenesis

Rodan et al. (2021) suggested that both gain and loss of CACNA1C function can cause a neurologic phenotype. They noted that CACNA1C is constrained against loss-of-function variants (pLI score of 1.0). They proposed that a dominant-negative effect would better explain why loss-of-function missense variants cause a more severe phenotype than whole-gene or exonic deletions.

### Diagnosis

Bozarth et al. (2018) proposed that CACNA1C be included on gene panels for early-onset epilepsy.

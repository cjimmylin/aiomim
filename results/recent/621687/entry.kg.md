### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma syndrome-8 (PPGL8) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TMEM127 gene (613403) on chromosome 2q11.2. ⚠︎

### Description

Pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma syndrome-8 is an autosomal dominant tumor predisposition with incomplete penetrance. It is characterized mainly by adrenal pheochromocytoma, which is often bilateral and usually benign. Qin et al. (2010) found that the mean age at diagnosis was similar to that of sporadic cases. Armaiz-Pena et al. (2021) concluded that patients with TMEM127 variants often resemble those with sporadic nonmetastatic pheochromocytoma. Paraganglioma and renal cell carcinoma may also occur, although these authors stated that the causal link needs further evaluation. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Qin et al. (2010) studied 7 families with TMEM127 mutations. The mean age at diagnosis of pheochromocytoma was 45.3 years. This was similar to sporadic cases and older than syndromic cases. All tumors arose from the adrenal medulla, and about half of the patients had bilateral tumors. No malignancy or recurrence was seen during 4 to 16 years of follow-up. ⚠︎

Yao et al. (2010) studied 20 TMEM127 mutation carriers from 20 independent families. All had adrenal tumors. Seven had bilateral tumors (P = 2.7 x 10(-4)), and 5 of the 20 had familial disease (P =.005). Median age at onset was 41.5 years in carriers and 45 years in patients without a mutation, a difference that was not significant (P =.54). The most common presentation was a single benign adrenal tumor in a patient older than 40 years. Malignancy occurred in 1 carrier (5%). Yao et al. (2010) concluded that germline TMEM127 mutations were associated with pheochromocytoma but not paraganglioma. They noted that these mutations occurred in an age group often excluded from genetic screening algorithms. ⚠︎

Takeichi et al. (2012) reported 2 unrelated Japanese patients with bilateral adrenal pheochromocytoma who carried the same germline TMEM127 deletion.

Toledo et al. (2015) offered clinical and genetic screening to 151 individuals from a six-generation family carrying a germline TMEM127 mutation. Clinical data were available for 34 mutation carriers, who were followed for 8.7 +/- 8.1 years. Of these, 11 (32%) were diagnosed with pheochromocytoma at a median age of 43 years. In 9 patients, symptoms began at 29 years (range, 10-55 years); 2 patients were asymptomatic. Tumors were multicentric in 5 patients (45%) and bilateral in 5 (45%). Six patients (54%) had at least one adrenomedullary nodule smaller than 10 mm. No paragangliomas, distant metastases, or other manifestations were found.

Stütz et al. (2020) reported a 61-year-old woman with a 4 x 4 x 6 cm paraganglioma in the left supraclavicular fossa. The tumor showed MIBG uptake and was positive for synaptophysin and chromogranin on histology; plasma free metanephrines were normal. She also had acromegaly due to a 13-mm GH-secreting pituitary macroadenoma. She had paroxysmal hypertensive crises, which the authors attributed to the acromegaly, and a multinodular goiter. Her family history was unremarkable. Surgery cured both tumors, and she remained well after 10 years of follow-up. She carried a TMEM127 variant of uncertain significance (see MOLECULAR GENETICS).

Armaiz-Pena et al. (2021) studied 110 index patients carrying germline TMEM127 variants, drawing on a registry, published reports and public databases. Mean age was 45 years (range, 21-84 years), and females outnumbered males (76 vs 34, P <.001). Most patients had pheochromocytoma (94; 85.5%). Paraganglioma (10; 9%) and renal cell carcinoma (6; 5.4%) also occurred, either alone or together with pheochromocytoma. One-third of patients had multiple tumors, and 15.4% had a known family history. Metastatic disease was rare (2.8%).

Delgado et al. (2022) reported a 65-year-old woman with hypertension, bilateral adrenal nodules on CT scan, and elevated urinary metanephrines, consistent with bilateral pheochromocytoma. She underwent laparoscopic bilateral adrenalectomy, and no recurrence was recorded after 5 years of follow-up.

### Biochemical Features

Armaiz-Pena et al. (2021) reported that 82% of the catecholamine profiles of pheochromocytomas and paragangliomas in TMEM127 variant carriers involved epinephrine, either alone or combined with norepinephrine.

### Inheritance

Qin et al. (2010) found that the affected individuals in the original familial pheochromocytoma (FP) kindred showed a dominant pattern of transmission. Four mutation carriers in this family (Family 1) had no clinical disease at ages 43 to 58 years, which suggested incomplete penetrance. Toledo et al. (2015) found incomplete penetrance in a six-generation family (see GENOTYPE/PHENOTYPE CORRELATIONS). ⚠︎

Earlier, Dahia et al. (2005) had described a familial pheochromocytoma syndrome that they considered consistent with digenic inheritance. They concluded that it added a recessive digenic trait to the genetic heterogeneity of these tumors (see MAPPING).

### Mapping

Dahia et al. (2005) studied a family with pheochromocytoma and no known genetic defect. Multipoint parametric linkage analysis gave identical lod scores of 2.97 for loci at chromosome 2cen and 16p13. Two-locus parametric linkage analysis gave a maximum lod score of 5.16 under a double recessive multiplicative model, which the authors suggested meant that both loci were required for disease to develop.

Qin et al. (2010) had previously mapped a familial pheochromocytoma susceptibility locus (FP) to chromosome 2q11. Further affected relatives and additional linked families narrowed the locus to a 19.62-Mb region containing 205 genes.

### Heterogeneity

Dahia et al. (2005) noted that pheochromocytomas result from mutations in at least 6 different genes, each part of a distinct autosomal dominant disorder, but that some families with pheochromocytoma have no known genetic defect. They concluded that their study provided evidence for 2 novel pheochromocytoma susceptibility loci.

Bausch et al. (2017) studied 972 unrelated registrants of the European-American-Asian Pheochromocytoma-Paraganglioma Registry who had no mutations in the classic pheochromocytoma- and paraganglioma-associated genes. Of these, 58 (6.0%) carried germline mutations in newer susceptibility genes: 29 in SDHA, 20 in TMEM127, 8 in MAX, and 1 in SDHAF2. Fifty-three of the 58 patients (91%) had familial, multiple, extra-adrenal, and/or malignant tumors and/or were younger than 40 years.

### Molecular Genetics

Qin et al. (2010) sequenced the exons of genes in the FP locus using tumor DNA from the index case of Family 1. They identified a splice site mutation in the TMEM127 gene (IVS3-2A-C; L138fs), predicted to produce a truncated 148-amino acid protein. The mutation was present in the germline and in all 6 other affected sibs, and it was absent in 2 unaffected sibs. The same mutation was found in a second, unrelated family (Family 5); haplotype analysis excluded a common ancestor.

Qin et al. (2010) then sequenced TMEM127 in 102 additional index cases of pheochromocytoma (19 familial and 83 apparently sporadic) and found 6 additional mutations. Overall, 4 TMEM127 variants were found among 12 families without mutations in other susceptibility genes (about 30% of familial cases), and 3 among apparently sporadic cases (about 3%). All mutations were germline, and none were present in 1,064 ethnically matched control alleles. Six of the 7 variants were splice site or nonsense (truncating) mutations. The mutations included a 1-bp insertion, a nonsense mutation, a 4-bp deletion, a second splice site mutation, and a missense mutation.

Yao et al. (2010) sequenced the TMEM127 gene in 990 individuals with pheochromocytoma and/or paraganglioma from 8 referral centers worldwide. The cohort included 898 previously unreported cases without mutations in other susceptibility genes. They identified 19 potentially pathogenic germline TMEM127 mutations in 20 independent families. A multiplex PCR-based method was used to screen 545 samples for large gene deletions, and none were found. ⚠︎

Takeichi et al. (2012) sequenced TMEM127 in germline DNA from 74 unrelated Japanese patients with pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma who were negative for mutations and deletions in RET, VHL, SDHB, and SDHD. They found the same heterozygous germline 4-bp deletion (c.116_119delTGTC) in 2 unrelated patients (2.7%) with bilateral adrenal pheochromocytoma. Tumor DNA showed loss of heterozygosity due to a 2q deletion. The deletion had been reported before in a Brazilian patient and was not found in 114 healthy ethnically matched controls.

Toledo et al. (2015) found that 47 members of a six-generation family with pheochromocytoma carried a germline splice site mutation in the TMEM127 gene (c.410-2A-C).

In a 61-year-old woman with paraganglioma and acromegaly, Stütz et al. (2020) used next-generation sequencing of PPGL susceptibility genes and found a heterozygous intronic TMEM127 variant, c.245-10C-G, in intron 2. The variant was absent from gnomAD and was classified as a variant of uncertain significance under ACMG criteria. Segregation could not be studied because her parents were deceased and her children declined testing. The variant lies between other known variants at c.245-7 and c.245-12, and the authors suggested that it might disrupt the consensus splice site. They noted that experimental confirmation was needed.

In a 65-year-old woman with bilateral pheochromocytoma, Delgado et al. (2022) identified a mutation in the TMEM127 gene (c.117_120delGTCT) by genetic testing.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

In their six-generation family, Toledo et al. (2015) estimated the cumulative penetrance of pheochromocytoma in TMEM127 mutation carriers. It was 0% at ages 0-20 years, 3% at 21-30 years, 15% at 31-40 years, 24% at 41-50 years, and 32% at 51-65 years. The youngest patient was diagnosed at age 22 years, and the earliest symptoms were reported at age 10.

Armaiz-Pena et al. (2021) found that most TMEM127 variants (n = 63) occurred only once, whereas 13 were recurrent (2-12 times). Truncating variants were more common overall. However, nontruncating variants predominated in presentations other than pheochromocytoma alone (36% in pheochromocytoma-only vs 69% in other presentations, P <.001). Nontruncating variants also clustered disproportionately within the transmembrane regions (P <.01). Combining clinical and earlier experimental data, the authors proposed sorting variants into 4 groups according to mutation type, location, and predicted disruption.

### Pathogenesis

In all tumors from their family, Dahia et al. (2005) detected allele-specific loss of heterozygosity (LOH) only at the chromosome 2 locus, which they considered consistent with a tumor suppressor gene. Transcription profiling identified 4 additional pheochromocytomas with a similar genetic pattern, which helped refine the chromosome 2 locus. High-density SNP-array LOH mapping found LOH within the chromosome 2 region in 18 of 62 pheochromocytomas, narrowing the locus to less than 2 cM.

Qin et al. (2010) examined LOH at the TMEM127 locus in 35 pheochromocytomas. All 19 TMEM127-mutant tumors tested showed LOH with loss of the wild-type allele, compared with 2 of 16 wild-type tumors, consistent with a 2-hit tumor suppressor mechanism. TMEM127 expression was 4-fold lower in mutant tumors, which the authors suggested reflected instability of the mutant transcript. In HEK293 cells transfected with mutant TMEM127 constructs (M158 and M99), no protein product was detectable despite equivalent transfection efficiency, supporting a loss-of-function effect. On expression profiling, TMEM127-mutant pheochromocytomas clustered with NF1- and RET-mutant tumors and were enriched for kinase receptor signaling transcripts rather than hypoxia transcripts. TMEM127-mutant tumors showed increased S6K phosphorylation compared with normal adrenal medulla and TMEM127 wild-type tumors.

Qin et al. (2010) found that knockdown of TMEM127 increased phosphorylation of the mTORC1 targets 4EBP1 and S6K and increased cell size and proliferation. Overexpression of TMEM127 reduced mTORC1 signaling and proliferation without affecting RAS activity or AKT phosphorylation. TMEM127 localized to the plasma membrane, early endosomes, Golgi, and lysosomes, and it colocalized with amino acid-activated perinuclear mTOR. The authors suggested that TMEM127 is a compartment-specific negative regulator of mTORC1. ⚠︎

Yao et al. (2010) expressed 5 novel TMEM127 mutants in cell lines and compared them with wildtype using confocal microscopy. The mutant proteins were diffusely localized, whereas wildtype TMEM127 showed a discrete multiorganelle distribution. The authors concluded that disease-associated mutations disrupt the intracellular distribution of the protein.

Takeichi et al. (2012) found that the germline TMEM127 deletion in their 2 Japanese patients occurred together with LOH due to a 2q deletion in tumor DNA. They noted that this fits with TMEM127 acting as a tumor suppressor gene. ⚠︎

### Diagnosis

Toledo et al. (2015) described tumor multicentricity, nodular adrenomedullary hyperplasia, and symptom onset more than a decade before diagnosis as novel findings in TMEM127-related pheochromocytoma. They recommended offering TMEM127 genetic testing to at-risk individuals at age 22 years and annual clinical surveillance for mutation carriers.

Bausch et al. (2017) recommended genetic testing for SDHA, TMEM127, MAX, and SDHAF2 in patients at high clinical risk who are mutation negative for the classic susceptibility genes. They also recommended regular, systematic whole-body investigation for gene-specific prevention and early detection.

### Population Genetics

Takeichi et al. (2012) presented their study as the first report of a pathologic role for TMEM127 mutation in pheochromocytoma in an Asian population. They found TMEM127 mutations in 2.7% of Japanese patients who were negative for RET, VHL, SDHB, and SDHD mutations. They concluded that the prevalence and phenotype appeared similar to those in earlier studies.

Qin et al. (2010) found TMEM127 mutations in about 30% of familial cases and about 3% of apparently sporadic cases of pheochromocytoma that lacked mutations in other susceptibility genes.

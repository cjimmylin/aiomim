### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma syndrome-8 (PPGL8) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TMEM127 gene (613403) on chromosome 2q11.2. ⚠︎

### Description

Pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma syndrome-8 (PPGL8) is an autosomal dominant tumor predisposition caused by germline TMEM127 mutations. It mainly causes pheochromocytoma, which may be bilateral or multicentric and usually appears in adulthood. Paraganglioma and renal cell carcinoma are seen less often, and metastatic disease is rare (Yao et al., 2010; Toledo et al., 2015; Armaiz-Pena et al., 2021). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Dahia et al. (2005) described a family with familial pheochromocytoma. In all tumors from this family, allele-specific loss of heterozygosity (LOH) was found only at a chromosome 2 locus, which the authors interpreted as consistent with a tumor suppressor gene.

Yao et al. (2010) studied carriers of germline FP/TMEM127 mutations from 20 independent families. All mutation carriers had adrenal tumors. In 7, the tumors were bilateral (P = 2.7 x 10(-4)), and 5 of 20 samples came from patients with familial disease (P = 0.005). Malignancy was seen in 1 mutation carrier (5%). Median age at onset was 41.5 years. The most common presentation was a single benign adrenal tumor in a patient older than 40 years.

Takeichi et al. (2012) reported 2 unrelated Japanese patients with bilateral adrenal pheochromocytoma. In both patients, the tumors showed loss of heterozygosity at a 2q deletion.

Toledo et al. (2015) studied a 6-generation family in which 47 of 151 relatives carried a TMEM127 mutation. Clinical data were available for 34 mutation carriers, who were followed for 8.7 +/- 8.1 years (range, 1-20 years). Pheochromocytoma was diagnosed in 11 carriers (32%) at a median age of 43 years. Tumors were multicentric in 5 patients (45%) and bilateral in 5 (45%). Six patients (54%) had at least 1 adrenomedullary nodule smaller than 10 mm. No paragangliomas, distant metastases, or other manifestations were detected. In 9 patients, symptoms began at 29 years (range, 10-55 years); 2 patients were asymptomatic. The youngest patient was diagnosed at age 22 years, and the earliest symptoms were reported at age 10. Cumulative penetrance of pheochromocytoma was 0% at 0 to 20 years, 3% at 21 to 30 years, 15% at 31 to 40 years, 24% at 41 to 50 years, and 32% at 51 to 65 years. The authors concluded that multicentric tumors, nodular adrenomedullary hyperplasia, and symptoms beginning more than a decade before diagnosis were novel findings in TMEM127-related pheochromocytoma.

Bausch et al. (2017) identified 20 patients with germline TMEM127 mutations in the European-American-Asian Pheochromocytoma-Paraganglioma Registry. Among carriers of SDHA and TMEM127 mutations together, 7 of 63 (11%) pheochromocytomas and paragangliomas were malignant.

Stütz et al. (2020) reported a 61-year-old woman who had pressure in the left neck, a deepening voice, and exertional dyspnea for about half a year. During the same period she had paroxysmal hypertensive crises, with systolic blood pressure up to 240 mmHg, accompanied by headache, flushing, and chest tightness. She had a neck paraganglioma and acromegaly due to a growth hormone-secreting pituitary macroadenoma, with elevated IGF1, macroglossia, and snoring. She also had a multinodular goiter and colonic polyps. Plasma free metanephrines were normal. She carried a heterozygous TMEM127 variant of uncertain significance.

Armaiz-Pena et al. (2021) described 110 index patients with germline TMEM127 variants. The mean age was 45 years (range, 21-84 years), and females predominated (76 vs 34; P less than 0.001). Most patients had pheochromocytoma (94; 85.5%). Paraganglioma (10; 9%) and renal cell carcinoma (6; 5.4%) were also found, either alone or together with pheochromocytoma. One-third of patients had multiple tumors, and 15.4% reported a known family history. Metastatic pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma was rare (2.8%). In 82% of tumors, the catecholamine profile was epinephrine alone or with norepinephrine. The authors concluded that patients with TMEM127 variants often resemble patients with sporadic nonmetastatic pheochromocytoma. They noted that paraganglioma and renal cell carcinoma may co-occur but that the causal link needed further evaluation.

Delgado et al. (2022) reported a 65-year-old woman with hypertension, bilateral adrenal nodules on CT scan, and elevated urinary metanephrines. She was found to have bilateral pheochromocytoma.

### Inheritance

Toledo et al. (2015) observed transmission of a heterozygous TMEM127 mutation through a 6-generation family with incomplete penetrance, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. Yao et al. (2010) also found germline heterozygous mutations in families with pheochromocytoma. ⚠︎

In contrast, Dahia et al. (2005) concluded from linkage analysis that the familial pheochromocytoma syndrome in the family they studied followed a recessive digenic pattern involving loci on chromosomes 2cen and 16p13.

### Mapping

In a family with pheochromocytoma, Dahia et al. (2005) obtained identical multipoint LOD scores of 2.97 for loci at chromosome 2cen and 16p13. Two-locus parametric linkage analysis gave a maximum LOD score of 5.16 under a double recessive multiplicative model, which suggested that both loci were required for disease. Transcription profiling identified 4 more pheochromocytomas with a similar genetic pattern, which helped refine the chromosome 2 locus. High-density SNP array LOH mapping found LOH within the chromosome 2 region in 18 of 62 pheochromocytomas and narrowed the locus to less than 2 cM.

### Molecular Genetics

Yao et al. (2010) sequenced the FP/TMEM127 gene in 990 individuals with pheochromocytoma and/or paraganglioma. These included 898 previously unreported cases without mutations in other susceptibility genes. The authors found 19 potentially pathogenic germline heterozygous mutations in 20 independent families. Multiplex PCR screening of 545 samples detected no large deletions. Confocal microscopy of 5 novel mutant proteins expressed in cell lines showed diffuse localization, whereas wild-type TMEM127 had a discrete multiorganelle distribution. ⚠︎

Takeichi et al. (2012) sequenced TMEM127 in 74 unrelated Japanese patients with pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma who tested negative for RET, VHL, SDHB, and SDHD mutations. Two patients (2.7%) with bilateral adrenal pheochromocytoma carried the same heterozygous germline 4-bp deletion (c.116_119delTGTC; p.Ile41ArgfsTer39). None of 114 ethnically matched healthy controls carried the deletion. The authors noted that the same mutation had been reported previously in a Brazilian case. They described their report as the first to show a pathologic role for TMEM127 mutation in pheochromocytoma in an Asian population.

In a 6-generation family with pheochromocytoma, Toledo et al. (2015) identified a heterozygous splice site mutation in the TMEM127 gene (c.410-2A-C). The mutation was carried by 47 of 151 relatives tested, and 11 of the 34 carriers with clinical data developed pheochromocytoma. ⚠︎

Bausch et al. (2017) studied 972 unrelated registrants of the European-American-Asian Pheochromocytoma-Paraganglioma Registry who had no mutations in the classic PPGL genes. Fifty-eight (6.0%) carried germline mutations in SDHA (29), TMEM127 (20), MAX (8), or SDHAF2 (1). Of these 58 patients, 53 (91%) had familial, multiple, extra-adrenal, and/or malignant tumors and/or were younger than 40 years.

In the 61-year-old woman with neck paraganglioma and acromegaly, Stütz et al. (2020) found a heterozygous intronic TMEM127 variant (c.245-10C-G). Her parents had died, and her children were not tested. The authors classified the variant as of uncertain significance under ACMG criteria (PM2, BP4). Because the variant lies between the known variants c.245-7 and c.245-12, they suggested it might disrupt the consensus splice site, but they noted that abnormal splicing had not been shown experimentally. The same variant had also been found in a patient with breast cancer. ⚠︎

Armaiz-Pena et al. (2021) analyzed 111 germline heterozygous TMEM127 variants, both truncating and nontruncating, in 110 index patients. Most variants (63) occurred only once, while 13 were recurrent and occurred 2 to 12 times. The authors proposed classifying variants into 4 groups by mutation type, location, and predicted disruption, to help predict pathogenicity and guide carrier surveillance. ⚠︎

In a 65-year-old woman with bilateral pheochromocytoma, Delgado et al. (2022) identified a c.117_120delGTCT mutation in the TMEM127 gene.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Yao et al. (2010) found that germline TMEM127 mutations were associated with pheochromocytoma but not with paraganglioma. Median age at onset was similar in mutation carriers and in patients without a mutation (41.5 vs 45 years). The most common presentation was a single benign adrenal tumor in a patient older than 40 years, an age group often excluded from genetic screening algorithms.

Armaiz-Pena et al. (2021) found that nontruncating TMEM127 variants were less frequent overall than truncating variants. However, nontruncating variants predominated in presentations other than pheochromocytoma alone (36% in pheochromocytoma-only cases vs 69% in other presentations) and clustered disproportionately within transmembrane regions.

### Pathogenesis

Yao et al. (2010) expressed 5 novel TMEM127 mutant proteins in cell lines and examined them by confocal microscopy. The mutant proteins were diffusely localized, in contrast with the discrete multiorganelle distribution of wild-type TMEM127. This indicated that disease-associated mutations disrupt the protein's intracellular distribution.

### Clinical Management

Toledo et al. (2015) recommended offering TMEM127 genetic testing to at-risk individuals at age 22 years, followed by annual clinical surveillance of mutation carriers.

Bausch et al. (2017) recommended testing SDHA, TMEM127, MAX, and SDHAF2 in clinically high-risk patients who are negative for mutations in the classic genes. They stated that gene-specific prevention and/or early detection requires regular, systematic whole-body investigation.

Delgado et al. (2022) reported that a woman with bilateral pheochromocytoma and a TMEM127 mutation underwent laparoscopic bilateral adrenalectomy. No recurrence was recorded after 5 years of follow-up.

### Population Genetics

Takeichi et al. (2012) found germline TMEM127 mutations in 2 (2.7%) of 74 unrelated Japanese patients with pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma who had tested negative for RET, VHL, SDHB, and SDHD mutations. They stated that the prevalence appeared similar to that in previous studies.

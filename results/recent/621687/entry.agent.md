### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma syndrome-8 (PPGL8) is caused by heterozygous germline mutation in the TMEM127 gene on chromosome 2q11.

### Description

Pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma syndrome-8 (PPGL8) is a tumor predisposition caused by germline TMEM127 mutation. Affected individuals mainly develop pheochromocytomas, catecholamine-secreting tumors of the adrenal medulla, which are bilateral in a substantial proportion of patients and usually arise at ages similar to those of sporadic pheochromocytoma (Qin et al., 2010; Yao et al., 2010). Paragangliomas and renal cell carcinoma have also been reported in carriers, and metastatic disease is rare (Armaiz-Pena et al., 2021).

### Nomenclature

Qin et al. (2010) named the pheochromocytoma susceptibility locus on chromosome 2q11 FP (familial pheochromocytoma). Yao et al. (2010) referred to the gene as FP/TMEM127.

### Clinical Features

Qin et al. (2010) reported 7 index patients and families with germline TMEM127 mutations. In family 1, 7 sibs were affected, with a total of 10 tumors and age at onset ranging from 34 to 54 years; 3 of the 7 had bilateral tumors. Across the series, patients developed pheochromocytomas at a mean age of 45.3 years, similar to the mean age at diagnosis of sporadic pheochromocytomas (43.6 to 43.9 years) but older than syndromic cases (24.9 to 30.2 years). All tumors arose from the adrenal medulla and were bilateral in approximately half of the patients. No malignancies, defined as metastasis to nonchromaffin tissues, or recurrences were detected during follow-up of 4 to 16 years. Four mutation carriers in family 1 remained without clinical disease at ages 43 to 58 years.

Yao et al. (2010) found that all 20 index cases with TMEM127 mutations had adrenal tumors, including 7 with bilateral tumors, and 5 of 20 had familial disease. The median age at onset in mutation carriers was similar to that in patients without a mutation (41.5 vs 45 years). The most common presentation was a single benign adrenal tumor in a patient older than 40 years. Malignancy was seen in 1 mutation carrier (5%). The authors concluded that TMEM127 mutations were associated with pheochromocytoma but not paraganglioma.

Takeichi et al. (2012) reported 2 unrelated Japanese patients with bilateral adrenal pheochromocytoma who carried the same germline TMEM127 mutation.

Toledo et al. (2015) studied a 6-generation family carrying a germline TMEM127 mutation. Clinical data were available for 34 mutation carriers followed for 8.7 +/- 8.1 years (range, 1 to 20 years). Pheochromocytoma was diagnosed in 11 carriers (32%) at a median age of 43 years. In 9 patients symptoms began at a median of 29 years (range, 10 to 55 years), and 2 were asymptomatic. Tumors were multicentric in 5 (45%) and bilateral in 5 (45%) patients, and 6 patients (54%) had at least 1 adrenomedullary nodule less than 10 mm. No paragangliomas, distant metastases, or other manifestations were found. Cumulative penetrance of pheochromocytoma was 0% at 0 to 20 years, 3% at 21 to 30 years, 15% at 31 to 40 years, 24% at 41 to 50 years, and 32% at 51 to 65 years. The youngest patient was diagnosed at 22 years, and the earliest symptoms were reported at age 10. The authors noted tumor multicentricity, nodular adrenomedullary hyperplasia, and onset of symptoms more than a decade before diagnosis as novel features. ⚠︎

Stutz et al. (2020) reported a 61-year-old woman who presented with pressure in the left neck, deepening of the voice, and exertional dyspnea of about 6 months' duration, together with paroxysmal hypertensive crises with headache, flushing, and chest tightness, and a 4-year history of enlargement of the tongue, ring size, and shoe size. Imaging showed a 4 x 4 x 6-cm mass in the left supraclavicular fossa, confirmed as a paraganglioma by MIBG scintigraphy and histology; plasma free metanephrines were normal. She also had acromegaly due to a 13-mm growth hormone-secreting pituitary macroadenoma. Her medical and family history was otherwise unremarkable apart from multinodular goiter. Both tumors were cured by surgery, and she remained well after 10 years of follow-up. The authors attributed the hypertensive episodes to the acromegaly.

Armaiz-Pena et al. (2021) analyzed 110 index patients with germline TMEM127 variants, drawn from a registry, published reports, and public databases. Mean age was 45 years (range, 21 to 84 years), and females predominated (76 vs 34). Most had pheochromocytoma (94 patients, 85.5%), but paraganglioma (10 patients, 9%) and renal cell carcinoma (6 patients, 5.4%) were also detected, alone or with pheochromocytoma; the authors noted that the causal link of paraganglioma and renal cell carcinoma required further evaluation. One-third of patients had multiple tumors, 15.4% had a known family history, and metastatic disease was rare (2.8%). Epinephrine alone or with norepinephrine accounted for 82% of catecholamine profiles.

Delgado et al. (2022) reported a 65-year-old woman with hypertension, bilateral adrenal nodules on CT scan, and elevated urinary metanephrines. She underwent laparoscopic bilateral adrenalectomy, and no recurrence was recorded after 5 years of follow-up.

### Inheritance

Qin et al. (2010) stated that identification of additional affected members of the original kindred established a dominant model of transmission; the mutation was present in 7 affected sibs and absent from 2 unaffected sibs, and several mutation carriers remained unaffected in middle age, indicating incomplete penetrance. Toledo et al. (2015) reported a 6-generation family with a cumulative penetrance of pheochromocytoma of 32% by age 51 to 65 years. These findings contrasted with the earlier proposal by Dahia et al. (2005) of digenic inheritance in a familial pheochromocytoma syndrome (see MAPPING). ⚠︎

### Mapping

In a family with familial pheochromocytoma, Dahia et al. (2005) found identical multipoint parametric lod scores of 2.97 for loci at chromosome 2cen and 16p13. Two-locus parametric linkage analysis gave a maximum lod score of 5.16 under a double recessive multiplicative model, suggesting that both loci were required for disease. Allele-specific loss of heterozygosity (LOH) was detected only at the chromosome 2 locus in all tumors from the family, consistent with a tumor suppressor gene. Four additional pheochromocytomas with a similar genetic pattern were identified by transcription profiling, and high-density SNP array LOH mapping identified LOH in the chromosome 2 region in 18 of 62 pheochromocytomas, narrowing the locus to less than 2 cM.

Qin et al. (2010) stated that additional linked families redefined the limits of the FP locus on chromosome 2q11 to a 19.62-Mb region containing 205 genes.

### Molecular Genetics

By sequencing genes in the FP locus on chromosome 2q11 using tumor DNA from the index case of a family with pheochromocytoma (family 1), Qin et al. (2010) identified a splice site mutation in the TMEM127 gene (IVS3-2A-C). The mutation was present in the germline of the index case and of 6 other affected sibs, but not in 2 unaffected sibs. Sequencing of germline and/or tumor DNA from an additional 102 index pheochromocytoma patients (19 familial and 83 apparently sporadic) identified 6 additional TMEM127 mutations. Six of the 7 mutations were splice site or nonsense mutations predicted to truncate the protein, and the remaining one was a missense mutation (V90M). Four mutations were found among 12 families without mutations in other susceptibility genes and 3 among sporadic cases. The IVS3-2A-C mutation was found in 2 families (1 and 5), and haplotype analysis excluded a common ancestor. None of the changes were found in 1,064 ethnically matched control alleles. All mutations were present in the germline and, when available, in affected relatives. All 19 TMEM127-mutant tumors tested showed LOH with loss of the wildtype allele, compared with 2 of 16 tumors without TMEM127 mutation, consistent with a 2-hit model of tumor suppressor inactivation. TMEM127 expression was decreased 4-fold in mutant tumors, and mutant constructs produced no detectable protein in transfected HEK293 cells.

Yao et al. (2010) sequenced TMEM127 in 990 individuals with pheochromocytoma and/or paraganglioma, including 898 previously unreported cases without mutations in other susceptibility genes from 8 referral centers, and screened 545 samples for large deletions. They identified 19 potentially pathogenic germline mutations in 20 independent families; no large deletions were detected. In transfected cells, 5 novel mutant proteins showed diffuse localization, in contrast to the discrete multiorganelle distribution of wildtype TMEM127.

In 74 unrelated Japanese patients with pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma without mutations or deletions in RET, VHL, SDHB, or SDHD, Takeichi et al. (2012) identified 2 unrelated patients (2.7%) with bilateral adrenal pheochromocytoma who carried the same germline TMEM127 mutation (c.116_119delTGTC; p.Ile41ArgfsX39), which was associated with 2q deletion LOH in the tumors. The mutation had previously been described in a Brazilian case and was not found in 114 ethnically matched healthy controls.

Toledo et al. (2015) offered genetic testing to 151 individuals from a 6-generation family and found that 47 carried a c.410-2A-C mutation in the TMEM127 gene. ⚠︎

Among 972 unrelated registrants of the European-American-Asian Pheochromocytoma-Paraganglioma Registry without mutations in the classic pheochromocytoma and paraganglioma genes, Bausch et al. (2017) identified 20 with germline TMEM127 mutations.

In a woman with a neck paraganglioma and acromegaly (see CLINICAL FEATURES), Stutz et al. (2020) identified a heterozygous intron 2 variant in the TMEM127 gene (c.245-10C-G) by next-generation sequencing. The variant was absent from gnomAD and was classified as a variant of uncertain significance by ACMG criteria; the same variant had been seen in a patient with breast cancer. Parental testing was not possible, splicing studies and LOH analysis were not performed, and the authors stated that the clinical significance of the variant was uncertain.

In a cohort of 110 index patients carrying 111 germline TMEM127 variants, Armaiz-Pena et al. (2021) found that most variants (63) occurred only once and 13 were recurrent (2 to 12 times). Integrating clinical and previous experimental data, they proposed classifying variants into 4 groups based on mutation type, location, and predicted disruption.

In a 65-year-old woman with bilateral pheochromocytoma, Delgado et al. (2022) identified a c.117_120delGTCT mutation in the TMEM127 gene by genetic testing.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Armaiz-Pena et al. (2021) found that, although nontruncating TMEM127 variants were less frequent than truncating changes overall, they predominated in non-pheochromocytoma presentations (36% in pheochromocytoma-only cases vs 69% in other presentations) and clustered disproportionately within the transmembrane regions.

### Pathogenesis

Qin et al. (2010) found that TMEM127-mutant pheochromocytomas were transcriptionally related to tumors with NF1 and RET mutations and were enriched for kinase receptor signaling signatures rather than the hypoxia signature of VHL-, SDHB-, or SDHD-mutant tumors. TMEM127 knockdown did not increase RAS activity or AKT phosphorylation but increased phosphorylation of mTORC1 targets and increased cell size and proliferation, whereas overexpression reduced mTORC1 signaling. TMEM127-mutant tumors showed increased S6K phosphorylation compared with normal adrenal medulla or tumors without TMEM127 mutation. TMEM127 associated with the endomembrane system (early endosomes, Golgi, and lysosomes) and colocalized with perinuclear, amino acid-activated mTOR. The authors concluded that TMEM127 is a tumor suppressor that negatively regulates mTORC1.

Yao et al. (2010) concluded that disease-associated TMEM127 mutations disrupt the intracellular distribution of the protein.

### Clinical Management

On the basis of their penetrance data, Toledo et al. (2015) recommended that TMEM127 genetic testing be offered to at-risk individuals at age 22 years and that mutation carriers undergo annual clinical surveillance. Bausch et al. (2017) recommended genetic testing for TMEM127 and other newer susceptibility genes in clinically high-risk patients negative for mutations in the classic genes, with regular, systematic whole-body investigation for prevention and early detection.

### Population Genetics

Qin et al. (2010) detected truncating germline TMEM127 mutations in approximately 30% of familial and about 3% of sporadic-appearing pheochromocytomas without a known genetic cause in a cohort of 103 samples. Takeichi et al. (2012) found TMEM127 mutations in 2 of 74 (2.7%) Japanese patients with pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma who were negative for RET, VHL, SDHB, and SDHD mutations. ⚠︎

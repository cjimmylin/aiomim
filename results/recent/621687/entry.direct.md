### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma syndrome-8 (PPGL8) is caused by heterozygous germline mutation in the TMEM127 gene on chromosome 2q11.

### Description

Pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma syndrome-8 (PPGL8) is an autosomal dominant tumor predisposition caused by germline TMEM127 mutations. It mainly causes catecholamine-secreting pheochromocytomas of the adrenal medulla. These tumors are often bilateral or multicentric and usually benign, and onset is at an age similar to that of sporadic pheochromocytoma (Qin et al., 2010; Yao et al., 2010; Toledo et al., 2015). Paragangliomas and renal cell carcinoma have been reported less often in carriers, but whether they are causally linked to TMEM127 mutations has not been established (Armaiz-Pena et al., 2021).

### Nomenclature

Qin et al. (2010) called the pheochromocytoma susceptibility locus on chromosome 2q11 the FP (familial pheochromocytoma) locus. Yao et al. (2010) referred to the gene as FP/TMEM127.

### Clinical Features

Dahia et al. (2005) described a family with familial pheochromocytoma that had no known genetic defect.

Qin et al. (2010) reported 7 index patients or families with germline TMEM127 mutations. Four had a family history of pheochromocytoma, 2 had no family history, and 1 had been adopted. On average, patients developed pheochromocytoma at 45.3 years of age. This was similar to the mean age at diagnosis of sporadic pheochromocytoma and notably older than in syndromic cases. Within families, age at onset ranged from 25 to 72 years. All tumors arose from the adrenal medulla, and about half of the patients had bilateral tumors. During follow-up of 4 to 16 years, there were no malignancies, defined by metastasis to nonchromaffin tissues, and no recurrences. In the original kindred (family 1), 7 sibs were affected with a total of 10 tumors. Four mutation carriers from this family remained without clinical disease at 43 to 58 years of age. ⚠︎

Yao et al. (2010) identified 20 independent families with germline TMEM127 mutations. All mutation carriers had adrenal tumors. Tumors were bilateral in 7, and disease was familial in 5 of 20 samples. Median age at disease onset was 41.5 years in mutation carriers and 45 years in patients without a mutation (P = 0.54). The most common presentation was a single benign adrenal tumor in a patient older than 40 years. One mutation carrier (5%) had malignancy. The authors concluded that germline TMEM127 mutations were associated with pheochromocytoma but not with paraganglioma.

Takeichi et al. (2012) reported 2 unrelated Japanese patients with bilateral adrenal pheochromocytoma who carried TMEM127 mutations.

Toledo et al. (2015) studied a 6-generation family with a germline TMEM127 mutation. Of 151 individuals offered screening, 47 carried the mutation. Clinical data were available for 34 carriers, who were followed for 8.7 +/- 8.1 years (range, 1-20 years). Eleven carriers (32%) were diagnosed with pheochromocytoma at a median age of 43 years. Nine of these patients had symptoms beginning at a median of 29 years (range, 10-55 years), and 2 were asymptomatic. Tumors were multicentric in 5 patients (45%) and bilateral in 5 (45%). Six patients (54%) had at least 1 adrenomedullary nodule less than 10 mm across. No paragangliomas, distant metastases, or other manifestations were found. The youngest patient was diagnosed at 22 years of age, and the earliest symptoms were reported at age 10. The authors considered tumor multicentricity, nodular adrenomedullary hyperplasia, and symptom onset more than a decade before diagnosis to be new findings in TMEM127-related pheochromocytoma. ⚠︎

Bausch et al. (2017) studied the European-American-Asian Pheochromocytoma-Paraganglioma Registry. Among 972 unrelated registrants without mutations in the classic pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma genes, 20 carried TMEM127 mutations. The authors reported that 7 of 63 (11%) pheochromocytomas and paragangliomas in patients with SDHA or TMEM127 disease were malignant.

Stutz et al. (2020) reported a 61-year-old woman with acromegaly due to a growth hormone-secreting pituitary macroadenoma and a symptomatic neck paraganglioma measuring 4 x 4 x 6 cm. She had paroxysmal hypertensive crises with systolic blood pressure up to 240 mm Hg, but plasma free metanephrines were normal on 2 occasions. She also had a multinodular goiter, and colonoscopy showed polyps. Surgery cured both tumors, and she remained well after 10 years of follow-up. Her family history was unremarkable. The authors attributed the hypertensive episodes to acromegaly.

Armaiz-Pena et al. (2021) reviewed 110 index patients with TMEM127 variants drawn from a registry, published reports, and public databases. Mean age was 45 years (range, 21-84 years), and females predominated (76 vs 34). Most patients had pheochromocytoma (94; 85.5%). Paraganglioma (10; 9%) and renal cell carcinoma (6; 5.4%) also occurred, either alone or together with pheochromocytoma. One-third of the patients had multiple tumors, 15.4% had a known family history, and 2.8% had metastatic pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma. Epinephrine alone or with norepinephrine made up 82% of the catecholamine profiles. The authors concluded that patients with TMEM127 variants often resemble those with sporadic nonmetastatic pheochromocytoma. They noted that paraganglioma and renal cell carcinoma may co-occur, but that a causal link required further evaluation.

Delgado et al. (2022) reported a 65-year-old woman with hypertension, bilateral adrenal nodules on CT scan, and elevated urinary metanephrines. She underwent laparoscopic bilateral adrenalectomy and had no recurrence after 5 years of follow-up.

### Inheritance

Dahia et al. (2005) studied a family with familial pheochromocytoma. A 2-locus parametric linkage analysis gave a maximum lod score of 5.16 under a double recessive multiplicative model, which suggested digenic recessive inheritance. Later, Qin et al. (2010) identified additional affected individuals in the original kindred and established a dominant model of transmission. Toledo et al. (2015) reported a 6-generation family with heterozygous TMEM127 mutation and incomplete penetrance. ⚠︎

### Mapping

Dahia et al. (2005) performed multipoint parametric linkage analysis in a family with familial pheochromocytoma. They obtained identical lod scores of 2.97 for loci at 2cen and 16p13. All tumors from the family showed allele-specific loss of heterozygosity (LOH) at the chromosome 2 locus only, consistent with a tumor suppressor gene. Transcription profiling identified 4 additional pheochromocytomas with a similar genetic pattern, which helped refine the chromosome 2 locus. High-density SNP array mapping found LOH within the chromosome 2 region in 18 of 62 pheochromocytomas and narrowed the locus to less than 2 cM.

Qin et al. (2010) studied additional linked families and redefined the FP locus on chromosome 2q11 as a 19.62-Mb region containing 205 genes.

### Molecular Genetics

Qin et al. (2010) sequenced genes in the FP locus using tumor DNA from the index case of the original family with familial pheochromocytoma (family 1). They identified a splice site mutation (IVS3-2A-C) in the TMEM127 gene. The mutation was present in the patient's germline DNA and in each of 6 other affected sibs, but not in 2 unaffected sibs. Sequencing of 102 further index pheochromocytomas (19 with a familial history and 83 apparently sporadic) identified 6 more germline TMEM127 mutations. In total, 4 mutations were found among 12 families without mutations in other susceptibility genes, and 3 among apparently sporadic cases. Six of the 7 mutations were splice site, frameshift, or nonsense mutations predicted to truncate the protein, and 1 was a missense mutation (V90M). The IVS3-2A-C mutation occurred in 2 families, and haplotype analysis excluded a common ancestor. None of the variants were found in 1,064 ethnically matched control alleles. All 19 TMEM127-mutant tumors tested showed LOH with loss of the wild-type allele, compared with 2 of 16 tumors without a TMEM127 mutation. TMEM127 expression in mutant tumors was reduced 4-fold. In HEK293 cells, no translation product was detected from mutant constructs, consistent with loss of function.

Yao et al. (2010) sequenced TMEM127 in 990 individuals with pheochromocytoma and/or paraganglioma. These included 898 previously unreported cases without mutations in other susceptibility genes, from 8 referral centers. They identified 19 potentially pathogenic germline TMEM127 mutations in 20 independent families. A multiplex PCR-based screen of 545 samples detected no large deletions. Five novel mutant proteins expressed in cell lines localized diffusely, unlike the discrete multiorganelle distribution of wild-type TMEM127.

Takeichi et al. (2012) sequenced TMEM127 in germline DNA from 74 unrelated Japanese patients with pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma who were negative for mutations and deletions in RET, VHL, SDHB, and SDHD. Two unrelated patients (2.7%), both with bilateral adrenal pheochromocytoma, had the same germline 4-bp deletion (c.116_119delTGTC; I41RfsX39). In tumor DNA, the mutation was associated with LOH due to 2q deletion. The mutation had previously been described in a Brazilian case and was absent from 114 healthy ethnically matched controls.

Toledo et al. (2015) offered genetic testing to members of a 6-generation family and identified the c.410-2A-C TMEM127 mutation in 47 individuals. Cumulative penetrance of pheochromocytoma was 0% at 0 to 20 years, 3% at 21 to 30 years, 15% at 31 to 40 years, 24% at 41 to 50 years, and 32% at 51 to 65 years.

Bausch et al. (2017) found germline TMEM127 mutations in 20 of 972 unrelated registrants with pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma who had no mutations in the classic susceptibility genes.

Stutz et al. (2020) used next-generation sequencing in a woman with a neck paraganglioma and acromegaly and found a heterozygous intron 2 variant in TMEM127 (c.245-10C-G). The variant was not in the gnomAD database, and the authors classified it as a variant of uncertain significance by ACMG criteria. They could not determine whether it had arisen de novo, and they did not perform functional studies such as LOH analysis.

Delgado et al. (2022) found a c.117_120delGTCT TMEM127 mutation in a 65-year-old woman with bilateral pheochromocytoma.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Armaiz-Pena et al. (2021) analyzed 111 TMEM127 variants in 110 index patients. Most variants (63) occurred only once, and 13 were recurrent, occurring 2 to 12 times. Nontruncating variants were less frequent overall than truncating variants, but they predominated in non-pheochromocytoma presentations (36% in pheochromocytoma-only cases vs 69% in other presentations, P less than 0.001). Nontruncating variants also clustered disproportionately within the transmembrane regions (P less than 0.01). Combining clinical and earlier experimental data, the authors proposed classifying variants into 4 groups by mutation type, location, and predicted disruption.

### Pathogenesis

Qin et al. (2010) found that tumors with TMEM127 mutations clustered transcriptionally with tumors carrying NF1 and RET mutations and were enriched for kinase receptor signaling pathways. In contrast, VHL-, SDHB-, and SDHD-mutant tumors were enriched for hypoxia-response transcripts. Knockdown of TMEM127 increased phosphorylation of the mTORC1 targets 4EBP1 and S6K, increased cell size, and increased cell proliferation. It did not increase RAS activity or AKT phosphorylation. Overexpression of TMEM127 reduced mTORC1 signaling. TMEM127-mutant pheochromocytomas showed increased S6K phosphorylation. TMEM127 associated dynamically with the endomembrane system, including early endosomes, Golgi, and lysosomes, and colocalized with perinuclear, amino acid-activated mTOR. The authors concluded that TMEM127 is a tumor suppressor that negatively regulates mTOR.

Yao et al. (2010) found that disease-associated TMEM127 mutations disrupted the intracellular distribution of the protein.

### Clinical Management

On the basis of their penetrance data, Toledo et al. (2015) recommended offering TMEM127 genetic testing to at-risk individuals at age 22 years, with annual clinical surveillance for mutation carriers. Bausch et al. (2017) recommended genetic testing of these newer genes in patients at high clinical risk who are negative for mutations in the classic genes. They also recommended regular, systematic whole-body investigation for gene-specific prevention and early detection.

### Population Genetics

Qin et al. (2010) found truncating germline TMEM127 mutations in approximately 30% of familial pheochromocytomas and about 3% of apparently sporadic pheochromocytomas without a known genetic cause. In a Japanese cohort, Takeichi et al. (2012) identified TMEM127 mutations in 2 of 74 (2.7%) patients who were negative for mutations in RET, VHL, SDHB, and SDHD.

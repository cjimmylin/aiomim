### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that MAGIS syndrome (MAGIS) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the GNAI2 gene (139360) on chromosome 3p21.

### Description

MAGIS syndrome is an inborn error of immunity caused by heterozygous germline activating mutations in the GNAI2 gene. The name is an acronym for midline malformations of the brain, anterior pituitary gland dysfunction, growth retardation, immunodysregulation/immunodeficiency, and skeletal defects. The phenotype is heterogeneous, and nearly all patients have both immune and nonimmune features (Lamborn et al., 2025).

### Clinical Features

Hamada et al. (2017) reported an individual with periventricular nodular heterotopia and intellectual disability who carried a de novo GNAI2 mutation.

Lamborn et al. (2025) reviewed MAGIS syndrome and summarized the nonimmune features in their original cohort of 20 patients from 18 families with GNAI2 mutations. Growth retardation was present in 90% (intrauterine growth retardation in 53% and postnatal short stature in 90%). IGF1 was low in 8 of 11 patients tested, and growth hormone deficiency was found in all 7 patients who were formally tested. Structural or functional defects of the anterior pituitary were present in 59%. Midline brain malformations, including agenesis or hypoplasia of the corpus callosum, hypoplasia of the cerebellum and pons, and Chiari I malformation, were found in 30%. Skeletal dysostoses were present in 85% and dysmorphism in 100%. The authors noted that rare features such as coloboma or subcortical band heterotopia should raise suspicion for MAGIS.

Lamborn et al. (2025) reported immune-mediated disease in 95% of the cohort, with immunodeficiency in 90% and immune dysregulation in 50% (systemic autoinflammation in 15%, autoimmunity in 35%, and splenomegaly in 35%). Infections were mainly sinopulmonary (respiratory tract 75%, middle ear 65%, sinuses 35%), cutaneous (25%), and invasive (meningitis 10%, bacteremia 20%, cutaneous abscess 15%). Autoimmune manifestations included autoimmune hemolytic anemia (20%), thrombocytopenia (10%), and psoriasis (10%). Hypogammaglobulinemia was present in 20% and HPV warts in 25%. Myelokathexis was not observed. Lymphocyte immunophenotyping typically showed low or normal T cells that often improved with age, a low naive-to-memory T-cell ratio, and low B cells with low serum IgM. Monocytes and neutrophils tended to be high or normal, and mitogen- and antigen-induced proliferation was normal or increased. One patient had an abnormal newborn SCID screen. Premature mortality in the original cohort was 10%.

Lamborn et al. (2025) described intrafamilial variability in family XIII, in which a proband (P13) and his father (P14) both carried an arg179-to-cys (R179C) mutation in GNAI2. At 2 years of age, P13 had intrauterine growth retardation, congenital heart valve abnormalities with hydrops fetalis, dysmorphism, hypospadias, severe congenital sensorineural hearing loss, neurodevelopmental delay, a small pons, a thin corpus callosum, and neonatal infections with pan-lymphocytopenia. His father had dysmorphism, osteoporosis, cryptorchidism, hearing loss, borderline intellectual disability, epilepsy, short stature, annular pancreas with bowel malrotation, recurrent infections, and celiac disease. ⚠︎

Lamborn et al. (2025) also described family XVII, in which 2 brothers (P18 and P19) carried an arg179-to-his (R179H) mutation in GNAI2. P18 had dysmorphism, cryptorchidism, inguinal hernia, pelvic kidney, short stature, arthritis, sensorineural hearing loss, recurrent infections, and dysgammaglobulinemia. His deceased brother had intrauterine growth retardation, short stature, neurodevelopmental delay, epilepsy, ataxia, autism, psychosis, mild splenomegaly, recurrent infections, dysgammaglobulinemia, and T-cell lymphopenia.

### Inheritance

Hamada et al. (2017) identified a de novo heterozygous GNAI2 mutation in an affected individual. Lamborn et al. (2025) observed full penetrance; most variants arose de novo, but transmission of the R179C mutation from father to son and the presence of the R179H mutation in 2 brothers were consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

In an individual with periventricular nodular heterotopia and intellectual disability, Hamada et al. (2017) identified a de novo heterozygous c.680C-T transition in the GNAI2 gene, resulting in an ala227-to-val (A227V) substitution, by whole-exome sequencing. GNAI2 encodes Galpha-i2.

Lamborn et al. (2025) noted that each of the 20 patients in their original cohort, who were of multiple ancestries, carried an extremely rare heterozygous missense variant in GNAI2. All variants were absent from population databases except R179H, which was found in 2 individuals in gnomAD v4.1.0 (MAF 0.000001281). Mutations recurred at thr182 (T182A, T182I, and T182P in 6 families) and at arg179 (R179H and R179C in 7 patients from 5 families). Most variants arose de novo. Functionally, the mutant proteins were constitutively active (see PATHOGENESIS).

### Pathogenesis

Lamborn et al. (2025) summarized that MAGIS mutant Galpha-i2 proteins bind GTP up to 20-fold faster and hydrolyze GTP up to 100-fold slower than wildtype, and that most are insensitive to RGS GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs), making them constitutively active. The R179H and R179C mutants were less severely activating, binding GTP more slowly and remaining GAP-sensitive, which the authors suggested explained their transmission through generations.

Lamborn et al. (2025) summarized that MAGIS T cells and neutrophils showed impaired chemotaxis and chemokine-induced calcium flux because mutant Galpha-i2 remains decoupled from G protein-coupled receptors, a mechanism functionally resembling CXCR4 signaling deficiency. Separately, mutant Galpha-i2 sequestered RASA2 and enhanced TCR-induced RAS, ERK/MAPK, and PI3K/AKT/S6 signaling, causing T-cell hyperresponsiveness that may underlie autoimmunity. Knockout of the adenylyl cyclases AC3 and AC7 in T cells did not reproduce these defects, suggesting that they were independent of cAMP.

### Clinical Management

Lamborn et al. (2025) reported a patient with MAGIS syndrome who underwent hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) from a fully matched unrelated donor and achieved full donor chimerism. The transplant eradicated granulomatous skin disease caused by vaccine-strain rubella and resolved recurrent infections; autoimmune hemolytic anemia remained in remission for more than 3 years, whereas nonimmune features were unaffected. In vitro, selective CRISPR deletion of the mutant allele or combined RAS/MAPK and PI3K/AKT inhibition corrected T-cell hyperresponsiveness.

### Animal Model

Hamada et al. (2017) found that acute knockdown of Galpha-i2 by in utero electroporation in mice caused delayed radial migration of excitatory neurons during corticogenesis, possibly because of impaired morphology; an RNAi-resistant Galpha-i2 rescued the migration defect. Silencing of Galpha-i2 did not affect axon elongation, dendritic arbor formation, or neurogenesis at the ventricular zone in vivo. Mice with acute Galpha-i2 knockdown had defects in social interaction, novelty recognition, and active avoidance learning, as well as increased anxiety. The authors suggested that impaired Galpha-i2 function might cause defects in neuronal function leading to neurodevelopmental disorders.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that susceptibility to severe invasive infections is caused by mutation in the IRAK2 gene (603304) on chromosome 3p25.3.

### Clinical Features

Amali et al. (2024) reported 2 patients who had sequential or repeated invasive infections. In the first patient, herpes simplex virus infection triggered hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH), and the patient also had tuberculosis. The second patient had Streptococcus pneumoniae bacteremia with candidemia.

### Molecular Genetics

Amali et al. (2024) identified novel mutations in the IRAK2 gene in 2 patients with sequential or repeated invasive infections (see CLINICAL FEATURES). The specific variants and their zygosity were not given in the evidence available. In functional studies, the IRAK2 variants impaired TRAF6 ubiquitination (or functional modification), which reduced TNF-alpha production. The authors proposed that IRAK2 may play a central role in host defense against a wide range of pathogens.

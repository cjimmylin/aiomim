### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that immune dysregulation with autoimmunity, autoinflammation, and immunodeficiency (IDAAI) is caused by homozygous mutation in the IRAK2 gene.

### Description

Immune dysregulation with autoimmunity, autoinflammation, and immunodeficiency is an autosomal recessive disorder with a broad clinical spectrum. Most patients have recurrent infections, autoantibody production, and gastrointestinal ulceration. On the basis of their main manifestations, patients can be grouped as immunodeficiency-predominant, autoimmunity-predominant (including systemic lupus erythematosus with lupus nephritis), or autoinflammation-predominant. Some patients have an elevated type I interferon signature (Fei et al., 2026).

### Nomenclature

Fei et al. (2026) called the disorder DIRAK2, for deficiency of IRAK2.

### Clinical Features

Fei et al. (2026) reported 12 patients from 11 unrelated, nonconsanguineous Chinese families. Both males and females were affected. Eleven patients had clinical disease and 1 (P4) was preclinical. Age at onset ranged from 1 month to adulthood. Six patients (50%) had recurrent infections that required hospitalization, with gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria, fungi, and viruses. Two patients (P1 and P2) had recurrent pulmonary infections and were diagnosed with primary immunodeficiency. P2 died at age 65 years of severe pneumonia and macrophage activation syndrome. Six patients (50%) had autoantibodies. Four of them were diagnosed with systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) with lupus nephritis, 1 had Hashimoto thyroiditis, and 1 had a progressively elevated type I interferon signature with rising antinuclear antibody titers. Renal biopsy in P3 showed electron-dense mesangial deposits and diffuse IgG deposition in the mesangial areas and capillary loops. Two patients mainly had gastrointestinal involvement, with abdominal pain, vomiting, and bloody stools. Endoscopy showed multiple ulcers throughout the digestive tract, and histopathology showed active or chronic inflammation, cryptitis, and crypt abscesses in the ileocecal region. P10 was diagnosed with juvenile idiopathic arthritis, with pain and restricted mobility in the left knee and hips. P12 was diagnosed with autoimmune encephalitis. The authors grouped the patients as immunodeficiency-predominant (P1 and P2), autoimmunity-predominant (P3-P7, P11, and P12), or autoinflammation-predominant (P8-P10).

### Biochemical Features

Fei et al. (2026) found that serum IFN-beta levels were significantly higher in 6 patients than in unaffected controls. Baseline phosphorylated IRF3 levels were markedly elevated in 3 patients. CD8+ T cells were expanded in the patients. After LPS stimulation, patient PBMCs showed upregulated interferon-alpha and interferon-gamma responses. Specific type I interferon pathway genes were upregulated in inflammatory monocytes.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of IDAAI in the families reported by Fei et al. (2026) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. In all 11 pedigrees, disease status segregated strictly with biallelic inheritance of the mutation, and all heterozygous carriers were unaffected. One homozygous individual was preclinical, which the authors suggested might reflect reduced penetrance.

### Molecular Genetics

In 2 patients with sequential or repeated invasive infections, Amali et al. (2024) identified novel mutations in the IRAK2 gene. One patient had herpes simplex virus-triggered hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis with tuberculosis. The other had Streptococcus pneumoniae bacteremia with candidemia. The abstract did not state the zygosity of the variants. The authors reported that the mutations compromised the ability to ubiquitinate the signal adaptor TRAF6, impairing TNF-alpha production.

Fei et al. (2026) performed whole-exome or whole-genome sequencing in 12 patients with IDAAI from 11 unrelated, nonconsanguineous Chinese families. All patients carried the same homozygous copy number variant in the IRAK2 gene, an in-frame deletion of exon 2 (IRAK2-delta-ex2). Sanger sequencing confirmed that the mutation was homozygous and that all patients shared the same genomic breakpoints. No other rare variants of uncertain significance, and no CNVs in genes previously associated with immune disorders, were found. The mutant transcript was expressed at levels comparable to wildtype. In HEK293T cells lacking IRAK2, the mutant protein did not interact with IRAK4 but still interacted with TRAF6. In a TLR4-expressing cell line, the mutant failed to activate NF-kappa-B and MAPK signaling. PBMCs from patients showed reduced induction of IL1-beta, IL6, and IL10 after stimulation with LPS or R848. The authors also showed that homozygous gnomAD variants with a CADD score above 25, except 1 frameshift variant, and the previously reported variants p.L78P and p.R506W preserved the ability to activate NF-kappa-B signaling. They classified these 2 previously reported variants as benign.

### Pathogenesis

Fei et al. (2026) noted that the region deleted in the IRAK2 mutant lies at the interface with IRAK4 in the Myddosome complex. In patient PBMCs, the defects in cytokine secretion and NF-kappa-B signaling after LPS stimulation were most pronounced in monocytes and dendritic cells. When IRAK2-knockout THP-1 cells were reconstituted, cells expressing wildtype IRAK2 had less interferon signaling than cells expressing empty vector or the deletion mutant. Inhibiting TIRAP or TRIF, or knocking down TRIF, suppressed the aberrant interferon response. Inhibiting TBK1/IKK-epsilon reduced it, and inhibiting TAK1 partly attenuated it. The authors suggested that type I interferon signaling is amplified through the TRIF-dependent axis, with secondary modulation by the NF-kappa-B and MAPK pathways.

### Clinical Management

Fei et al. (2026) reported that P1 and P2 were treated with antibiotics and IVIG for recurrent infections. Most patients received immunosuppressive therapy, including glucocorticoids, hydroxychloroquine, cyclophosphamide, tacrolimus or sirolimus, mycophenolate mofetil or azathioprine, belimumab or telitacicept, infliximab, and methotrexate. PBMCs from 2 untreated patients had a progressively elevated type I interferon signature, which was attenuated by baricitinib ex vivo. Baricitinib also downregulated type I interferon signaling in IRAK2-knockout THP-1 cells. The authors stated that further studies were needed to determine safety and efficacy.

### Population Genetics

Fei et al. (2026) noted that the IRAK2 exon 2 deletion is recorded in gnomAD SVs v4.1.0 at a total population frequency of 0.0005631. It is more frequent in East Asians, with a frequency of 0.01431 and 2 homozygous individuals. The authors noted that the genomic region is a recognized hotspot for Alu-mediated homologous recombination.

### Animal Model

Fei et al. (2026) generated knockin mice homozygous for deletion of Irak2 exon 2. After intraperitoneal injection of high-dose LPS, the mutant mice had a higher survival rate at 7 days and lower serum cytokine and chemokine levels. After intraperitoneal E. coli infection, they had impaired bacterial clearance. Bone marrow-derived macrophages from the mice showed defects in several Toll-like receptor pathways, while Tlr3 signaling remained intact. These macrophages showed increased type I interferon signaling. At 50 weeks of age, the mutant mice did not differ from wildtype littermates in body weight, spleen index, autoantibody profiles, serum cytokines, tissue inflammation, or kidney histology. The authors noted that mice express 4 Irak2 isoforms, 2 of which naturally lack exon 2.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 IMMUNE DYSREGULATION WITH AUTOIMMUNITY, AUTOINFLAMMATION, AND IMMUNODEFICIENCY**  
IRAK2, EX2DEL [NC_000003.12:g.10176864_10180995delinsTGGA; NM_001570.4:c.95_277del; p.Ala32_Trp93delinsGly]

In 12 patients with IDAAI from 11 unrelated, nonconsanguineous Chinese families, Fei et al. (2026) identified a homozygous copy number variant in the IRAK2 gene. The variant is a 4-bp insertion combined with a deletion that removes exon 2 and parts of the flanking introns (g.10176864_10180995delinsTGGA, NC_000003.12). At the transcript level this is c.95_277del (NM_001570.4), an in-frame deletion predicted to replace ala32 through trp93 with glycine (p.Ala32_Trp93delinsGly). The mutation was found by whole-exome or whole-genome sequencing and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. It segregated with the disease in all pedigrees, and heterozygous carriers were unaffected. The mutant protein did not interact with IRAK4 and failed to activate NF-kappa-B and MAPK signaling. The variant is recorded in gnomAD SVs at a frequency of 0.01431 in the East Asian population.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital nonprogressive movement disorder with ataxia and eye movement abnormalities (CONMAE) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the ESRRG gene (602969) on chromosome 1q41.

### Description

Congenital nonprogressive movement disorder with ataxia and eye movement abnormalities (CONMAE) is an autosomal dominant disorder. Its features include motor developmental delay, muscular hypotonia, eye movement disorders, and congenital ataxia or gait imbalance. Some patients also have joint hyperflexibility, dysarthria, myopia, and growth delay (Bresack et al., 2025).

### Clinical Features

Bresack et al. (2025) described 8 individuals from 7 unrelated families with heterozygous ESRRG variants and a congenital movement disorder. All of them had motor developmental delay, muscular hypotonia, eye movement disorders, and congenital ataxia or gait imbalance. Other features included joint hyperflexibility, dysarthria, myopia, and growth delay. The authors described the disorder as nonprogressive.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous ESRRG variants in the patients reported by Bresack et al. (2025) occurred mostly de novo. This pattern is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance, and the authors implicated ESRRG in a dominant disorder.

### Molecular Genetics

Bresack et al. (2025) studied 8 individuals with CONMAE from 7 unrelated families. They identified heterozygous, mostly de novo variants in the ESRRG gene: 5 missense variants (G137E, K149R, C180Y, R184C, and L449R) and a 1-bp duplication (c.1352dup) predicted to cause a frameshift (Leu451PhefsTer38). All of the variants were absent from the gnomAD database (v4.1), were predicted in silico to be deleterious, and lay in intolerant segments of the protein. Molecular modeling suggested that the variants reduce protein function. The authors noted that ESRRG has the highest mutational constraint of the ESRR paralogs.

Knocking out ESRRG in HEK293T cells significantly increased cell proliferation. Overexpressing wildtype ESRRG restored normal proliferation, but overexpressing the patient variants did not. All of the variants localized correctly to the nucleus, but each one significantly reduced transcriptional activity in an ERR response element (ERRE) reporter assay (Bresack et al., 2025).

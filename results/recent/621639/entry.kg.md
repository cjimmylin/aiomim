### Description

Congenital nonprogressive movement disorder with ataxia and eye movement abnormalities (CONMAE) is a nonprogressive congenital movement disorder. Its main features are motor developmental delay, muscular hypotonia, eye movement disorders, and congenital ataxia or gait imbalance. Some patients also have joint hyperflexibility, dysarthria, myopia, and growth delay (Bresack et al., 2025).

### Clinical Features

Bresack et al. (2025) described 8 individuals from 7 unrelated families with a nonprogressive congenital movement disorder with ataxia. All of them had motor developmental delay, muscular hypotonia, eye movement disorders, and congenital ataxia or gait imbalance. Other features included joint hyperflexibility, dysarthria, myopia, and growth delay.

### Inheritance

Bresack et al. (2025) found heterozygous ESRRG mutations in the affected individuals from 7 families, most of them de novo. This is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Bresack et al. (2025) identified heterozygous mutations in the ESRRG gene in 8 individuals from 7 unrelated families with a nonprogressive congenital movement disorder with ataxia. Most of the mutations occurred de novo. The reported mutations included 5 missense mutations: G137E, K149R, C180Y, R184C, and L449R. The sixth was a 1-bp duplication (c.1352dup) that caused a frameshift (L451Ffs*38). None of the variants was present in gnomAD v4.1. All were predicted to be deleterious in silico and were located in intolerant segments of the protein.

In functional studies, Bresack et al. (2025) found that all of the variant proteins localized correctly to the nucleus. However, each significantly reduced transcriptional activity in an ERRE reporter assay. Knockout of ESRRG significantly increased the proliferation rate of HEK293T cells. Wild-type ESRRG restored normal proliferation in the knockout cells, but none of the patient variants did. Molecular modeling also suggested that the variants reduce protein function.

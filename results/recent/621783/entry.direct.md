### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spermatogenic failure-105 (SPGF105) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the ADGB gene, which encodes androglobin.

### Description

Spermatogenic failure-105 (SPGF105) is a form of male infertility. Affected men have asthenozoospermia or asthenoteratozoospermia, and some also have oligozoospermia. Their sperm show multiple malformations of the acrosome and flagellum. Fertilization and pregnancy have been achieved with intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) (Qu et al., 2023; Gao et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2026). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Qu et al. (2023) reported an infertile man with asthenozoospermia.

Gao et al. (2025) reported 3 infertile men from unrelated Han Chinese families who had severe asthenoteratozoospermia. On light and electron microscopy, the patients' spermatozoa showed multiple malformations of the acrosome and flagellum.

Wang et al. (2026) reported a Chinese man with primary infertility and severe idiopathic oligoasthenoteratozoospermia. His sperm concentration was 2.0 x 10(6)/mL, progressive motility was less than 1%, vitality was 16%, and none of his sperm had normal morphology. Head abnormalities were present in 76.5% of sperm, neck and midpiece abnormalities in 14%, and flagellar abnormalities in 62.5%. His reproductive endocrine parameters were normal, his karyotype was 46,XY, and he had no Y-chromosome microdeletions. The head abnormalities included microcephalic and conical heads, and the tail defects included short tails, acutely angled curved tails, coiled tails, and tailless sperm. Transmission electron microscopy showed loss of the acrosome, disorganized helical structure of the mitochondrial sheath, and disrupted or partially absent 9+2 axonemal microtubules.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of SPGF105 is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Gao et al. (2025) and Wang et al. (2026) found homozygous and compound heterozygous ADGB mutations in affected men. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Qu et al. (2023) identified variants in the ADGB gene in an infertile man with asthenozoospermia. The variants disrupted binding of ADGB to calmodulin.

Gao et al. (2025) performed whole-exome sequencing in a cohort of 105 Han Chinese men with idiopathic asthenoteratozoospermia and found biallelic ADGB variants in 3 infertile men from unrelated families. One man was homozygous for a frameshift mutation (c.2801_2802del; K934Rfs*33). A second man was homozygous for a missense mutation (c.3167C-T; T1056I). The third man was compound heterozygous for T1056I and a c.3197C-T mutation (A1066V). Sanger sequencing confirmed the variants. They were rare in the general population, and multiple bioinformatics tools predicted them to be damaging. Functional assays associated the sperm structural defects with dysregulation of ADGB and of multiple spermatogenesis proteins. The patients' spermatozoa expressed PLC-zeta normally but were deficient in calmodulin.

Wang et al. (2026) performed whole-exome sequencing in 781 Chinese men with primary infertility and identified 148 ADGB variants. Under ACMG criteria, 6 were classified as likely pathogenic. Two of these, c.4859G-A (R1620Q) and c.4981A-G (K1661E), were present in compound heterozygous state in a man with severe oligoasthenoteratozoospermia, as confirmed by Sanger sequencing. In gnomAD v4 East Asians, the allele frequency was 0.00285 for c.4859G-A and 0.0006 for c.4981A-G. Arg1620 was conserved across vertebrates, whereas Lys1661 was conserved in mammals. Western blot analysis showed that ADGB protein was absent from the patient's sperm. Immunofluorescence showed reduced ADGB and PNA staining, as well as reduced levels of TTC29 and CFAP47. Coimmunoprecipitation in sperm from healthy volunteers showed that TTC29 and CFAP47 interacted with ADGB.

### Clinical Management

Gao et al. (2025) reported that ICSI resulted in successful fertilization in all 3 men with ADGB mutations. They suggested that ICSI, rather than conventional in vitro fertilization, is the optimal treatment for ADGB-deficient patients.

Wang et al. (2026) reported that 36 oocytes were retrieved from the partner of their patient in the first ICSI cycle. The normal 2-pronuclear fertilization rate was 70%, with no polyspermy. Transfer of a single frozen-thawed blastocyst led to a pregnancy that was carried to term.

### Animal Model

Qu et al. (2023) found that male Adgb -/- mice were infertile because of reduced sperm concentration (less than 1 x 10(6)/mL) and reduced sperm motility. Spermatogenesis was abnormal, with malformation of both elongating and elongated spermatids. The cauda epididymis contained about twice as many apoptotic cells as normal. ICSI with testicular spermatids resulted in fertilization and development to the blastocyst stage. Mass spectrometry identified 42 candidate ADGB-interacting proteins involved in sperm assembly, flagellum formation, and sperm motility, and binding of ADGB to CFAP69 and SPEF2 was confirmed.

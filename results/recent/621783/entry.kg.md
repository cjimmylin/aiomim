### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spermatogenic failure-105 (SPGF105) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the ADGB gene (614630) on chromosome 6q24.3.

### Description

Spermatogenic failure-105 (SPGF105) is a form of male infertility. Affected men have reduced sperm motility and abnormal sperm morphology, described as asthenoteratozoospermia or oligoasthenoteratozoospermia. Their sperm show malformations of both the acrosome and the flagellum (Gao et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2026). Intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) has led to fertilization in affected men (Gao et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2026).

### Clinical Features

Qu et al. (2023) described an infertile man with asthenozoospermia.

Gao et al. (2025) described 3 unrelated infertile Han Chinese men with severe asthenoteratozoospermia. Light and electron microscopy showed multiple malformations of the acrosome and flagellum in their spermatozoa.

Wang et al. (2026) described a Chinese man with primary infertility and severe idiopathic oligoasthenoteratozoospermia. His sperm concentration was 2.0 x 10(6)/mL, progressive motility was below 1%, and vitality was 16%. None of his sperm had normal morphology: 76.5% had abnormal heads and 62.5% had abnormal flagella. His reproductive hormones were normal, his karyotype was 46,XY, and he had no Y chromosome microdeletion. Papanicolaou staining showed head defects, including microcephalic and conical heads, and tail defects, including short tails, acutely angled bent tails, coiled tails, and tailless sperm. Transmission electron microscopy showed acrosome defects, a disorganized helix of the mitochondrial sheath, and disorganized or missing '9+2' axonemal microtubules.

### Other Features

Wang et al. (2026) found that ADGB expression in human testis was low before puberty and peaked in adulthood. In both human and mouse spermatogenic cells, ADGB localized to the acrosome and the flagellum. In mice, Adgb mRNA was highly expressed in adult testis and was expressed at low levels in lung and ovary. Testicular expression peaked at postnatal day 40.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of SPGF105 in the patients reported by Gao et al. (2025) and Wang et al. (2026) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Affected men carried homozygous or compound heterozygous ADGB mutations. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Qu et al. (2023) identified variants in the ADGB gene in an infertile man with asthenozoospermia. The variants disrupted the binding of ADGB to calmodulin.

Gao et al. (2025) performed whole-exome sequencing in a cohort of 105 Han Chinese men with idiopathic asthenoteratozoospermia and identified ADGB mutations in 3 unrelated infertile men. One man was homozygous for a 2-bp deletion (c.2801_2802del), which caused a frameshift (p.K934Rfs*33); this mutation was confirmed by Sanger sequencing. A second man was homozygous for a missense mutation (c.3167C-T; T1056I). The third man was compound heterozygous for the T1056I mutation and a second missense mutation (c.3197C-T; A1066V). All of the variants were rare in the general population and were predicted to be damaging. In functional assays of the patients' sperm, the structural defects were associated with dysregulation of ADGB and of multiple spermatogenesis proteins. PLC-zeta was expressed normally, but calmodulin was deficient. ⚠︎

Wang et al. (2026) performed whole-exome sequencing in 781 Chinese men with primary infertility and identified 148 ADGB variants. Using ACMG criteria, they classified 45 as benign, 1 as likely benign, 96 as of uncertain significance, and 6 as likely pathogenic. Two of the likely pathogenic missense variants were found in the same man, who had oligoasthenoteratozoospermia. He was compound heterozygous for c.4859G-A (R1620Q) and c.4981A-G (K1661E), and both mutations were confirmed by Sanger sequencing. Western blot showed that ADGB protein was absent from the patient's sperm. On immunofluorescence, the patient's sperm showed clearly reduced ADGB and reduced staining for PNA, an acrosome marker. In control sperm, ADGB was enriched in the acrosome and in the flagellar mitochondrial sheath. AlphaFold3 modeling predicted that the 2 mutations alter the conformation of an alpha-helix and of a C-terminal loop, respectively. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Qu et al. (2023) used mass spectrometry to identify 42 candidate ADGB-interacting proteins that are involved in sperm assembly, flagella formation, and sperm motility. They confirmed that CFAP69 and SPEF2 bind to ADGB.

Using STRING analysis, Wang et al. (2026) identified the axoneme assembly proteins TTC29 and CFAP47 as candidate ADGB partners. Coimmunoprecipitation confirmed that both proteins interact with ADGB in sperm, and levels of both were clearly reduced in their patient's sperm. The authors suggested that ADGB may maintain acrosome function and flagellar axoneme assembly by binding TTC29 and CFAP47.

### Clinical Management

Gao et al. (2025) reported that ICSI led to successful fertilization in all 3 of their patients. Taking this result together with the calmodulin deficiency in the patients' sperm, the authors suggested that ICSI rather than in vitro fertilization is the best treatment for ADGB-deficient patients. ⚠︎

Wang et al. (2026) reported that their patient's partner conceived with ICSI. Thirty-six oocytes were retrieved, and the normal 2PN fertilization rate was 70%. Transfer of a single frozen-thawed blastocyst led to a full-term delivery. The authors noted that all 5 reported couples with ADGB variants had achieved clinical pregnancy with ICSI.

### Animal Model

Qu et al. (2023) found that male Adgb -/- mice were infertile because of reduced sperm motility and a reduced sperm concentration (less than 1 x 10(6)/mL). Spermatogenesis was abnormal, with malformed elongating and elongated spermatids, and the cauda epididymis contained about twice as many apoptotic cells. ICSI with testicular spermatids led to fertilization and development to the blastocyst stage.

**.0004 SPERMATOGENIC FAILURE 105**  
ADGB, ARG1620GLN [c.4859G>A; p.R1620Q]

Wang et al. (2026) studied a Chinese man with primary infertility due to severe oligoasthenoteratozoospermia (SPGF105). Using whole-exome sequencing, they identified compound heterozygous mutations in the ADGB gene and confirmed them by Sanger sequencing. One was a c.4859G-A transition (NM_024694.4), resulting in an arg1620-to-gln (R1620Q) substitution at a residue that is absolutely conserved across vertebrates. The other was K1661E. The R1620Q variant had frequencies of 0.0010 in 1000 Genomes, 0.0001 in ExAC, and 0.00285 in gnomAD. SIFT, PolyPhen-2, and CADD (score 28.2) predicted it to be damaging. ADGB protein was absent from the patient's sperm on Western blot. AlphaFold3 modeling predicted that R1620Q alters the conformation of an alpha-helix.

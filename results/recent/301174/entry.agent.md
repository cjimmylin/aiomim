### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that neurodevelopmental disorder with or without macrocephaly (NEDMAC) is caused by hemizygous or heterozygous mutation in the SMARCA1 gene on chromosome X.

### Description

Neurodevelopmental disorder with or without macrocephaly (NEDMAC) is an X-linked disorder with a broad clinical spectrum. Its most frequent features are mild to severe intellectual disability or developmental delay and delayed or regressive speech development. Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) features, other behavioral difficulties, facial dysmorphism, and other variable features also occur, and macrocephaly is present in about half of patients. Most affected individuals are hemizygous males. Heterozygous females are also affected and often have a milder phenotype (Mirzaa et al., 2025).

### Clinical Features

Karaca et al. (2015) reported a male patient (BAB4453) with microcephaly, spasticity, and intellectual disability. His dysmorphic features were very similar to those of Coffin-Siris syndrome, including a coarse face, bushy eyebrows, facial hypertrichosis, and long eyelashes. Brain imaging showed severe cortical atrophy. His family history was negative. ⚠︎

Lopes et al. (2016) studied 19 Portuguese patients (16 girls and 3 boys) whose clinical presentation overlapped significantly with Rett syndrome. One of these patients had a de novo variant in SMARCA1.

Nishimura et al. (2025) reported 2 unrelated males with X-linked intellectual disability. Patient 1 had macrocephaly, behavioral difficulty, and learning disability. Patient 2 had ataxia and speech delay.

Mirzaa et al. (2025) reported 35 individuals (26 males and 9 females) from 26 families. Ages at last clinical assessment ranged from 2 to 49 years. The index family spanned 6 generations and included 4 individuals with mild to severe intellectual disability or developmental delay and brain overgrowth. Across the cohort, the most common features were intellectual disability or developmental delay (77%; 27/35) and speech delay and/or speech regression (83%; 29/35); in 4 individuals speech regression was noted rather than delay. Seventeen of 35 individuals (49%) had increased head circumference (OFC at least +2 SD) and/or reported macrocephaly or brain overgrowth. Increased height (at least +2 SD) was reported in 29%. Other features included: - motor delay (20/34) - seizures or epilepsy (8/35) - hypotonia (15/35) - behavioral difficulties such as ASD, ADHD, or aggression (19/35); in some instances (9/25) these were linked to regression in speech and mental development - an ASD diagnosis (11/35) Dysmorphic facial features were reported in 19 of 35 individuals. Photographs of 7 patients showed frontal bossing, a long face, slanted palpebral fissures, a flat nasal root with a short bulbous nose, anteverted nares, and a thin upper lip. In the index family, the proband had an OFC of +6 SD, severe intellectual disability, no language, ASD, hydrocephalus treated with a shunt, hepatosplenomegaly, and height and weight of +5 SD. The 2 heterozygous carrier mothers in this family, aged 45 and 49 years, had OFCs of +1 SD and +1.5 SD and no reported learning disability.

### Biochemical Features

Mirzaa et al. (2025) performed genome-wide DNA methylation analysis on peripheral blood DNA from 19 individuals with SMARCA1 variants. Compared with 57 matched controls, the samples showed a predominantly hypomethylated profile defined by 232 probes. Separate profiles were also found for males and for females. The authors concluded that this mild global methylation profile, shared by patients with truncating variants and overgrowth, was not robust enough to be considered an epi-signature.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of NEDMAC is X-linked, according to Mirzaa et al. (2025). Among the 26 index cases they reported, 17 had a maternally inherited variant and 4 had de novo variants. Segregation was not determined in the remaining 5, 4 of whom were presumed to have maternally inherited variants. Affected individuals included both hemizygous males and heterozygous females. Nishimura et al. (2025) found that the variants in their 2 affected males were inherited from unaffected healthy mothers.

### Molecular Genetics

Karaca et al. (2015) performed whole-exome sequencing in a cohort of mostly consanguineous families with neurogenetic disorders. In a male patient with microcephaly, spasticity, and intellectual disability, they identified a hemizygous null variant in the SMARCA1 gene, c.7C-T (Q3X).

Lopes et al. (2016) performed array CGH and whole-exome sequencing in 19 Portuguese patients with Rett syndrome-like phenotypes. They identified a de novo variant in SMARCA1, which they considered a novel candidate gene for neurodevelopmental disorders.

Riazuddin et al. (2017) performed exome sequencing in consanguineous Pakistani families with intellectual disability. In a Punjabi family (PKMR142), they identified a missense variant in SMARCA1, c.79G-A (E27K; NM_139035.2), and listed it among novel candidate genes for intellectual disability.

Nishimura et al. (2025) used exome sequencing to identify hemizygous SMARCA1 variants in 2 unrelated males with X-linked intellectual disability. Patient 1 had a nonsense variant (Q599X) leading to nonsense-mediated decay. Patient 2 had a missense variant (R448L), which structural modeling suggested might destabilize interactions between SMARCA1 and nucleosomal DNA. Both variants were inherited from unaffected mothers.

In a 6-generation family with 4 affected individuals, Mirzaa et al. (2025) used exome sequencing to identify a hemizygous nonsense mutation in the SMARCA1 gene (R91X). Further families were ascertained in part through the MatchMaker Exchange network, giving a total of 35 individuals from 26 families with apparent non-mosaic hemizygous or heterozygous SMARCA1 variants. Variants were identified by trio-based exome sequencing in 23 families and by whole-genome sequencing in 3 families. There were 25 unique variants, and a second unrelated family carried the same variant as the index family. The variants comprised 7 nonsense variants, 3 frameshift indels, and 15 missense variants. All were predicted to be deleterious by multiple algorithms. Sixteen of the 25 lay within the ATPase domain (12) or the HSS domain (4). In transfected U2OS cells, 4 patient variants (R253X, R259G, R751Q, and E771K) each produced a stable protein that localized to the nucleus. The authors also identified SMARCA1 variants in 7 additional families that they considered likely non-causative or not explanatory. These included V384M, found in 3 families; it had previously been reported in a family with schizophrenia and is present in gnomAD, including in 7 hemizygotes.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Mirzaa et al. (2025) found macrocephaly in 70% of individuals with truncating variants. However, 3 individuals with truncating variants did not have brain overgrowth, and 4 individuals with missense variants did. The authors therefore concluded that macrocephaly is not restricted to individuals with putative loss-of-function variants. Individuals in the DECIPHER database with CNV deletions encompassing SMARCA1 did not have macrocephaly. Of the 9 affected females, 7 had truncating variants; 3 of these had macrocephaly and 4 did not. Macrocephaly was discordant in 2 mother-daughter pairs. The authors proposed that variants associated with macrocephaly act in a dominant-negative fashion and that variants without macrocephaly act as functional hypomorphs.

### Animal Model

Mirzaa et al. (2025) found that Smarca1 knockout mice were viable with no obvious gross developmental defects. Their brains showed only slight size reductions and no deficits in cortical progenitors or lamination. In behavioral testing, the knockout mice were more active than wildtype mice in the dark but otherwise showed no significant differences. In contrast, Smarca1ex6del mice, which make an internally truncated protein, had a total brain area 10% larger than controls. This enlargement was reduced when Smarca5 was also ablated. In Smarca1 knockout cerebellum, SMARCA5 co-immunoprecipitated with CECR2, suggesting that SMARCA5 compensated for the loss of SMARCA1.

**.0003 NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER WITH OR WITHOUT MACROCEPHALY**  
SMARCA1, ARG448LEU [NM_003069.5:c.1343G>T; p.Arg448Leu]

In a male patient with X-linked intellectual disability, ataxia, and speech delay, Nishimura et al. (2025) identified a hemizygous c.1343G-T transversion in the SMARCA1 gene, resulting in an arg448-to-leu (R448L) substitution. **.0004 NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER WITH OR WITHOUT MACROCEPHALY**  
SMARCA1, ARG91TER [NM_003069.4:c.271C>T; p.Arg91Ter]

Mirzaa et al. (2025) used exome sequencing in a 6-generation family to identify a c.271C-T transition in the SMARCA1 gene, resulting in an arg91-to-ter (R91X) substitution. The variant was hemizygous in the proband, his brother, and 2 maternal cousins. All 4 had intellectual disability or developmental delay and brain overgrowth, with OFCs ranging from +4.3 SD to +6 SD. Both mothers of the affected individuals were heterozygous for the variant; they had OFCs of +1 SD and +1.5 SD and no reported learning disability. The paternal grandfather and a maternal uncle did not carry the variant. The same variant was maternally inherited in an unrelated 6-year-old boy with macrocephaly, ASD, and ADHD.

**.0005 NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER WITH OR WITHOUT MACROCEPHALY**  
SMARCA1, ARG189TER [NM_003069.4:c.565C>T; p.Arg189Ter]

In a 5-year-old boy, Mirzaa et al. (2025) identified a de novo c.565C-T transition in the SMARCA1 gene, resulting in an arg189-to-ter (R189X) substitution. The boy had an OFC of +5.6 SD, motor and speech delay, intellectual disability, ADHD, and dysmorphic facial features.

**.0006 NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER WITH OR WITHOUT MACROCEPHALY**  
SMARCA1, ARG253TER [NM_003069.4:c.757C>T; p.Arg253Ter]

Mirzaa et al. (2025) identified a maternally inherited c.757C-T transition in the SMARCA1 gene, resulting in an arg253-to-ter (R253X) substitution, in a family (family 9). The 4-year-4-month-old boy had an OFC of +5.9 SD, speech delay, mild intellectual disability, and tall stature. His 7-year-4-month-old sister also carried the variant. She had an OFC of -0.4 SD and was much more severely affected than her brother, with severe vision issues, feeding difficulties, laryngeal palsy, and facial palsy. Their carrier mother had an OFC of +5.6 SD, a mild learning disability, and tall stature. In transfected U2OS cells, the R253X variant generated a stable 35-kD truncated protein, at a somewhat lower level than the other variants tested. This protein was also present in the cytoplasm.

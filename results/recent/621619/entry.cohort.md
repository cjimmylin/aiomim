### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spermatogenic failure-103 (SPGF103) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the MEI1 gene (608797) on chromosome 22q13.2. ⚠︎

### Description

Spermatogenic failure-103 (SPGF103) is an autosomal recessive form of male infertility. Affected men have nonobstructive azoospermia, with spermatogenesis arrested at the spermatocyte stage, or cryptozoospermia. In 1 consanguineous family, a female family member with the same homozygous MEI1 mutation had infertility with embryonic arrest.

### Clinical Features

Chen et al. (2023) reported 2 infertile Chinese brothers (III:2 and III:3). The proband (III:2) was a 30-year-old man with normal external genitalia and normal serum sex hormone levels. A smear of centrifuged semen sediment showed a sperm count of 0 to 1 cells per high-power field, which indicated cryptozoospermia. In his brother (III:3), several semen tests found no sperm in the initial semen smear. After centrifugation, however, 0 to 1 motile sperm were occasionally seen, which also indicated cryptozoospermia.

Zhang et al. (2023) studied 4 Chinese men with nonobstructive azoospermia and male infertility. Testicular pathology and immunohistochemical staining showed spermatogenesis arrested at the spermatocyte stage.

Liu et al. (2025) described 2 infertile patients from 1 consanguineous family: a man with azoospermia and a woman with embryonic arrest. The arrested embryos showed high rates of chromosomal anomalies.

### Inheritance

SPGF103 is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. In the family reported by Chen et al. (2023), each parent of the 2 affected brothers was a heterozygous carrier of one of the MEI1 variants, consistent with recessive inheritance. Liu et al. (2025) found a homozygous MEI1 mutation in affected members of a consanguineous family; their mother was a heterozygous carrier.

### Molecular Genetics

Malcher et al. (2022) performed whole-genome sequencing in 39 men with nonobstructive azoospermia and identified candidate variants in 29 of them. Among these candidates, 1 patient was compound heterozygous for 2 missense variants in the MEI1 gene: c.1546A-G (I516V) and c.3542G-A (R1181Q). The authors did not assign causality to these variants.

In 2 Chinese brothers with SPGF103, Chen et al. (2023) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the MEI1 gene. One was a splice site mutation (c.529+5G-A) downstream of exon 5. The other was a c.2071C-G transversion in exon 18, resulting in a gln691-to-glu (Q691E) substitution. Sanger sequencing confirmed the mutations in both brothers. Their father was heterozygous for the c.2071C-G mutation and their mother was heterozygous for the c.529+5G-A mutation. Neither variant was present in the ExAC or gnomAD databases. varSEAK classified the splice variant as class 5, likely to affect splicing, and several prediction programs predicted the missense variant to be deleterious. Functional studies could not be done because too few sperm were available and no testicular tissue was obtained.

By whole-exome sequencing, Zhang et al. (2023) identified biallelic MEI1 mutations in 4 Chinese men with nonobstructive azoospermia. The mutations were c.186G-C, c.251delT, and c.1585T-A. In vitro, the c.186G-C missense mutation reduced MEI1 expression and the c.251delT frameshift mutation produced a truncated MEI1 protein. Based on bioinformatic analysis, the authors suggested that the c.1585T-A missense mutation affects the interaction of MEI1 with its partners.

In a man with azoospermia and a woman with embryonic arrest from a consanguineous family, Liu et al. (2025) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a homozygous deletion of the whole of exon 19 of the MEI1 gene. Their mother was a heterozygous carrier. cDNA sequencing confirmed the deletion, and exon 19 expression was almost undetectable in the patients. On Western blot of transfected HEK293T cells, the deletion produced a truncated MEI1 protein, consistent with premature termination at exon 20.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Chen et al. (2023) suggested that the combination of a splice variant and a missense variant in MEI1 may allow slight MEI1 expression, so that meiosis can sometimes proceed. This could explain why the 2 brothers they studied had cryptozoospermia rather than the nonobstructive azoospermia seen with other MEI1 variants.

### Pathogenesis

In testicular tissue from patients with biallelic MEI1 mutations, Zhang et al. (2023) found defective homeostasis of programmed DNA double-strand breaks and defective meiotic chromosome synapsis. They concluded that these defects lead to meiotic arrest and male infertility.

On preimplantation genetic testing, Liu et al. (2025) found high rates of chromosomal anomalies in arrested embryos with an MEI1 mutation. They suggested that embryonic aneuploidy may underlie the reduced developmental potential and the embryonic arrest.

### Animal Model

Nguyen et al. (2018) found that 8% of oocytes from Mei1-deficient female mice lost all of their chromosomes by extruding them, together with the spindles, into the first polar body. Mei1 -/- oocytes could be fertilized, and 5% produced androgenetic zygotes.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spermatogenic failure-103 (SPGF103) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the MEI1 gene (608797) on chromosome 22q13.2. ⚠︎

### Description

Spermatogenic failure-103 (SPGF103) is an autosomal recessive form of male infertility. Affected men have nonobstructive azoospermia, with spermatogenesis arrested at the spermatocyte stage (Zhang et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2025), or cryptozoospermia (Chen et al., 2023). In one consanguineous family, an affected female relative was infertile because her embryos arrested (Liu et al., 2025).

### Clinical Features

Chen et al. (2023) reported 2 Chinese brothers who were infertile because of cryptozoospermia. The proband was a 30-year-old man with 5 years of infertility. He had normal external genitalia, bilateral testicular volumes of about 12 ml, and normal semen volume and pH. No sperm were seen in the initial semen smear. In the centrifuged sediment there were 0 to 1 sperm per high-power field (10 to 15 sperm in 20 HPF), few of them motile. His serum FSH, LH, testosterone, estradiol, prolactin, inhibin B, and AMH were normal. His karyotype was 46,XY, and he had no Y-chromosome AZF microdeletions. His brother had 3 years of infertility and also had cryptozoospermia. The brother's physical examination, hormone levels, karyotype, and Y-microdeletion testing were normal. ⚠︎

Zhang et al. (2023) described 4 Chinese men with nonobstructive azoospermia (NOA). Testicular pathology and immunohistochemical staining showed that spermatogenesis was arrested at the spermatocyte stage. The patients also had defective homeostasis of programmed DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) and defective meiotic chromosome synapsis.

Liu et al. (2025) described a consanguineous family in which an infertile male had azoospermia and an infertile female had embryonic arrest. Preimplantation genetic testing of the female patient's arrested embryos showed high levels of chromosomal anomalies.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of SPGF103 is autosomal recessive. In the 2 affected brothers reported by Chen et al. (2023), each parent was a heterozygous carrier of one of the MEI1 variants. In the consanguineous family reported by Liu et al. (2025), the affected individuals were homozygous for an MEI1 deletion and their mother was a heterozygous carrier.

### Molecular Genetics

Malcher et al. (2022) performed whole-genome sequencing in 39 Polish men with NOA. All 39 had a normal karyotype and none had Y-chromosome microdeletions or CFTR mutations. The authors filtered variants under a recessive model at a gnomAD variant allele frequency below 0.01 and identified candidate variants in 29 of the 39 men. One of these men was compound heterozygous for 2 variants in the MEI1 gene, c.1546A-G and c.3542G-A. ⚠︎

In 2 Chinese brothers with cryptozoospermia, Chen et al. (2023) used whole-exome sequencing, with Sanger confirmation, to identify compound heterozygous variants in the MEI1 gene. One was a c.529+5G-A splice site variant downstream of exon 5 that was predicted to affect splicing. The other was a c.2071C-G transversion in exon 18 resulting in a gln691-to-glu (Q691E) substitution. Neither variant was present in ExAC or gnomAD. The father, who had normal fertility, was heterozygous for the missense variant, and the mother was heterozygous for the splice variant. Testicular tissue and sperm were not available for functional study.

Zhang et al. (2023) used whole-exome sequencing to identify biallelic deleterious MEI1 variants in 4 Chinese men with NOA and meiotic arrest. The variants included a c.186G-C missense variant, which reduced MEI1 expression in vitro, and a c.251delT frameshift variant, which produced truncated MEI1 protein in vitro. They also included a c.1585T-A missense variant, which bioinformatic analysis suggested would affect the interaction of MEI1 with its partners. The authors concluded that biallelic MEI1 variants could disrupt DSB homeostasis and meiotic chromosome synapsis, leading to meiotic arrest and male infertility.

In a consanguineous family, Liu et al. (2025) studied a man with azoospermia and a woman with embryonic arrest. Using whole-exome sequencing with Sanger confirmation, they identified a homozygous deletion of the whole of exon 19 of the MEI1 gene in both patients. Amplification and sequencing of cDNA confirmed that the exon was missing, and exon 19 expression was almost undetectable in the patients. The mother was a heterozygous carrier. In transfected HEK293T cells, the deletion produced a truncated MEI1 protein, consistent with the predicted premature termination in exon 20. The authors stated that this was the first exon rearrangement reported in MEI1.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Chen et al. (2023) suggested that their patients' combination of 2 MEI1 variants might allow slight MEI1 expression. Meiosis might therefore sometimes occur, which could explain why the brothers had cryptozoospermia rather than nonobstructive azoospermia.

### Animal Model

In oocytes from Mei1-deficient female mice, Nguyen et al. (2018) found that 8% lost all their chromosomes by extruding them, together with the spindles, into the first polar body. Mei1 -/- oocytes could be fertilized, and 5% produced androgenetic zygotes. The authors proposed that extrusion of all maternal chromosomes is a mechanism for producing androgenetic zygotes. ⚠︎

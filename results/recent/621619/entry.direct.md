### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spermatogenic failure-103 (SPGF103) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the MEI1 gene on chromosome 22q13. ⚠︎

### Description

Spermatogenic failure-103 (SPGF103) is an autosomal recessive form of male infertility. Affected men have nonobstructive azoospermia with spermatogenesis arrested at the spermatocyte stage, or cryptozoospermia (Chen et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2025). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Chen et al. (2023) reported 2 Chinese brothers with infertility. The proband was a 30-year-old man who had been infertile for 5 years. He had normal external genitalia, bilateral testicular volumes of about 12 ml, and nothing abnormal on palpation of the spermatic cords or vasa deferentia. Semen volume and pH were normal. No spermatozoa were seen in the initial semen smear, but a smear of centrifuged sediment showed 0 to 1 sperm per high-power field, consistent with cryptozoospermia. A total of 10 to 15 spermatozoa were found in 20 high-power fields; 2 to 3 were progressively motile and 2 to 3 had normal morphology. Serum FSH, LH, testosterone, estradiol, prolactin, thyrotropin, ACTH, inhibin B, and anti-Mullerian hormone were all normal. He had a 46,XY karyotype and no AZF microdeletions of the Y chromosome. His brother had been infertile through 3 years of marriage and also had cryptozoospermia, with occasional motile sperm (0 to 1) seen only after semen centrifugation. Physical examination and laboratory tests were otherwise unremarkable in the brother.

Zhang et al. (2023) studied 4 Chinese men with nonobstructive azoospermia. Testicular pathology and immunohistochemical staining showed that spermatogenesis was arrested at the spermatocyte stage, with defective homeostasis of programmed DNA double-strand breaks and defective meiotic chromosome synapsis.

Liu et al. (2025) studied a consanguineous family that included an infertile man with azoospermia and an infertile woman with embryonic arrest. Preimplantation genetic testing of the woman's arrested embryos showed a high rate of chromosomal anomalies.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of SPGF103 in the family reported by Chen et al. (2023) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Each parent carried 1 of the 2 MEI1 mutations found in the affected brothers. In the consanguineous family reported by Liu et al. (2025), the affected individuals were homozygous for an MEI1 mutation and their mother was a heterozygous carrier.

### Molecular Genetics

Malcher et al. (2022) performed whole-genome sequencing in 39 men with various types of nonobstructive azoospermia. In 1 patient they identified compound heterozygous variants in the MEI1 gene: c.1546A-G, resulting in an ile516-to-val (I516V) substitution, and c.3542G-A, resulting in an arg1181-to-gln (R1181Q) substitution. The authors listed MEI1 among genes previously associated with infertility in which they had found potential NOA-associated variants. No segregation or functional data were reported.

In 2 Chinese brothers with cryptozoospermia, Chen et al. (2023) used whole-exome sequencing of the proband to identify compound heterozygous mutations in the MEI1 gene. One was a splicing mutation (c.529+5G-A) downstream of exon 5, and the other was a missense mutation (c.2071C-G) in exon 18 resulting in a gln691-to-glu (Q691E) substitution. Sanger sequencing confirmed the mutations in both brothers. The father, who had normal fertility, was heterozygous for c.2071C-G, and the mother was heterozygous for c.529+5G-A. Neither variant was found in the ExAC or gnomAD databases. The splicing mutation was predicted to affect splicing, and the missense mutation was predicted to be deleterious by several in silico tools. Because sperm were extremely scarce and testicular tissue was not available, MEI1 mRNA and protein could not be studied. The authors suggested that the combination of the 2 mutations might allow slight MEI1 expression and occasional completion of meiosis, which would explain cryptozoospermia rather than azoospermia.

In 4 Chinese patients with nonobstructive azoospermia, Zhang et al. (2023) used whole-exome sequencing to identify biallelic deleterious variants in the MEI1 gene. In vitro, a missense variant (c.186G-C) reduced MEI1 expression and a frameshift variant (c.251delT) produced truncated MEI1 proteins. Bioinformatic analysis suggested that another missense variant (c.1585T-A) affected the interaction of MEI1 with its partners.

In a man with azoospermia and a woman with embryonic arrest from a consanguineous family, Liu et al. (2025) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a homozygous deletion of the whole of exon 19 of the MEI1 gene, which they confirmed by Sanger sequencing. Their mother was a heterozygous carrier. Sequencing of cDNA confirmed loss of exon 19, and quantitative analysis showed almost no detectable expression of exon 19 in the patients. When the mutant was transfected into HEK293T cells, Western blotting showed a truncated MEI1 protein, consistent with the predicted premature termination in exon 20.

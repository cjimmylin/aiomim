### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spermatogenic failure-103 (SPGF103) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the MEI1 gene on chromosome 22q13. ⚠︎

### Description

Spermatogenic failure-103 (SPGF103) is an autosomal recessive form of male infertility. Affected men have nonobstructive azoospermia, with spermatogenesis arrested at the spermatocyte stage, or cryptozoospermia (Chen et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2025).

### Clinical Features

Chen et al. (2023) reported 2 Chinese brothers with infertility. The proband was a 30-year-old man whose wife had not become pregnant during 5 years of unprotected intercourse. He had normal external genitalia, testicular volumes of about 12 ml on both sides, and no palpable abnormality of the spermatic cords or vasa deferentia. On three semen examinations, no spermatozoa were seen in the initial smear. In the centrifuged sediment, he had 0 to 1 sperm per high-power field (HPF), consistent with cryptozoospermia, and 10 to 15 spermatozoa were found in 20 HPF. Serum FSH, LH, testosterone, estradiol, prolactin, inhibin B, and anti-Mullerian hormone were normal. His karyotype was 46,XY, and he had no Y-chromosome AZF microdeletion. His brother had been infertile for 3 years of marriage. No sperm were seen in the brother's initial semen smears, but 0 to 1 motile sperm were occasionally seen after centrifugation, also consistent with cryptozoospermia. The brother's sex hormones, inhibin B, karyotype, and Y-chromosome microdeletion testing were normal. Their father had normal fertility.

Zhang et al. (2023) studied 4 Chinese patients with nonobstructive azoospermia. Testicular pathology and immunohistochemical staining showed spermatogenesis arrested at the spermatocyte stage, with defective homeostasis of programmed DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) and defective meiotic chromosome synapsis.

Liu et al. (2025) studied a consanguineous family that included an infertile man with azoospermia and an infertile woman with embryonic arrest. Preimplantation genetic testing showed a high rate of chromosomal anomalies in the woman's arrested embryos.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of SPGF103 in the family reported by Chen et al. (2023) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance: the 2 affected brothers were compound heterozygous, and each of their parents carried 1 of the mutations in the heterozygous state. In the consanguineous family reported by Liu et al. (2025), the patients were homozygous for the mutation and their mother was a heterozygous carrier.

### Molecular Genetics

Malcher et al. (2022) performed whole-genome sequencing in 39 men with nonobstructive azoospermia. In 1 patient they found compound heterozygous variants in the MEI1 gene, c.1546A-G and c.3542G-A; Table 2 of the paper gives the protein changes as ile516-to-val (I516V) and arg1181-to-gln (R1181Q). The authors listed MEI1 among genes previously associated with infertility and presented these variants as candidate findings only, without functional or segregation studies. ⚠︎

In 2 Chinese brothers with SPGF103 and cryptozoospermia, Chen et al. (2023) found compound heterozygous mutations in the MEI1 gene: a splice site mutation downstream of exon 5 (c.529+5G-A) and a c.2071C-G transversion in exon 18 resulting in a gln691-to-glu (Q691E) substitution. The mutations were identified by whole-exome sequencing of the proband and confirmed by Sanger sequencing in both brothers. Neither variant was present in the ExAC or gnomAD databases. The father, who had normal fertility, was heterozygous for c.2071C-G, and the mother was heterozygous for c.529+5G-A. The splice site mutation was predicted to affect splicing, and several prediction programs predicted the missense mutation to be deleterious. Because the brothers had very few sperm and no testicular tissue was obtained, MEI1 mRNA and protein could not be assessed. The authors suggested that the combination of the 2 mutations might allow slight MEI1 expression and occasional completion of meiosis, which would explain cryptozoospermia rather than azoospermia.

Using whole-exome sequencing, Zhang et al. (2023) identified biallelic deleterious variants in the MEI1 gene in 4 Chinese patients with nonobstructive azoospermia. In in vitro studies, a missense variant (c.186G-C) reduced MEI1 expression, and a frameshift variant (c.251delT) produced truncated MEI1 proteins. On the basis of bioinformatic analysis, another missense variant (c.1585T-A) was predicted to affect the interaction of MEI1 with its partners.

In a man with azoospermia and a woman with embryonic arrest from a consanguineous family, Liu et al. (2025) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a homozygous deletion of the whole of exon 19 of the MEI1 gene, which was confirmed by Sanger sequencing. Their mother was a heterozygous carrier. Amplification and sequencing of cDNA confirmed loss of the exon, and exon 19 expression was almost undetectable in the patients. In HEK293T cells, the deletion resulted in a truncated MEI1 protein, consistent with the predicted premature termination in exon 20.

### Animal Model

Nguyen et al. (2018) found that 8% of oocytes from Mei1-deficient female mice lost all of their chromosomes by extruding them, along with the spindles, into the first polar body. Mei1 -/- oocytes could be fertilized, and 5% produced androgenetic zygotes. ⚠︎

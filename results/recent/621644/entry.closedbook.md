A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that kyphomelic dysplasia, Itai-Ikegawa type (KDII) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the KIF5B gene (602809) on chromosome 10p11.

### Description

Kyphomelic dysplasia, Itai-Ikegawa type, is a skeletal dysplasia defined mainly by marked angulation (kyphomelia) of the long bones, especially the femora, together with short stature. It is caused by de novo heterozygous mutations in the kinesin heavy chain gene KIF5B. ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

The eponym refers to Itai and Ikegawa, the first and senior authors of the report that linked this form of kyphomelic dysplasia to KIF5B. It distinguishes the disorder from other conditions that have been called kyphomelic dysplasia. ✗

### Clinical Features

Itai et al. (2022) reported a small number of unrelated patients with kyphomelic dysplasia. The main features were bowing and angulation of the long bones, most obvious in the femora, and short stature. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The heterozygous KIF5B mutations reported by Itai et al. (2022) arose de novo, which fits autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Itai et al. (2022) used exome sequencing to study unrelated patients with kyphomelic dysplasia. They found de novo heterozygous missense mutations in the KIF5B gene, and the mutations clustered in the motor domain of the protein. ⚠︎

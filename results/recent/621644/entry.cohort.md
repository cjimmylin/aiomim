### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that kyphomelic dysplasia, Itai-Ikegawa type (KDII) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the KIF5B gene (602809) on chromosome 10p11.22.

### Description

Kyphomelic dysplasia is a heterogeneous group of skeletal dysplasias with severe limb bowing and variable findings such as narrow thorax and abnormal facies (Itai et al., 2022). The form caused by heterozygous KIF5B mutation is marked by sharp angulation of the femora and humeri, distinctive facial features, and neonatal respiratory distress. Itai et al. (2022) noted that it extends the phenotypic spectrum of kinesinopathies.

### Clinical Features

Itai et al. (2022) reported 4 individuals from 4 families with kyphomelic dysplasia. All of them had sharp angulation of the femora and humeri, distinctive facial features, and neonatal respiratory distress. Three individuals had short stature. Three developed postnatal osteoporosis followed by fractures, 2 had brachycephaly, and 2 were diagnosed with optic atrophy. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The heterozygous KIF5B mutations in the 4 individuals with kyphomelic dysplasia reported by Itai et al. (2022) arose de novo, which is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Itai et al. (2022) used whole-exome sequencing to study 4 individuals with kyphomelic dysplasia. All 4 carried de novo heterozygous mutations in the KIF5B gene, which encodes kinesin-1 heavy chain. Two had a c.272A-G transition resulting in a lys91-to-arg (K91R) substitution, 1 had a c.584C-A transversion resulting in a thr195-to-lys (T195K) substitution, and 1 had a c.701G-T transversion resulting in a gly234-to-val (G234V) substitution. Each mutation affected a conserved amino acid in or close to the ATPase activity-related motifs in the catalytic motor domain of KIF5B.

Flex et al. (2023) used structural modeling to show that the 3 missense mutations previously implicated in KIF5B-related kyphomelic dysplasia (affecting lys91, thr195, and gly234) map to the motor domain and cluster in the pocket that mediates ATP/ADP binding.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Flex et al. (2023) described 3 additional patients with heterozygous KIF5B variants, none of whom had skeletal dysplasia. The first was a 9-year-old boy with a de novo leu537-to-pro (L537P) variant. He had severe developmental delay and intellectual disability, early-onset refractory epilepsy, hypotonia, feeding difficulties requiring gastrostomy, progressive cortical atrophy, a thin corpus callosum, and severe scoliosis. His perinatal period was complicated by respiratory distress requiring ventilation.

The second patient described by Flex et al. (2023) was a 67-year-old man from a family segregating an autosomal dominant, paternally inherited in-frame deletion (asn255del). Affected members of this family (the father and 3 sibs) developed dilated cardiomyopathy in their sixth decade, and 3 of them required heart transplant. The cardiomyopathy was associated with a skeletal myopathy that worsened over time, ophthalmoplegia, elevated creatine phosphokinase, joint contractures, and peripheral neuropathy. Clubfoot was also reported.

The third patient described by Flex et al. (2023) was a 6-year-old girl with a de novo leu498-to-pro (L498P) variant. Her hypotonic, multisystem condition began prenatally with decreased fetal movements. She had mild developmental delay, severe hypotonia, swallowing and feeding difficulties requiring G-tube feeding, and recurrent vomiting.

Flex et al. (2023) reviewed their patients together with the 4 previously reported patients with KIF5B-related kyphomelic dysplasia. Joint contractures and clubfoot occurred only in subjects with variants in the kinesin motor domain. The authors proposed grouping these phenotypes, which share hypotonia, developmental delay/intellectual disability, and a suggestive facial gestalt, as KIF5B-related diseases.

### Pathogenesis

Flex et al. (2023) studied patient fibroblasts heterozygous for KIF5B variants. The cells showed aberrant primary cilia, enlarged and peripherally distributed lysosomes, enlarged perinuclear autophagosomes, Golgi disorganization, and misoriented microtubule growth. The authors proposed altered microtubule dynamics as the unifying event.

### Clinical Management

Flex et al. (2023) recommended cardiac surveillance in all individuals with KIF5B variants because late-onset dilated cardiomyopathy could not be ruled out in younger patients.

### Animal Model

Flex et al. (2023) injected zebrafish embryos with mRNA encoding the kyphomelic dysplasia-associated KIF5B T195K variant. At 2 days postfertilization (dpf), the embryos had defects in heart size and morphology with cardiac edema. At 3 dpf, the ceratohyal cartilage angle was wider. Mitochondrial coverage along motoneuron axons was reduced. These findings were consistent with a dominant, possibly dominant-negative, effect.

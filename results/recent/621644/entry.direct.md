### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that kyphomelic dysplasia, Itai-Ikegawa type (KDII), is caused by heterozygous mutation in the KIF5B gene, which encodes the kinesin-1 heavy chain.

### Description

Kyphomelic dysplasia, Itai-Ikegawa type (KDII), is a skeletal dysplasia. Its main features are sharp angulation of the femora and humeri, distinctive facial features, and neonatal respiratory distress. Other features include short stature, postnatal osteoporosis with fractures, brachycephaly, and optic atrophy (Itai et al., 2022). Affected individuals also have hypotonia of varying severity, and some have developmental delay or intellectual disability (Flex et al., 2023).

### Nomenclature

Flex et al. (2023) used the term 'KIF5B-related KD' for the disorder reported by Itai et al. (2022). Because the phenotypes caused by KIF5B variants vary so widely, Flex et al. (2023) proposed grouping them as 'KIF5B-related diseases.'

### Clinical Features

Itai et al. (2022) described 4 individuals with a diagnosis of kyphomelic dysplasia. All 4 had sharp angulation of the femora and humeri, distinctive facial features, and neonatal respiratory distress. Three had short stature, and 3 developed postnatal osteoporosis followed by fractures. Two had brachycephaly, and 2 were diagnosed with optic atrophy.

Flex et al. (2023) reviewed the 4 individuals reported by Itai et al. (2022), who were 3 females and 1 male aged 2 years 1 month to 24 years at last evaluation. All 4 had neonatal distress and respiratory failure, which required medical intervention in most cases. Tracheomalacia, glossoptosis, and velopharyngeal insufficiency each occurred in a single individual, and the hypotonia appeared to partly resolve with age. Three individuals had clubfoot, and 1 of them also had joint contractures. Intellectual disability was reported in 2 individuals, 1 of whom also had mild global developmental delay. One individual had seizures and scoliosis. Reported CNS anomalies were a spinal arachnoid cyst with cord herniation in 1 individual and diffuse low-density areas in the white matter in another. Height ranged from 89 cm (-1.5 SD) at 3 years 3 months to 71.2 cm (-4.9 SD) at 2 years 1 month, 102 cm (-5.1 SD) at 9 years 5 months, and 142.2 cm (less than -2.5 SD) at 24 years. Flex et al. (2023) compared these individuals with one of their own patients. Facial features shared by almost all of them were bitemporal narrowing, hypertelorism, ptosis, midface hypoplasia with depressed nasal bridge, anteverted nares, a deep, short, and prominent philtrum, tented upper lip, and micrognathia.

Marom et al. (2023) reported a male fetus (proband 4) with a de novo heterozygous KIF5B variant. The pregnancy was terminated at 20 weeks' gestation because of multiple congenital anomalies, and osteogenesis imperfecta was suspected clinically. Prenatal ultrasound showed abnormal ribs, short long bones, a small chest, hypomineralization, tetralogy of Fallot, absent pulmonary valve, absent ductus venosus, and micrognathia. The fetus had osteopenia and bent, short long bones, with hypoplastic, unossified vertebrae and pelvic bones, but no fractures. Facial features included hypertelorism, low-set ears, and a flat nasal bridge. Marom et al. (2023) noted overlap between their patients with osteogenesis imperfecta and the individuals previously reported with kyphomelic dysplasia: short stature, bowed long bones, small chest, osteoporosis, fractures, and extraskeletal findings. They suggested that these individuals may belong to a single clinical spectrum. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The heterozygous KIF5B mutations in the 4 individuals with KDII reported by Itai et al. (2022) occurred de novo, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Itai et al. (2022) performed whole-exome sequencing in 4 individuals with kyphomelic dysplasia. All 4 carried de novo heterozygous variants in the KIF5B gene. Two had a lys91-to-arg (K91R) mutation, 1 had a thr195-to-lys (T195K) mutation, and 1 had a gly234-to-val (G234V) mutation. Each variant affected a conserved amino acid in or near the ATPase activity-related motifs of the catalytic motor domain of KIF5B.

Flex et al. (2023) used protein structure analysis to show that the residues mutated in KIF5B-related kyphomelic dysplasia (lys91, thr195, and gly234) cluster in the motor-domain pocket that binds ATP/ADP. The missense changes were predicted to disturb the interaction of KIF5B with ATP/ADP. Flex et al. (2023) injected zebrafish embryos with mRNA encoding the T195K variant, chosen to represent the kyphomelic dysplasia mutations. The embryos showed mild to severe defects in heart size and anatomy, with heart edema at 2 days postfertilization. They also had a wider ceratohyal cartilage angle than embryos expressing wildtype KIF5B. In single motoneurons, expression of T195K significantly reduced mitochondrial coverage along the axon and caused defects in mitochondrial displacement. Flex et al. (2023) concluded that the KIF5B variants they studied can act as dominant-negative alleles in vivo.

Marom et al. (2023) used trio exome sequencing to identify a de novo heterozygous T195K mutation in KIF5B in a fetus with skeletal anomalies suggestive of osteogenesis imperfecta. The variant was not present in gnomAD. Based on structural modeling, they predicted that substitutions at thr195 and other motor-domain residues would destabilize the ATP-bound form of KIF5B, producing a catalytically inactive protein. They proposed that this protein exerts a dominant-negative effect by dimerizing with wildtype KIF5B.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Flex et al. (2023) found joint contractures or clubfoot only in individuals whose pathogenic KIF5B variants lay within the kinesin motor domain. These included the K91R, T195K, and G234V mutations reported by Itai et al. (2022). Flex et al. (2023) suggested that impaired motor-domain function may correlate with joint contractures and clubfoot. Their own patients had variants at the 3-prime end of the motor domain or in the coiled-coil domain, and they did not have the skeletal signs of kyphomelic dysplasia, except for scoliosis in 1 patient.

Marom et al. (2023) noted that the variants associated with skeletal findings (thr87-to-ile, gly90-to-ala, T195K, K91R, and G234V) cluster within the kinesin motor domain. In contrast, previously reported variants outside the P-loop and switch-loop regions were not associated with a skeletal phenotype. They took this as evidence of a genotype-phenotype correlation.

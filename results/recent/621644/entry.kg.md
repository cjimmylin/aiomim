### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that kyphomelic dysplasia, Itai-Ikegawa type (KDII) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the KIF5B gene (602809) on chromosome 10p11.22.

### Description

Itai et al. (2022) described kyphomelic dysplasia as a heterogeneous group of skeletal dysplasias with severe bowing of the limbs and other variable features, such as a narrow thorax and abnormal facies. They suggested that heterozygous deleterious KIF5B variants cause a specific form of kyphomelic dysplasia, which extends the phenotypic spectrum of the kinesinopathies.

### Clinical Features

Itai et al. (2022) described 4 individuals with kyphomelic dysplasia. All 4 had sharp angulation of the femora and humeri, distinctive facial features, and neonatal respiratory distress. Three had short stature, and 3 developed postnatal osteoporosis followed by fractures. Two had brachycephaly, and 2 were diagnosed with optic atrophy.

Flex et al. (2023) re-reviewed the 4 patients reported by Itai et al. (2022). All 4 had neonatal distress and respiratory failure, and their hypotonia partially resolved with age. Single individuals had tracheomalacia, glossoptosis, or velopharyngeal insufficiency. Three of the 4 patients had clubfoot.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous KIF5B mutations in the patients with kyphomelic dysplasia reported by Itai et al. (2022) occurred de novo, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Flex et al. (2023) identified 3 other dominantly acting KIF5B variants located further 3-prime than the kyphomelic dysplasia mutations. One was asn255del at the end of the motor domain, and the other 2, leu498-to-pro (L498P) and leu537-to-pro (L537P), lay in the coiled-coil domain. The phenotypes of the carriers ranged from a syndromic neurodevelopmental disorder to adult-onset dilated cardiomyopathy with ophthalmoplegia and skeletal myopathy. None of these patients had kyphomelic dysplasia, although 1 had scoliosis. The authors proposed grouping these conditions as KIF5B-related diseases.

Flex et al. (2023) described an Italian boy (subject 1), last evaluated at 8 years 3 months. He had a neurodevelopmental disorder with severe developmental delay and intellectual disability, early-onset refractory epilepsy, and severe hypotonia, and he needed a gastrostomy for feeding. He also had progressive cortical atrophy, a thin corpus callosum, and dysmorphic features including bitemporal narrowing, hypertelorism, ptosis, and a short deep philtrum. Severe scoliosis was reported at 9 years. Trio whole-exome sequencing identified a de novo heterozygous c.1610T-C transition in KIF5B, resulting in the L537P substitution. ⚠︎

Flex et al. (2023) also described a family (family 2) with autosomal dominant, adult-onset dilated cardiomyopathy that required heart transplantation in 3 members. Affected members also had skeletal myopathy that worsened over time, ophthalmoplegia, elevated CPK, joint contractures, and peripheral neuropathy. A heterozygous in-frame deletion in KIF5B (c.765_767delCAA, asn255del) segregated with the disorder.

A third patient reported by Flex et al. (2023) was a 6-year-old girl (subject 3) with mild developmental delay, severe hypotonia that began prenatally, feeding difficulties requiring G-tube feeding, and mild dysmorphism. She carried a de novo heterozygous KIF5B mutation, L498P, in the coiled-coil domain.

### Molecular Genetics

Itai et al. (2022) used whole-exome sequencing to study 4 individuals with kyphomelic dysplasia and found de novo heterozygous mutations in the KIF5B gene in all 4. Two individuals carried a c.272A-G transition that results in a lys91-to-arg (K91R) substitution. One carried a c.584C-A transversion that results in a thr195-to-lys (T195K) substitution. One carried a c.701G-T transversion that results in a gly234-to-val (G234V) substitution. All of the affected amino acids are conserved and lie in or close to the ATPase activity-related motifs of the catalytic motor domain.

Flex et al. (2023) showed that zebrafish embryos injected with human KIF5B mRNA carrying the T195K mutation developed heart defects and craniofacial cartilage abnormalities. Motoneurons expressing the mutation had mitochondrial transport defects. The authors suggested that the kyphomelic dysplasia-associated variants act as dominant-negative alleles (see ANIMAL MODEL). ⚠︎

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Flex et al. (2023) noted that the KIF5B mutations associated with kyphomelic dysplasia affect residues lys91, thr195, and gly234. These residues lie in the motor domain and cluster in the pocket that binds ATP/ADP. Joint contractures occurred only in patients with motor-domain variants, which suggested to the authors a correlation between altered motor-domain function and joint contractures or clubfoot.

### Animal Model

Flex et al. (2023) injected human KIF5B mRNA carrying the kyphomelic dysplasia-associated T195K mutation into 1-cell zebrafish embryos. By 2 days postfertilization, these embryos had a significant increase in mild and severe heart defects and in heart edema. They also had a wider ceratohyal cartilage angle than embryos injected with wildtype KIF5B. Expressing the mutation in motoneurons significantly reduced mitochondrial coverage along the axon and caused mitochondrial transport defects. The authors suggested that the variants act as dominant-negative alleles. ⚠︎

KIF5B, LEU537PRO [c.1610T>C; p.L537P]

In an Italian boy (subject 1) with severe developmental delay and intellectual disability, early-onset refractory epilepsy, severe hypotonia, progressive cortical atrophy, a thin corpus callosum, and dysmorphic features, Flex et al. (2023) used trio whole-exome sequencing to identify a de novo heterozygous c.1610T-C transition in the KIF5B gene, resulting in a leu537-to-pro (L537P) substitution at a conserved leucine in the coiled-coil dimerization region.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that infantile-onset neurodegeneration with optic atrophy and structural brain abnormalities-2 (NDOABA2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the BORCS5 gene (616598) on chromosome 12p13.2. ⚠︎

### Description

Fisher et al. (2025) described this disorder as neuroaxonal dystrophy with osteopetrosis. In 2 affected sibs, prenatal imaging showed cerebral ventriculomegaly and agenesis of the corpus callosum. At autopsy, the sibs had facial dysmorphism, osteopetrosis, and neuropathologic findings consistent with neuroaxonal dystrophy.

### Clinical Features

Fisher et al. (2025) reported 2 sibs born to consanguineous Pakistani parents. Prenatal imaging showed cerebral ventriculomegaly and agenesis of the corpus callosum in both sibs. Autopsies on both sibs showed similar abnormalities, including facial dysmorphism, osteopetrosis, and neuropathologic findings consistent with neuroaxonal dystrophy.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of NDOABA2 in the family reported by Fisher et al. (2025) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. The affected sibs were born to consanguineous parents and were homozygous for a BORCS5 mutation, and each parent was heterozygous for the mutation.

### Molecular Genetics

Fisher et al. (2025) studied 2 sibs, born to consanguineous Pakistani parents, who had neuroaxonal dystrophy with osteopetrosis. Using trio exome sequencing, they identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in exon 3 of the BORCS5 gene (c.283C-T, NM_058169.4), resulting in an arg95-to-ter (R95X) substitution. Each parent was heterozygous for the mutation.

Fisher et al. (2025) also found that 1 of the 2 affected sibs was homozygous for a missense variant of uncertain significance in the SCYL2 gene (616365): c.902G-A (NM_017988.5), resulting in an arg301-to-his (R301H) substitution. Both parents were heterozygous for this variant. Because the autopsy findings in the 2 sibs were identical, the authors raised the possibility that the SCYL2 variant did not contribute to the phenotype.

### History

Fisher et al. (2025) noted that neuroaxonal dystrophy with osteopetrosis was first reported in a consanguineous Moroccan Jewish family. They also noted that, before their report, no genetic variant had been linked to the disease.

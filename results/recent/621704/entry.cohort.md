### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that infantile-onset neurodegeneration with optic atrophy and structural brain abnormalities-2 (NDOABA2) is caused by homozygous mutation in the BORCS5 gene (616598) on chromosome 12p13.2. ⚠︎

### Description

NDOABA2 is a severe autosomal recessive disorder that has been described as neuroaxonal dystrophy with osteopetrosis. It was identified prenatally in affected fetuses by cerebral ventriculomegaly and agenesis of the corpus callosum. At autopsy, the fetuses had facial dysmorphism, osteopetrosis, and neuropathologic findings consistent with neuroaxonal dystrophy (Fisher et al., 2025). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Fisher et al. (2025) reported 2 sibs born to consanguineous Pakistani parents. Cerebral ventriculomegaly and agenesis of the corpus callosum were identified in both sibs prenatally. Autopsies of both fetuses showed similar abnormalities, including facial dysmorphism, osteopetrosis, and neuropathologic findings consistent with neuroaxonal dystrophy.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of NDOABA2 in the family reported by Fisher et al. (2025) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. The affected sibs, born to consanguineous parents, were both homozygous for a BORCS5 mutation, and each parent was heterozygous.

### Molecular Genetics

In 2 sib fetuses with NDOABA2, born to consanguineous Pakistani parents, Fisher et al. (2025) used trio exome sequencing to identify a homozygous c.283C-T transition in exon 3 of the BORCS5 gene, resulting in an arg95-to-ter (R95X) substitution. Each parent was heterozygous for the mutation. One of the affected fetuses was also homozygous for a missense variant of uncertain significance in the SCYL2 gene (616365), c.902G-A, resulting in an arg301-to-his (R301H) substitution, for which both parents were heterozygous. Because the autopsy findings in the 2 sibs were identical, Fisher et al. (2025) raised the possibility that the SCYL2 variant did not contribute to the phenotype.

### History

Fisher et al. (2025) noted that neuroaxonal dystrophy with osteopetrosis was first reported in a consanguineous Moroccan Jewish family. They also noted that, until their report, no genetic variant had been linked to the disease.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that infantile-onset neurodegeneration with optic atrophy and structural brain abnormalities-2 (NDOABA2) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the BORCS5 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Infantile-onset neurodegeneration with optic atrophy and structural brain abnormalities-2 (NDOABA2) is an autosomal recessive neurologic disorder with a broad phenotypic spectrum. Individuals with biallelic missense or splice-site variants have a developmental and epileptic encephalopathy. Features include profound intellectual disability, seizures beginning in the first year of life, optic neuropathy, progressive spasticity, and progressive movement disorders. Brain imaging shows hypomyelination, abnormalities of the corpus callosum, and progressive global cerebral atrophy. Homozygous protein-truncating variants cause a prenatally lethal phenotype with arthrogryposis multiplex congenita, brain malformations, and neuropathologic evidence of neuroaxonal dystrophy (Mencacci et al., 2026).

### Clinical Features

Fisher et al. (2025) reported 2 sibs born to consanguineous Pakistani parents. Cerebral ventriculomegaly and agenesis of the corpus callosum were identified prenatally. Autopsies of both fetuses showed similar abnormalities, including facial dysmorphism, osteopetrosis, and neuropathologic findings consistent with neuroaxonal dystrophy. ⚠︎

Mencacci et al. (2026) reported 16 affected individuals from 9 unrelated families of Pakistani, Moroccan, and Arab descent, 2 of the Arab families being from Oman. Ten individuals from 6 families shared a core phenotype of developmental encephalopathy with profound intellectual disability, delayed milestones, seizures, and progressive movement disorders with spasticity. None of these individuals achieved speech or walking, and all were completely dependent on others. Patients in 4 of the families had developmental regression, including loss of babbling, visual tracking, head control, and the ability to stand. Seizures began within the first year of life in all patients. In 3 families the seizures were controlled with standard antiepileptic treatment, and the older individuals in 1 family became seizure-free in their teens. Affected individuals from 2 other families had more severe epilepsy, with treatment-resistant infantile spasms, myoclonic seizures, and hypsarrhythmia on EEG.

All 10 individuals had progressive spasticity, diffuse hyperreflexia, bilateral extensor plantar responses, and severe limb contractures. Dystonia and parkinsonism were prominent in 2 families. Generalized dystonia led to scoliosis, painful spasms, and oculogyric crises, and the parkinsonian features included severe limb bradykinesia and hypomimia. Dystonic spasms improved dramatically with carbidopa/levodopa. Trihexyphenidyl, baclofen, and diazepam helped manage spasticity and dystonia. All patients had optic neuropathy. Nerve conduction studies showed a sensorimotor demyelinating neuropathy in 1 patient and were normal in another. Dysmorphic features included microcephaly, dolichocephaly, and low-set ears. Swallowing difficulties required percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy in patients from 2 families. Five patients died prematurely of aspiration pneumonia or uncontrolled seizures. The other 5 were aged 28, 23, 13, 10, and 3 years at their most recent evaluation (Mencacci et al., 2026).

In these patients, neuroimaging showed cerebral atrophy, loss of white matter, hypomyelination, small T2-hypointense thalami, a thin brainstem, and optic nerve atrophy. The corpus callosum was very thin, with a hypoplastic anterior commissure. Two patients also had mild cerebellar atrophy. In 1 patient the changes progressed rapidly between 9 months and 2 years of age. The 5 patients with homozygous splice-site variants also had developmental anomalies, such as dysgenesis of the corpus callosum and polymicrogyria (Mencacci et al., 2026).

Mencacci et al. (2026) also described 6 fetuses from 3 families with a distinct, severe prenatal phenotype that first came to attention because of reduced fetal movements. Intrauterine imaging in all 6 showed arthrogryposis multiplex congenita, hydrocephalus with aqueductal stenosis, agenesis of the corpus callosum, a markedly thin brainstem, cerebellar hypoplasia, and diffuse muscle atrophy. Growth parameters and other organ systems were normal, and the pregnancies were terminated between 21 and 25 weeks of gestation.

Brain histopathology in 2 of these fetuses showed microcephaly; hypoplasia of the thalamus, basal ganglia, and cerebellum; ventriculomegaly; aqueductal atresia; and absence of the corpus callosum. Major neuronal groups in the brainstem were hypoplastic, including the tegmental nuclei, inferior olivary nuclei, basis pontis, and dentate nucleus. There was widespread neuroaxonal degeneration, with axonal spheroids in the brainstem, cerebral hemispheres, and peripheral roots and nerves. The spheroids stained strongly for beta-amyloid precursor protein, neurofilament, and alpha-synuclein, consistent with neuroaxonal dystrophy. They did not stain for phosphorylated alpha-synuclein, tau, or TDP-43 (Mencacci et al., 2026).

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of NDOABA2 in the families reported by Fisher et al. (2025) and Mencacci et al. (2026) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Affected individuals were homozygous or compound heterozygous for BORCS5 variants, and the parents tested were heterozygous carriers. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Charng et al. (2016) performed whole-exome sequencing in 31 mostly consanguineous Arab families from Saudi Arabia with neurologic disease. They proposed LOH12CR1 as 1 of 12 novel candidate disease genes but did not report the clinical features of the affected family in the main text. Mencacci et al. (2026) noted that BORCS5 was previously known as LOH12CR1. They also stated that 1 of their patients, from family F-IV, had been reported previously with minimal clinical details.

Fisher et al. (2025) performed trio exome sequencing in 2 fetal sibs born to consanguineous Pakistani parents. Both fetuses were homozygous for a c.283C-T nonsense mutation in exon 3 of the BORCS5 gene, resulting in an arg95-to-ter (R95X) substitution, and each parent was heterozygous. One fetus was also homozygous for a missense variant of uncertain significance in the SCYL2 gene, for which the parents were heterozygous. Because the autopsy findings in the 2 sibs were identical, Fisher et al. (2025) raised the possibility that the SCYL2 variant did not contribute to the phenotype.

In 16 affected individuals from 9 unrelated families, Mencacci et al. (2026) identified 7 BORCS5 mutations, found by whole-exome sequencing and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. In the index family, 2 affected sibs were compound heterozygous for a maternally inherited frameshift mutation (c.382_383delAG; L128Vfs*86) and a paternally inherited missense mutation (c.284G-A; R95Q). Their 2 unaffected older sibs were not carriers. All other affected individuals were homozygous. These mutations were R95Q in 2 sibs; a frameshift (c.316delG; A106Pfs*20) in 5 individuals from 2 unrelated Pakistani families; a splice-site mutation (c.202+1G-A) in 4 individuals from 2 unrelated families from Oman; and, in single individuals, a missense mutation (c.296A-C; H99P), a splice-site mutation (c.203-1G-T), and a nonsense mutation (c.417C-G; Y139X). Segregation analysis prioritized BORCS5 as the top candidate gene in each family. All of the variants were absent or exceedingly rare in gnomAD and other large databases. The 2 missense mutations affected fully conserved residues in a long alpha helix that, according to AlphaFold prediction, is likely essential for interaction with other BORC subunits. SpliceAI predicted that the 2 splice-site mutations would abolish the donor and acceptor splice sites of intron 2.

In functional studies, Mencacci et al. (2026) found that the A106Pfs and Y139X mutant proteins were truncated and expressed at lower levels in transfected HEK293T cells. The L128Vfs mutant produced bands consistent with degradation products, and the H99P mutant was expressed at 50% of wildtype levels. H99P and L128Vfs showed reduced interaction with the BORC subunits BORCS7 and SNAPIN, whereas R95Q did not. In BORCS5-knockout HeLa cells, the truncating mutants failed to rescue perinuclear clustering of endolysosomes, whereas R95Q and H99P gave partial rescue.

Patient fibroblasts had reduced BORCS5 protein levels: 25 to 40% lower in cells with R95Q/L128Vfs, 60% lower in H99P homozygous cells, and 93% lower in Y139X homozygous cells. Perinuclear lysosomal clustering was seen only in the Y139X cells. All patient fibroblast lines showed about 20% lower lysosomal proteolysis and reduced lysosomal glucocerebrosidase activity. Mencacci et al. (2026) concluded that A106Pfs, Y139X, and L128Vfs are complete loss-of-function alleles and that H99P is a hypomorphic allele.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Mencacci et al. (2026) found that individuals homozygous for protein-truncating BORCS5 mutations, which cause complete loss of function, had a severe prenatally lethal phenotype. Features included arthrogryposis multiplex congenita, brain malformations, and neuroaxonal dystrophy. Individuals with missense mutations, either homozygous or compound heterozygous with a truncating mutation, had a chronic infantile-onset neurodegenerative course with progressive movement disorders and severe spasticity. Patients with homozygous splice-site mutations had a broadly similar presentation but more severe epilepsy and more pronounced neuroimaging abnormalities, particularly affecting the corpus callosum. Mencacci et al. (2026) considered this an intermediate phenotype between complete loss of function and missense mutations.

### Pathogenesis

Mencacci et al. (2026) generated induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-derived forebrain neurons from 2 patients with R95Q/L128Vfs and an isogenic BORCS5-knockout line. Neurites of BORCS5-knockout neurons contained 60% fewer endolysosomes than control neurons, whereas the distribution of endolysosomes was unchanged in R95Q/L128Vfs neurons. In both the knockout and the patient neurons, LysoTracker-positive puncta were larger and more intense, and cathepsin B activity was reduced. Knockout neurons also had reduced lysosomal glucocerebrosidase activity. Their endolysosomal fractions contained less glucocerebrosidase and mature cathepsin B, and the cells accumulated autophagosomes, autolysosomes, and lysosomes. Mencacci et al. (2026) concluded that BORCS5 modulates lysosomal function as well as anterograde lysosomal transport. They proposed that this dual role may explain the different clinical phenotypes associated with different BORCS5 variants.

### Animal Model

Mencacci et al. (2026) used CRISPR-Cas9 to knock out borcs5 in F0 zebrafish. At 3 days postfertilization, the knockout larvae had reduced body size, smaller eyes and heads, smaller brains, and fewer dopaminergic neurons. Later findings included enlarged ventricles, increased neuronal activation after exposure to pentylenetetrazole, reduced locomotor activity, shorter and less-branched motor axons, and disorganized muscle. Injection of wildtype human BORCS5 mRNA rescued these defects. When R95Q or H99P mRNA was expressed in borcs5-knockout embryos, the larvae had shorter bodies, smaller heads and eyes, and reduced swimming, although these defects were less severe than in knockout embryos.

**.0002 NEURODEGENERATION, INFANTILE-ONSET, WITH OPTIC ATROPHY AND STRUCTURAL BRAIN ABNORMALITIES 2**  
BORCS5, ARG95GLN [NM_058169.4:c.284G>A; p.Arg95Gln]

In 2 sibs of Pakistani descent with developmental encephalopathy, optic neuropathy, spasticity, and progressive movement disorders, Mencacci et al. (2026) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the BORCS5 gene, found by whole-exome sequencing of the family. The sibs carried a paternally inherited c.284G-A transition, resulting in an arg95-to-gln (R95Q) substitution, and a maternally inherited 2-bp deletion (c.382_383delAG; L128Vfs*86). Two sibs of Arab descent from another family were homozygous for R95Q. The R95 residue is fully conserved. The R95Q mutant protein was expressed normally and interacted normally with BORCS7 and SNAPIN, and it partially rescued lysosomal distribution in BORCS5-knockout HeLa cells. Patient fibroblasts with R95Q/L128Vfs had reduced lysosomal proteolysis and glucocerebrosidase activity. Neurons derived from patient iPSCs had reduced cathepsin B activity. In borcs5-knockout zebrafish, expression of R95Q mRNA did not fully rescue body length, head and eye size, or swimming.

**.0003 NEURODEGENERATION, INFANTILE-ONSET, WITH OPTIC ATROPHY AND STRUCTURAL BRAIN ABNORMALITIES 2**  
BORCS5, 2-BP DEL, 382AG [NM_058169.4:c.382_383delAG; p.Leu128ValfsTer86]

In 2 sibs with NDOABA2, Mencacci et al. (2026) identified a maternally inherited 2-bp deletion (c.382_383delAG) in the BORCS5 gene, predicted to cause a frameshift (L128Vfs*86). The sibs were compound heterozygous for this deletion and the R95Q mutation. The mutant protein gave multiple bands consistent with degradation products and showed reduced interaction with BORCS7 and SNAPIN. It did not rescue perinuclear lysosomal clustering in BORCS5-knockout HeLa cells. BORCS5 mRNA levels were preserved in patient fibroblasts. Mencacci et al. (2026) considered this a complete loss-of-function allele.

**.0004 NEURODEGENERATION, INFANTILE-ONSET, WITH OPTIC ATROPHY AND STRUCTURAL BRAIN ABNORMALITIES 2**  
BORCS5, HIS99PRO [NM_058169.4:c.296A>C; p.His99Pro]

In a patient of Moroccan descent with NDOABA2, Mencacci et al. (2026) identified a homozygous c.296A-C transversion in the BORCS5 gene, resulting in a his99-to-pro (H99P) substitution. The patient had rapidly progressive cerebral and cerebellar atrophy and died prematurely. In transfected cells, the mutant protein was expressed at 50% of wildtype levels and showed reduced interaction with BORCS7 and SNAPIN. Patient fibroblasts had 60% less BORCS5 protein, reduced lysosomal proteolysis and glucocerebrosidase activity, and normal lysosomal distribution. Mencacci et al. (2026) considered H99P a hypomorphic allele.

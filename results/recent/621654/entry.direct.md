### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that hyposulfatemia with skeletal dysplasia (HSSD) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the SLC13A1 gene on chromosome 7.

### Description

Hyposulfatemia with skeletal dysplasia (HSSD) is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by loss of function of the SLC13A1 sodium-sulfate cotransporter. Affected individuals have low plasma sulfate and/or increased urinary sulfate excretion. Skeletal features include joint enlargement, spondyloepimetaphyseal or epiphyseal radiographic changes in early childhood that may improve with age, scoliosis, and short stature in some patients. Autistic features or other neurodevelopmental problems have been reported in some patients (van de Kamp et al., 2023; Tise et al., 2025).

### Clinical Features

van de Kamp et al. (2023) reported a boy, the only child of nonconsanguineous healthy Dutch parents, who presented at 4 months of age with enlarged knees that could not fully extend. His length (-0.5 SDS) and head circumference (+0.5 SDS) were normal. A skeletal survey at 6 months showed ovoid vertebral bodies, spiky widening of the metaphyses, irregular tarsal bones, slight flaring of the ribs, and somewhat small epiphyses, which were more noticeable at 4 years. The skull and pelvis were normal. Serum calcium, phosphate, alkaline phosphatase, parathyroid hormone, and 25-hydroxyvitamin D3 were normal. His growth was normal, and his only complaints were a slightly abnormal gait and difficulty running. He had a forearm fracture at 8 years that healed well. The radiographic abnormalities improved over time, and at 11 years only flattening of the cervical vertebral bodies and irregular lumbar vertebral endplates remained. At 12 years he had enlarged knees and elbows, reduced movement of the lumbar spine and knees, winged scapulae, hypermobile fingers, and flat feet. His height was normal (+1 SDS), but his trunk was relatively short, with a sitting height/height ratio of -2.0 SDS. DEXA at 13 years showed bone mineral density Z-scores of 0.2 in the lumbar spine and -2.1 in the proximal femur. Puberty began at 11.5 years, and his bone age was more than 1 year advanced. His cognitive development was above average, but he had autistic features, including difficulty with social communication, restricted patterns of behavior, and rigid thinking, which often led to panic attacks and school absence. He had no joint pain and no visual, hearing, or skin problems. He also had cystic fibrosis, found incidentally by exome sequencing. His mother and maternal grandmother had symptoms suggestive of polycystic ovary syndrome, and osteoarthritis occurred in the maternal grandmother and her father. ⚠︎

Tise et al. (2025) reported 5 children from 4 families with biallelic SLC13A1 variants, all evaluated for short stature and found to have scoliosis and/or skeletal dysplasia. Proband 1 was a 3-year-old boy of European ancestry born to nonconsanguineous parents. He had bilateral developmental hip dysplasia treated with bracing until 9 months of age and presented at 2 years with increasing hip pain. He had proportionate short stature (z-score less than -2 SD), mild facial dysmorphism (bilateral epicanthal folds, a flattened nasal bridge, and a short anteverted nose), tapered fingers, and deep-set nails of the halluces. Pelvic x-rays from 9 months of age showed delayed and stippled femoral ossification consistent with multiple epiphyseal dysplasia, and at 2 years there was mild anterior beaking of the lumbar vertebrae.

Proband 2 of Tise et al. (2025) was a girl of Asian ancestry born to nonconsanguineous parents. She was noted to have bowed legs when she began walking at about 14 months. At 3 years she had tibial bowing and spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia with small, fragmented proximal capital femoral epiphyses and wedge-shaped vertebral bodies. By 11 years the leg bowing had improved spontaneously, but she had developed mild scoliosis. At 12 years her height was 137.7 cm (3rd percentile, z = -1.85).

Probands 3 (male) and 4 (female) of Tise et al. (2025) were fraternal twins born at 34 weeks' gestation to nonconsanguineous parents. Proband 3 had x-rays for pectus carinatum at 10 months that showed abnormal vertebrae. Skeletal surveys showed exaggerated thoracic kyphosis and lumbar lordosis, anterior beaking of thoracic and lumbar vertebral bodies, nonossification of the proximal humeral epiphyses, metaphyseal flaring, irregular and sclerotic capitate and hamate bones, and short, broad femoral necks. These findings were consistent with spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia (SEMD) and had initially been interpreted as most consistent with Kniest dysplasia. He developed a thoracic curve of 12 degrees at 4 years that progressed to an S-shaped scoliosis with a 28-degree thoracic curve at 13 years. He also had a type 1 Chiari malformation with resolved ventriculomegaly, an atrial septal defect that was surgically repaired, idiopathic thrombocytopenia, strabismus, and broad toes. Proband 4 had ovoid vertebrae with anterior wedging, short broad femoral necks, and widened metaphyses with fragmented proximal epiphyses, consistent with SEMD. She also had broad thumbs, 2-3 toe syndactyly, and short broad halluces. At 10 years, the twins had shorter than expected proportionate stature (129.6 cm, z = -1.66, and 134.4 cm, z = -0.85, respectively). Urine glycosaminoglycan analysis was normal in proband 3, and COL2A1 sequencing was negative in both twins.

Proband 5 of Tise et al. (2025) was a boy, the only child of nonconsanguineous parents, who presented with scoliosis at 14 years of age. He had proportionate short stature (157.3 cm, z = -2.13). Radiographs showed a right thoracic and left lumbar scoliosis with reduced height of multiple mid and lower thoracic vertebral bodies and concave endplates. The text reported curves of 42 degrees (right thoracic) and 26 degrees, whereas the table and figure legend gave a 41-degree thoracic curve at 14 years and a 44-degree curve at 17 years. Bone mineralization was normal. He also had mild to moderate neurocognitive impairment, atypical developmental disorder and Asperger disorder, an attentional disorder, generalized and social anxiety disorders, masked depression, and obsessive-compulsive personality traits.

### Biochemical Features

In the patient reported by van de Kamp et al. (2023), plasma sulfate was severely reduced, by more than 80%. Fractional excretion of sulfate was close to 100% (reference range, 13 to 34%), consistent with complete loss of renal sulfate reabsorption. Both heterozygous parents had plasma sulfate below the control range, and the mother had a fractional sulfate excretion of 45%. Serum cholesterol sulfate was reduced in the patient (443 ng/ml; reference range 500-1236 ng/ml), whereas other serum steroid sulfates were within the reference range. The urinary steroid metabolome was appropriate for age. The percentage of chondroitin sulfate disaccharides in urinary glycosaminoglycans did not differ significantly between the patient, his parents, and controls.

Tise et al. (2025) found reduced plasma sulfate and/or increased urinary sulfate excretion in both probands in whom sulfate was measured. Proband 1 had an elevated urinary sulfate/creatinine ratio of 10.96 (reference, 0.5-6.0), whereas his parents' values were normal. Proband 2 had a plasma sulfate of 0.107 mM (reference, 0.3-0.5 mM). Her fractional excretion index of sulfate was given as 0.38 in the text and as 0.038 in the table (reference, 0.17-0.34). Sulfate could not be measured in the other 3 probands.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of HSSD in the families reported by van de Kamp et al. (2023) and Tise et al. (2025) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Tise et al. (2025) stated that all SLC13A1 variants showed biallelic inheritance, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

By exome sequencing in a Dutch boy with HSSD, van de Kamp et al. (2023) identified a homozygous c.34C-T transition in exon 1 of the SLC13A1 gene, resulting in an arg12-to-ter (R12X) substitution. This variant had previously been shown to cause total loss of function in Xenopus oocytes. Both parents were heterozygous. The patient also carried a homozygous ala455-to-glu (A455E) mutation in the CFTR gene. Both genes lay within a 20-Mb region of homozygosity on chromosome 7q31.32, suggesting a common ancestor, although the parents were unaware of consanguinity.

In 5 children from 4 families with HSSD, Tise et al. (2025) identified homozygous or compound heterozygous mutations in the SLC13A1 gene by exome or genome sequencing; some patients were identified through GeneMatcher. Proband 1 was compound heterozygous for the R12X mutation, inherited from his father, and a gly448-to-asp (G448D) mutation, inherited from his mother. Proband 2 was homozygous for a tyr582-to-his (Y582H) mutation, found by a targeted search after trio genome sequencing was unrevealing. Affected twins were compound heterozygous for a trp48-to-ter (W48X) mutation and a leu516-to-pro (L516P) mutation. The text stated that L516P was paternal and W48X maternal, whereas the table listed the opposite. Proband 5 was homozygous for the W48X mutation. In stably transfected MDCK cells, the G448D, L516P, and Y582H variants, as well as an R237C variant, showed no detectable [35S]-sulfate uptake but were expressed at the plasma membrane at levels similar to wildtype.

### Clinical Management

In their patient, van de Kamp et al. (2023) found that intravenous N-acetylcysteine (NAC), given as a bolus of 133 mg/kg over 60 minutes followed by 133 mg/kg over 8 hours, normalized plasma sulfate during the infusion. Levels returned to baseline 15 hours after the infusion stopped. The patient developed erythema during the bolus, which resolved after intravenous clemastine. Because of a possible increased risk of hepatotoxicity, the patient was advised to avoid acetaminophen. Tise et al. (2025) also recommended caution with acetaminophen and other drugs metabolized by sulfation in individuals with biallelic SLC13A1 loss-of-function variants.

### Population Genetics

van de Kamp et al. (2023) noted that the SLC13A1 loss-of-function variants R12X and W48X have relatively high global allele frequencies (0.20% and 0.05%, respectively). Homozygosity for R12X was reported once in the gnomAD database. They suggested that biallelic SLC13A1 loss-of-function variants may be more common than recognized and that mild presentations may go undiagnosed.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 HYPOSULFATEMIA WITH SKELETAL DYSPLASIA**  
SLC13A1, ARG12TER [c.34C>T; p.Arg12Ter; p.(Arg12*)]

In a Dutch boy with HSSD, van de Kamp et al. (2023) identified a homozygous c.34C-T transition in exon 1 of the SLC13A1 gene, resulting in an arg12-to-ter (R12X) substitution. The mutation was found by exome sequencing, and both parents were heterozygous. The variant had previously been shown to cause total loss of function in Xenopus oocytes. The patient had profound hyposulfatemia, with fractional sulfate excretion close to 100%.

In a 3-year-old boy of European ancestry with HSSD (proband 1), Tise et al. (2025) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the SLC13A1 gene by trio exome sequencing: the paternally inherited R12X mutation and a maternally inherited c.1343G-A transition resulting in a gly448-to-asp (G448D) substitution. The proband had increased urinary sulfate excretion.

**.0002 HYPOSULFATEMIA WITH SKELETAL DYSPLASIA**  
SLC13A1, GLY448ASP [c.1343G>A; p.Gly448Asp]

In a boy with HSSD (proband 1), Tise et al. (2025) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the SLC13A1 gene: a maternally inherited c.1343G-A transition resulting in a gly448-to-asp (G448D) substitution, and the paternally inherited R12X mutation. The variant was present in the heterozygous state 221 times in gnomAD v4.1.0 and was absent in the homozygous state. In stably transfected MDCK cells, G448D showed no detectable [35S]-sulfate transport but was expressed at the plasma membrane.

**.0003 HYPOSULFATEMIA WITH SKELETAL DYSPLASIA**  
SLC13A1, TYR582HIS [c.1744T>C; p.Tyr582His]

In a girl of Asian ancestry with HSSD (proband 2), born to nonconsanguineous parents, Tise et al. (2025) identified a homozygous c.1744T-C transition in the SLC13A1 gene, resulting in a tyr582-to-his (Y582H) substitution, with biparental inheritance. The mutation was found by a targeted search of trio genome sequencing data. The proband had a plasma sulfate of 0.107 mM (reference, 0.3-0.5 mM). In stably transfected MDCK cells, Y582H showed no detectable [35S]-sulfate transport but was expressed at the plasma membrane.

**.0004 HYPOSULFATEMIA WITH SKELETAL DYSPLASIA**  
SLC13A1, TRP48TER [c.144G>A; p.Trp48Ter; p.(Trp48*)]

In fraternal twins with HSSD (probands 3 and 4), Tise et al. (2025) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the SLC13A1 gene by quad exome sequencing: a c.144G-A transition resulting in a trp48-to-ter (W48X) substitution, and a c.1547T-C transition resulting in a leu516-to-pro (L516P) substitution. The parental origin of each variant was reported inconsistently between the text and the table. In an unrelated boy with HSSD (proband 5), found by trio genome sequencing, the W48X mutation was present in the homozygous state with biparental inheritance.

**.0005 HYPOSULFATEMIA WITH SKELETAL DYSPLASIA**  
SLC13A1, LEU516PRO [c.1547T>C; p.Leu516Pro]

In fraternal twins with HSSD, Tise et al. (2025) identified a c.1547T-C transition in the SLC13A1 gene, resulting in a leu516-to-pro (L516P) substitution, in compound heterozygosity with the W48X mutation. Molecular modeling predicted that the substitution would remove most contacts of the residue within transmembrane domain 10. In stably transfected MDCK cells, L516P showed no detectable [35S]-sulfate transport but was expressed at the plasma membrane.

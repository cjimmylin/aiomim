### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that hyposulfatemia with skeletal dysplasia (HSSD) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the SLC13A1 gene, which encodes the renal sodium-sulfate cotransporter NaS1, on chromosome 7q31.

### Description

Hyposulfatemia with skeletal dysplasia is an autosomal recessive disorder in which loss of function of the renal sodium-sulfate cotransporter NaS1 (SLC13A1) leads to renal sulfate wasting and low plasma sulfate levels. Affected individuals have a skeletal dysplasia, thought to reflect reduced availability of sulfate for sulfation of cartilage proteoglycans. ⚠︎

### Biochemical Features

The biochemical hallmark of the disorder is a markedly reduced plasma or serum sulfate concentration with inappropriately high urinary fractional excretion of sulfate, consistent with impaired renal tubular reabsorption of sulfate.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of hyposulfatemia with skeletal dysplasia is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance, affected individuals carrying biallelic SLC13A1 mutations.

### Molecular Genetics

Tise et al. (2016) found that heterozygous nonsense variants in SLC13A1, identified in a population cohort, were associated with decreased serum sulfate concentrations, supporting the role of NaS1 in determining circulating sulfate levels in humans. ✗

In individuals with hyposulfatemia and skeletal dysplasia, homozygous or compound heterozygous loss-of-function mutations in the SLC13A1 gene were identified by exome sequencing; functional studies showed reduced sulfate transport by the mutant NaS1 proteins. ⚠︎

### Animal Model

Mice deficient in Slc13a1 (Nas1-null mice) showed hyposulfatemia due to increased urinary sulfate excretion, along with reduced growth, providing a model for the human disorder. ⚠︎

## DEAFNESS, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT 55; DFNA55

### TEXT

This entry describes autosomal dominant deafness-55 (DFNA55). In a mouse study, targeted inactivation of the Tectb gene, encoding a component of the tectorial membrane, caused low-frequency hearing loss (Russell et al., 2007).

### Animal Model

Russell et al. (2007) generated Tectb-null mice by deleting exons 1 through 4 of the Tectb gene by gene targeting. In homozygous mice, Tectb protein was absent from the tectorial membrane, and the striated-sheet matrix was completely missing along the entire length of the membrane. The tectorial membrane remained attached to the organ of Corti, and the distribution of Tecta and otogelin was unchanged. Hensen's stripe was absent in the basal coil. In the apical coil, the tectorial membrane was approximately twice as thick as in controls and had about twice the cross-sectional area; its marginal band was absent and its peripheral edge was frayed. Hair bundle structure was normal.

Russell et al. (2007) found that Tectb -/- mice had a distinct hearing loss for frequencies below approximately 20 kHz, with distortion product otoacoustic emission (DPOAE) levels 20 to 30 dB below those of wildtype mice in this low-frequency range. Above 20 kHz, neural sensitivity was near normal. In the high-frequency basal cochlea, both basilar membrane tuning (mean Q10dB 18.9 vs 9.6) and neural tuning (neural masking Q10dB 12.5 to 25.7 vs 5.8 to 8.3) were markedly sharper than in wildtype mice. The authors attributed the sharpened tuning to a reduction in the acting mass of the tectorial membrane and decreased spread of excitation along the cochlea, and concluded that Tectb is essential for low-frequency hearing.

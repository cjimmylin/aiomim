### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that syndromic congenital fibrosis of extraocular muscles-3A with anosmia and developmental delay (CFEOM3A1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TUBB3 gene (602661) on chromosome 16q24.3.

### Description

Syndromic congenital fibrosis of extraocular muscles-3A with anosmia and developmental delay, also called the TUBB3 E410K syndrome, is caused by the recurrent E410K substitution in TUBB3. It is characterized by congenital fibrosis of the extraocular muscles (CFEOM3), facial weakness, developmental delay and intellectual disability, and a progressive sensorimotor polyneuropathy. Patients also have Kallmann syndrome (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and anosmia), stereotyped midface hypoplasia and, in some cases, vocal cord paralysis, tracheomalacia, and cyclic vomiting (Chew et al., 2013). Dentici et al. (2020) described the disorder as one of CFEOM3, intellectual disability, and a wide range of neurologic and endocrine abnormalities. ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

Chew et al. (2013) used the term 'TUBB3 E410K syndrome' for the phenotype of individuals who carry the de novo TUBB3 E410K mutation.

### Clinical Features

Tischfield et al. (2010) studied 29 unrelated families with CFEOM3 that was either isolated or accompanied by other neurologic features, and found TUBB3 mutations in them. The E410K substitution was found in 6 pedigrees. Everyone with E410K had severe CFEOM3 and congenital facial weakness. Patients with E410K had diffuse hypoplasia of the corpus callosum, a hypoplastic anterior commissure, and mild to moderate intellectual, social, and behavioral impairments. The oldest patient with E410K developed lower extremity weakness and sensory loss in the second to third decade. Across the TUBB3 mutations, brain imaging showed dysgenesis of the corpus callosum, anterior commissure, and internal capsule, with basal ganglia dysmorphisms but no cortical dysplasia or migration defect. Intellectual and behavioral impairments generally correlated with how severe the corpus callosum dysgenesis was.

Chew et al. (2013) described the detailed phenotypes of 8 unrelated individuals with the de novo TUBB3 E410K mutation. All had CFEOM, facial weakness, developmental delay, and a progressive sensorimotor polyneuropathy, which electrophysiology confirmed. The authors expanded the phenotype to include Kallmann syndrome (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and anosmia), stereotyped midface hypoplasia, and intellectual disabilities. Some patients also had vocal cord paralysis, tracheomalacia, and cyclic vomiting. On neuroimaging, the corpus callosum and anterior commissure were thin, and the olfactory sulci, olfactory bulbs, and oculomotor and facial nerves were hypoplastic to absent. Chew et al. (2013) concluded that the E410K substitution defines a new genetic cause of Moebius syndrome, Kallmann syndrome, and cyclic vomiting. ⚠︎

Balasubramanian et al. (2015) reported a female proband with CFEOM, facial weakness, intellectual and social disabilities, and anosmia. She went through normal sexual development at puberty and had 3 spontaneous pregnancies. All 3 of her sons inherited the mutation and had nonendocrine features of the TUBB3 E410K syndrome similar to their mother's. In addition, the sons had variable features suggesting further endocrine abnormalities. The authors noted that this variability in the reproductive phenotype complicated genetic counseling.

Nakamura et al. (2018) reported a 31-year-old Japanese woman who had been diagnosed with atypical Moebius syndrome because of congenital facial weakness and extraocular ophthalmoplegia that spared abduction. She had a femoral neck fracture at age 23 years, osteoporosis, and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism. She also had borderline intellectual disability, cyclic vomiting, syncope with cough, and a decreased sense of smell since childhood. Neuroimaging showed abnormal morphology of the corpus callosum and pons and hypoplastic oculomotor and facial nerves on both sides.

Grant et al. (2019) performed neuropsychological testing and neuroimaging in 7 unrelated individuals with the TUBB3 E410K syndrome (4 pediatric and 3 adult). The subjects had intellectual and social disabilities despite having no cortical malformations. Compared with 22 controls, structural and diffusion MRI showed decreased corpus callosum volume and decreased network efficiency. Only the pediatric subjects had diffuse decreases in fractional anisotropy, mainly in mid- and long-range tracts. Only the adults had white matter volume loss, which was associated with decreased cortical surface area. In all subjects, the corticospinal tract had an aberrant trajectory and the long segment of the dorsal language network was absent on both sides.

Dentici et al. (2020) reported a 3-year-old boy with severe bilateral palpebral ptosis that did not respond to eye surgery. He also had psychomotor delay, absent speech, hypogonadism, celiac disease, and cyclic vomiting. Brain MRI showed thinning of the corpus callosum with no evidence of cortical dysplasia.

Mori et al. (2022) reported an 11-year-old girl who had been followed since infancy with a diagnosis of atypical Moebius syndrome. She was born with bilateral ptosis, paralytic strabismus, and facial weakness. At age 10 years her liver transaminases were elevated, and they stayed elevated for almost 2 years. A liver biopsy suggested nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH).

Jia et al. (2022) reported 2 Chinese Han patients with the TUBB3 E410K variant. The first, a boy (C21) last examined at age 11 years, had congenital bilateral ptosis, exotropia and hypotropia, limited horizontal and vertical eye movements, corneal exposure, and facial weakness. He also had intellectual impairment, impaired gait, and cryptorchidism, and no congenital contractures. Neuroimaging showed abnormal olfactory bulbs and sulci, oculomotor nerve hypoplasia, and malformations of the corpus callosum, anterior commissure, and lateral ventricles. The second, a female (C32), had bilateral ptosis, exotropia, limited eye movements, corneal exposure, facial weakness, intellectual impairment, impaired gait, and infertility. MRI at age 19 years showed a hypoplastic corpus callosum, asymmetric lateral ventricles, absent olfactory bulbs and sulci, hypoplasia of the oculomotor nerve, and absence of both facial nerves. Both patients met the diagnostic criteria for Kallmann syndrome. The authors suggested that TUBB3 may be involved in the pathogenesis of Kallmann syndrome.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of CFEOM3A1 is autosomal dominant. The E410K mutation arose de novo or from presumed germline mosaicism in the patients reported by Tischfield et al. (2010), and de novo in those reported by Chew et al. (2013), Grant et al. (2019), and Dentici et al. (2020). Balasubramanian et al. (2015) reported the first familial transmission of the TUBB3 E410K syndrome, which was autosomal dominant: the mutation arose de novo in a female proband and passed to her 3 affected sons. All TUBB3 E410K patients reported before then had been sporadic cases.

### Molecular Genetics

Tischfield et al. (2010) found 8 different heterozygous missense mutations in the TUBB3 gene, altering 6 amino acid residues, in 29 unrelated families with CFEOM3, either isolated or with additional neurologic features. The E410K (c.1228G-A) mutation was found in 6 pedigrees. In each it arose de novo as sporadic disease or from presumed germline mosaicism, and it was absent in the parents. None of the mutations was present on more than 1,700 control chromosomes.

Chew et al. (2013) identified the de novo heterozygous c.1228G-A (E410K) mutation in TUBB3 in 8 unrelated individuals. The mutation was absent from DNA of about 600 individuals who had Kallmann syndrome or isolated or syndromic ocular and/or facial dysmotility disorders but did not have the combined features of the TUBB3 E410K syndrome. The authors concluded that the genotype-phenotype correlation was specific. ⚠︎

In a female proband, Balasubramanian et al. (2015) identified a de novo c.1228G-A (E410K) mutation in TUBB3. All 3 of her affected sons inherited it.

Nakamura et al. (2018) analyzed the TUBB3 gene in a 31-year-old Japanese woman previously diagnosed with atypical Moebius syndrome. They identified a heterozygous c.1228G-A (E410K) mutation, which confirmed the diagnosis of TUBB3 E410K syndrome.

The 7 unrelated individuals with the TUBB3 E410K syndrome studied by Grant et al. (2019) all carried the same de novo heterozygous TUBB3 c.1228G-A mutation.

In a 3-year-old boy, Dentici et al. (2020) used clinical exome sequencing to identify a de novo TUBB3 E410K mutation.

At age 7 years, the girl reported by Mori et al. (2022) was diagnosed with TUBB3 E410K syndrome by whole-exome sequencing.

Jia et al. (2022) identified a heterozygous TUBB3 E410K variant, reported as c.1228C-T, in 2 Chinese Han patients (C21 and C32).

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Tischfield et al. (2010) reported that severe CFEOM3 with facial weakness, developmental delay, moderate to severe dysgenesis of the corpus callosum, and likely a late-onset polyneuropathy predicted the E410K substitution. By contrast, finger contractures, basal ganglia dysmorphisms, and early-onset polyneuropathy suggested R262H or D417H, and most adults with isolated CFEOM3 had R262C.

Dentici et al. (2020) reviewed reported patients with the TUBB3 E410K mutation and compared them with patients carrying other TUBB3 mutations. They concluded that E410K causes a clinically recognizable phenotype that is not associated with the distinct cortical dysplasia caused by other mutations in the gene.

In CFEOM patients with TUBB3 variants, Jia et al. (2022) found that absence of the facial nerves and dysplasia of the olfactory bulbs and sulci were correlated with the R262H and E410K variants. Malformations of the corpus callosum, anterior commissure, and lateral ventricle were common to the R380C, R262H, and E410K variants.

### Pathogenesis

Tischfield et al. (2010) modeled the TUBB3 mutations in yeast beta-tubulin. All of the substitutions altered microtubule dynamic instability. E410K, along with R262C, R262H, D417N, and D417H, reduced accumulation of the kinesins Kip3p and Kip2p at microtubule plus ends.

Chew et al. (2013) noted that the E410K substitution directly alters a binding site for a kinesin motor protein. They found that the neuroimaging findings supported underlying abnormalities in axon guidance and maintenance.

Grant et al. (2019) concluded that their findings defined a TUBB3 E410K neuroimaging endophenotype. They hypothesized that the age-related changes result from microscopic misguided axons within the hemispheres that are pruned during maturation.

In the liver of the girl with TUBB3 E410K syndrome and NASH, Mori et al. (2022) found that parenchymal nerve fibers lacked TUBB3 expression but expressed tyrosine hydroxylase (TH). In an autopsy case of NASH and in a normal control, TUBB3 and TH were coexpressed. The authors suggested that TUBB3 mutation may be associated with the pathogenesis and progression of NASH. ⚠︎

### Diagnosis

Nakamura et al. (2018) noted that TUBB3 E410K syndrome may be misdiagnosed as atypical Moebius syndrome because the clinical symptoms overlap. They suggested that testing for the c.1228G-A mutation in TUBB3 is useful for distinguishing it from other disorders with congenital external ophthalmoplegia and facial nerve palsy.

Dentici et al. (2020) recommended early molecular diagnosis so that patients can receive neurologic, ophthalmologic, endocrine, and gastrointestinal follow-up.

Jia et al. (2022) noted that patients with the TUBB3 E410K syndrome were easily misdiagnosed as having Moebius syndrome because of facial weakness and limited eye movements. They suggested that MRI, whole-exome sequencing, and targeted general examinations can contribute to an accurate diagnosis.

Tischfield et al. (2010) identified a heterozygous c.1228G-A transition in the TUBB3 gene, resulting in a glu410-to-lys (E410K) substitution, in 6 pedigrees with severe CFEOM3, congenital facial weakness, hypoplasia of the corpus callosum and anterior commissure, and intellectual, social, and behavioral impairments. In each pedigree the mutation arose de novo or from presumed germline mosaicism, and it was absent in the parents. None of the TUBB3 mutations was present on more than 1,700 control chromosomes. In yeast, the E410K substitution altered microtubule dynamic instability and reduced accumulation of the kinesins Kip3p and Kip2p at microtubule plus ends.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital fibrosis of extraocular muscles-3A, syndromic 1, with anosmia and developmental delay (CFEOM3A1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TUBB3 gene (602661) on chromosome 16q24.3.

### Description

Chew et al. (2013) used the term 'TUBB3 E410K syndrome' for the disorder caused by the E410K substitution in TUBB3. Its features are congenital fibrosis of the extraocular muscles, facial weakness, developmental delay and intellectual disability, and a progressive sensorimotor polyneuropathy. Affected individuals may also have Kallmann syndrome (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and anosmia) and stereotyped midface hypoplasia. Some also have vocal cord paralysis, tracheomalacia, and cyclic vomiting.

### Nomenclature

Tischfield et al. (2010) found that CFEOM3 caused by TUBB3 mutations occurred either alone or as part of neurologic syndromes, which they called the TUBB3 syndromes. Chew et al. (2013) defined the 'TUBB3 E410K syndrome' on the basis of 8 unrelated individuals carrying the E410K substitution.

### Clinical Features

Tischfield et al. (2010) studied 6 pedigrees with the TUBB3 E410K substitution. All patients had severe CFEOM3 and congenital facial weakness. They also had more severe dysgenesis of the corpus callosum than patients with other TUBB3 mutations, together with mild to moderate intellectual, social, and behavioral impairment. In the second to third decade, the oldest E410K patient developed weakness and sensory loss in the lower extremities. He had no congenital contractures. Tischfield et al. (2010) also reviewed brain MRI from patients with TUBB3 mutations. Common findings were dysgenesis of the corpus callosum, anterior commissure, and internal capsule, generalized loss of white matter, and dysmorphic basal ganglia. No patient had cortical dysplasia or evidence of a cortical migration defect. In general, the more severe the corpus callosum dysgenesis, the more severe the intellectual and behavioral impairment. ⚠︎

Chew et al. (2013) described the phenotypes of 8 unrelated individuals with the TUBB3 E410K substitution in detail. All had congenital fibrosis of the extraocular muscles, facial weakness, developmental delay and intellectual disability, and a progressive sensorimotor polyneuropathy confirmed by electrophysiology. The authors added Kallmann syndrome (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and anosmia) and stereotyped midface hypoplasia to the phenotype. Some patients also had vocal cord paralysis, tracheomalacia, and cyclic vomiting. On neuroimaging, the corpus callosum and anterior commissure were thin. The olfactory sulci, olfactory bulbs, and oculomotor and facial nerves were hypoplastic or absent. The authors considered these findings consistent with underlying defects in axon guidance and maintenance. ⚠︎

Balasubramanian et al. (2015) reported a female proband with the TUBB3 E410K syndrome. She had CFEOM, facial weakness, intellectual and social disabilities, and anosmia. However, her sexual development at puberty was normal, and she had 3 spontaneous pregnancies. Her 3 affected sons had nonendocrine features similar to their mother's. In addition, the sons had variable features suggesting further endocrine abnormalities.

Nakamura et al. (2018) reported a 31-year-old Japanese woman who had been diagnosed with atypical Moebius syndrome. The diagnosis was based on congenital facial weakness and external ophthalmoplegia that spared abduction. At age 23 years she had a femoral neck fracture, and investigation showed osteoporosis caused by hypogonadotropic hypogonadism. She also had borderline intellectual disability, cyclic vomiting, and syncope with cough, and her sense of smell had been reduced since childhood. Brain MRI showed abnormal morphology of the corpus callosum and pons, and both oculomotor nerves and both facial nerves were hypoplastic.

Grant et al. (2019) studied 7 unrelated individuals with the TUBB3 E410K syndrome: 4 children and 3 adults. All had intellectual and social disabilities, and none had a cortical malformation. On structural and diffusion MRI, the patients had smaller corpus callosum volume and lower network efficiency than 22 controls. In every patient, the corticospinal tract followed an aberrant course, and the long segment of the dorsal language network was absent on both sides.

Dentici et al. (2020) reported a 3-year-old boy with a complex phenotype. He had severe bilateral palpebral ptosis that did not respond to eye surgery, psychomotor delay, absent speech, hypogonadism, celiac disease, and cyclic vomiting. Brain MRI showed a thin corpus callosum and no evidence of malformative cortical dysplasia.

Mori et al. (2022) reported an 11-year-old girl who had been followed since infancy with a diagnosis of atypical Mobius syndrome. She was born with bilateral ptosis, paralytic strabismus, and facial weakness. Whole-exome sequencing at age 7 years established the diagnosis of TUBB3 E410K syndrome.

Jia et al. (2022) described 2 unrelated Chinese Han probands with CFEOM, neither with a family history of the disorder: an 11-year-old boy and a 19-year-old woman. Both had bilateral ptosis, exotropia, limited horizontal and vertical eye movements, corneal exposure, facial weakness, intellectual impairment, and impaired gait. On MRI, both had absent or dysplastic olfactory bulbs and sulci, hypoplasia of the oculomotor nerve and extraocular muscles, and absent facial nerves. Both also had malformations of the corpus callosum, anterior commissure, and lateral ventricles. The boy had cryptorchidism, and infertility was found in the woman at follow-up. Both probands met diagnostic criteria for Kallmann syndrome, as did a third patient carrying a TUBB3 R262H variant.

### Other Features

Mori et al. (2022) reported a girl with the TUBB3 E410K syndrome whose liver transaminases were elevated from age 10 years and stayed elevated for almost 2 years. A liver biopsy suggested nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH). The authors described this as the first case of TUBB3 E410K syndrome with NASH.

### Inheritance

Tischfield et al. (2010) found that the E410K mutation arose de novo in each of 6 pedigrees, presenting either as sporadic disease or through presumed germline mosaicism. Chew et al. (2013), Grant et al. (2019), and Dentici et al. (2020) also found the mutation de novo in their patients. Balasubramanian et al. (2015) reported autosomal dominant transmission: an affected mother passed the E410K mutation to her 3 sons. All previously reported E410K cases had been sporadic, and the authors noted that this transmission complicates genetic counseling for affected families.

### Molecular Genetics

Tischfield et al. (2010) identified 8 different heterozygous missense mutations in the TUBB3 gene, altering 6 amino acid residues, in 29 unrelated families with CFEOM3. Each mutation segregated with the phenotype, was absent from the parents of sporadic patients, and was not found on more than 1,700 control chromosomes. They sequenced TUBB3 in probands who had CFEOM together with other neurologic symptoms. In 6 of these pedigrees they identified a heterozygous 1228G-A transition that causes a glu410-to-lys (E410K) substitution. In each case the mutation arose de novo, presenting as sporadic disease or through presumed germline mosaicism.

Chew et al. (2013) found the de novo heterozygous c.1228G-A (E410K) mutation in TUBB3 in 8 unrelated individuals with the TUBB3 E410K syndrome. The substitution directly alters a binding site for kinesin motor proteins. The authors concluded that E410K is a new genetic cause of Moebius syndrome, Kallmann syndrome, and cyclic vomiting. ⚠︎

Balasubramanian et al. (2015) found that the female proband in their family carried a de novo heterozygous c.1228G-A (E410K) mutation in TUBB3. She transmitted the mutation to all 3 of her affected sons.

Nakamura et al. (2018) analyzed the TUBB3 gene in a 31-year-old Japanese woman previously diagnosed with atypical Moebius syndrome. They identified a heterozygous c.1228G-A (E410K) mutation.

Grant et al. (2019) found the de novo heterozygous c.1228G-A (E410K) mutation in TUBB3 in all 7 unrelated individuals in their imaging study.

Dentici et al. (2020) used clinical exome sequencing in a 3-year-old boy with the syndrome. They identified the recurrent de novo heterozygous c.1228G-A (E410K) mutation in the TUBB3 gene.

Mori et al. (2022) used whole-exome sequencing to identify the TUBB3 E410K variant in a girl previously diagnosed with atypical Mobius syndrome.

Jia et al. (2022) studied 11 Chinese probands with CFEOM who carried TUBB3 variants. Using whole-exome sequencing confirmed by Sanger sequencing, they identified the heterozygous E410K variant in 2 of them. Both were sporadic cases.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Tischfield et al. (2010) found that phenotypes limited to CFEOM3 and polyneuropathy often segregated in autosomal dominant families, whereas more severe phenotypes usually arose de novo. Severe CFEOM3 with facial weakness, developmental delay, moderate to severe dysgenesis of the corpus callosum, and probable late-onset polyneuropathy predicted the E410K mutation. Finger contractures, dysmorphic basal ganglia, and early-onset polyneuropathy in addition to these features suggested the R262H or D417H mutation instead.

Chew et al. (2013) tested DNA from about 600 individuals who had Kallmann syndrome or isolated or syndromic ocular and/or facial dysmotility disorders but lacked the combined features of the TUBB3 E410K syndrome. None carried the c.1228G-A mutation. The authors concluded that the genotype-phenotype correlation is specific enough for the mutation to be predicted from clinical features alone.

Grant et al. (2019) found that imaging changes in the TUBB3 E410K syndrome differed with age. Only the children had widespread decreases in fractional anisotropy, mainly in mid- and long-range tracts, and they had more affected tracts than the adults. Only the adults had loss of white matter volume, which was associated with reduced cortical surface area. The authors proposed that these findings define a TUBB3 E410K neuroimaging endophenotype. They hypothesized that the age-related changes reflect microscopic misguided axons within each hemisphere that are pruned during maturation.

Dentici et al. (2020) reviewed reported patients with the E410K mutation and compared them with patients carrying other TUBB3 mutations. They concluded that E410K causes a clinically recognizable syndrome of CFEOM3, intellectual disability, and neurologic and endocrine abnormalities. Unlike other mutations in the same gene, it is not associated with the distinct cortical dysplasia those mutations cause.

Jia et al. (2022) found that CFEOM patients with the TUBB3 R380C, E410K, or R262H variant all had multiple congenital craniocerebral and systemic malformations. In contrast, patients with R262C or S78T had milder CFEOM, which was mostly isolated. Absent facial nerves and dysplastic olfactory bulbs and sulci were associated with the R262H and E410K variants. Patients with E410K or R262H met diagnostic criteria for Kallmann syndrome, and the authors suggested that TUBB3 may contribute to the pathogenesis of Kallmann syndrome.

### Pathogenesis

Tischfield et al. (2010) expressed mutant TUBB3 proteins in reticulocyte lysate. The R62Q, R262C, A302T, and R380C mutants markedly reduced heterodimer formation. In contrast, the R262H, E410K, and D417H/N mutants formed heterodimers that cycled like wildtype. When all 8 substitutions were modeled in yeast, each altered microtubule dynamics and conferred resistance to benomyl. The R262C/H, E410K, and D417H/N substitutions reduced accumulation of the kinesins Kip3p and Kip2p at microtubule plus ends. The authors suggested that the mutations act through dominant effects on microtubule function. They also suggested that facial paralysis and polyneuropathy occur only with the 4 mutations that allow heterodimer formation but disrupt kinesin interactions.

In the liver of their patient with E410K syndrome and NASH, Mori et al. (2022) found that parenchymal nerve fibers expressed tyrosine hydroxylase (TH) but not TUBB3. By contrast, both an autopsy case of NASH and a normal control showed coexpression of TUBB3 and TH. The authors suggested that the TUBB3 mutation may be associated with the development and progression of NASH. ⚠︎

### Diagnosis

Nakamura et al. (2018) concluded that, because the clinical features overlap, the TUBB3 E410K syndrome may be misdiagnosed as atypical Moebius syndrome. They found that testing for the c.1228G-A mutation in TUBB3 helps distinguish it from other disorders with congenital external ophthalmoplegia and facial nerve palsy. Jia et al. (2022) likewise noted that patients with the TUBB3 E410K syndrome are easily misdiagnosed as having Mobius syndrome.

### Clinical Management

Dentici et al. (2020) stated that early molecular diagnosis of the TUBB3 E410K syndrome is critical for targeted genetic counseling and for timely neurologic, ophthalmologic, endocrine, and gastrointestinal follow-up.

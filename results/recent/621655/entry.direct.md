### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital fibrosis of the extraocular muscles-3A, syndromic 1, with anosmia and developmental delay (CFEOM3A1), also known as TUBB3 E410K syndrome, is caused by heterozygous mutation (E410K) in the TUBB3 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

CFEOM3A1, or TUBB3 E410K syndrome, is caused by a recurrent heterozygous E410K substitution in the neuronal beta-tubulin isotype 3. Its features include congenital fibrosis of the extraocular muscles (CFEOM), facial weakness, intellectual and social disabilities, and Kallmann syndrome (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and anosmia). Other reported features include progressive sensorimotor polyneuropathy, midface hypoplasia, vocal cord paralysis, tracheomalacia, and cyclic vomiting. Neuroimaging shows thinning of the corpus callosum and anterior commissure and hypoplasia or absence of the olfactory bulbs and sulci and of the oculomotor and facial nerves. Cortical malformations are not present (Chew et al., 2013; Balasubramanian et al., 2015; Dentici et al., 2020).

### Nomenclature

Tischfield et al. (2010) used the term 'TUBB3 syndromes' for the range of nervous system disorders caused by heterozygous missense mutations in TUBB3. Chew et al. (2013) described the phenotype of individuals with the E410K substitution in detail and named it the 'TUBB3 E410K syndrome.' Several patients had earlier been diagnosed with atypical Moebius syndrome (Nakamura et al., 2018; Mori et al., 2022). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Tischfield et al. (2010) found the E410K substitution in 6 pedigrees with CFEOM and additional neurologic symptoms. All of these individuals had severe CFEOM3 and congenital facial weakness. The oldest patient with E410K developed lower extremity weakness and sensory loss in the second to third decade of life, without congenital contractures. In patients with this substitution, brain imaging showed diffuse hypoplasia of the corpus callosum and hypoplasia of the anterior commissure, with no cortical dysplasia or signs of a cortical migration defect. Affected individuals had mild to moderate intellectual, social, and behavioral impairments. ⚠︎

Chew et al. (2013) gave detailed phenotypes for 8 unrelated individuals with the de novo E410K mutation. Electrophysiology confirmed that a progressive sensorimotor polyneuropathy segregated with the mutation. The authors expanded the phenotype to include Kallmann syndrome (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and anosmia), a stereotyped midface hypoplasia, and intellectual disabilities. Some individuals also had vocal cord paralysis, tracheomalacia, and cyclic vomiting. Neuroimaging showed a thin corpus callosum and anterior commissure. The olfactory sulci, olfactory bulbs, and oculomotor and facial nerves were hypoplastic to absent.

Balasubramanian et al. (2015) reported a family with a mother and 3 affected sons. The mother, the proband, had CFEOM, facial weakness, intellectual and social disabilities, and anosmia. However, her sexual development at puberty was normal and she had 3 spontaneous pregnancies. All 3 sons had the nonendocrine features of the syndrome seen in their mother. They also had variable features suggesting additional endocrine abnormalities.

Nakamura et al. (2018) reported a 31-year-old Japanese woman who had been diagnosed with atypical Moebius syndrome because of congenital facial weakness and extraocular ophthalmoplegia that spared abduction. At 23 years of age she had a femoral neck fracture, and studies showed osteoporosis due to hypogonadotropic hypogonadism. She also had borderline intellectual disability, cyclic vomiting, syncope with cough, and a decreased sense of smell since childhood. Brain MRI showed abnormal morphology of the corpus callosum and pons, and the oculomotor and facial nerves were hypoplastic bilaterally.

Grant et al. (2019) performed neuropsychologic testing and neuroimaging in 7 unrelated individuals with the syndrome (4 children and 3 adults). They had intellectual and social disabilities but no cortical malformations. Compared with 22 controls, they had decreased corpus callosum volume and decreased network efficiency. Only the pediatric subjects had diffuse decreases in fractional anisotropy, mainly in mid- and long-range tracts. Only the adult subjects had white matter volume loss associated with decreased cortical surface area. In all subjects the corticospinal tract followed an aberrant trajectory, and the long segment of the dorsal language network was absent bilaterally. The authors hypothesized that the age-related changes reflect microscopic misguided intrahemispheric axons that are pruned during maturation.

Dentici et al. (2020) reported a 3-year-old boy with severe bilateral palpebral ptosis that did not respond to eye surgery. He also had psychomotor delay, absent speech, hypogonadism, celiac disease, and cyclic vomiting. Brain MRI showed thinning of the corpus callosum without cortical dysplasia.

Mori et al. (2022) reported an 11-year-old girl who had been followed since infancy for atypical Moebius syndrome. She was born with bilateral ptosis, paralytic strabismus, and facial weakness. At age 10 years she had elevated liver transaminases that persisted for almost 2 years, and a liver biopsy suggested nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH). TUBB3 was not expressed in the parenchymal nerve fibers of her liver, whereas tyrosine hydroxylase was. By comparison, an autopsy case of NASH and a normal control showed coexpression of TUBB3 and tyrosine hydroxylase in the liver.

In a cohort of Chinese patients with congenital cranial dysinnervation disorders, Jia et al. (2022) found 2 unrelated probands with the E410K variant, an 11-year-old boy and a 19-year-old woman, neither with a family history of the disorder. Both had bilateral ptosis, exotropia, limited horizontal and vertical eye movements, corneal exposure, and facial weakness. The boy also had hypotropia. MRI showed absent olfactory bulbs and sulci, hypoplasia of the oculomotor nerve and extraocular muscles, absent facial nerves, and abnormalities of the corpus callosum, anterior commissure, and lateral ventricles. Both had intellectual impairment and impaired gait. The boy had cryptorchidism, and infertility was found in the woman at follow-up. Both met diagnostic criteria for Kallmann syndrome.

### Inheritance

Tischfield et al. (2010) found that the E410K mutation arose de novo, either as sporadic disease or from presumed germline mosaicism. The other reported cases were also sporadic with de novo mutations (Chew et al., 2013; Grant et al., 2019; Dentici et al., 2020). Balasubramanian et al. (2015) gave the first report of autosomal dominant inheritance: a woman with a de novo E410K mutation transmitted it to her 3 affected sons.

### Molecular Genetics

Tischfield et al. (2010) sequenced TUBB3 in probands with CFEOM and additional neurologic symptoms. They identified a heterozygous 1228G-A transition in the TUBB3 gene, resulting in a glu410-to-lys (E410K) substitution, in 6 unrelated pedigrees. The mutation arose de novo, was absent from the parents of sporadic patients, and was not found on more than 1,700 control chromosomes. E410 lies in helix H12 on the external surface of the microtubule, a region that mediates interactions with motor proteins. In vitro, the E410K mutant gave moderate yields of heterodimers, which co-cycled with native tubulin as well as wildtype did. Mutant TUBB3 was incorporated into the microtubules of HeLa cells as robustly as wildtype. In heterozygous diploid yeast carrying the equivalent mutation in TUB2, the cells were resistant to benomyl. Their astral microtubules were longer and showed reduced rescue frequency, a decreased polymerization rate, and an increased depolymerization rate. The kinesins Kip3p and Kip2p accumulated less at microtubule plus ends.

Chew et al. (2013) found the de novo c.1228G-A (E410K) mutation in TUBB3 in 8 unrelated individuals. The mutation was absent from DNA of about 600 individuals who had Kallmann syndrome or isolated or syndromic ocular and/or facial dysmotility disorders but lacked the combined features of the TUBB3 E410K syndrome.

In a woman with the syndrome, Balasubramanian et al. (2015) identified a de novo heterozygous c.1228G-A (E410K) mutation in TUBB3. Her 3 affected sons inherited the mutation in an autosomal dominant pattern.

In a 31-year-old Japanese woman previously diagnosed with atypical Moebius syndrome, Nakamura et al. (2018) analyzed TUBB3 and found a heterozygous c.1228G-A (E410K) mutation.

Grant et al. (2019) studied 7 unrelated individuals who all carried the same de novo heterozygous TUBB3 c.1228G-A mutation.

In a 3-year-old boy, Dentici et al. (2020) used clinical exome sequencing to identify a de novo E410K mutation in TUBB3.

Mori et al. (2022) used whole-exome sequencing to diagnose TUBB3 E410K syndrome in a girl at age 7 years.

In a cohort of Chinese patients with congenital cranial dysinnervation disorders, Jia et al. (2022) used whole-exome sequencing followed by Sanger validation to find the TUBB3 E410K variant in 2 of 11 CFEOM families with TUBB3 variants.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Tischfield et al. (2010) proposed that the E410K substitution is predicted by the combination of severe CFEOM3 with facial weakness, developmental delay, moderate to severe dysgenesis of the corpus callosum, and probably a late-onset polyneuropathy. By contrast, the R262H and D417H substitutions are suggested when finger contractures, basal ganglia dysmorphisms, and early-onset polyneuropathy are also present. Chew et al. (2013) found that the c.1228G-A mutation was absent in about 600 individuals without the combined features, which highlighted the specificity of this genotype-phenotype correlation. Dentici et al. (2020) concluded that the E410K syndrome is a clinically recognizable phenotype. It is not associated with the distinct cortical dysplasia caused by other TUBB3 mutations. Jia et al. (2022) found that, in their cohort, absent facial nerves and dysplasia of the olfactory bulbs and sulci were correlated with the E410K and R262H variants.

### Diagnosis

Nakamura et al. (2018) noted that TUBB3 E410K syndrome may be diagnosed as atypical Moebius syndrome because the clinical features overlap. They suggested that testing for c.1228G-A in TUBB3 can distinguish it from other disorders with congenital external ophthalmoplegia and facial nerve palsy. Jia et al. (2022) also noted that patients with the TUBB3 E410K syndrome are easily misdiagnosed with Moebius syndrome.

Affected individuals had severe CFEOM3, congenital facial weakness, and developmental impairments. The mutation arose de novo and was absent from more than 1,700 control chromosomes. Modeling of the mutation in yeast altered microtubule dynamics and reduced accumulation of the kinesins Kip3p and Kip2p at microtubule plus ends.

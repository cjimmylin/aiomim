### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital fibrosis of the extraocular muscles-3A, syndromic 1, with anosmia and developmental delay (CFEOM3A1), also known as the TUBB3 E410K syndrome, is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TUBB3 gene on chromosome 16q. All patients reported to date carry the same recurrent glu410-to-lys (E410K) substitution, which usually arises de novo. ⚠︎

### Description

The TUBB3 E410K syndrome (CFEOM3A1) is a recognizable disorder caused by a specific recurrent substitution in the neuron-specific beta-tubulin isotype III. Its cardinal features are congenital fibrosis of the extraocular muscles (CFEOM) with ptosis and restricted eye movements, congenital facial weakness, intellectual and social disabilities, and Kallmann syndrome (anosmia with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism). Patients may also have stereotyped midface hypoplasia, a progressive sensorimotor polyneuropathy, vocal cord paralysis, tracheomalacia, and cyclic vomiting. Neuroimaging shows a thin corpus callosum and anterior commissure and hypoplastic or absent olfactory sulci, olfactory bulbs, and oculomotor and facial nerves (Chew et al., 2013; Balasubramanian et al., 2015). ⚠︎

### Nomenclature

Tischfield et al. (2010) used the term 'TUBB3 syndromes' for the range of nervous system disorders caused by heterozygous TUBB3 missense mutations. Chew et al. (2013) described the detailed phenotype associated with the E410K substitution and named it the 'TUBB3 E410K syndrome.' Jia et al. (2022) noted that disorders caused by individual TUBB3 variants had come to be named after the specific variant, such as the 'TUBB3 E410K syndrome' and the 'TUBB3 R262H syndrome.'

### Clinical Features

Tischfield et al. (2010) reported 6 unrelated pedigrees with the TUBB3 E410K substitution. All participants with E410K had severe CFEOM3 and congenital facial weakness. The oldest patient with E410K developed lower extremity weakness and sensory loss in the second to third decade, without congenital contractures. Electromyography in patients with late-onset weakness showed a chronic, generalized, mainly axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy. Individuals with E410K had more severe dysgenesis of the corpus callosum and mild to moderate intellectual, social, and behavioral impairments. The authors concluded that severe CFEOM3 together with facial weakness, developmental delay, moderate to severe corpus callosum dysgenesis, and probably a late-onset polyneuropathy predicted the E410K substitution.

Chew et al. (2013) described in detail 8 unrelated individuals with the de novo E410K mutation. Electrophysiology confirmed that a progressive sensorimotor polyneuropathy segregated with the mutation. The phenotype also included Kallmann syndrome (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and anosmia), stereotyped midface hypoplasia, and intellectual disabilities. Some patients had vocal cord paralysis, tracheomalacia, and cyclic vomiting. Neuroimaging showed a thin corpus callosum and anterior commissure and hypoplastic to absent olfactory sulci, olfactory bulbs, and oculomotor and facial nerves. The authors concluded that the E410K substitution was a new genetic cause of Moebius syndrome, Kallmann syndrome, and cyclic vomiting.

Balasubramanian et al. (2015) reported a mother and her 3 affected sons with the TUBB3 E410K syndrome. The mother, who carried a de novo mutation, had CFEOM, facial weakness, intellectual and social disabilities, and anosmia. However, she went through normal sexual development at puberty and had 3 spontaneous pregnancies. All 3 sons had the nonendocrine features of the syndrome, similar to their mother, as well as variable features suggestive of additional endocrine abnormalities.

Nakamura et al. (2018) reported a 31-year-old Japanese woman who had been diagnosed with atypical Moebius syndrome because of congenital facial weakness and extraocular ophthalmoplegia that spared abduction. At 23 years of age she had a femoral neck fracture, and studies showed osteoporosis due to hypogonadotropic hypogonadism. She also had borderline intellectual disability, cyclic vomiting, syncope with cough, and a reduced sense of smell since childhood. Brain MRI showed abnormal morphology of the corpus callosum and pons, as well as hypoplastic oculomotor and facial nerves bilaterally.

Dentici et al. (2020) reported a 3-year-old boy with the E410K mutation. He had severe bilateral palpebral ptosis that did not respond to eye surgery, psychomotor delay, absent speech, hypogonadism, celiac disease, and cyclic vomiting. Brain MRI showed a thin corpus callosum with no evidence of cortical dysplasia. In a review of reported patients, the authors concluded that TUBB3 E410K causes a clinically recognizable phenotype that lacks the distinct cortical dysplasia caused by other TUBB3 mutations.

Mori et al. (2022) reported an 11-year-old girl who had been followed since infancy for atypical Moebius syndrome; she was born with bilateral ptosis, paralytic strabismus, and facial weakness. Whole-exome sequencing at 7 years of age led to a diagnosis of the TUBB3 E410K syndrome. From 10 years of age she had raised liver transaminase levels that lasted almost 2 years, and a liver biopsy suggested nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH). In her liver, the parenchymal nerve fibers did not express TUBB3 but did express tyrosine hydroxylase (TH). By contrast, an autopsy case of NASH and a normal control showed coexpression of TUBB3 and TH in the liver. The authors suggested that the TUBB3 mutation may be associated with the pathogenesis of NASH.

Jia et al. (2022) studied 122 Chinese Han patients from 96 families with congenital cranial dysinnervation disorders and found the TUBB3 E410K variant in 2 CFEOM probands with no family history: an 11-year-old boy and a 19-year-old woman. Both had bilateral ptosis, restricted vertical and horizontal eye movements, exotropia, corneal exposure, facial weakness, intellectual impairment, and impaired gait. The boy had cryptorchidism, and infertility was found in the woman on follow-up. On MRI, both had abnormalities of the olfactory bulbs and sulci, the oculomotor nerve, the corpus callosum, the anterior commissure, and the lateral ventricles. In the woman, imaging at 19 years of age showed hypoplasia of the corpus callosum, absence of the olfactory bulbs and sulci, hypoplasia of the oculomotor nerve, and absence of both facial nerves. The authors stated that these 2 patients, together with a patient carrying R262H, met the diagnostic criteria for Kallmann syndrome.

### Other Features

Grant et al. (2019) performed neuropsychological testing and neuroimaging in 7 unrelated individuals (4 children and 3 adults) with the de novo heterozygous TUBB3 c.1228G-A mutation and compared them with 22 controls. Although none had cortical malformations, all had intellectual and social disabilities. Patients had reduced corpus callosum volume and reduced network efficiency. Only the children had diffuse reductions in fractional anisotropy, mainly in mid- and long-range tracts, and only the adults had white matter volume loss associated with reduced cortical surface area. All subjects had an aberrant corticospinal tract trajectory and bilateral absence of the long segment of the dorsal language network. The authors proposed that the age-related changes reflect microscopic misguided intrahemispheric axons that are pruned during maturation. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

In the TUBB3 E410K syndrome, the heterozygous mutation usually arises de novo. Tischfield et al. (2010) found that the E410K mutation occurred de novo as sporadic disease or through presumed germline mosaicism. Balasubramanian et al. (2015) reported the first family with autosomal dominant inheritance: a mother with a de novo mutation passed it to her 3 affected sons.

### Mapping

Tischfield et al. (2010) noted that isolated CFEOM3 had previously been mapped to chromosome 16q, within a 3.5-Mb critical region flanked by D16S498 and 16qter. They screened positional candidate genes in this region and identified TUBB3.

### Molecular Genetics

After identifying heterozygous TUBB3 missense mutations in families with isolated CFEOM3, Tischfield et al. (2010) sequenced TUBB3 in probands with CFEOM and additional neurologic signs. They found a heterozygous c.1228G-A transition, resulting in a glu410-to-lys (E410K) substitution, in 6 unrelated pedigrees. In each case the mutation arose de novo, either as sporadic disease or through presumed germline mosaicism. The mutation was absent in the parents of sporadic patients and in more than 1,700 control chromosomes. E410 lies in helix H12 on the external surface of the microtubule, a region that mediates interactions with motor proteins and microtubule-associated proteins. Mutant E410K heterodimers formed in vitro in moderate yield, cycled with native tubulin as efficiently as wildtype, and were incorporated robustly into microtubules in HeLa cells. In heterozygous diploid yeast carrying the equivalent mutation, microtubule dynamics were altered: polymerization was slower and depolymerization faster, and rescue events were less frequent. Plus-end accumulation of the kinesins Kip3p and Kip2p was markedly reduced. The authors concluded that the mutation has dominant effects on microtubule function by perturbing kinesin interactions. ⚠︎

Chew et al. (2013) identified the de novo c.1228G-A (E410K) mutation in 8 unrelated individuals with the syndrome. The mutation was absent in DNA from about 600 individuals who had Kallmann syndrome or isolated or syndromic ocular and/or facial dysmotility disorders but not the combined features of the TUBB3 E410K syndrome. The authors noted that this highlights the specificity of the phenotype-genotype correlation.

In a mother with the TUBB3 E410K syndrome, Balasubramanian et al. (2015) identified a de novo heterozygous c.1228G-A mutation in TUBB3. She transmitted it to her 3 affected sons in an autosomal dominant pattern.

In a 31-year-old Japanese woman previously diagnosed with atypical Moebius syndrome, Nakamura et al. (2018) analyzed the TUBB3 gene and identified a heterozygous c.1228G-A (E410K) mutation.

In a 3-year-old boy, Dentici et al. (2020) used clinical exome sequencing to identify a de novo TUBB3 E410K mutation.

In an 11-year-old girl previously diagnosed with atypical Moebius syndrome, Mori et al. (2022) used whole-exome sequencing to diagnose the TUBB3 E410K syndrome.

In a cohort of Chinese patients with congenital cranial dysinnervation disorders, Jia et al. (2022) used whole-exome sequencing with Sanger validation and identified heterozygous TUBB3 variants in 11 CFEOM families. The E410K variant was found in 2 of the 11 (18.2%). Their table listed the E410K cDNA change as c.1228C>T.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Tischfield et al. (2010) found that facial paralysis and progressive sensorimotor polyneuropathy occurred only in individuals with R262H, E410K, or D417H/N. These are the 4 mutations that permit heterodimer formation and efficient incorporation into microtubules and that perturb kinesin interactions in yeast.

Jia et al. (2022) found that, among Chinese CFEOM patients with TUBB3 variants, those with R380C, E410K, and R262H had syndromic phenotypes with multiple congenital craniocerebral and systemic malformations. In contrast, most patients with R262C had isolated CFEOM. Absence of the facial nerves and dysplasia of the olfactory bulbs and sulci were associated with the R262H and E410K variants.

### Diagnosis

Chew et al. (2013) stated that defining the TUBB3 E410K syndrome would allow clinicians to recognize affected individuals and predict the mutation from clinical features alone. Nakamura et al. (2018) noted that the syndrome may be misdiagnosed as atypical Moebius syndrome because the symptoms overlap. They recommended genetic analysis for c.1228G-A in TUBB3 to distinguish it from other disorders with congenital external ophthalmoplegia and facial nerve palsy. Jia et al. (2022) likewise noted that patients with the TUBB3 E410K syndrome were easily misdiagnosed as having Moebius syndrome.

### Animal Model

No model carrying E410K was described in these papers. Tischfield et al. (2010) generated knock-in mice carrying a different TUBB3 mutation, the R262C substitution. Homozygous mutant mice died within hours of birth and had defects in guidance of commissural axons and cranial nerves, but normal cortical layering.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

In 6 unrelated pedigrees with severe CFEOM3, congenital facial weakness, and developmental disabilities, Tischfield et al.

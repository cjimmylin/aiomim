### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that congenital fibrosis of extraocular muscles 3A, syndromic 1, with anosmia and developmental delay (CFEOM3A1) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TUBB3 gene (602661) on chromosome 16q24.

### Description

CFEOM3A1 is a syndromic form of congenital fibrosis of the extraocular muscles caused by a recurrent de novo heterozygous mutation in the TUBB3 gene, glu410-to-lys (E410K). Affected individuals have congenital ophthalmoplegia with ptosis, congenital facial weakness, developmental delay/intellectual disability, and Kallmann syndrome (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism with anosmia). Other features can include vocal cord paralysis and a progressive axonal sensorimotor peripheral neuropathy beginning later in life.

### Clinical Features

Tischfield et al. (2010) described individuals with TUBB3 mutations causing CFEOM3, either isolated or with additional features. Patients carrying the E410K substitution, which arose de novo, had CFEOM together with facial weakness, intellectual and social disabilities, and in some cases later-onset peripheral neuropathy and features of Kallmann syndrome. ✗

Chew et al. (2013) characterized the 'TUBB3 E410K syndrome' in a series of unrelated patients with the de novo E410K mutation. All had congenital ophthalmoplegia and congenital facial weakness, together with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and anosmia (Kallmann syndrome), developmental delay or intellectual disability, and progressive peripheral axonal neuropathy. Some had vocal cord paralysis, and cyclic vomiting was also reported. Brain imaging showed hypoplasia of the olfactory sulci/bulbs and of the oculomotor and facial nerves, but no malformations of cortical development. ✗

### Inheritance

The E410K mutation in TUBB3 arose de novo in reported patients (Tischfield et al., 2010; Chew et al., 2013), consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

In patients with syndromic CFEOM3, Tischfield et al. (2010) identified a de novo heterozygous E410K mutation in the TUBB3 gene. Functional studies of TUBB3 mutations showed altered microtubule dynamics and impaired interaction with kinesin motors, and the authors proposed that this disrupts axon guidance. ⚠︎

Chew et al. (2013) identified the same recurrent de novo heterozygous E410K mutation in TUBB3 in all patients in their series and defined the associated syndrome. ⚠︎

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Tischfield et al. (2010) found that different TUBB3 substitutions produced different phenotypes. Some, such as R262C, could cause isolated CFEOM3 that was often inherited. The E410K substitution caused a recognizable syndrome with facial weakness, intellectual disability, Kallmann syndrome, and later-onset neuropathy. ⚠︎

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CONGENITAL FIBROSIS OF EXTRAOCULAR MUSCLES 3A, SYNDROMIC 1, WITH ANOSMIA AND DEVELOPMENTAL DELAY**  
TUBB3, GLU410LYS [c.1228G>A; p.Glu410Lys]

In patients with CFEOM3 accompanied by facial weakness, intellectual disability, and Kallmann syndrome, Tischfield et al. (2010) identified a de novo heterozygous c.1228G-A transition in the TUBB3 gene, resulting in a glu410-to-lys (E410K) substitution. ⚠︎

Chew et al. (2013) reported additional unrelated patients with the de novo E410K mutation and defined the TUBB3 E410K syndrome.

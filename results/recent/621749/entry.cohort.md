### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that ectodermal dysplasia-19, hypohidrotic/tooth/nail type (ECTD19), is caused by heterozygous mutation in the IRF6 gene (607199) on chromosome 1q32.2.

### Description

Ectodermal dysplasia-19 (ECTD19) is defined by the combination of oligodontia, hypo- or anhidrosis, hidradenitis suppurativa-like skin abscesses, poikiloderma, onychodysplasia, and bilateral hearing impairment. Natal teeth and sparse hair are irregular or variable features. Lip pits and cleft lip/palate were absent in all 5 patients known at the time of the report by Schneider et al. (2026).

### Nomenclature

Schneider et al. (2026) noted that they had first called this IRF6-associated ectodermal dysplasia 'ectodermal dysplasia with natal teeth' (EDNAT). Because natal teeth were absent in 3 of the 5 known patients, they concluded that natal teeth are not as typical of the disorder as first thought.

### Clinical Features

Peschel et al. (2025) reported a new type of ectodermal dysplasia with natal teeth.

Schneider et al. (2026) reported 2 unrelated teenage boys, aged 17 (P1) and 14 (P2) years. Both had drawn attention on their first day of life because of natal teeth. P1 had delayed eruption of the deciduous teeth and oligodontia, with absent mandibular incisors, canines, and permanent premolars. He also had enamel hypomineralization, dental fistulas or cysts, and an atrophic mandibular alveolar crest. P2 developed only 9 permanent teeth, and 2 molars had to be extracted because they were displaced and impacted. Both boys had anhidrosis with episodes of overheating. Sweat glands were absent in P1, and confocal laser scanning microscopy found no sweat glands on the palms of P2. Both boys had recurrent hidradenitis suppurativa-like skin abscesses, dry skin, plantar hyperkeratosis, blistering on the backs of the toes (in winter in P1), and poikiloderma. Some toenails and fingernails were dysplastic, thickened, or deformed with distal onycholysis. Both boys also had increased thick nasal mucus, dry cerumen, and bilateral sensorineural hearing impairment since infancy, treated with hearing aids. P1 tended toward allergies, with several anaphylactic reactions to milk protein among other antigens. Skin histology in P1 showed focal subcorneal clefting and basal spongiosis. Neither boy had cleft lip/palate, submucous cleft palate, lip pits, or sparse hair. ⚠︎

Schneider et al. (2026) also reported a 22-year-old woman (P3), born to healthy nonconsanguineous parents. She was referred for anhidrosis, dry skin, and intolerance to environmental temperatures above 22 degrees C. Examination showed diffuse xerosis, dilated follicular ostia, hidradenitis suppurativa-like skin abscesses in the groins, axillae, and intergluteal fold, pincer nails on both big toes, and poikiloderma. She had bilateral acquired sensorineural hearing loss with tympanic membrane retraction and atelectasis. Other findings were epistaxis, crusted rhinitis, insufficient salivation, bilateral superficial punctate keratitis, and a reduced tear film break-up time. After delayed tooth eruption she had 16 persisting deciduous teeth and only 2 permanent teeth. Skin biopsy showed absent sweat glands, focal subcorneal clefting, and basal spongiosis. She did not have natal teeth, and her scalp hair, eyebrows, eyelashes, breasts, and nipples were normal. She had no syndactyly and no lip or palate abnormality. ⚠︎

Schneider et al. (2026) found a common pattern of tooth agenesis in 3 unrelated patients. All had permanent upper incisors but lacked the mandibular incisors and canines and all permanent premolars, with pronounced atrophy of the lower alveolar crest.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous IRF6 mutation in the 2 unrelated boys reported by Schneider et al. (2026) arose de novo. In a third patient from the same report, the IRF6 mutation was de novo and present in mosaic state.

### Molecular Genetics

Peschel et al. (2025) reported a previously undescribed variant in the IRF6 gene and concluded that it caused a new type of ectodermal dysplasia with natal teeth.

In 2 unrelated boys with ECTD19, Schneider et al. (2026) analyzed DNA from blood cells and found the same heterozygous c.653T-A transversion in exon 6 of the IRF6 gene. The change was predicted to be deleterious and results in an ile218-to-asn (I218N) substitution. It was classified as de novo because the parents' DNA showed no alteration at this position. In a 22-year-old woman with ECTD19, trio whole-genome sequencing found a de novo c.658T-C transition in IRF6, resulting in a ser220-to-pro (S220P) substitution. The variant allele frequency of 30.5% indicated mosaicism. Schneider et al. (2026) noted that these substitutions in exon 6 lie between the DNA-binding and protein-binding domains, in a region with no assigned function. AlphaFold3 modeling placed both substitutions in a short alpha-helix at the end of this region, which was predicted with higher confidence than the rest of the region.

Zhang et al. (2026) reported that gain-of-function variants in IRF6 cause a syndrome of hidradenitis suppurativa, ectodermal dysplasia, and deafness.

### Pathogenesis

By immunofluorescence, Schneider et al. (2026) found stronger nuclear IRF6 staining in keratinocytes from a patient with the I218N mutation than in controls. Keratinocytes from the patient with the mosaic S220P mutation showed patchy nuclear staining. Keratin-17 staining was normal. Taking these findings together with genotype-phenotype correlations, the authors suggested a gain-of-function mechanism.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 ECTODERMAL DYSPLASIA 19, HYPOHIDROTIC/TOOTH/NAIL TYPE**  
IRF6, ILE218ASN [c.653T>A; p.I218N]

Schneider et al. (2026) reported 2 unrelated boys, aged 17 and 14 years, with ECTD19. Both had natal teeth, oligodontia, anhidrosis, hidradenitis suppurativa-like abscesses, poikiloderma, nail dysplasia, and bilateral sensorineural hearing impairment. In both, the authors found the same heterozygous c.653T-A transversion in exon 6 of the IRF6 gene, which results in an ile218-to-asn (I218N) substitution predicted to be deleterious. The mutation was de novo in each boy: it was absent from the parents' DNA. Keratinocyte nuclei from a patient with this mutation showed stronger IRF6 immunofluorescence staining than controls.

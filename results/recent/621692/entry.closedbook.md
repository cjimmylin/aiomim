### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-42B (DEE42B) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the CACNA1A gene (601011) on chromosome 19p13. ⚠︎

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-42B (DEE42B) is a severe autosomal recessive neurologic disorder. Seizures begin in early infancy and are hard to treat. Affected children have profound global developmental delay, hypotonia, and poor or absent visual contact. Brain imaging has shown progressive cerebral and cerebellar atrophy, and optic atrophy has been reported. The disorder is the biallelic, usually loss-of-function, counterpart of the CACNA1A-related disorders caused by heterozygous mutation, including DEE42 (DEE42A), episodic ataxia type 2, familial hemiplegic migraine, and spinocerebellar ataxia type 6.

### Nomenclature

The autosomal dominant form of developmental and epileptic encephalopathy caused by heterozygous, usually de novo, CACNA1A mutation is designated DEE42A. The autosomal recessive form caused by biallelic CACNA1A mutation is designated DEE42B. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Reinson et al. (2016) reported sibs with early-onset epileptic encephalopathy, severe developmental delay, and progressive cerebral, cerebellar, and optic nerve atrophy. They died in childhood. Their heterozygous carrier relatives had milder CACNA1A-related neurologic features consistent with episodic ataxia. ✗

### Inheritance

DEE42B shows autosomal recessive inheritance. Affected individuals carry biallelic CACNA1A mutations. Heterozygous carrier parents can show milder dominant CACNA1A-related phenotypes, such as episodic ataxia.

### Molecular Genetics

In affected sibs with early-onset epileptic encephalopathy and progressive brain atrophy, Reinson et al. (2016) used exome sequencing to identify biallelic truncating mutations in the CACNA1A gene. Each parent carried one mutation and had a milder phenotype. ✗

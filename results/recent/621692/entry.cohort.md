### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-42B (DEE42B) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the CACNA1A gene (601011) on chromosome 19p13.13. ⚠︎

### Description

DEE42B is a severe autosomal recessive neurologic disorder. Seizures begin in early infancy and are accompanied by profound hypotonia, global developmental delay, and visual impairment with optic atrophy. Brain imaging shows progressive cerebral and cerebellar atrophy, and lifespan is reduced. Reinson et al. (2016) concluded that compound heterozygous CACNA1A mutations presumably cause early-onset epileptic encephalopathy with progressive cerebral, cerebellar, and optic nerve atrophy and reduced lifespan. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Reinson et al. (2016) reported a sister and brother. Both were normal at birth but developed daily recurrent seizures in early infancy, together with extreme muscular hypotonia, hypokinesia, and global developmental delay. Brain MRI showed progressive cerebral, cerebellar, and optic nerve atrophy. At age 5 years, both patients were blind and bedridden with profound developmental delay. The elder sister died at that age.

Arteche-López et al. (2021) reported patients with a homozygous loss-of-function CACNA1A variant. The patients had a more severe clinical phenotype than the typical dominant form.

Wong-Spracklen et al. (2022) reported a White British girl born to nonconsanguineous parents and followed to age 10 years. She had infantile hypotonia with a weak suck. Seizures began at 6 months, and she later had tonic-clonic status epilepticus, drug-resistant generalized tonic-clonic seizures, tonic seizures, and myoclonus. Poor bulbar function required gastrostomy, and she had recurrent chest infections, respiratory arrests, and drooling. Other findings were: - secondary microcephaly - fixed flexion contractures of the elbows, hips, and knees - no purposeful movement and inability to sit unsupported at 3 years - an alternating convergent squint and delayed visual maturation - impaired social function - severe intellectual disability, with a Vineland Adaptive Scales composite score of 26 She had no spasticity and no dysmorphic features. MRI at 6 months showed mild cerebellar hypoplasia and a thin corpus callosum. MRI at 3 years showed cerebellar volume loss involving both hemispheres, the vermis, and the middle cerebellar peduncles, and mildly prominent ventricles and extraaxial CSF spaces reflecting mild cerebral volume loss. EEG showed continuous focal sharp and slow wave complexes over the posterior regions. Nerve conduction, EMG, ERG, audiology, and a metabolic screen were normal.

Wong-Spracklen et al. (2022) reviewed the reported biallelic CACNA1A cases and identified consistent features: - infantile hypotonia - treatment-refractory seizures beginning in the first 6 months of life - cerebral visual impairment - profound developmental delay with persistent motor impairment - progressive cerebellar changes on MRI

Mammadova et al. (2024) reported a family in which 3 of 4 boys born to nonconsanguineous parents were severely affected. The index patient (II-4) had neonatal muscular hypotonia, and his first seizures occurred at 4 weeks of age. He developed early-onset epileptic encephalopathy with severe muscular hypotonia, absent deep tendon reflexes, and respiratory and swallowing insufficiency requiring tube feeding. His seizures were highly frequent and refractory, and included bilateral clonic seizures, myoclonic seizures, and ictal apneic spells. EEG showed burst suppression, and later generalized background slowing with multifocal sharp waves. He had severe global developmental delay, blindness, and optic atrophy, but cerebral MRI was normal. He died at 2.5 years. His brother (II-2) had muscular hypotonia from the first week of life, and seizures began at 5 months. He had hypsarrhythmia, deep apneic spells, and refractory seizures, together with severe developmental impairment, blindness, optic atrophy, edema, and dysphagia requiring tube feeding. Respiratory problems required mechanical ventilation. Imaging showed cerebellar hypoplasia. PEHO (progressive encephalopathy, edema, hypsarrhythmia, optic atrophy) syndrome had been suspected on clinical grounds. He died at 14 months. The eldest brother (II-1) had neonatal-onset muscular hypotonia and severe developmental impairment. He died at 5 months with suspected sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS). Mammadova et al. (2024) proposed that ictal apneic spells were the most prominent seizure type and the probable cause of death in the affected sibs. They suggested that the sib diagnosed with SIDS probably died of ictal apnea.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of DEE42B in the families reported by Reinson et al. (2016), Wong-Spracklen et al. (2022), and Mammadova et al. (2024) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Arteche-López et al. (2021) suggested that codominant or recessive inheritance could explain why patients with a homozygous loss-of-function CACNA1A variant differed from patients with the dominant CACNA1A phenotypes.

Heterozygous carriers in these families had variable phenotypes. Reinson et al. (2016) found that the heterozygous parents and 2 heterozygous sibs had a milder phenotype that included intellectual disability. In contrast, Wong-Spracklen et al. (2022) noted that the heterozygous carrier parents of their patient did not show clear signs of a CACNA1A-associated syndrome. They noted that this complicated segregation analysis and genetic counseling and left uncertainty about whether the recurrence risk was 25% or 50%. In the family reported by Mammadova et al. (2024), the heterozygous carriers were either asymptomatic or had milder neurologic phenotypes. The authors concluded that CACNA1A belongs to the genes associated with both autosomal dominant and autosomal recessive disease. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In a sister and brother with DEE42B, Reinson et al. (2016) used exome sequencing to identify compound heterozygous mutations in the CACNA1A gene. One was a missense mutation in exon 27 (c.4315T-A, W1439R), and the other was a 7-bp deletion in exon 3 (c.472_478delGCCTTCC) predicted to cause a frameshift and premature termination (Ala158ThrfsTer6). The parents and 2 sibs were each heterozygous for one of the mutations and had a milder phenotype that included intellectual disability. The mother also had adult-onset cerebellar ataxia with slowly progressive cerebellar atrophy. ⚠︎

Arteche-López et al. (2021) reported the first patients carrying a homozygous loss-of-function variant in the CACNA1A gene.

In a girl with DEE42B, Wong-Spracklen et al. (2022) identified compound heterozygous variants in the CACNA1A gene (NM_001127221.2). One was a 2-bp deletion (c.2042_2043delAG) predicted to cause a frameshift (Gln681ArgfsTer100), and the other was a c.1693G-A transition resulting in a glu565-to-lys (E565K) substitution. Parental testing by fluorescent sequencing showed that the truncating variant was inherited from the mother, who was asymptomatic. The missense variant was inherited from the father, who had infrequent migraines and suspected mild learning difficulties. The sibs were not tested.

In 2 affected brothers (II-4 and II-2) with DEE42B, Mammadova et al. (2024) identified compound heterozygous frameshift variants in the CACNA1A gene (NM_001127222.1): c.2602delG (Ala868ProfsTer24) and c.5476delC (His1826ThrfsTer30). The variants were found by exome sequencing in the index patient (II-4) and confirmed in material from his deceased brother (II-2). The c.2602delG variant was inherited from the mother and c.5476delC from the father. The mother and a maternal uncle, who both carried c.2602delG, had episodic ataxia type 2. The father, who carried c.5476delC, was asymptomatic. Material from the eldest affected brother (II-1) was not available for testing. A fourth brother (II-3) was reported in the text to be heterozygous for the maternal c.2602delG variant, although Table 1 of the report lists him as heterozygous for the paternal c.5476delC variant. He had a milder phenotype: primary generalized epilepsy beginning at 5 years with atypical absences, mild intellectual impairment, episodic unsteady gait, dizziness, attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder, and mild muscular hypotonia, with normal cranial MRI.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Wong-Spracklen et al. (2022) suggested that the effect of calcium channel dysfunction is dose-dependent. Monoallelic CACNA1A variants are associated with a highly variable spectrum ranging from no symptoms, to motor-only syndromes, to epilepsy with cognitive impairment. Biallelic variants, by contrast, lead to severe, progressive neurologic symptoms and cognitive disability.

Mammadova et al. (2024) suggested that biallelic truncating CACNA1A variants are associated with severe early-onset epileptic encephalopathy with a fatal outcome. They contrasted this with longer survival in patients who are compound heterozygous for a missense and a truncating variant, and with late-onset myoclonic epilepsy in a patient with a homozygous in-frame variant. They proposed that this supports a correlation between the deleteriousness of biallelic loss-of-function variants and disease severity. ⚠︎

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Kruer syndrome (KRRS) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the FBXO31 gene (609102) on chromosome 16q24.2.

### Description

Kruer syndrome is a neurodevelopmental disorder caused by a recurrent de novo heterozygous FBXO31 mutation. Its core features are cerebral palsy with mixed hypotonia, spasticity and dystonia; global developmental delay or intellectual disability; and speech impairment. Some patients also have neuropsychiatric features and abnormalities of the corpus callosum and white matter (Galaz-Montoya et al., 2026). Dzinovic et al. (2021) suggested that emerging hallmarks of FBXO31-related disease included delayed psychomotor development, pyramidal and extrapyramidal dysfunction (spasticity and dystonia), impaired expressive and receptive language, and intellectual disability.

### Nomenclature

Galaz-Montoya et al. (2026) proposed the descriptive term 'autosomal dominant FBXO31-associated neurodevelopmental disorder' and the eponym 'Kruer syndrome' for the phenotype caused by the recurrent de novo FBXO31 asp334-to-asn (D334N) mutation.

### Clinical Features

Jin et al. (2020) reported 2 unrelated patients (F218 and F699) with spastic diplegic cerebral palsy, intellectual disability, expressive language disorder, and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder. Patient F218 also had gut malrotation and constipation, cleft palate, and strabismus, and brain MRI was normal. Patient F699 had strabismus, severe constipation, and ventricular dilation with a thin corpus callosum on MRI.

Dzinovic et al. (2021) reported a 9-year-old Slovakian boy, born to nonconsanguineous parents, with a complex, nonprogressive movement disorder syndrome dominated by spasticity and dystonia, with onset in the first months of life. He had neonatal muscular hypotonia and global developmental delay. He lost speech at 18 months of age and had absent expressive language and a receptive language disorder. He had generalized dystonia of the arms, legs and trunk. Signs of pyramidal tract dysfunction included spasticity, which was worse in the legs than the arms, hyperreflexia, and clonus in both legs. He walked with an unsteady spastic-dystonic gait, had reduced manual dexterity, and had moderate intellectual disability. He was diagnosed with spastic-dystonic cerebral palsy. Brain MRI showed reduced parietal and occipital white matter volume and global white matter dysmyelination. Blood and urine lactate were slightly elevated on one occasion. He had no distinctive craniofacial features, behavioral abnormalities, or congenital malformations. Dzinovic et al. (2021) compared him with the 2 patients reported by Jin et al. (2020). All 3 had motor and/or speech delay, mild to moderate intellectual disability, abnormal speech, and spasticity that mainly affected the lower limbs and led to a diagnosis of cerebral palsy. Dystonia was seen only in their patient. ⚠︎

Galaz-Montoya et al. (2026) studied 7 unrelated probands with the recurrent FBXO31 mutation. They re-evaluated 3 previously published cases and found 4 more probands through advocacy networks, GeneMatcher, and clinician referral. The core neurodevelopmental profile was cerebral palsy (mixed hypotonia, spasticity, and dystonia), global developmental delay/intellectual disability, and speech impairment. Neuropsychiatric features were sometimes prominent and included attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder, anxiety, stereotypies, autistic features, and behavioral dysregulation. Neuroimaging often showed a hypoplastic corpus callosum and posterior-predominant white matter changes, and 1 individual had gray matter heterotopias. The authors also described a subtle, consistent facial gestalt. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The heterozygous FBXO31 mutation in Kruer syndrome occurred de novo in the 2 patients reported by Jin et al. (2020), in the patient reported by Dzinovic et al. (2021), and in all 7 probands studied by Galaz-Montoya et al. (2026). This is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Jin et al. (2020) performed whole-exome sequencing in 250 parent-offspring trios with cerebral palsy. In 2 unrelated patients with spastic diplegic cerebral palsy (F218 and F699), they identified the same de novo heterozygous missense mutation in the FBXO31 gene, D334N. Sanger sequencing of the probands and their parents confirmed de novo occurrence. The authors considered this identical de novo variant in 2 unrelated patients unlikely to have occurred by chance, and identified FBXO31 as a novel monogenic cause of cerebral palsy. The D334N variant affected a conserved residue within the cyclin D1-binding pocket. Patient fibroblasts had lower cyclin D levels than controls, consistent with an apparent gain of function in cyclin D degradation. ⚠︎

In a 9-year-old Slovakian boy with Kruer syndrome, Dzinovic et al. (2021) used trio whole-exome sequencing to identify a de novo heterozygous c.1000G-A transition in the FBXO31 gene, resulting in an asp334-to-asn (D334N) substitution. The variant was absent from both healthy parents and from control databases. It changed a conserved residue in a domain that interacts with the FBXO31 substrate cyclin D1. The authors classified it as pathogenic under ACMG guidelines and found no other potentially disease-relevant variants in the exome.

Galaz-Montoya et al. (2026) found the recurrent de novo heterozygous FBXO31 c.1000G-A (D334N) mutation in all 7 probands they studied.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Jin et al. (2020) concluded that the de novo heterozygous FBXO31 D334N mutation caused a phenotype distinct from the nonsyndromic intellectual disability associated with homozygous truncating FBXO31 mutations, representing a phenotypic expansion. Galaz-Montoya et al. (2026) likewise noted that biallelic loss-of-function variants in FBXO31 cause autosomal recessive intellectual disability, whereas the recurrent de novo c.1000G-A (D334N) variant is associated with an autosomal dominant phenotype.

### Diagnosis

Dzinovic et al. (2021) recommended considering a de novo FBXO31 mutation in patients with cerebral palsy or spastic-dystonic disorders that have no other explanation. Galaz-Montoya et al. (2026) concluded that the recurrent FBXO31 D334N mutation causes a recognizable neurodevelopmental syndrome. They recommended including FBXO31 in diagnostic algorithms for cerebral palsy and neurodevelopmental disorders.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 KRUER SYNDROME**  
FBXO31, ASP334ASN [c.1000G>A; p.D334N]

In 2 unrelated patients with Kruer syndrome who had spastic diplegic cerebral palsy, intellectual disability, expressive language disorder, and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder, Jin et al. (2020) identified a de novo heterozygous asp334-to-asn (D334N) mutation in the FBXO31 gene. The mutation was found by whole-exome sequencing of parent-offspring trios, and de novo occurrence was confirmed by Sanger sequencing. The residue is conserved and lies within the cyclin D1-binding pocket. Patient fibroblasts had lower cyclin D levels than controls, consistent with an apparent gain of function. ⚠︎

In a 9-year-old Slovakian boy with spastic-dystonic cerebral palsy, moderate intellectual disability, and absent expressive language, Dzinovic et al. (2021) identified a de novo heterozygous c.1000G-A transition in the FBXO31 gene, resulting in an asp334-to-asn (D334N) substitution. The mutation was found by trio whole-exome sequencing and was absent from both healthy parents and from control databases.

Galaz-Montoya et al. (2026) found the recurrent de novo heterozygous D334N mutation in 7 unrelated probands with Kruer syndrome, 3 previously published and 4 newly ascertained. ⚠︎

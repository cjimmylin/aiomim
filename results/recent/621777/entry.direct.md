### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-123 (DEE123) is caused by homozygous mutation in the HECW2 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-123 (DEE123) is an autosomal recessive neurodevelopmental disorder. Features include global developmental delay, intellectual disability, hypotonia and seizures, which may be drug-resistant. Some patients also have microcephaly, failure to thrive, severe gastroesophageal reflux, and brain imaging abnormalities such as cerebral and cerebellar atrophy (Krami et al., 2022; Rodriguez-Garcia et al., 2022; Dehghanzad et al., 2025).

Krami et al. (2022), Rodriguez-Garcia et al. (2022), and Dehghanzad et al. (2025) noted that de novo heterozygous variants in HECW2, mostly missense variants, had earlier been associated with a neurodevelopmental disorder with hypotonia, seizures, and absent language (NDHSAL). That disorder is typically inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Krami et al. (2022) reported a proband from a consanguineous Moroccan family. The proband had developmental delay, intellectual disability, hypotonia, generalized tonic-clonic seizures, and a persistent tilted head.

Rodriguez-Garcia et al. (2022) reported a Caucasian girl with a severe neurodevelopmental disorder. She had drug-resistant epilepsy, hypotonia, severe gastroesophageal reflux, and abnormalities on brain MRI.

Dehghanzad et al. (2025) reported a 6-year-old girl of consanguineous Afghan descent. She had severe microcephaly, hypotonia, failure to thrive, recurrent seizures, and global neurodevelopmental delay. Brain MRI showed significant cerebral and cerebellar atrophy.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of DEE123 in the families reported by Krami et al. (2022), Rodriguez-Garcia et al. (2022), and Dehghanzad et al. (2025) was autosomal recessive. In the family reported by Dehghanzad et al. (2025), both parents were heterozygous carriers of the mutation and were clinically unaffected.

### Molecular Genetics

Using whole-exome sequencing, Krami et al. (2022) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in the HECW2 gene (c.736C-T; R246X) in the proband from a consanguineous Moroccan family with DEE123. The authors described this as the first reported homozygous HECW2 variant. It contrasted with the de novo heterozygous variants previously reported in HECW2 patients.

Using whole-exome sequencing, Rodriguez-Garcia et al. (2022) identified a homozygous deletion-insertion in the HECW2 gene (g.197092814_197092824delinsC) in a Caucasian girl with DEE123. The variant disrupted the splice donor site of intron 22. It caused skipping of exon 22 (r.3766_3917+1del), which led to an in-frame deletion in the protein (p.Leu1256_Trp1306del). In functional studies, RNA expression was increased twofold, but protein expression was reduced by 60%. The authors suggested a partial loss-of-function mechanism.

Using whole-exome sequencing, Dehghanzad et al. (2025) identified a homozygous 1-bp insertion in the HECW2 gene (c.3601_3602insT) in a girl of consanguineous Afghan descent with DEE123. The insertion was predicted to cause a frameshift and premature termination (Tyr1201LeufsTer7). Sanger sequencing confirmed the variant, and both unaffected parents were heterozygous carriers. In silico analyses supported pathogenicity. The authors suggested that the variant likely results in nonsense-mediated mRNA decay. This loss-of-function mechanism would be distinct from the one implicated for missense variants that cause the autosomal dominant disorder.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-123 (DEE123) is caused by homozygous mutation in the HECW2 gene (617245) on chromosome 2q32. ⚠︎

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-123 (DEE123) is an autosomal recessive neurodevelopmental disorder. Reported features include developmental delay, intellectual disability, hypotonia, and seizures, which may be drug-resistant. Some patients have microcephaly, failure to thrive, and cerebral and cerebellar atrophy on brain MRI (Krami et al., 2022; Rodriguez-Garcia et al., 2022; Dehghanzad et al., 2025).

### Clinical Features

Krami et al. (2022) reported a proband from a consanguineous Moroccan family. The proband had developmental delay, intellectual disability, hypotonia, generalized tonic-clonic seizures, and a persistent head tilt.

Rodriguez-Garcia et al. (2022) reported a Caucasian girl with a severe neurodevelopmental disorder, drug-resistant epilepsy, hypotonia, severe gastroesophageal reflux, and brain MRI anomalies.

Dehghanzad et al. (2025) reported a 6-year-old girl of Afghan descent born to consanguineous parents. She had severe microcephaly, hypotonia, failure to thrive, recurrent seizures, and global neurodevelopmental delay. Brain MRI showed significant cerebral and cerebellar atrophy.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of DEE123 in the families reported by Krami et al. (2022) and Dehghanzad et al. (2025) was autosomal recessive. In the family reported by Dehghanzad et al. (2025), both parents were clinically unaffected heterozygous carriers. Rodriguez-Garcia et al. (2022) also described their patient's homozygous splicing variant as autosomal recessive.

### Heterogeneity

Rodriguez-Garcia et al. (2022) noted that de novo heterozygous missense variants in HECW2 had previously been associated with neurodevelopmental disorder with hypotonia, seizures, and absent language (NDHSAL). Their patient, by contrast, carried an autosomal recessive splicing variant. Dehghanzad et al. (2025) likewise noted that their autosomal recessive case contrasted with the usual autosomal dominant inheritance of HECW2-related NDHSAL. They proposed that the loss-of-function mechanism in their patient differed from the mechanism implicated for the dominant missense variants.

### Molecular Genetics

Krami et al. (2022) performed whole-exome sequencing in a proband from a consanguineous Moroccan family with developmental delay, intellectual disability, hypotonia, and seizures. They identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in the HECW2 gene (c.736C-T, R246X). The authors noted that this was the first homozygous HECW2 variant reported, in contrast to the de novo heterozygous variants described earlier.

Rodriguez-Garcia et al. (2022) performed whole-exome sequencing in a Caucasian girl with a severe neurodevelopmental disorder and drug-resistant epilepsy. They identified a novel homozygous mutation in the HECW2 gene (g.197092814_197092824delinsC). The mutation disrupted the splice donor site of intron 22 and caused skipping of exon 22 (r.3766_3917+1del), resulting in an in-frame deletion (p.Leu1256_Trp1306del). Functional studies showed a twofold increase in RNA expression but a 60% reduction in protein level. The authors suggested a partial loss-of-function mechanism.

Dehghanzad et al. (2025) performed whole-exome sequencing in a 6-year-old Afghan girl with DEE born to consanguineous parents. They identified a novel homozygous frameshift mutation in the HECW2 gene (c.3601_3602insT, Tyr1201LeufsTer7), which was confirmed by Sanger sequencing. Both parents were unaffected heterozygous carriers. The authors suggested that the mutation likely causes nonsense-mediated mRNA decay, resulting in loss of function.

Dehghanzad et al. (2025) studied a 6-year-old Afghan girl born to consanguineous parents. She had severe microcephaly, hypotonia, failure to thrive, recurrent seizures, global neurodevelopmental delay, and cerebral and cerebellar atrophy. Using whole-exome sequencing, they identified a homozygous 1-bp insertion in the HECW2 gene (c.3601_3602insT), resulting in a frameshift and premature termination (Tyr1201LeufsTer7). The authors suggested that the mutation likely causes nonsense-mediated mRNA decay and loss of function.

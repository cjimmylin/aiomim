### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-123 (DEE123) is caused by homozygous mutation in the HECW2 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-123 (DEE123) is an autosomal recessive severe neurodevelopmental disorder. Features reported in affected individuals include global developmental delay, intellectual disability, hypotonia, and seizures, which may be drug-resistant. Some patients have had microcephaly, failure to thrive, and brain MRI abnormalities, including cerebral and cerebellar atrophy (Krami et al., 2022; Rodriguez-Garcia et al., 2022; Dehghanzad et al., 2025).

### Clinical Features

Krami et al. (2022) reported a proband from a consanguineous Moroccan family who had developmental delay, intellectual disability, hypotonia, generalized tonic-clonic seizures, and a persistent tilted head.

Rodriguez-Garcia et al. (2022) reported a Caucasian girl with a severe neurodevelopmental disorder, drug-resistant epilepsy, hypotonia, severe gastroesophageal reflux, and abnormalities on brain MRI.

Dehghanzad et al. (2025) reported a 6-year-old girl of consanguineous Afghan descent who had severe microcephaly, hypotonia, failure to thrive, recurrent seizures, and global neurodevelopmental delay. Brain MRI showed significant cerebral and cerebellar atrophy.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of DEE123 in the families reported by Krami et al. (2022), Rodriguez-Garcia et al. (2022), and Dehghanzad et al. (2025) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. In the family reported by Dehghanzad et al. (2025), both clinically unaffected parents were heterozygous carriers of the mutation.

### Molecular Genetics

In a proband from a consanguineous Moroccan family with DEE123, Krami et al. (2022) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in the HECW2 gene (c.736C-T; R246X). The mutation was found by whole-exome sequencing. The authors noted that this contrasted with the de novo heterozygous HECW2 variants previously reported in patients with neurodevelopmental disorders.

In a Caucasian girl with DEE123, Rodriguez-Garcia et al. (2022) identified a homozygous deletion-insertion in the HECW2 gene (g.197092814_197092824delinsC) by whole-exome sequencing. The mutation disrupted the splice donor site of intron 22 and caused skipping of exon 22 (r.3766_3917+1del), resulting in an in-frame deletion (p.Leu1256_Trp1306del). Functional studies showed a twofold increase in RNA expression, but protein expression was reduced by 60%. The authors suggested a partial loss-of-function mechanism.

In a 6-year-old girl of consanguineous Afghan descent with DEE123, Dehghanzad et al. (2025) identified a homozygous 1-bp insertion in the HECW2 gene (c.3601_3602insT), predicted to cause a frameshift and premature termination (Tyr1201LeufsTer7). The mutation was found by whole-exome sequencing and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. Both clinically unaffected parents were heterozygous carriers. The authors predicted that the variant would result in nonsense-mediated mRNA decay. They suggested a loss-of-function mechanism that differs from the mechanism of the missense variants associated with autosomal dominant disease.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Rodriguez-Garcia et al. (2022) and Dehghanzad et al. (2025) noted that de novo heterozygous missense variants in HECW2 had been associated with an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder with hypotonia, seizures, and absent language (NDHSAL). Dehghanzad et al. (2025) proposed that the recessive null variant acted through a loss-of-function mechanism distinct from that of the dominant missense variants.

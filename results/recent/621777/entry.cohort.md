### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-123 (DEE123) is caused by homozygous mutation in the HECW2 gene (617245) on chromosome 2q32.3. ⚠︎

### Description

Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-123 (DEE123) is an autosomal recessive neurodevelopmental disorder. Its main features are developmental delay, hypotonia and seizures, which may be drug-resistant. Some patients also have intellectual disability, microcephaly, failure to thrive, gastroesophageal reflux, and brain MRI anomalies such as cerebral and cerebellar atrophy.

### Clinical Features

Krami et al. (2022) reported a proband from a consanguineous Moroccan family. The proband had developmental delay, intellectual disability, hypotonia, generalized tonic-clonic seizures, and a persistently tilted head.

Rodríguez-García et al. (2022) reported a Caucasian girl with a severe neurodevelopmental disorder. She had drug-resistant epilepsy, hypotonia, severe gastroesophageal reflux, and anomalies on brain MRI. The authors called her disorder neurodevelopmental disorder with hypotonia, seizures, and absent language (NDHSAL).

Dehghanzad et al. (2025) reported a 6-year-old girl of consanguineous Afghan descent. She had severe microcephaly, hypotonia, failure to thrive, recurrent seizures, and global neurodevelopmental delay. Brain MRI showed significant cerebral and cerebellar atrophy.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of DEE123 is autosomal recessive. Krami et al. (2022) were the first to report a homozygous HECW2 variant inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. Previously reported HECW2 variants had been de novo and heterozygous. In the family reported by Dehghanzad et al. (2025), both parents were unaffected heterozygous carriers of their daughter's homozygous HECW2 variant.

### Molecular Genetics

In a proband from a consanguineous Moroccan family with DEE123, Krami et al. (2022) identified a homozygous nonsense mutation in the HECW2 gene (c.736C-T, R246X). The authors stated that the mutation was inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern.

In a Caucasian girl with DEE123, Rodríguez-García et al. (2022) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a homozygous mutation in the HECW2 gene (g.197092814_197092824delinsC). The mutation disrupted the splice donor site of intron 22. This caused skipping of exon 22 (r.3766_3917+1del) and an in-frame deletion (p.Leu1256_Trp1306del). In functional studies, RNA expression was increased twofold but protein levels were reduced by 60%, suggesting partial loss of function.

In a 6-year-old girl of consanguineous Afghan descent with DEE123, Dehghanzad et al. (2025) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a homozygous 1-bp insertion in the HECW2 gene (c.3601_3602insT). The insertion causes a frameshift and premature termination (Tyr1201LeufsTer7). Sanger sequencing confirmed the variant. Both unaffected parents were heterozygous carriers. In silico analyses supported pathogenicity.

### Pathogenesis

Dehghanzad et al. (2025) suggested that the homozygous HECW2 frameshift mutation in their patient probably leads to nonsense-mediated mRNA decay. They proposed a loss-of-function mechanism, different from the mechanism implicated for the missense variants that cause autosomal dominant disease.

Dehghanzad et al. (2025) identified a homozygous 1-bp insertion in the HECW2 gene (c.3601_3602insT) in a 6-year-old girl of consanguineous Afghan descent with DEE123. The insertion results in a frameshift and premature termination (Tyr1201LeufsTer7). The authors suggested that the mutation probably leads to nonsense-mediated mRNA decay and loss of function.

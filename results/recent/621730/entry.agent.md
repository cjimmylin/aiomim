### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest-28 (OZEMA28) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the CDC23 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest-28 (OZEMA28) is a form of female infertility characterized by oocyte maturation defects. Fan et al. (2023) noted that CDC23 is one of the core subunits of the anaphase promoting complex or cyclosome (APC/C), which is essential in the metaphase-to-anaphase transition. Cui et al. (2025) described female infertility associated with biallelic APC/C gene mutations as characterized by disturbances in oocyte maturation, failure of fertilization, and cessation of early embryonic growth.

### Clinical Features

Fan et al. (2023) reported 3 infertile women with female infertility characterized by oocyte maturation defects.

Cui et al. (2025) studied 3 Chinese probands with female infertility and biallelic mutations in APC/C genes, 2 of whom carried CDC23 variants. As a group, the patients were characterized by disturbances in oocyte maturation, failure of fertilization, and cessation of early embryonic growth; the abstract did not specify the phenotype of each individual case.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OZEMA28 in the patients reported by Fan et al. (2023) and Cui et al. (2025) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Cui et al. (2025) stated that the APC/C gene mutations they identified all showed a recessive inheritance pattern. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In 3 infertile women with oocyte maturation defects, Fan et al. (2023) identified 2 homozygous missense mutations in the CDC23 gene: c.986A-G (Y329C) and c.988C-T (R330C). In vitro experiments showed that the Y329C variant led to a decrease in CDC23 protein level, and the R330C variant changed the localization of CDC23 in HeLa cells and mouse oocytes.

By whole-exome sequencing of patients with female infertility from 639 unrelated Chinese families, Cui et al. (2025) identified 3 probands with APC/C gene mutations. Cases 1 and 2 carried compound heterozygous missense variants in the CDC23 gene (c.1277A-G, c.833A-G, c.182C-T, and c.301C-T), and case 3 carried compound heterozygous variants in APC13 (c.6C-A and c.116_126del). The variants were predicted to be deleterious, and structural analysis suggested that they may alter chemical bonds with other APC/C subunits. In vitro data suggested that the CDC23 mutations resulted in dysregulated protein expression.

### Animal Model

Fan et al. (2023) found that Cdc23 Y329C/Y329C mice mimicked the phenotype of the patients, with low expression of CDC23 and APC4 and accumulation of securin and cyclin B1 in oocytes. Treatment with AZ3146 rescued the phenotype.

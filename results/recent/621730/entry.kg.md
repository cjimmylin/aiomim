A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest-28 (OZEMA28) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the CDC23 gene (603462) on chromosome 5q31.2. ⚠︎

### Description

Oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest-28 (OZEMA28) is a form of female infertility. Fan et al. (2023) described it as infertility characterized by defects in oocyte maturation. Cui et al. (2025) reported that the infertility involved disturbed oocyte maturation, fertilization failure, and arrest of early embryonic development.

### Clinical Features

Fan et al. (2023) described 3 infertile women whose infertility was characterized by defects in oocyte maturation.

Cui et al. (2025) performed whole-exome sequencing in infertile women from 639 unrelated Chinese families and identified 3 probands with variants in anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C) genes. In these families, the infertility was characterized by disturbed oocyte maturation, fertilization failure, and arrest of early embryonic development.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OZEMA28 in the families reported by Fan et al. (2023) and Cui et al. (2025) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Fan et al. (2023) found homozygous variants, and Cui et al. (2025) found compound heterozygous variants that followed a recessive pattern.

### Molecular Genetics

In 3 infertile women with defects in oocyte maturation, Fan et al. (2023) identified homozygous missense mutations in the CDC23 gene, which encodes a core subunit of the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C). One mutation was a c.986A-G transition that resulted in a tyr329-to-cys (Y329C) substitution. In vitro experiments showed that it reduced the level of CDC23 protein. Another mutation was a c.988C-T transition that resulted in an arg330-to-cys (R330C) substitution. It changed where CDC23 localized in HeLa cells and in mouse oocytes.

Cui et al. (2025) carried out whole-exome sequencing in infertile women from 639 unrelated Chinese families. In 2 unrelated women (cases 1 and 2), they identified compound heterozygous missense mutations in the CDC23 gene: c.1277A-G, c.833A-G, c.182C-T, and c.301C-T. The mutations followed a recessive inheritance pattern. The affected residues were conserved across species, and MutationTaster, SIFT, and PolyPhen-2 predicted the mutations to be deleterious. In vitro studies suggested that the CDC23 mutations dysregulated protein expression.

In a third infertile Chinese woman in the same study (case 3), Cui et al. (2025) identified compound heterozygous variants in a different APC/C gene, ANAPC13 (614484): c.6C-A and c.116_126del. The variants were found by whole-exome sequencing and followed a recessive pattern. In vitro studies suggested that the ANAPC13 missense variant caused the protein to localize abnormally in the cell. Structural modeling suggested that the APC/C variants might alter chemical bonds with other APC/C subunits.

### Animal Model

Fan et al. (2023) found that knockin mice homozygous for the Cdc23 Y329C mutation had a phenotype resembling that of the patients. Oocytes from these mice had low expression of CDC23 and APC4 and accumulated securin and cyclin B1. Treatment with AZ3146 rescued the phenotype.

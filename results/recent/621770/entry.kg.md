### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest-29 (OZEMA29) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the TBPL2 gene (608964) on chromosome 14q22.3. ⚠︎

### Description

Oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest-29 (OZEMA29) is a form of female infertility. Reported features include oocyte maturation arrest and degeneration, with only immature metaphase I oocytes or no oocytes retrieved at in vitro fertilization (IVF). Some patients have impaired ovarian folliculogenesis and fail to respond to superovulation (Yang et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2021; Du et al., 2023). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Yang et al. (2021) studied women from 2 unrelated families who were infertile because of defects in oocyte maturation. The patients had repeated IVF failures, and only 2 immature metaphase I (MI) oocytes could be obtained from them. ⚠︎

Wang et al. (2021) described 2 sisters from a consanguineous family who were infertile because of oocyte maturation arrest and degeneration.

Du et al. (2023) reported a 32-year-old woman with unexplained primary infertility of 10 years' duration. Most of her basal hormone levels were roughly normal, but her basal FSH was elevated. Her anti-Mullerian hormone level was normal. Two attempts at oocyte collection had failed, which was consistent with impaired ovarian folliculogenesis and failure of superovulation.

In the Finnish population, Ruotsalainen et al. (2024) found that women homozygous for the TBPL2 p.Arg299Ter variant had significantly fewer offspring than women with other genotypes, an average of 0.16 compared with 1.75 (p = 1.4 x 10(-15)). Of 41 homozygous women, only 4 had given birth. All 4 had undergone IVF, and it was not known whether donor oocytes had been used. The authors concluded that the variant is associated with a fairly severe form of infertility.

### Inheritance

OZEMA29 is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. Yang et al. (2021) found homozygous TBPL2 mutations in affected women from 2 unrelated families, and Wang et al. (2021) found them in 2 affected sisters from a consanguineous family. Du et al. (2023) found compound heterozygous TBPL2 mutations in an affected woman. In a population-based study, Ruotsalainen et al. (2024) found that the TBPL2 locus acts recessively: the proportion of infertile women was significantly increased only among those carrying 2 copies of the effect allele. The association was seen equally in early-onset and late-onset infertility.

### Molecular Genetics

He et al. (2020) reported that a recurrent mutation in the TBPL2 gene caused diminished ovarian reserve and female infertility.

Yang et al. (2021) studied affected women from 2 unrelated families with oocyte maturation defects and infertility. Using whole-exome sequencing with Sanger confirmation, they identified a homozygous splice site mutation in the TBPL2 gene (c.788+3A-G). Haplotype analysis showed that the disease alleles in the 2 families had arisen independently. In a minigene assay, the mutation disrupted the integrity of TBPL2 mRNA.

Wang et al. (2021) studied 2 infertile sisters from a consanguineous family who had oocyte maturation arrest and degeneration. Using whole-exome sequencing, they identified a homozygous missense mutation in the TBPL2 gene (c.895T-C), resulting in a cys299-to-arg (C299R) substitution. The authors described the mutation as rare and pathogenic. In functional studies, the mutation impaired the transcription initiation function of the protein.

Du et al. (2023) performed whole-exome sequencing in a woman with primary infertility, impaired ovarian folliculogenesis, and failed superovulation, and confirmed the results in her family by Sanger sequencing. They identified compound heterozygous mutations in the TBPL2 gene. One was a novel nonsense mutation, c.802C-T, resulting in an arg268-to-ter (R268X) substitution. The other was the previously reported c.788+3A-G mutation, which Du et al. (2023) reported as resulting in an arg233-to-ter (R233X) substitution. To assess pathogenicity, the authors checked allele frequencies in the ExAC and gnomAD databases and used SIFT, MutationTaster, and CADD predictions.

Ruotsalainen et al. (2024) performed a genomewide association study in the Finnish FinnGen cohort, comparing 22,849 women with infertility with 198,989 control women who had given birth. Under a recessive model, they found a genome-wide significant association with a stop-gained variant in the TBPL2 gene, rs144313315 (c.895A-T, NM_199047.3), resulting in an arg299-to-ter (R299X) substitution (odds ratio, 650.1; p = 4.11 x 10(-25)). The variant introduces a premature stop codon in the DNA-binding part of the protein and was predicted to cause loss of function (CADD score, 40). It was associated with infertility only and with no other diseases.

### Pathogenesis

Yang et al. (2021) sequenced the transcriptome of affected oocytes carrying the TBPL2 c.788+3A-G mutation. Genes vital for oocyte maturation and fertilization were widely and markedly downregulated. The authors suggested that mutation in the transcription factor TBPL2 leads to global changes in gene expression in oocytes.

### Population Genetics

Ruotsalainen et al. (2024) reported that the TBPL2 R299X variant has a minor allele frequency of 1.1% in Finland, 43 times higher than in non-Finnish Europeans. The variant is distributed fairly evenly across Finland, with regional allele frequencies of 0.84% to 2.38%. Loss-of-function variants in TBPL2 are rare in gnomAD, with this variant as the exception.

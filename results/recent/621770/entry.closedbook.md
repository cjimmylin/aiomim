This entry describes oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest-29 (OZEMA29), a form of female infertility in which oocytes, zygotes or early embryos fail to develop normally. The causal gene and the reports defining this subtype are not recalled here. ⚠︎

### Description

Oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest (OZEMA) is a group of genetically different disorders that cause primary female infertility. Affected women have normal menstrual cycles and respond to ovarian stimulation. During assisted reproduction, however, their oocytes arrest at a stage of meiotic maturation, fail to be fertilized, or produce zygotes or embryos that arrest during early cleavage and cannot be transferred. ✗

### Heterogeneity

OZEMA is genetically heterogeneous, and different numbered subtypes are caused by mutations in different genes. Examples include OZEMA1, caused by mutation in TUBB8, OZEMA2, caused by mutation in PATL2, and OZEMA3, caused by mutation in WEE2. ✗

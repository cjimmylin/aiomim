### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest-29 (OZEMA29) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the TBPL2 gene on chromosome 14. ⚠︎

### Description

Oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest-29 (OZEMA29) is an autosomal recessive form of female infertility. Reported features include oocyte maturation arrest and degeneration, impaired ovarian folliculogenesis, failure of superovulation, and repeated failure of in vitro fertilization (IVF) (Yang et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2021; Du et al., 2023). He et al. (2020) reported diminished ovarian reserve in women with a recurrent TBPL2 mutation. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

He et al. (2020) reported that a recurrent mutation in the TBPL2 gene caused diminished ovarian reserve and female infertility. Only the title of this report was available.

Yang et al. (2021) studied 2 unrelated infertile families with oocyte maturation defects. Two immature metaphase I (MI) oocytes were obtained from the patients. Transcriptome sequencing of these affected oocytes showed widespread, marked downregulation of genes needed for oocyte maturation and fertilization. The authors suggested that the findings could help in the genetic diagnosis of infertile women with repeated IVF failure.

Wang et al. (2021) reported 2 infertile sisters from a consanguineous family who had oocyte maturation arrest and degeneration.

Du et al. (2023) reported a 32-year-old woman with 10 years of unexplained primary infertility. Her basal hormone levels were roughly normal, except that basal follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) was elevated. Her anti-Mullerian hormone level was normal. Two attempts at oocyte collection had failed. The authors described the phenotype as impaired ovarian folliculogenesis with failure of superovulation.

In the Finnish population cohort FinnGen, Ruotsalainen et al. (2024) found that women homozygous for a TBPL2 stop-gained variant had significantly fewer offspring than other women: an average of 0.16 (SD = 0.58) offspring. Among 41 homozygous women, 2 had given birth to 1 child and 2 to more than 1 child. All 4 of these women had undergone IVF, but whether donor oocytes had been used was not known. In phenome-wide association analysis, the variant was associated only with infertility and with no other disease.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OZEMA29 is autosomal recessive. Affected women carried homozygous TBPL2 mutations, including 2 sisters from a consanguineous family (Yang et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2021), or compound heterozygous TBPL2 mutations (Du et al., 2023). In the FinnGen cohort, Ruotsalainen et al. (2024) found a recessive mode of action at the TBPL2 locus: the proportion of women with infertility was significantly increased only among those carrying 2 copies of the effect allele.

### Molecular Genetics

Yang et al. (2021) performed whole-exome sequencing with Sanger confirmation in 2 unrelated infertile families with oocyte maturation defects. They identified a homozygous splicing mutation in the TBPL2 gene, c.788+3A-G. Haplotype analysis showed that the disease alleles in the 2 families were independent. In a minigene assay, the variant disrupted the integrity of TBPL2 mRNA. The authors noted that direct functional evidence was lacking.

Using whole-exome sequencing in 2 infertile sisters from a consanguineous family with oocyte maturation arrest and degeneration, Wang et al. (2021) identified a homozygous missense mutation in the TBPL2 gene, c.895T-C, predicted to cause a cys299-to-arg (C299R) substitution. The mutation was rare and impaired the transcription initiation function of the protein.

In a 32-year-old woman with primary infertility and impaired ovarian folliculogenesis, Du et al. (2023) used whole-exome sequencing and Sanger confirmation to identify compound heterozygous mutations in the TBPL2 gene. One was a novel c.802C-T transition, predicted to cause an arg268-to-ter (R268X) substitution. The other was the previously reported c.788+3A-G mutation, designated p.Arg233Ter by the authors. DNA was also obtained from her family members. The authors searched ExAC and gnomAD for the allele frequencies of the variants and assessed them with SIFT, Mutation Taster, and CADD.

Ruotsalainen et al. (2024) performed a genomewide association study of 22,849 Finnish women with infertility and 198,989 control women from the FinnGen cohort. Recessive analysis identified a stop-gained variant in the TBPL2 gene, c.895A-T (NM_199047.3), predicted to cause an arg299-to-ter (R299X) substitution. This was the strongest association in the study (OR = 650.1, p = 4.11 x 10(-25)). The variant introduces a premature stop codon in the DNA-binding part of the protein. It is a high-confidence predicted loss-of-function variant with a CADD score of 40.

### Population Genetics

Ruotsalainen et al. (2024) reported that the TBPL2 R299X variant had a minor allele frequency of 1.1% in Finland. The abstract of the same paper gave the frequency as 1.2%. The variant was about 43 times more frequent in Finland than in non-Finnish Europeans. It was fairly evenly distributed across Finland, with allele frequencies ranging from 0.84% to 2.38%.

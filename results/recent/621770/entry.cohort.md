### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest-29 (OZEMA29) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the TBPL2 gene (608964) on chromosome 14q22. ⚠︎

### Description

Oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest-29 (OZEMA29) is an autosomal recessive form of female infertility. It is characterized by defective oocyte maturation, with oocyte maturation arrest and degeneration. Impaired ovarian folliculogenesis and failed superovulation have also been reported (Yang et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2021; Du et al., 2023). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Yang et al. (2021) studied 2 unrelated infertile families with oocyte maturation defects. The affected women were infertile.

Wang et al. (2021) reported 2 infertile sisters from a consanguineous family who had oocyte maturation arrest and oocyte degeneration.

Du et al. (2023) reported a 32-year-old woman who had been diagnosed with unexplained primary infertility for 10 years. Her basal hormone levels were roughly normal, but her basal follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) level was elevated. Her anti-Mullerian hormone level was within the normal range. She had undergone 2 failed oocyte collection attempts. The authors described impaired ovarian folliculogenesis and failure of superovulation.

Ruotsalainen et al. (2024) studied Finnish women in the FinnGen cohort. Women homozygous for the TBPL2 R299X variant had an average of 0.16 (SD = 0.58) offspring, compared with 1.75 in the other genotype groups. According to registry data, only 4 women homozygous for the variant had children: of 41 homozygous individuals, 2 had given birth to 1 child and 2 had given birth to more than 1 child. All 4 had received infertility treatment, including IVF. In a phenome-wide analysis, the variant was associated only with infertility.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OZEMA29 in the families reported by Wang et al. (2021) and Du et al. (2023) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Affected women in those families carried homozygous or compound heterozygous TBPL2 mutations. Ruotsalainen et al. (2024) confirmed that the TBPL2 locus acts recessively: the proportion of women with infertility was significantly increased only among women carrying 2 copies of the effect allele. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

He et al. (2020) reported that a recurrent mutation in the TBPL2 gene causes diminished ovarian reserve and female infertility.

Yang et al. (2021) performed whole-exome sequencing in 2 unrelated infertile families with oocyte maturation defects. In both families they identified a homozygous splicing variant (c.788+3A-G) in the TBPL2 gene. In a minigene assay, the variant disrupted the integrity of TBPL2 mRNA. Haplotype analysis indicated that the disease alleles in the 2 families arose independently.

Wang et al. (2021) performed whole-exome sequencing in 2 infertile sisters from a consanguineous family who had oocyte maturation arrest and degeneration. They identified a homozygous missense mutation (c.895T-C, C299R) in the TBPL2 gene. The authors reported that the variant was rare and pathogenic and that it impaired the transcription initiation function of the protein.

Du et al. (2023) performed whole-exome sequencing in an infertile woman with impaired ovarian folliculogenesis and identified compound heterozygous mutations in the TBPL2 gene. One was a novel nonsense mutation (c.802C-T, R268X). The other was the previously reported splice variant (c.788+3A-G), which the authors designated p.Arg233Ter. The mutations were confirmed by Sanger sequencing. The authors proposed that they caused the patient's impaired folliculogenesis, failure of superovulation, and infertility.

Ruotsalainen et al. (2024) performed a genomewide association study of 22,849 Finnish women with infertility and 198,989 control women from FinnGen. Under a recessive model, they found a genomewide-significant association with a nonsense variant in the TBPL2 gene (c.895A-T, R299X; NM_199047.3), with an odds ratio of 650.1 (p = 4.11 x 10(-25)). The variant introduces a premature stop codon in the DNA-binding part of TBPL2. It was predicted with high confidence to be a loss-of-function variant (CADD score 40), and the authors expected it to significantly impair transcription of downstream targets.

### Pathogenesis

Yang et al. (2021) performed single-oocyte RNA sequencing on 2 immature metaphase I oocytes from patients with a homozygous TBPL2 splicing variant. Genes vital for oocyte maturation and fertilization were widely and markedly downregulated. The authors suggested that mutation of the transcription factor TBPL2 leads to global gene alterations in oocytes, but they noted that direct functional evidence was lacking.

### Population Genetics

Ruotsalainen et al. (2024) found that the TBPL2 R299X variant had a minor allele frequency of 1.1% in Finland, 43 times higher than in non-Finnish Europeans. Allele frequencies were fairly even across Finland, ranging from 0.84% to 2.38%.

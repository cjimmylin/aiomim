### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest-29 (OZEMA29) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the TBPL2 gene on chromosome 14. ⚠︎

### Description

Oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest-29 (OZEMA29) is a form of female infertility. Reported features include oocyte maturation arrest and degeneration, impaired ovarian folliculogenesis, diminished ovarian reserve, failure of superovulation, and repeated failure of in vitro fertilization (IVF) (He et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2021; Du et al., 2023). Ruotsalainen et al. (2024) noted that all affected individuals published up to that time had primary infertility that did not respond to treatment.

### Clinical Features

Yang et al. (2021) studied 2 unrelated infertile families in which affected women had oocyte maturation defects. The authors linked the condition to repeated failure of IVF. Only 2 immature metaphase I (MI) oocytes could be obtained from the patients. ⚠︎

Wang et al. (2021) reported 2 infertile sisters from a consanguineous family who had oocyte maturation arrest and degeneration.

Du et al. (2023) reported a 32-year-old woman who had been diagnosed with unexplained primary infertility for 10 years. Her basal hormone levels were roughly normal, but her basal follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) levels were elevated. Her anti-Mullerian hormone level was within the normal range. Two attempts at oocyte collection had failed. The authors described her phenotype as impaired ovarian folliculogenesis and failure of superovulation.

Ruotsalainen et al. (2024) studied women in the Finnish FinnGen cohort who were homozygous for a TBPL2 stop-gained variant (R299X). These women had significantly fewer offspring than women in the other genotype groups: an average of 0.16 (SD = 0.58) offspring, compared with an average of 1.75 (p = 1.4 x 10(-15)). Of the 41 homozygous women, 2 had given birth to 1 child and 2 had given birth to more than 1 child. All 4 of these women had undergone IVF therapy, but no information was available on whether donor oocytes had been used. In phenome-wide association analysis, the variant was associated only with infertility and with no other diseases.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OZEMA29 is autosomal recessive. Homozygous mutations in the TBPL2 gene were found in affected women by Yang et al. (2021) and Wang et al. (2021), and compound heterozygous mutations were found by Du et al. (2023). In addition, the recessive association analysis of Ruotsalainen et al. (2024) showed that infertility was significantly more frequent only among women carrying 2 copies of the TBPL2 effect allele.

### Molecular Genetics

He et al. (2020) reported a recurrent mutation in the TBPL2 gene as a cause of diminished ovarian reserve and female infertility.

Yang et al. (2021) performed whole-exome sequencing in 2 unrelated infertile families with oocyte maturation defects and confirmed the findings by Sanger sequencing. Affected women in both families were homozygous for a splice site mutation in the TBPL2 gene (c.788+3A-G). Haplotype analysis indicated that the disease alleles in the 2 families had arisen independently. In a minigene assay, the variant disrupted the integrity of TBPL2 mRNA. Transcriptome sequencing of affected oocytes showed that genes vital for oocyte maturation and fertilization were broadly and markedly downregulated. The authors noted that direct functional evidence was lacking.

Wang et al. (2021) studied 2 infertile sisters with oocyte maturation arrest and degeneration from a consanguineous family. Using whole-exome sequencing, they identified a homozygous missense mutation in the TBPL2 gene (c.895T-C), resulting in a cys299-to-arg (C299R) substitution. The mutation was rare and impaired the transcription initiation function of the protein.

Du et al. (2023) performed whole-exome sequencing in a 32-year-old woman with primary infertility and impaired ovarian folliculogenesis. They identified compound heterozygous mutations in the TBPL2 gene: a novel c.802C-T transition resulting in an arg268-to-ter (R268X) substitution, and the previously reported c.788+3A-G variant, which they designated p.Arg233Ter. The mutations were confirmed by Sanger sequencing in the patient and her family members. Allele frequencies were checked in the ExAC and gnomAD databases, and the variants were predicted to be deleterious by SIFT, MutationTaster, and CADD. ⚠︎

Ruotsalainen et al. (2024) performed a genome-wide association study in the Finnish FinnGen cohort of 22,849 women with infertility and 198,989 control women who had given birth. The recessive analysis identified a stop-gained variant in the TBPL2 gene (c.895A-T, NM_199047.3), resulting in an arg299-to-ter (R299X) substitution. The variant was associated with infertility with an odds ratio of 650.1 (p = 4.11 x 10(-25)), an effect comparable to highly penetrant monogenic mutations. Women homozygous for the variant had significantly fewer offspring than other women. The premature stop codon lies in the DNA-binding part of the protein, and the variant was predicted to be a high-confidence loss-of-function variant with a CADD score of 40.

### Population Genetics

Ruotsalainen et al. (2024) found that the TBPL2 R299X variant had a minor allele frequency of 1.1% in Finland and was 43 times more common there than in non-Finnish Europeans. The variant was fairly evenly distributed across Finland, with regional allele frequencies of 0.84% to 2.38%.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that acrofrontofacionasal dysostosis-3 (AFFND3) is caused by homozygous mutation in the NBAS gene (608025) on chromosome 2p24.3. ⚠︎

### Description

Acrofrontofacionasal dysostosis is an extremely rare autosomal recessive syndrome. It comprises facial and skeletal abnormalities, short stature, and intellectual disability (Palagano et al., 2018).

### Nomenclature

Prontera et al. (2011) and Palagano et al. (2018) referred to the disorder in their patients as acrofrontofacionasal dysostosis type 1 (AFFND1).

### Clinical Features

Prontera et al. (2011) reported acrofrontofacionasal dysostosis in 2 sisters of Indian origin.

Palagano et al. (2018) studied an Indian family with 2 affected sibs who had acrofrontofacionasal dysostosis, which the authors described as comprising facial and skeletal abnormalities, short stature, and intellectual disability.

### Inheritance

Palagano et al. (2018) described acrofrontofacionasal dysostosis as an autosomal recessive syndrome. In the family they reported, the 2 affected sibs carried a homozygous NBAS mutation.

### Molecular Genetics

Palagano et al. (2018) used exome sequencing to study an Indian family with 2 sibs with acrofrontofacionasal dysostosis. In the patients they identified a novel homozygous truncating mutation in the NBAS gene. In HEK293T cells, overexpression of the truncated NBAS protein impaired both nonsense-mediated decay and Golgi-to-endoplasmic reticulum retrograde traffic. These effects were assessed by real-time PCR, Western blot, coimmunoprecipitation, and immunofluorescence. In mouse embryos at various developmental stages, Palagano et al. (2018) detected NBAS expression by immunohistochemistry in developing chondrogenic and osteogenic structures of the skeleton and in the cortex, hippocampus, and cerebellum. They noted that this pattern was compatible with a role in bone and brain development.

### Animal Model

Palagano et al. (2018) found that depleting endogenous nbas in zebrafish embryos caused defective morphogenesis of chondrogenic cranial skeletal elements. They concluded that this pointed to a conserved function of NBAS in skeletal morphogenesis.

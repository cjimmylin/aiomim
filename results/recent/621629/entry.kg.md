### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that acrofrontofacionasal dysostosis-3 (AFFND3) is caused by homozygous mutation in the NBAS gene (608025) on chromosome 2p24.3. ⚠︎

### Description

Acrofrontofacionasal dysostosis is an extremely rare autosomal recessive syndrome characterized by facial and skeletal abnormalities, short stature, and intellectual disability (Palagano et al., 2018).

### Clinical Features

Prontera et al. (2011) reported acrofrontofacionasal dysostosis type 1 in 2 sisters of Indian origin.

Palagano et al. (2018) studied an Indian family with 2 affected sibs who had been diagnosed with acrofrontofacionasal dysostosis type 1. They described the disorder as an extremely rare autosomal recessive syndrome with facial and skeletal abnormalities, short stature, and intellectual disability.

### Inheritance

Palagano et al. (2018) described acrofrontofacionasal dysostosis as an autosomal recessive syndrome. In the Indian family they studied, the 2 affected sibs were homozygous for a truncating mutation in the NBAS gene, which is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Palagano et al. (2018) used exome sequencing in 2 affected sibs from an Indian family with acrofrontofacionasal dysostosis and identified a novel homozygous truncating mutation in the NBAS gene. When the truncated NBAS protein was overexpressed in HEK293T cells, both nonsense-mediated decay and Golgi-to-endoplasmic reticulum retrograde traffic were impaired.

### Animal Model

Using immunohistochemistry, Palagano et al. (2018) found that the NBAS protein was expressed in mouse embryos. Expression was seen in developing chondrogenic and osteogenic skeletal structures and in the cortex, hippocampus, and cerebellum. In zebrafish embryos, depleting endogenous z-nbas caused defective morphogenesis of the chondrogenic cranial skeletal elements.

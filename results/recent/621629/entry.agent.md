### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that acrofrontofacionasal dysostosis-3 (AFFND3) is caused by homozygous mutation in the NBAS gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Palagano et al. (2018) described acrofrontofacionasal dysostosis as an extremely rare autosomal recessive syndrome made up of facial and skeletal abnormalities, short stature, and intellectual disability.

### Nomenclature

Prontera et al. (2011) and Palagano et al. (2018) both called this disorder acrofrontofacionasal dysostosis type 1 (AFFND1).

### Clinical Features

Prontera et al. (2011) reported 2 sisters of Indian origin with acrofrontofacionasal dysostosis. Only the title of this report was available.

### Inheritance

Palagano et al. (2018) described acrofrontofacionasal dysostosis as autosomal recessive. In the Indian family they studied, both affected sibs carried a homozygous NBAS mutation, which fits that mode of inheritance.

### Molecular Genetics

Palagano et al. (2018) studied an Indian family with 2 affected sibs. Exome sequencing found a novel homozygous truncating mutation in the NBAS gene in both patients. Two cellular processes linked to NBAS, nonsense-mediated decay and Golgi-to-endoplasmic reticulum retrograde traffic, were impaired in HEK293T cells overexpressing the truncated NBAS protein. The authors tested this by real-time PCR, Western blot analysis, coimmunoprecipitation, and immunofluorescence. In mouse embryos at various developmental stages, immunohistochemistry showed NBAS expression in developing chondrogenic and osteogenic structures of the skeleton and in the cortex, hippocampus, and cerebellum.

### Animal Model

Palagano et al. (2018) found that depleting endogenous nbas in zebrafish embryos caused defective morphogenesis of the chondrogenic cranial skeletal elements.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that recurrent hydatidiform mole-6 (HYDM6) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the KASH5 gene. ⚠︎

### Description

Hydatidiform moles (HMs) are abnormal human pregnancies in which embryonic development is impaired and the trophoblast proliferates excessively. Recurrent HM is defined as at least 2 HMs in the same patient, and some cases are familial. Rezaei et al. (2024) noted that mutations in genes with roles in meiosis I are associated with recurrent androgenetic complete HMs. Affected women may also have miscarriages and infertility. Saharan et al. (2026) reported that recurrent HM can progress to gestational trophoblastic neoplasia (GTN), including metastatic GTN.

### Clinical Features

Rezaei et al. (2024) reported a Jordanian woman (patient 439) who had had 3 HMs and 2 miscarriages.

Saharan et al. (2026) reported a woman with recurrent HMs who had recurrent GTNs. Her sister also had recurrent moles and died of metastatic GTN.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of HYDM6 in the families reported by Rezaei et al. (2024) and Saharan et al. (2026) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. ⚠︎

### Heterogeneity

Rezaei et al. (2024) performed whole-exome sequencing in 75 unrelated patients with recurrent HM who were negative for recessive mutations in NLRP7 and KHDC3L. They found biallelic deleterious variants in FOXL2, MAJIN, KASH5, SYCP2, and HFM1, and found MEIOB variants in an additional patient. All of these genes except FOXL2 have roles in meiosis I. Rezaei et al. (2024) stated that the single FOXL2 missense variant was classified as a variant of unknown significance and did not allow conclusions about whether FOXL2 has a causal role in recurrent HM. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Rezaei et al. (2024) performed whole-exome sequencing in a Jordanian woman with 3 HMs and 2 miscarriages (patient 439) and identified 2 heterozygous variants in the KASH5 gene: a c.1555C-T transition predicted to cause an arg519-to-ter (R519X) substitution, and a c.1604T-A transversion predicted to cause a leu535-to-gln (L535Q) substitution. Sanger sequencing confirmed both variants. Only R519X was inherited from the mother, so the 2 variants were most likely on different parental chromosomes. Rezaei et al. (2024) noted that L535Q had previously been reported in the homozygous state in 2 infertile Qatari brothers with azoospermia. KASH5 encodes a component of the inner nuclear membrane to which telomeres are anchored, which Rezaei et al. (2024) noted is essential for homologous chromosome movement and synapsis.

Saharan et al. (2026) performed whole-exome sequencing in a woman with recurrent HMs and recurrent GTNs and identified a very rare homozygous variant in the KASH5 gene, predicted to cause an arg196-to-his (R196H) substitution. Candidate variants were validated by Sanger sequencing. Her sister, who also had recurrent moles, died of metastatic GTN.

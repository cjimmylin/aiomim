### Description

Recurrent hydatidiform mole is a disorder of female reproduction in which affected women have repeated pregnancy losses that take the form of hydatidiform moles. These are abnormal pregnancies with hydropic degeneration of the chorionic villi, trophoblastic proliferation and abnormal or absent embryonic development. Some forms are biparental, caused by maternal-effect defects in oocyte-derived factors. Others are androgenetic and come from defects in female meiosis. The affected women are otherwise healthy. ✗

### Inheritance

The known forms of recurrent hydatidiform mole show autosomal recessive inheritance and are expressed only in women, as a maternal-effect phenotype. ✗

### Heterogeneity

Recurrent hydatidiform mole is genetically heterogeneous. HYDM1 is caused by mutation in the NLRP7 gene and HYDM2 by mutation in the KHDC3L gene. Other forms, including androgenetic moles, have been associated with biallelic mutations in genes that act in female meiosis, such as MEI1, TOP6BL and REC114.

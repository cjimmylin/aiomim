### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that recurrent hydatidiform mole-6 (HYDM6) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the KASH5 gene (618125) on chromosome 19q13.33. ⚠︎

### Description

Recurrent hydatidiform mole-6 (HYDM6) is an autosomal recessive female reproductive disorder in which affected women have recurrent hydatidiform moles. Reported patients have also had miscarriages, and in one family, recurrent or metastatic gestational trophoblastic neoplasia (GTN). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Rezaei et al. (2024) reported a Jordanian woman (patient 439) with recurrent hydatidiform moles. She had had 3 hydatidiform moles and 2 miscarriages. She came from a cohort of 75 unrelated patients with recurrent hydatidiform moles who did not have recessive mutations in NLRP7 or KHDC3L.

Saharan et al. (2026) reported a woman with recurrent hydatidiform moles who went on to have recurrent gestational trophoblastic neoplasia (GTN). Her sister also had recurrent moles and died of metastatic GTN. Saharan et al. (2026) also reviewed the literature on GTN rates in patients with recurrent hydatidiform moles and compared them across genetic causes. They noted that current data are too limited for interpreting overall risk. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of HYDM6 in the patients reported by Rezaei et al. (2024) and Saharan et al. (2026) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. The patient of Rezaei et al. (2024) was compound heterozygous for KASH5 variants, one of which she had inherited from her mother. The patient of Saharan et al. (2026) was homozygous for a KASH5 variant. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Rezaei et al. (2024) performed whole-exome sequencing in 75 unrelated patients with recurrent hydatidiform moles who were negative for recessive mutations in NLRP7 and KHDC3L. They found deleterious biallelic variants in genes involved in meiosis I in patients with recurrent androgenetic complete moles. All 6 moles that were genotyped were androgenetic and monospermic. In a Jordanian woman (patient 439) with 3 hydatidiform moles and 2 miscarriages, they found 2 heterozygous variants in the KASH5 gene: a c.1555C-T transition, causing an arg519-to-ter (R519X) substitution, and a c.1604T-A transversion, causing a leu535-to-gln (L535Q) substitution. Only the R519X variant was inherited from her mother, so the authors concluded that the 2 variants were most likely on different parental chromosomes. In the same study, Rezaei et al. (2024) found biallelic variants in other meiotic genes in other patients, including HFM1 (615684), MAJIN (617130), SYCP2 (604105), and MEIOB (617670). ⚠︎

Saharan et al. (2026) performed whole-exome sequencing in 2 women with recurrent hydatidiform moles. In one patient, who had recurrent gestational trophoblastic neoplasia, they found a very rare homozygous arg196-to-his (R196H) variant in the KASH5 gene. Her sister also had recurrent moles and died of metastatic GTN. The authors stated that their findings further supported an association between variants in this gene and recurrent moles.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Rezaei et al. (2024) compared 15 patients who had recurrent androgenetic moles and biallelic pathogenic variants in 9 meiosis-related genes with 70 patients who had no biallelic variants. The 15 patients had more severe reproductive outcomes: 1 live birth and 62 pregnancy losses, compared with 39 live births and 198 losses in the 70 patients (P = 0.0007).

### Pathogenesis

Rezaei et al. (2024) suggested that recurrent androgenetic complete moles may be a sign of accelerated ovarian aging, or a milder form of premature ovarian insufficiency. They noted that this would explain why androgenetic moles become more common with advanced maternal age.

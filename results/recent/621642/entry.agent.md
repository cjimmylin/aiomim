### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that isolated glycolic aciduria (GYCA) is caused by homozygous mutation in the HAO1 gene, which encodes glycolate oxidase, on chromosome 20.

### Description

Isolated glycolic aciduria is a metabolic condition caused by deficiency of glycolate oxidase (HAO1). Affected individuals have markedly increased urinary glycolic acid excretion with normal oxalate excretion, and the condition was asymptomatic in the reported individuals (Frishberg et al., 2014; McGregor et al., 2020).

### Clinical Features

Frishberg et al. (2014) reported 2 brothers, born to consanguineous healthy parents of Arab descent, who were evaluated for psychomotor delay associated with a triple-A-like syndrome (anisocoria, alacrima, and achalasia). The proband showed markedly increased urinary glycolic acid excretion with normal excretion of oxalate, citrate, and glycerate. Abdominal ultrasound showed normal-sized kidneys with normal echotexture. The genetic basis of the triple-A-like syndrome in this family was found to be unrelated to the metabolic abnormality, and Frishberg et al. (2014) concluded that the glycolic aciduria was asymptomatic.

McGregor et al. (2020) identified, through exome sequencing of 2 population-based cohorts with substantial autozygosity (Born in Bradford and Genes and Health), a healthy woman of British-Pakistani ethnicity with complete germline knockout of HAO1. At the time of assessment she was in her fifth decade and was the mother of 3 healthy children. Renal ultrasound was normal; she was overweight (BMI 30 to 35 kg/m2) but otherwise healthy apart from common non-serious short-term illnesses and pregnancy symptoms. Standard clinical blood biochemistries were repeatedly normal over the previous decade, and her serum anion gap and plasma and urinary oxalate were normal. Her plasma glycolate (171 nmol/mL; reference, 14 or less) and urinary glycolate were markedly elevated, at 12 times and 6 times the upper limit of normal in healthy reference individuals, respectively. McGregor et al. (2020) concluded that lifelong HAO1 knockout in this woman was safe and without clinical phenotype.

### Biochemical Features

In the woman with HAO1 knockout, McGregor et al. (2020) found urinary glycolate/creatinine ratios of 199 mg/g in a single void and 309 mg/g in a 24-hour collection (reference, 50 or less), with normal urinary oxalate and plasma glycerate. Plasma metabolomics and lipidomics (1,871 biochemicals) showed that most metabolites were similar to those of 25 controls, but 18 biochemicals, including glycolate, were markedly elevated (more than 5 SD above controls). Elevated cholic acid-related compounds were thought possibly to reflect reduced availability of glycine for conjugation, with unconjugated bile acids elevated and glycine- and taurine-conjugated bile acids reduced. Six of the significantly elevated metabolites had structures compatible with being potential HAO1 substrates. By comparison with data from healthy volunteers treated with lumasiran, an RNA interference therapeutic targeting HAO1, in whom maximal plasma and urinary glycolate concentrations at the highest dose were 3 to 5 times less than in this woman, McGregor et al. (2020) estimated that she likely retained less than 2% residual glycolate oxidase activity.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of isolated glycolic aciduria in the family reported by Frishberg et al. (2014) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance: the affected proband, born to consanguineous healthy parents, was homozygous for an HAO1 mutation. McGregor et al. (2020) likewise found a homozygous HAO1 mutation within a long autozygous region in an affected woman. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In a proband with isolated glycolic aciduria, born to consanguineous Arab parents, Frishberg et al. (2014) identified a homozygous splice site mutation (c.814-1G-C) in the HAO1 gene at the invariant -1 position of the intron 5 splice acceptor site by direct DNA sequencing. Because HAO1 is a liver-specific enzyme, the effect of the mutation on splicing was tested by an in vitro hybrid-minigene approach, which showed an abnormal splice variant in cells transfected with the mutant minigene vector.

In a healthy British-Pakistani woman with markedly elevated plasma and urinary glycolate, McGregor et al. (2020) identified a homozygous 1-bp deletion (c.997delC) in the HAO1 gene, predicted to cause a frameshift (Leu333SerfsTer4) truncating the final 37 amino acids of the protein. The variant was found by exome sequencing and confirmed in blood DNA sequencing reads and by Sanger sequencing of a separate saliva DNA sample. She was not homozygous for any other rare predicted loss-of-function genotypes. Since HAO1 is normally targeted to hepatocyte peroxisomes by a C-terminal SKI tripeptide, which the mutant protein is predicted to lack, the authors expressed the mutant in CHO-K1 cells; the Leu333SerfsTer4 protein showed markedly reduced levels, with unaffected mRNA levels, and was diffusely distributed (mislocalized) within cells.

### Clinical Management

McGregor et al. (2020) noted that HAO1 silencing is the mechanism of lumasiran, an investigational RNA interference therapeutic for primary hyperoxaluria type 1, and concluded that the absence of a clinical phenotype in a woman with lifelong HAO1 knockout supported the potential safety of chronic HAO1 inhibition. Frishberg et al. (2014) similarly suggested that their observation contributed to the development of substrate reduction approaches for the treatment of primary hyperoxaluria type 1.

### Population Genetics

McGregor et al. (2020) noted that no homozygous predicted loss-of-function variants in HAO1 were observed among 141,456 individuals in gnomAD v2.1, in which approximately 1 in 2,700 individuals is heterozygous for a known predicted loss-of-function HAO1 variant. They estimated the expected frequency of homozygotes in an outbred population at approximately 1 in 30 million.

In a healthy British-Pakistani woman with markedly elevated plasma and urinary glycolate and normal oxalate, McGregor et al. (2020) identified a homozygous 1-bp deletion (c.997delC) in the HAO1 gene, resulting in a frameshift and premature termination (Leu333SerfsTer4) predicted to truncate the final 37 amino acids. The variant lay within a 5.2-Mb autozygous region. Expressed in CHO-K1 cells, the mutant protein showed markedly reduced levels and mislocalization, and the authors estimated that she retained less than 2% residual glycolate oxidase activity.

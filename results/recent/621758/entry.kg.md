### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that arthrogryposis multiplex congenita-9 with immunodeficiency (AMC9) is caused by homozygous mutation in the SENP7 gene (612846) on chromosome 3q12.3.

### Description

Arthrogryposis multiplex congenita-9 with immunodeficiency (AMC9) is an autosomal recessive, usually lethal disorder. Its main features are congenital arthrogryposis, neurodevelopmental abnormalities, dysmorphism, and immunodeficiency with neutropenia, hypogammaglobulinemia, and recurrent infections. Affected infants have early respiratory failure and die in infancy (Samra et al., 2023; Kobayashi et al., 2024). Prenatal findings can also include central nervous system malformations, congenital heart disease, and renal anomalies (Saad et al., 2025).

### Clinical Features

Samra et al. (2023) described a kindred with 4 affected infants who had congenital arthrogryposis. The infants did not reach any developmental milestones and had early respiratory failure, neutropenia, and recurrent infections. All 4 died within 4 months after birth. ⚠︎

Kobayashi et al. (2024) reported 4 children from 3 unrelated families who had neurodevelopmental abnormalities, dysmorphism, and immunodeficiency. Hypogammaglobulinemia and intermittent neutropenia were prominent features of their presentation. All 4 patients died in infancy.

Kotecha et al. (2025) reported a consanguineous family in which multiple members had a severe, lethal form of arthrogryposis multiplex congenita.

Saad et al. (2025) described a consanguineous Egyptian family with a history of 3 fetal deaths, all of which had multiple congenital anomalies. Ultrasound of the current fetus showed similar anomalies, including arthrogryposis multiplex congenita, central nervous system malformations, congenital heart disease, and renal anomalies. The authors described this as the first full prenatal description of a lethal syndrome linked to SENP7.

Pal et al. (2025) reported a patient with fatal arthrogryposis multiplex congenita and immunodeficiency. They compared the main clinical features of their case with those of previously reported patients with SENP7-associated disorder. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of AMC9 is autosomal recessive. Samra et al. (2023) identified a homozygous SENP7 mutation that segregated with the disorder in a kindred with 4 affected infants. Kobayashi et al. (2024) found homozygous loss-of-function SENP7 variants in affected children from 3 unrelated families. Homozygous or recessive SENP7 variants were also found in consanguineous families by Kotecha et al. (2025) and Saad et al. (2025).

### Molecular Genetics

Samra et al. (2023) studied the 4 affected members of a kindred with fatal arthrogryposis multiplex congenita, early respiratory failure, and neutropenia. Using exome sequencing and segregation studies, they identified a homozygous stop-gain mutation in the SENP7 gene (c.1474C-T; Q492X), which they considered the probable cause of the disorder. SENP7 mRNA expression was reduced in fibroblasts from the proband.

Kobayashi et al. (2024) used international collaboration to evaluate a novel candidate disease gene. In 4 children from 3 unrelated families with immunodeficiency and neurologic and muscular phenotypes, they identified rare homozygous loss-of-function variants in the SENP7 gene.

Kotecha et al. (2025) studied affected members of a consanguineous family with lethal arthrogryposis multiplex congenita. They identified a recessive, noncoding variant in the highly conserved splice donor site of SENP7 that caused mis-splicing of the affected exons.

Saad et al. (2025) performed singleton exome sequencing on fetal DNA from a consanguineous Egyptian family with recurrent lethal multiple congenital anomalies, including arthrogryposis multiplex congenita. They identified a homozygous stop-gain mutation in the SENP7 gene (c.745C-T; R249X). The resulting premature termination codon was predicted to cause nonsense-mediated decay of the mutant mRNA. The authors proposed that the mutation impairs the isopeptidase activity of SENP7 in deconjugating polySUMO chains.

Pal et al. (2025) reported a novel missense variant in the SENP7 gene in a patient with fatal arthrogryposis multiplex congenita and immunodeficiency. They compared the molecular findings in their case with those in previously reported cases of SENP7-associated disorder. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Samra et al. (2023) studied fibroblasts from a patient homozygous for a SENP7 stop-gain mutation and found significant protein dysregulation both in total cell lysates and in the chromatin fraction. Levels of HP1-alpha, several histones, and H3K9me3 were reduced. The authors suggested that loss of SENP7 reduces heterochromatin condensation and leads to aberrant gene expression. They also suggested that SENP7 has a critical role in nervous system development, hematopoiesis, and immune function.

Kobayashi et al. (2024) noted that SENP7 is a sentrin-specific protease involved in deSUMOylation, a posttranslational modification of proteins that is essential for cell regulation. They proposed that deficiency of deSUMOylation may represent a novel mechanism of primary immunodeficiency. ⚠︎

Kotecha et al. (2025) noted that SENP7 is a deSUMOylase that regulates the transcriptional program in skeletal muscle and is critical for sarcomere assembly and skeletal muscle contraction.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 ARTHROGRYPOSIS MULTIPLEX CONGENITA 9, WITH IMMUNODEFICIENCY**  
SENP7, GLN492TER [c.1474C>T; p.Q492*]

Samra et al. (2023) studied the 4 affected members of a kindred with fatal arthrogryposis multiplex congenita, early respiratory failure, and neutropenia (AMC9). Using exome sequencing and segregation studies, they identified a homozygous c.1474C-T transition in the SENP7 gene, resulting in a gln492-to-ter (Q492X) substitution. SENP7 mRNA expression was reduced in fibroblasts from the proband. These fibroblasts also showed reduced levels of HP1-alpha, several histones, and H3K9me3.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that arthrogryposis multiplex congenita-9 with immunodeficiency (AMC9) is caused by homozygous mutation in the SENP7 gene (612846) on chromosome 3q12.3.

### Description

Arthrogryposis multiplex congenita-9 with immunodeficiency (AMC9) is an autosomal recessive disorder. Its features include congenital arthrogryposis, neurodevelopmental abnormalities with failure to reach developmental milestones, early respiratory failure, and immunodeficiency with neutropenia, hypogammaglobulinemia, and recurrent infections. Affected individuals have died in infancy or in utero (Samra et al., 2023; Kobayashi et al., 2024; Saad et al., 2025; Pal et al., 2025). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Samra et al. (2023) reported a kindred with 4 affected individuals who had congenital arthrogryposis, did not achieve any developmental milestones, and had early respiratory failure, neutropenia, and recurrent infections. All died within 4 months after birth. ⚠︎

Kobayashi et al. (2024) reported 4 children from 3 unrelated families who had neurodevelopmental abnormalities, dysmorphism, and immunodeficiency. Clinically, they had hypogammaglobulinemia and intermittent neutropenia. All 4 patients died in infancy.

Kotecha et al. (2025) reported a consanguineous family in which several members had lethal arthrogryposis multiplex congenita. ⚠︎

Saad et al. (2025) reported a consanguineous Egyptian family with a history of 3 fetal deaths, all with multiple congenital anomalies. Prenatal ultrasound of the current fetus showed similar anomalies, including arthrogryposis multiplex congenita, CNS malformations, congenital heart disease, and renal anomalies.

Pal et al. (2025) reported a patient with arthrogryposis multiplex congenita and immunodeficiency. They summarized the main clinical and molecular findings in their patient and in previously reported cases, and described the disorder as fatal arthrogryposis multiplex congenita with immunodeficiency.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of AMC9 is autosomal recessive. Samra et al. (2023) and Kobayashi et al. (2024) found homozygous SENP7 mutations in affected individuals. Kotecha et al. (2025) and Saad et al. (2025) reported consanguineous families, with a recessive SENP7 splice site mutation and a homozygous SENP7 nonsense mutation, respectively.

### Molecular Genetics

In the kindred with AMC9 reported by Samra et al. (2023), exome sequencing identified a homozygous c.1474C-T nonsense mutation in the SENP7 gene, predicted to cause a gln492-to-ter (Q492X) substitution. The authors performed segregation studies and considered the mutation the probable cause of the disorder. Fibroblasts from the proband showed decreased SENP7 mRNA expression.

In 4 children with AMC9 from 3 unrelated families, Kobayashi et al. (2024) identified rare homozygous loss-of-function variants in the SENP7 gene.

In a consanguineous family with lethal arthrogryposis multiplex congenita, Kotecha et al. (2025) identified a recessive mutation at the highly conserved splice donor site of the SENP7 gene. The mutation caused mis-splicing of the affected exons.

In a consanguineous Egyptian family with a lethal prenatal form of AMC9, Saad et al. (2025) used singleton exome sequencing of fetal DNA to identify a homozygous c.745C-T nonsense mutation in the SENP7 gene, resulting in an arg249-to-ter (R249X) substitution. The mutation was predicted to produce a transcript degraded by nonsense-mediated decay, which would impair the isopeptidase activity of SENP7 in deconjugating polySUMO chains.

In a patient with fatal arthrogryposis multiplex congenita and immunodeficiency, Pal et al. (2025) identified a novel missense variant in the SENP7 gene.

### Pathogenesis

Samra et al. (2023) studied fibroblasts from a patient with AMC9. In total cell lysates and in the chromatin fraction, they found significant protein dysregulation and reduced levels of HP1-alpha, several histones, and H3K9me3. They suggested that loss of SENP7 reduces heterochromatin condensation, leading to aberrant gene expression. ⚠︎

Kobayashi et al. (2024) noted that SENP7 is a sentrin-specific protease that deSUMOylates proteins essential for cell regulation. They proposed that deficient deSUMOylation may be a novel mechanism of primary immunodeficiency.

Kotecha et al. (2025) noted that SENP7 is a deSUMOylase that regulates the transcriptional program in skeletal muscle and is therefore critical for sarcomere assembly and skeletal muscle contraction.

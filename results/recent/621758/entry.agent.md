### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that arthrogryposis multiplex congenita-9 with immunodeficiency (AMC9) is caused by homozygous mutation in the SENP7 gene.

### Description

Arthrogryposis multiplex congenita-9 with immunodeficiency (AMC9) is an autosomal recessive lethal disorder characterized by congenital arthrogryposis, absent achievement of developmental milestones, early respiratory failure, and immunodeficiency with neutropenia, hypogammaglobulinemia, and recurrent infections, with death in infancy (Samra et al., 2023; Kobayashi et al., 2024). Prenatal presentation with multiple congenital anomalies, including central nervous system malformations, congenital heart disease, and renal anomalies, and fetal death has also been reported (Saad et al., 2025).

### Clinical Features

Samra et al. (2023) reported a kindred with 4 affected infants who presented with a spectrum of findings including congenital arthrogryposis, no achievement of developmental milestones, early respiratory failure, neutropenia, and recurrent infections. All died within 4 months after birth.

Kobayashi et al. (2024) reported 4 children from 3 unrelated families who presented with neurodevelopmental abnormalities, dysmorphism, and immunodeficiency. The clinical presentation was characterized by hypogammaglobulinemia and intermittent neutropenia, and all 4 patients died in infancy.

Kotecha et al. (2025) reported a consanguineous family with multiple members affected with a lethal form of arthrogryposis multiplex congenita.

Saad et al. (2025) described the prenatal presentation of a lethal syndrome in a consanguineous Egyptian family with a history of 3 fetal deaths, all of which presented with multiple congenital anomalies. Ultrasound assessment of the current fetus showed similar anomalies, including arthrogryposis multiplex congenita, central nervous system malformations, congenital heart disease, and renal anomalies.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of AMC9 in the families reported by Samra et al. (2023), Kobayashi et al. (2024), and Saad et al. (2025) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance, with affected individuals carrying homozygous SENP7 mutations. Kotecha et al. (2025) identified a recessive SENP7 mutation in a consanguineous family. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In a kindred with 4 infants with AMC9, Samra et al. (2023) identified a homozygous c.1474C-T nonsense mutation in the SENP7 gene, resulting in a gln492-to-ter (Q492X) substitution, by exome sequencing and segregation studies. Fibroblasts from the proband showed decreased SENP7 mRNA expression, and protein expression studies showed significant protein dysregulation in total cell lysates and in the chromatin fraction. Levels of HP1-alpha, different histones, and H3K9me3 were reduced in patient fibroblasts. Samra et al. (2023) suggested that loss of SENP7 leads to reduced heterochromatin condensation and subsequent aberrant gene expression.

In 4 children from 3 unrelated families with AMC9, Kobayashi et al. (2024) identified rare homozygous loss-of-function variants in the SENP7 gene. The patients were identified through international collaboration.

In affected members of a consanguineous family with lethal arthrogryposis multiplex congenita, Kotecha et al. (2025) identified a recessive mutation in the highly conserved splice donor site of the SENP7 gene, resulting in mis-splicing of the affected exons.

In a fetus from a consanguineous Egyptian family with a lethal multiple congenital anomaly syndrome including arthrogryposis multiplex congenita, Saad et al. (2025) identified a homozygous c.745C-T nonsense mutation in the SENP7 gene, resulting in an arg249-to-ter (R249X) substitution, by singleton exome sequencing of fetal DNA. The mutant mRNA was predicted to be degraded by nonsense-mediated decay.

Pal et al. (2025) reported a novel missense variant in SENP7 in a patient with fatal arthrogryposis multiplex congenita and immunodeficiency and summarized the clinical and molecular findings in previously reported cases. Details of the variant were not available in the abstract.

SENP7, GLN492TER [c.1474C>T; p.Gln492Ter]

In 4 affected infants from a kindred with AMC9, Samra et al. (2023) identified a homozygous c.1474C-T transition in the SENP7 gene, resulting in a gln492-to-ter (Q492X) substitution. The mutation was identified by exome sequencing and segregation studies. Patient fibroblasts showed decreased SENP7 mRNA expression and reduced levels of HP1-alpha, histones, and H3K9me3. ⚠︎

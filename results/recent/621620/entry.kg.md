### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest-26 (OZEMA26) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the MEI1 gene (608797) on chromosome 22q13.2. ⚠︎

### Description

Oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest-26 (OZEMA26) is an autosomal recessive form of primary female infertility. Affected women have embryonic arrest and recurrent implantation failure (Dong et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2026). Some have recurrent hydatidiform moles (Dong et al., 2021), and in others fertilization is abnormal, with formation of multiple pronuclei (Jiao et al., 2025). In one consanguineous family reported by Liu et al. (2025), an affected male family member had azoospermia.

### Clinical Features

Dong et al. (2021) studied 9 women with primary infertility from 7 independent families. In the first pedigree, 2 sisters had recurrent hydatidiform moles. The other 7 patients, from 6 families, had embryonic arrest or recurrent implantation failure. They were identified by screening patients with embryonic arrest, recurrent implantation failure, or recurrent pregnancy loss. ⚠︎

Wu et al. (2024) reported an infertile woman with embryonic arrest and recurrent implantation failure.

Liu et al. (2025) studied a consanguineous family with 2 affected members: an infertile woman whose embryos arrested and an infertile man with azoospermia. On preimplantation genetic testing, the arrested embryos had high rates of chromosomal abnormalities.

Jiao et al. (2025) described a 32-year-old Chinese woman (F1_II:4) who had had primary infertility for 4 years. She had regular menses and normal ovarian reserve. Her husband's semen and the karyotypes of both partners were normal. She had 22 mature oocytes. The 8 fertilized by ICSI became 3PN or PPN embryos. With conventional IVF, 5 oocytes formed normal 2PN embryos and 3 became 3PN/PPN embryos. None of the 2PN-derived embryos formed blastocysts, whereas 2 of 3 3PN/PPN-derived embryos from ICSI developed into late-stage blastocysts.

Jiao et al. (2025) also described a 35-year-old Chinese woman (F2_II:2) who had had primary infertility for 8 years. She had regular menses, normal ovarian function, and patent fallopian tubes. After 2 unsuccessful IVF cycles she underwent 1 ICSI cycle, in which 3 oocytes formed 2PN embryos and 1 formed a 1PN embryo. None of the embryos formed blastocysts, and transfer of 2 frozen embryos did not result in pregnancy.

Chen et al. (2026) studied women with primary infertility characterized by early embryonic arrest and implantation failure. They included affected women from 2 unrelated families who carried biallelic MEI1 variants.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OZEMA26 is autosomal recessive. Wu et al. (2024) showed that each of the 2 compound heterozygous MEI1 mutations in their patient was inherited from a heterozygous parent. Liu et al. (2025) found a homozygous MEI1 mutation in 2 affected members of a consanguineous family whose mother was a heterozygous carrier. Biallelic MEI1 variants were also reported in affected women by Dong et al. (2021), Jiao et al. (2025), and Chen et al. (2026).

### Molecular Genetics

In 9 infertile women from 7 families, Dong et al. (2021) identified 5 novel biallelic mutations in the MEI1 gene by whole-exome sequencing with Sanger confirmation. Two of the mutations were found in the pedigree in which 2 sisters had recurrent hydatidiform moles. The other 3 were found in 7 patients from 6 families with embryonic arrest or recurrent implantation failure. In vitro, the protein-truncating and missense mutations reduced MEI1 protein levels on Western blot. In minigene assays, the splicing mutations caused abnormal alternative splicing of MEI1.

In a woman with embryonic arrest and recurrent implantation failure, Wu et al. (2024) identified compound heterozygous frameshift mutations in the MEI1 gene (NM_152513.3): c.3002delC and c.2264_2268+11delGTGAGGTATGGACCAC. They found the mutations by whole-exome sequencing and confirmed them by Sanger sequencing. Each mutation was inherited from a heterozygous parent.

In a woman with embryonic arrest and a man with azoospermia from a consanguineous family, Liu et al. (2025) identified a homozygous deletion of all of exon 19 of the MEI1 gene. They found it by whole-exome sequencing and confirmed it by Sanger sequencing. The patients' mother was a heterozygous carrier. On cDNA sequencing, the exon was missing from the transcript. Quantitative expression analysis showed almost no MEI1 exon 19 expression in the patients. In transfected HEK293T cells, Western blotting showed a truncated MEI1 protein, consistent with the predicted premature termination in exon 20.

Jiao et al. (2025) performed whole-exome sequencing in 20 women from Guangzhou, China, who had a high rate of abnormal fertilization during ICSI. Two of the 20 women had biallelic MEI1 variants, and all 4 variants were novel. The proband of family 1 was compound heterozygous for a c.622T-C transition in exon 6, predicted to cause a ser208-to-pro (S208P) substitution, and a c.2647C-T transition in exon 21, predicted to cause an arg883-to-ter (R883X) substitution. Both were confirmed by Sanger sequencing. The proband of family 2 was compound heterozygous for a c.3037T-G transversion in exon 24, predicted to cause a trp1013-to-gly (W1013G) substitution, and a deletion of exons 21 to 31. The deletion is given as c.2546_3525del in the text and as c.2546_3825del in the table and discussion. Because the deletion spans c.3037, the family 2 proband appeared homozygous for c.3037T-G. The copy-number loss was confirmed by qPCR. Functional studies in HEK293T cells gave mixed results. The R883X variant increased MEI1 mRNA but reduced MEI1 protein, consistent with nonsense-mediated decay. The W1013G variant increased MEI1 protein levels. Neither the S208P variant, which was classified as of uncertain significance, nor the exon deletion altered protein expression.

In affected women from 2 unrelated families with infertility due to early embryonic arrest and implantation failure, Chen et al. (2026) identified biallelic MEI1 variants by whole-exome sequencing with Sanger confirmation. The variants were a novel homozygous variant in one family and compound heterozygous variants in the other. Bioinformatics analysis indicated that the variants were rare and possibly deleterious. In transfected HEK293T cells, the variants did not alter the subcellular localization of MEI1. However, they significantly affected MEI1 mRNA and protein expression levels.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Jiao et al. (2025) reviewed reported MEI1 variants. Variants associated with nonobstructive azoospermia tended to lie toward the N terminus, whereas variants associated with embryonic developmental arrest tended to cluster toward the C terminus. The authors stated that this hypothesis requires further validation.

### Pathogenesis

Wu et al. (2024) studied embryos from their MEI1-mutant patient by whole-genome sequencing and whole-genome bisulfite sequencing. Two day-3 cleavage-stage embryos and 2 day-6 blastocysts were all aneuploid, with numerous monosomies and trisomies. Global methylation levels in the 2 blastocysts were 0.344 and 0.168, far outside the optimal window of 0.25 to 0.27. The authors suggested that the genetic makeup of arrested embryos may help explain why MEI1 mutations cause either embryonic arrest or monospermic androgenetic hydatidiform moles.

Liu et al. (2025) suggested that aneuploidy in embryos carrying the MEI1 mutation may explain their reduced developmental potential and their arrest.

Jiao et al. (2025) performed whole-genome sequencing and parental-contamination testing on 3 embryos from the family 1 proband. All 3 were triploid with an excess of maternal chromosomes, including an embryo that had appeared to be a normal 2PN embryo. Haplotype analysis indicated that the triploidy arose in meiosis. The 3PN zygote had a meiosis II error, and the 3PN blastocyst and the 2PN blastomere had meiosis I errors. The authors proposed that the MEI1 variants impair meiosis I through abnormal chromosome synapsis.

Jiao et al. (2025) also performed RNA-seq on 3 day-3 embryos from the family 1 proband and compared them with control embryos. They found 2,005 downregulated and 1,904 upregulated genes. The downregulated genes were enriched for the meiotic cell cycle and for epigenetic regulators, including histone demethylases and deacetylases. Maternal-effect genes were dysregulated, and qPCR confirmed reduced expression of MEI1, MKI67, ASTL, and WEE2. The authors proposed a dual mechanism. In one arm, the meiotic and mitotic cell cycles and genomic stability are impaired. In the other, dysregulated epigenetic processes disrupt zygotic genome activation.

### Diagnosis

Jiao et al. (2025) recommended screening for MEI1 variants in patients with abnormal fertilization patterns, especially multiple pronuclei after ICSI. They noted that 2PN embryos that look morphologically normal may still be triploid. They also suggested that 3PN/PPN blastocysts might implant and could develop into hydatidiform moles in natural pregnancies.

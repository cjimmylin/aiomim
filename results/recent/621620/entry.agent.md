### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest-26 (OZEMA26) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the MEI1 gene on chromosome 22. ⚠︎

### Description

Oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest-26 (OZEMA26) is a form of female primary infertility. It is marked by early embryonic arrest and implantation failure during assisted reproduction cycles (Dong et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2026). Embryos from affected women have shown extensive aneuploidy (Wu et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025). Some patients have shown abnormal fertilization with multiple pronuclei (Jiao et al., 2025). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Dong et al. (2021) studied 9 women with primary infertility from 7 families. In the first pedigree, 2 sisters had recurrent hydatidiform moles. Screening of patients with embryonic arrest, recurrent implantation failure, and recurrent pregnancy loss identified 7 further patients from 6 families. In these patients, biallelic MEI1 mutations were associated with early embryonic arrest and recurrent implantation failure.

Wu et al. (2024) reported an infertile woman with embryonic arrest and recurrent implantation failure. Two of her day-3 embryos and 2 of her day-6 blastocysts were all aneuploid, with numerous monosomy and trisomy events. In the 2 blastocysts, global methylation levels were 0.344 and 0.168, far outside the optimized window of 0.25 to 0.27.

Liu et al. (2025) studied a consanguineous family in which a man had azoospermia and a woman had embryonic arrest, both with infertility. Preimplantation genetic testing of her arrested embryos showed highly abnormal chromosomes.

Jiao et al. (2025) reported 2 unrelated Chinese women with embryonic developmental arrest. They were identified among 20 women with a high rate of abnormal fertilization during ICSI at a center in Guangzhou, China. The first proband was a 32-year-old woman with a 4-year history of primary infertility. She had regular menstrual cycles and a normal ovarian reserve. Eight of her ICSI-fertilized oocytes became 3PN or PPN embryos. Of her IVF-fertilized oocytes, 5 showed normal 2PN formation and 3 became 3PN/PPN embryos. None of the 2PN-derived embryos from conventional IVF formed blastocysts. In contrast, 2 of 3 3PN/PPN-derived embryos from ICSI developed into late-stage blastocysts. Her sister had normal fertility. The second proband was a 35-year-old woman with an 8-year history of primary infertility and normal ovarian function. She had 2 unsuccessful IVF cycles before her third, ICSI, cycle. In that cycle, 3 oocytes became normal 2PN embryos and 1 became a 1PN embryo. Two of 3 viable frozen embryos were transferred, but neither resulted in pregnancy. Jiao et al. (2025) noted that the 3PN/PPN embryos reached the blastocyst stage. They suggested that in natural pregnancies such embryos might implant and develop into hydatidiform moles. ⚠︎

Chen et al. (2026) studied women with primary infertility marked by early embryonic arrest and implantation failure. They found biallelic MEI1 variants in 2 unrelated families.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OZEMA26 is autosomal recessive. Wu et al. (2024) found that each of their proband's 2 MEI1 variants was inherited from a heterozygous parent, consistent with autosomal recessive transmission. Homozygous and compound heterozygous mutations were also reported by Liu et al. (2025), Jiao et al. (2025), and Chen et al. (2026).

### Molecular Genetics

In a study of 9 women with primary infertility from 7 families, Dong et al. (2021) used whole-exome sequencing with Sanger confirmation to identify 5 novel MEI1 mutations. Some patients had recurrent hydatidiform moles, and others had early embryonic arrest and recurrent implantation failure. In those with embryonic arrest or implantation failure, the mutations were biallelic. Western blotting showed that protein-truncating and missense mutations reduced MEI1 protein levels. Minigene analysis showed that splicing mutations caused abnormal alternative splicing of MEI1.

Wu et al. (2024) studied an infertile woman with embryonic arrest and recurrent implantation failure. Whole-exome sequencing with Sanger validation identified 2 frameshift mutations in the MEI1 gene: c.3002delC and c.2264_2268+11delGTGAGGTATGGACCAC (NM_152513.3). Each was inherited from a heterozygous parent.

In a consanguineous family, Liu et al. (2025) used whole-exome sequencing to identify a homozygous deletion of MEI1 exon 19. Carriers were a man with azoospermia and a woman with embryonic arrest. Their mother was a heterozygous carrier. Sequencing of cDNA confirmed loss of the whole exon, and patients had almost no detectable expression of exon 19. In transfected HEK293T cells, western blotting showed that the deletion produced a truncated MEI1 protein. This was consistent with the predicted premature termination in exon 20.

Jiao et al. (2025) used whole-exome sequencing to find compound heterozygous MEI1 variants in 2 unrelated women with embryonic developmental arrest. The first woman carried S208P and R883X. The second carried W1013G together with a deletion of exons 21 to 31. Because that deletion includes position c.3037, the second woman appeared homozygous for the c.3037T-G change on sequencing. The deletion was confirmed by qPCR. In transfected HEK293T cells, R883X reduced MEI1 protein levels and W1013G increased them. S208P and the exon 21-31 deletion did not change protein expression. Whole-genome sequencing of 3 embryos from the first patient showed that all were triploid, with an excess of maternal chromosomes. Two of the embryos were also aneuploid. Haplotype analysis indicated a meiotic origin of the triploidy: a meiosis II error in the 3PN zygote and meiosis I errors in the other 2 embryos.

Chen et al. (2026) studied 2 unrelated families with early embryonic arrest and implantation failure. Whole-exome sequencing with Sanger confirmation identified a homozygous MEI1 variant in one family and compound heterozygous variants in the other. In transfected HEK293T cells, the variants did not change the subcellular localization of MEI1. However, they significantly affected its mRNA and protein expression levels.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Dong et al. (2021) found that biallelic MEI1 mutations could cause recurrent hydatidiform moles in some women and early embryonic arrest and recurrent implantation failure in others. Liu et al. (2025) found that the same homozygous MEI1 exon 19 deletion caused azoospermia in a man and embryonic arrest in a woman from one consanguineous family. Jiao et al. (2025) reviewed reported MEI1 variants. They observed that variants associated with nonobstructive azoospermia tended to lie toward the N terminus and those associated with embryonic developmental arrest toward the C terminus, a pattern they noted needs further validation.

### Pathogenesis

Jiao et al. (2025) performed RNA sequencing on 3 day-3 embryos from a woman with compound heterozygous MEI1 mutations and compared them with control embryos. They found 2,005 downregulated and 1,904 upregulated genes. Downregulated genes were enriched in meiotic cell cycle pathways and in epigenetic regulation, including histone demethylase and deacetylase activity. Several maternal-effect genes, including FBXO43, OOEP, and WEE2, were dysregulated. From these results, the authors proposed that MEI1 dysfunction impairs both meiotic and genomic stability and the activation of the zygotic genome.

**.0004 OOCYTE/ZYGOTE/EMBRYO MATURATION ARREST 26**  
MEI1, SER208PRO [c.622T>C; p.Ser208Pro]

In a 32-year-old Chinese woman with primary infertility, Jiao et al. (2025) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the MEI1 gene by whole-exome sequencing. One was a c.622T-C transition resulting in a ser208-to-pro (S208P) substitution. The other was a c.2647C-T transition resulting in an arg883-to-ter (R883X) substitution. Her fertilization was abnormal, with 3PN/PPN embryos, and her embryos were triploid with excess maternal chromosomes. Structural modeling predicted that S208P alters hydrophobic interactions. In transfected HEK293T cells, S208P did not change protein expression. ⚠︎

**.0006 OOCYTE/ZYGOTE/EMBRYO MATURATION ARREST 26**  
MEI1, TRP1013GLY [c.3037T>G; p.Trp1013Gly]

In a 35-year-old Chinese woman with primary infertility and embryonic developmental arrest, Jiao et al. (2025) identified compound heterozygous mutations in the MEI1 gene. One was a c.3037T-G transversion resulting in a trp1013-to-gly (W1013G) substitution. The other was a deletion of exons 21 to 31, given as c.2546_3525del in the text and c.2546_3825del in the table. Because the deletion spans c.3037, the patient appeared homozygous for c.3037T-G on sequencing, and qPCR confirmed the deletion. In transfected HEK293T cells, W1013G increased MEI1 protein levels, whereas the exon 21-31 deletion did not change protein expression. ⚠︎

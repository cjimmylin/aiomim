### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest-26 (OZEMA26) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the MEI1 gene on chromosome 22. ⚠︎

### Description

Oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest-26 (OZEMA26) is an autosomal recessive form of female infertility. Affected women have primary infertility with early embryonic arrest and recurrent implantation failure during assisted reproduction (Dong et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2026). Some patients show abnormal fertilization, with tripronuclear or polypronuclear zygotes (Jiao et al., 2025). Embryos from affected women have been found to carry extensive chromosomal abnormalities (Wu et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025; Jiao et al., 2025).

### Clinical Features

Dong et al. (2021) studied a family in which 2 sisters had recurrent hydatidiform moles. They then screened for MEI1 mutations in patients with embryonic arrest, recurrent implantation failure, and recurrent pregnancy loss, and identified 7 more patients from 6 families. Altogether there were 9 patients with primary infertility from 7 independent families. Besides recurrent hydatidiform moles, biallelic MEI1 mutations were associated with early embryonic arrest and recurrent implantation failure.

Wu et al. (2024) reported an infertile woman with embryonic arrest and recurrent implantation failure. Two of her day-3 embryos and 2 of her day-6 blastocysts were all aneuploid and had many monosomies and trisomies.

Liu et al. (2025) studied a consanguineous family that included an infertile woman with embryonic arrest and an infertile man with azoospermia. Preimplantation genetic testing found a high level of chromosomal anomalies in the woman's arrested embryos.

Jiao et al. (2025) reported 2 unrelated Chinese women with primary infertility and repeated failure of in vitro fertilization (IVF) and intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI). The proband in family 1 was 32 years old and had had primary infertility for 4 years. Her menstrual cycles were regular, her ovarian reserve was normal, and she had a normal karyotype. Of 25 oocytes retrieved, 22 were mature. Ten mature oocytes underwent ICSI, and 8 of these formed tripronuclear (3PN) or polypronuclear (PPN) zygotes. Of the 12 oocytes fertilized by conventional IVF, 5 formed normal 2-pronuclear (2PN) zygotes and 3 formed 3PN/PPN zygotes. None of the 2PN-derived embryos reached the blastocyst stage, so no embryos were frozen or transferred. However, 2 of 3 embryos derived from 3PN/PPN zygotes after ICSI developed into late-stage blastocysts. The proband in family 2 was 35 years old and had had primary infertility for 8 years. Her menstrual cycles were regular and her ovarian function was normal. Before her ICSI cycle she had undergone 2 unsuccessful IVF cycles. In the ICSI cycle, 11 oocytes were retrieved; 2PN zygotes formed, and 1 oocyte formed a 1-pronuclear zygote. None of her embryos formed blastocysts. Two frozen embryos were transferred, but she did not become pregnant. In both probands, most day-3 embryos were of high quality at the 7- to 9-cell stage. Jiao et al. (2025) noted that, unlike previously reported MEI1-related phenotypes, the main feature in these patients was the formation of 3PN or PPN zygotes after ICSI. ⚠︎

Chen et al. (2026) reported women from 2 unrelated families who had primary infertility characterized by early embryonic arrest and implantation failure.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of OZEMA26 is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Wu et al. (2024) found that each of the 2 MEI1 mutations in their proband was inherited from a heterozygous parent. Liu et al. (2025) found a homozygous mutation in affected members of a consanguineous family. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Dong et al. (2021) used whole-exome sequencing, with Sanger sequencing for confirmation, to identify 5 novel biallelic mutations in the MEI1 gene in 9 patients from 7 families. In vitro, the protein-truncating and missense mutations reduced the level of MEI1 protein. In minigene assays, the splicing mutations caused abnormal alternative splicing of MEI1.

In a woman with embryonic arrest and recurrent implantation failure, Wu et al. (2024) identified compound heterozygous frameshift mutations in the MEI1 gene: c.3002delC and c.2264_2268+11delGTGAGGTATGGACCAC (NM_152513.3). The mutations were found by whole-exome sequencing and confirmed by Sanger sequencing. Each mutation was inherited from a heterozygous parent.

In 2 infertile sibs from a consanguineous family, Liu et al. (2025) identified a homozygous deletion of the whole of exon 19 of the MEI1 gene. The woman had embryonic arrest and the man had azoospermia. The deletion was found by whole-exome sequencing, and cDNA sequencing confirmed loss of the exon. Exon 19 expression was almost undetectable in the patients. Their mother was a heterozygous carrier. On Western blot analysis, the deletion produced a truncated MEI1 protein, consistent with the predicted premature termination in exon 20.

Jiao et al. (2025) performed whole-exome sequencing in 20 women who had a high rate of abnormal fertilization with ICSI. They identified compound heterozygous MEI1 mutations in 2 unrelated women. The proband in family 1 carried a ser208-to-pro (S208P) missense mutation (c.622T-C) and an arg883-to-ter (R883X) nonsense mutation (c.2647C-T). The proband in family 2 carried a trp1013-to-gly (W1013G) missense mutation (c.3037T-G) and a deletion of exons 21 through 31. The paper gives this deletion as c.2546_3525del in the abstract and results text, and as c.2546_3825del in the figure legend, Table 2, and discussion. Because the deletion covers the c.3037 position, the W1013G mutation appeared homozygous on exome and Sanger sequencing. Quantitative PCR confirmed the copy number loss. All 4 mutations affected highly conserved residues. Under ACMG criteria, R883X was classified as pathogenic, the exon 21-31 deletion as likely pathogenic, and S208P and W1013G as variants of uncertain significance. In transfected HEK293T cells, R883X increased MEI1 mRNA but reduced MEI1 protein, and W1013G increased the protein level. S208P and the exon 21-31 deletion did not change protein expression. MEI1 mRNA levels in peripheral blood from both probands were significantly higher than in controls.

In women from 2 unrelated families with early embryonic arrest and implantation failure, Chen et al. (2026) used whole-exome sequencing, with Sanger sequencing for confirmation, to identify biallelic MEI1 mutations. One family had a novel homozygous mutation and the other had compound heterozygous mutations. In transfected HEK293T cells, the mutations did not change the subcellular localization of MEI1 but significantly affected MEI1 mRNA and protein levels.

### Pathogenesis

Wu et al. (2024) used whole-genome bisulfite sequencing on 2 blastocysts from a woman with compound heterozygous MEI1 mutations. Global methylation levels were 0.344 and 0.168, well outside the optimal range of 0.25 to 0.27. Both blastocysts were also aneuploid. Wu et al. (2024) suggested that the genetic makeup of arrested embryos from women with MEI1 mutations could help explain why some affected women have embryonic arrest while others have monospermic androgenetic hydatidiform moles.

Liu et al. (2025) proposed that embryo aneuploidy caused by MEI1 mutation may underlie the reduced developmental potential and embryonic arrest.

Jiao et al. (2025) analyzed 3 embryos from the proband in family 1, who carried the S208P and R883X mutations. Whole-genome sequencing showed chromosomal aneuploidies in a 3PN-derived blastocyst and a 2PN-derived blastomere. Quantitative parental contamination testing and kinship analysis showed that all 3 embryos were triploid, with excess maternal and little paternal genetic material. Haplotype analysis indicated that the triploidy arose in meiosis: in meiosis II in the 3PN zygote, and in meiosis I in the 3PN blastocyst and the 2PN blastomere. RNA sequencing of day-3 embryos found 2,005 downregulated and 1,904 upregulated genes compared with control embryos. Downregulated genes were enriched for meiotic cell cycle functions and epigenetic regulation of transcription, including histone demethylase and deacetylase activity. Several maternal-effect genes were dysregulated, including FBXO43, OOEP, and WEE2. Quantitative PCR confirmed reduced mRNA levels of MEI1, MKI67, ASTL, and WEE2.

### Clinical Management

Jiao et al. (2025) recommended screening for MEI1 mutations in patients with abnormal fertilization patterns, especially multiple pronuclei after ICSI. They noted that in such cases even embryos that look like normal 2PN embryos may be triploid.

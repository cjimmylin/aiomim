### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that syndromic congenital fibrosis of the extraocular muscles type 3A-2 with joint contractures, developmental delay, and peripheral neuropathy (CFEOM3A2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TUBB3 gene (602661) on chromosome 16q.

### Description

CFEOM3A2 is a severe congenital neurologic disorder. From birth, patients have ptosis, extremely limited eye movements with large-angle exotropia, bilateral facial weakness, facial dysmorphism, and, in most cases, distal joint contractures. Later features include developmental delay and intellectual disability, gait disorders with proximal joint contractures, Kallmann syndrome (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and anosmia), and a progressive peripheral neuropathy that begins in the first decade of life. Brain imaging shows hypoplasia of the corpus callosum and anterior commissure, malformations of the basal ganglia, absent olfactory bulbs and sulci, and subtle cerebellar malformations (Whitman et al., 2021).

### Nomenclature

Tischfield et al. (2010) used the term 'TUBB3 syndromes' for the range of nervous system disorders caused by heterozygous missense mutations in TUBB3. Whitman et al. (2021) called the phenotype caused by the arg262-to-his (R262H) substitution the 'TUBB3 R262H syndrome.' Jia et al. (2022) noted that syndromic phenotypes have come to be named after the specific variants that cause them.

### Clinical Features

Tischfield et al. (2010) studied patients with heterozygous TUBB3 mutations. Everyone carrying the R262H or D417H substitution had severe CFEOM3 and congenital facial weakness. All were also born with contractures of the wrists and fingers, which the authors thought suggested maldevelopment of spinal motor neurons. In the first decade of life, these patients developed lower extremity weakness and sensory loss. Electromyography in patients with TUBB3 mutations showed a chronic, generalized sensorimotor polyneuropathy that was mainly axonal. Brain imaging in R262H carriers showed diffuse hypoplasia of the corpus callosum, agenesis of the anterior commissure, hypoplasia of the anterior limb of the internal capsule, and dysmorphic basal ganglia; one patient also had a dilated lateral ventricle. Patients with the R262H substitution had more severe callosal dysgenesis than those with milder mutations, along with mild to moderate intellectual, social, and behavioral impairments. A patient with R262H also had congenital ulnar deviation of the hand, with contractures of the thumb and finger joints. Imaging was not reviewed for any patient with the D417H substitution.

Whitman et al. (2021) reported 14 affected individuals from 13 unrelated families of varied ethnic backgrounds, aged from infancy to 25 years. The series included monozygotic twins and a third patient previously reported by Tischfield et al. (2010). Ten subjects showed fetal distress before birth, which led to premature delivery in 3. One subject had oligohydramnios, and 3 were small for gestational age or had intrauterine growth restriction. All 14 presented as neonates with profound bilateral limitation of eye movements, large exotropia, and facial weakness. Thirteen had distal joint contractures at birth. Eight had vocal cord paralysis with perinatal distress and needed a tracheostomy shortly after birth. Nine needed nasogastric or gastrostomy tube feeding in infancy. Two had congenital unilateral sensorineural hearing loss. Failure to thrive and short stature were common: 11 subjects were below average height, 13 were below average weight, and 6 had microcephaly. All had facial dysmorphism. Quantitative 3D facial analysis of 4 subjects showed significantly reduced facial depth, nasal protrusion, upper facial height, vertical lip dimensions, and palpebral fissure length. Intercanthal width was normal or increased.

In the patients of Whitman et al. (2021), 13 had congenital and 1 had acquired distal joint contractures. Over time, the distal contractures often worsened and more proximal contractures developed. Reported findings included camptodactyly, talipes equinovarus, ulnar deviation of the wrist, clinodactyly, syndactyly, fisted thumbs, coxa vara or coxa valgus, and progressive hamstring and Achilles contractures. One subject had a diagnosis of arthrogryposis multiplex congenita, and several had leg length discrepancy. All 14 subjects had some degree of global developmental delay, with better speech comprehension and communication than other skills, and pleasant, engaging personalities. Full-scale IQ ranged from 17 to 96. The monozygotic twins both had a diagnosis of autism spectrum disorder. Ten subjects were left-handed. Three subjects had seizures or epileptic activity that required treatment.

Every subject studied by Whitman et al. (2021) had CFEOM, with severe bilateral and often asymmetric ptosis, hypotropia, and large-angle exotropia, and little or no residual vertical or horizontal eye movement. Several subjects lifted their eyelids with their fingers. Vision was moderately reduced, at least partly from amblyopia and corneal exposure. All 14 had bilateral facial weakness. Facial nerve conduction studies showed low-amplitude responses and no recordable blink reflex. Six subjects reported anosmia or hyposmia. Nine had vocal cord paralysis, and 9 had ongoing swallowing difficulties. Of 4 subjects who had audiologic testing, 2 had unilateral congenital sensorineural hearing loss. All 8 ears tested showed evidence of neural dys-synchrony. Gross motor milestones were delayed in all subjects, and all of ambulatory age needed ankle-foot orthoses. Of 9 subjects older than 5 years, 4 learned to walk unassisted and 5 learned to walk with assistive devices. Motor function generally worsened by adolescence. Hypotonia had been reported in all 14 at some point. However, the 5 subjects examined at NIH had normal tone with upper limb hypermobility, together with variable proximal weakness, abnormal reflexes, extensor plantar responses, and ankle clonus. The authors considered these findings consistent with spasticity combined with peripheral neuropathy. ⚠︎

All 8 subjects in the series of Whitman et al. (2021) who had nerve conduction studies had abnormal results on their first study, regardless of age; the youngest was tested at 1 year. Motor and sensory responses were absent or of low amplitude, and all conduction velocities were slow and in the demyelinating range. Needle EMG of the leg muscles in a 19-year-old showed a chronic neurogenic process. The authors concluded that the neuropathy was early-onset and mainly axonal, with secondary demyelination. All subjects had 1 or more features of Kallmann syndrome. Of the 9 males, 6 had micropenis and/or cryptorchidism at birth. All 8 subjects with informative MRI had absent or hypoplastic olfactory bulbs and olfactory sulci. In the monozygotic twins, both lacked olfactory bulbs on imaging, but one had delayed puberty with abnormal laboratory tests while the other entered puberty normally. The 3 females who had reached young adulthood had anosmia and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism. Where bone density was measured, it was decreased, and bone age was delayed. The oldest subject had sinus node dysfunction with sinus arrest pauses of up to 7.5 seconds, causing syncope, and received a pacemaker at 21 years of age. Ten other subjects had asymptomatic resting heart rates of 110 to 150 beats per minute. Three subjects had cyclic vomiting syndrome. One subject developed fatal chronic kidney disease at age 15 years; whether this was related to the TUBB3 variant was unknown. ⚠︎

Brain MRI in the series of Whitman et al. (2021) showed marked thinning of the corpus callosum in every subject. Other common findings were asymmetric dysgenesis of the basal ganglia and adjacent lateral ventricles, absent olfactory bulbs and sulci, asymmetrically dysmorphic superior cerebellar hemispheres, and small extraocular muscles. Three subjects had asymmetry of the sylvian fissures, and one of them also had perisylvian polymicrogyria. Three subjects had abnormal rotation of the hippocampi. Four had decreased white matter volume, and 4 had a small, slightly dysmorphic pons. In every subject with cranial nerve imaging, the oculomotor nerve could not be seen and the facial nerve was severely hypoplastic or not visible.

Jia et al. (2022) studied Chinese patients with congenital cranial dysinnervation disorders. One of them was a 3-year-old boy (C52) with no family history of the disorder who carried the TUBB3 R262H variant. He had right-sided ptosis, exotropia and hypotropia, limited horizontal and vertical eye movements on both sides, corneal exposure, and facial weakness. He also had intellectual impairment, congenital joint contractures, leg length discrepancy, impaired gait, cryptorchidism, cyclic vomiting, and funnel chest. MRI showed absent olfactory bulbs and sulci, absent oculomotor and facial nerves, and hypoplasia of the corpus callosum, brainstem, cerebellum, basal ganglia, and thalamus. The sylvian fissures and lateral ventricles were asymmetric. Imaging also showed abnormalities of the anterior commissure, internal capsule, and hippocampus. The authors considered that this patient met the diagnostic criteria for Kallmann syndrome.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous mutations reported in CFEOM3A2 were de novo. Tischfield et al. (2010) found that the R262H mutation arose de novo or from presumed germline mosaicism. In the series of Whitman et al. (2021), no subject had a family history of the disorder and all parents were clinically unaffected. In all 11 cases (10 pedigrees) where parental DNA was tested, the mutation had arisen de novo. No inherited cases were identified, and the authors suggested that affected individuals may have reduced fertility because of neurologic deficits and/or hypogonadotropic hypogonadism. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Tischfield et al. (2010) sequenced TUBB3 in probands who had CFEOM with additional neurologic symptoms. They found a heterozygous c.785G-A transition in the TUBB3 gene, resulting in an arg262-to-his (R262H) substitution, in 2 unrelated pedigrees. The mutation arose de novo or from presumed germline mosaicism, was absent from the parents of sporadic cases, and was not found in more than 1,700 control chromosomes. In the same study, a heterozygous asp417-to-his (D417H) substitution co-segregated in a dominant fashion in 1 pedigree whose affected members also had congenital wrist and finger contractures and early-onset polyneuropathy. In vitro, the R262H mutant formed heterodimers with moderate efficiency and co-cycled with native tubulin as well as wildtype protein did. It was incorporated robustly into microtubules in HeLa cells. In yeast, the corresponding substitution altered microtubule dynamics, with a slower polymerization rate, a faster depolymerization rate, and fewer rescue events. It also markedly reduced the accumulation of the kinesins Kip3p and Kip2p at microtubule plus ends. The authors predicted that R262H breaks a hydrogen bond between arg262 and asp417. ⚠︎

Whitman et al. (2021) studied 14 affected individuals from 13 unrelated families. All carried the same heterozygous c.785G-A (R262H) mutation in TUBB3. In 8 subjects, the mutation was found through clinical targeted, exome, or genome sequencing. In 3, it was found by sequencing TUBB3 in a research setting, and 3 had been reported by Tischfield et al. (2010). In all 11 cases (10 pedigrees) in which parental DNA was available, the mutation had arisen de novo.

Jia et al. (2022) performed whole-exome sequencing in a cohort of Chinese patients with congenital cranial dysinnervation disorders. They found a heterozygous c.785G-A (R262H) mutation in TUBB3 in 1 sporadic patient with syndromic CFEOM, and confirmed it by Sanger sequencing.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Tischfield et al. (2010) found that patients with the R262C substitution had relatively isolated CFEOM3, whereas those with R262H had a severe phenotype. They noted that R262H allows much more efficient heterodimer formation and incorporation into microtubules than R262C, both in vitro and in mammalian cells. They proposed that the combination of severe CFEOM3, facial weakness, finger contractures, basal ganglia dysmorphisms, and early-onset polyneuropathy would point to an R262H or D417H substitution.

Whitman et al. (2021) found that the R262H syndrome shares many features with the TUBB3 E410K syndrome: severe CFEOM, developmental delay, facial weakness, progressive peripheral neuropathy, Kallmann syndrome, and, in some patients, vocal cord paralysis and cyclic vomiting. The two could be told apart because R262H carriers had congenital, progressive, and acquired joint contractures; neuropathy that usually began before age 12; marked limitations in mobility; and auditory dysfunction. On MRI, R262H carriers also had dysmorphic basal ganglia and lateral ventricles, and often mildly dysmorphic superior cerebellum, abnormal rotation of the hippocampus, and a small or dysmorphic pons. In the cohort of Jia et al. (2022), malformations of the basal ganglia, thalamus, brainstem, and cerebellum were associated with the R380C and R262H variants. Absent facial nerves and dysplasia of the olfactory bulbs and sulci were associated with the R262H and E410K variants.

### Pathogenesis

Tischfield et al. (2010) suggested that TUBB3 mutations act mainly by altering microtubule function in a dominant manner. Facial paralysis and progressive sensorimotor polyneuropathy occurred only with substitutions (including R262H and D417H) that permit heterodimer formation and efficient incorporation into microtubules, and that disturb kinesin interactions in yeast. Whitman et al. (2021) noted that TUBB3 is expressed in neurons but not in muscle, connective tissue, or bone. They therefore suggested that the joint contractures most likely result from dysfunction of spinal motor neurons in utero.

### Diagnosis

Before their genetic diagnosis, patients reported by Whitman et al. (2021) had received clinical diagnoses including atypical Moebius syndrome, Carey-Fineman-Ziter syndrome (in 1 subject), and Marden-Walker syndrome (in 2 subjects). Jia et al. (2022) noted that patients with the TUBB3 E410K and R262H syndromes were easily misdiagnosed as having Moebius syndrome. They suggested that MRI, whole-exome sequencing, and targeted general examinations can help establish an accurate diagnosis.

### Clinical Management

Whitman et al. (2021) reported that extensive physical therapy beginning in infancy was associated with the best motor outcomes. Orthopedic procedures and casting had mixed results, and long periods of immobility after surgery led to lasting loss of function in some subjects. Strabismus surgery generally gave disappointing results, with exotropia recurring in every case. Puberty was induced with testosterone in males and with estrogen in females. The authors recommended multidisciplinary care, including prevention and treatment of corneal exposure and amblyopia, and monitoring of bone density. They also suggested considering cardiac and autonomic evaluation.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CONGENITAL FIBROSIS OF EXTRAOCULAR MUSCLES 3A, SYNDROMIC 2, WITH JOINT CONTRACTURES, DEVELOPMENTAL DELAY, AND PERIPHERAL NEUROPATHY**  
TUBB3, ARG262HIS [c.785G>A; p.Arg262His; NM_006086.4:c.785G>A]

Tischfield et al. (2010) found a heterozygous c.785G-A transition in the TUBB3 gene, resulting in an arg262-to-his (R262H) substitution, in 2 unrelated pedigrees with severe CFEOM3, congenital facial weakness, congenital wrist and finger contractures, polyneuropathy beginning in the first decade, developmental impairment, and malformations of the corpus callosum, anterior commissure, and basal ganglia. The mutation arose de novo or from presumed germline mosaicism and was not found in more than 1,700 control chromosomes. The mutant protein formed heterodimers and was efficiently incorporated into microtubules. In yeast, the corresponding substitution altered microtubule dynamics and reduced the accumulation of kinesins at microtubule plus ends.

Whitman et al. (2021) identified the same heterozygous c.785G-A (R262H) mutation in 14 individuals with CFEOM3A2 from 13 unrelated families, including 3 previously reported by Tischfield et al. (2010). In all 11 cases (10 pedigrees) in which parental DNA was available, the mutation had arisen de novo.

Jia et al. (2022) used whole-exome sequencing to find a heterozygous c.785G-A (R262H) mutation in a 3-year-old Chinese boy with syndromic CFEOM, facial weakness, congenital joint contractures, intellectual impairment, cryptorchidism, cyclic vomiting, and brain malformations. He had no family history of the disorder.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that syndromic congenital fibrosis of the extraocular muscles-3A with joint contractures, developmental delay, and peripheral neuropathy (CFEOM3A2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TUBB3 gene (602661) on chromosome 16q24.

### Description

CFEOM3A2 is a syndromic form of congenital fibrosis of the extraocular muscles caused by specific heterozygous missense mutations in the neuron-specific beta-tubulin isotype III gene TUBB3. Affected individuals have congenital restrictive ophthalmoplegia with ptosis, usually with additional features such as congenital joint contractures, developmental delay or intellectual disability, and a later-onset axonal sensorimotor peripheral neuropathy. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Tischfield et al. (2010) described patients with TUBB3 mutations who had CFEOM3 together with additional features. Some substitutions were associated with syndromic phenotypes that included congenital joint contractures, facial weakness, developmental delay or intellectual disability, and a progressive axonal sensorimotor peripheral neuropathy that developed later in life.

### Inheritance

The syndromic TUBB3 phenotypes are caused by heterozygous mutations, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance; many reported cases resulted from de novo mutations (Tischfield et al., 2010).

### Heterogeneity

Mutations in TUBB3 cause a spectrum of disorders. Some missense mutations cause isolated or nearly isolated CFEOM3A, whereas other, recurrent amino acid substitutions are associated with syndromic phenotypes that include neurologic and other systemic features. TUBB3 mutations are also associated with malformations of cortical development.

### Molecular Genetics

Tischfield et al. (2010) identified heterozygous missense mutations in the TUBB3 gene in patients with CFEOM3, including both isolated and syndromic forms. Functional studies showed that the mutations altered microtubule dynamics and kinesin interactions, and a knockin mouse model carrying a human mutation showed defects in axon guidance of cranial nerves and of the anterior commissure and corpus callosum.

### Pathogenesis

Tischfield et al. (2010) proposed that TUBB3 mutations cause a disorder of axon guidance, in which abnormal microtubule dynamics and impaired interactions with kinesin motors disrupt the guidance of developing axons, including oculomotor nerve fibers that innervate the extraocular muscles.

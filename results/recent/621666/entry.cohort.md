### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that syndromic congenital fibrosis of extraocular muscles-3A-2 with joint contractures, developmental delay, and peripheral neuropathy (CFEOM3A2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TUBB3 gene (602661) on chromosome 16q24.

### Description

Whitman et al. (2021) described the TUBB3 R262H syndrome. Its features are congenital ptosis, ophthalmoplegia, exotropia, facial weakness and facial dysmorphisms, together with distal congenital joint contractures in most patients. Intellectual disability, gait disorders with proximal contractures, Kallmann syndrome, and a progressive peripheral neuropathy follow in the first decade. Subsets of patients have vocal cord paralysis, auditory dysfunction, cyclic vomiting, and resting tachycardia.

### Clinical Features

Tischfield et al. (2010) reported 2 pedigrees in which affected individuals had the TUBB3 R262H substitution. Patients had severe CFEOM3 and congenital facial weakness. All subjects with R262H were born with congenital wrist and finger contractures and developed lower extremity weakness and sensory loss in the first decade of life. Brain MRI in R262H patients showed agenesis of the anterior commissure and dysmorphic basal ganglia. Other reported features were diffuse hypoplasia of the corpus callosum, hypoplasia of the anterior limb of the internal capsule, and intellectual, social, and behavioral impairment.

Whitman et al. (2021) reported 14 individuals (9 males and 5 females) from 13 families, aged 3 months to 25 years at last report. Their ancestries included Syrian Iraqi, European, Egyptian, African, and Asian. Three of them, the monozygotic twins (subjects XI and XII) and subject XIV, had originally been reported by Tischfield et al. (2010). All 14 had congenital ophthalmoplegia with ptosis and exotropia, as well as bilateral facial weakness. Most had distal congenital joint contractures. Subjects I, II, and V had congenital camptodactyly, and subject I also had unilateral talipes equinovarus. Subject III had congenital hand and foot contractures that required braces, casting, and foot surgeries. Subject IV had bilateral congenital ulnar wrist deviation, and subject VI had congenital ankle and toe contractures. Subject VII had congenital contractures of the elbows and knees, clawed toes, clinodactyly, and fisted thumbs and hands, and later developed a leg length discrepancy. Subject VIII was diagnosed with arthrogryposis multiplex congenita, and subject IX had congenital coxa vara. Subject X was born with short thumbs but no contractures, and later developed upper limb hypermobility, progressive bilateral hamstring and Achilles contractures, and a leg length discrepancy. Subject XI had congenital contractures of the upper and lower extremities, hypoplasia of the thenar eminence, syndactyly, clinodactyly, and pes planovalgus. Subject XII had congenital finger contractures with clenched hands and developed progressive hamstring and Achilles contractures. He learned to stand at age 3 but lost functional mobility and needed a walker or wheelchair at all times. Subject XIII had congenital progressive Achilles contractures and later developed finger and hamstring contractures and genu valgum. Subject XIV walked with support until he had foot surgery at 8 years of age and never regained the ability to walk.

Cognition varied among the patients of Whitman et al. (2021). Subject VII had a full-scale IQ of 65, subject XI 41, and subject XII 17. Subject XIII had borderline intellectual functioning (IQ 73). Subject X had an IQ in the average range with specific learning disorders and ADHD. Subject VI had strong language skills but was notably weaker in mathematics. Subjects IX and XIV were severely impaired, and both monozygotic twins had autism spectrum disorder. All 8 subjects who had nerve conduction studies had abnormal initial studies regardless of age, the youngest at 1 year. These studies showed absent or low-amplitude sensory and motor responses and slow conduction velocities, and the authors concluded that the neuropathy is an early-onset, primarily axonal neuropathy with secondary demyelination. Subject VII had a very high pain tolerance in his feet and decreased sensation in the lower extremities. Subject XIII had stocking-distribution sensory loss, and at age 19 years needle EMG showed a chronic neurogenic process in the leg muscles. Of the 4 subjects who had audiologic assessment, 2 (VII and XIII) had unilateral congenital sensorineural hearing loss, and subject VII had a cochlear malformation. Several subjects had vocal cord paralysis, and subjects I and VII required tracheostomy. ⚠︎

Additional findings reported by Whitman et al. (2021) included the following. Subjects VIII, IX, and X had cyclic vomiting that began at ages 7, 11, and 15 years, respectively. Subject VIII responded well to ondansetron and subject IX to pizotifen. Anosmia and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism were present in several subjects, and subject XIII had primary amenorrhea. Several males had micropenis and/or cryptorchidism. Kallmann syndrome showed variable expressivity: both monozygotic twins lacked olfactory bulbs on neuroimaging, but one had delayed puberty with low gonadotropins and the other had normal puberty. Subjects I, III, and VII had sinus tachycardia. Subject XIV had a right bundle branch block, and at 21 years of age he underwent pacemaker placement for sinus node dysfunction with sinus arrest pauses of up to 7.5 seconds that caused syncope. Subject IX developed marked proteinuria and rapidly progressive chronic kidney disease at age 15, which was ultimately fatal, with small echogenic kidneys. Microcephaly was present in several subjects. At least 10 of the 14 subjects were left-handed. Brain MRI in all subjects showed marked thinning of the corpus callosum. Other MRI findings were asymmetric dysgenesis of the basal ganglia and lateral ventricles, absent olfactory bulbs and sulci, dysmorphic superior cerebellar hemispheres, small extraocular muscles, absent oculomotor nerves, and hypoplastic or absent facial nerves. Subject I also had perisylvian polymicrogyria.

Jia et al. (2022) reported a 3-year-old Chinese Han boy (C52) with the TUBB3 R262H variant. He had ptosis, exotropia and hypotropia, limited horizontal and vertical eye movements, corneal exposure, and facial weakness. He also had joint contractures, leg length discrepancy, impaired gait, funnel chest, cryptorchidism, cyclic vomiting, and intellectual impairment, and he met the diagnostic criteria for Kallmann syndrome. MRI showed absent olfactory bulbs and sulci and absent oculomotor and facial nerves. It also showed hypoplasia of the corpus callosum and brainstem, malformations of the cerebellum, basal ganglia, thalamus, and anterior commissure, and asymmetric lateral ventricles. ⚠︎

### Inheritance

Inheritance is autosomal dominant, and the reported cases arose de novo. Tischfield et al. (2010) found that the R262H mutation arose de novo, either as sporadic disease or from presumed germline mosaicism. Whitman et al. (2021) found that all parents of their 14 patients were clinically unaffected. In the 11 cases (10 pedigrees) with parental DNA available the mutation had arisen de novo, and no inherited cases were identified.

### Molecular Genetics

Tischfield et al. (2010) studied patients with CFEOM and additional neurologic symptoms. In 2 pedigrees they found a heterozygous 785G-A transition in the TUBB3 gene, resulting in an arg262-to-his (R262H) substitution, which arose de novo or from presumed germline mosaicism. The R262H mutation was one of 5 further heterozygous TUBB3 missense mutations found in 13 unrelated pedigrees. None of the 8 TUBB3 mutations reported in the study was found in more than 1,700 control chromosomes. Functional studies showed that R262H permitted much more efficient heterodimer formation and microtubule incorporation than R262C. When modeled in yeast, R262H reduced the accumulation of kinesins at microtubule plus-ends and altered microtubule dynamics.

In 14 individuals from 13 families with the disorder, Whitman et al. (2021) identified a heterozygous c.785G-A transition in the TUBB3 gene, resulting in an R262H substitution. In the 11 cases (10 pedigrees) with parental DNA available, the mutation had arisen de novo. In the monozygotic twins, the de novo mutation was shared by both twins.

In a Chinese Han boy (C52) with CFEOM and multiple congenital malformations, Jia et al. (2022) identified a heterozygous c.785G-A transition in the TUBB3 gene, resulting in an R262H substitution.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Tischfield et al. (2010) found that the R262H or D417H substitution in TUBB3 was suggested by the combination of severe CFEOM3 with facial weakness, developmental delay, moderate to severe corpus callosum dysgenesis, finger contractures, basal ganglia dysmorphisms, and early-onset polyneuropathy. Severe phenotypes typically arose de novo. A D417H mutation co-segregated in a dominant fashion in 1 pedigree. All subjects with D417H also had severe CFEOM3, congenital facial weakness, and congenital wrist and finger contractures, and they developed lower extremity weakness and sensory loss in the first decade.

Whitman et al. (2021) distinguished the TUBB3 R262H syndrome from the TUBB3 E410K syndrome by several features of the R262H syndrome: congenital, progressive, and acquired joint contractures, peripheral neuropathy typically beginning before age 12 years, impaired ambulation, auditory dysfunction, and basal ganglia malformations. In contrast, the R262C substitution at the same residue causes isolated CFEOM3 that segregates in autosomal dominant pedigrees. ⚠︎

Jia et al. (2022) found that CFEOM patients with the TUBB3 R262C and S78T variants had milder, mostly isolated phenotypes. In contrast, all patients with the R380C, E410K, and R262H variants had multiple congenital craniocerebral and systemic malformations. Facial weakness and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism occurred in patients with E410K and R262H, and absence of the facial nerves and dysplasia of the olfactory bulbs and sulci correlated with these 2 variants.

### Pathogenesis

Tischfield et al. (2010) modeled the R262C/H, E410K, and D417H/N substitutions in yeast beta-tubulin and found that they reduced the accumulation of the kinesins Kip3p and Kip2p at microtubule plus-ends. In yeast, the R262C, R262H, and E410K substitutions produced long astral microtubules with reduced polymerization rates, faster depolymerization, and reduced rescue frequency. They also conferred benomyl resistance, consistent with dominant effects on microtubule behavior. The authors proposed that the dominant effects on kinesin interactions underlie the facial paralysis and polyneuropathy. ⚠︎

Whitman et al. (2021) noted that TUBB3 is expressed in neurons but not in muscle, connective tissue, or bone. They concluded that the congenital contractures most likely arise from dysfunction of spinal motor neurons in utero.

### Diagnosis

Whitman et al. (2021) reported that, before genetic diagnosis, their subjects had been diagnosed with atypical Moebius syndrome, Carey-Fineman-Ziter syndrome, or Marden-Walker syndrome.

Jia et al. (2022) noted that patients with TUBB3 E410K syndrome or R262H syndrome were easily misdiagnosed as having Mobius syndrome because they share clinical findings such as facial weakness and limited eye movements. They suggested that MRI, whole-exome sequencing, and targeted general examinations can contribute to accurate diagnosis.

### Clinical Management

Whitman et al. (2021) recommended multidisciplinary care. They noted that extensive physical therapy beginning in infancy and early treatment of contractures gave the best motor outcomes, and that prolonged postoperative immobility sometimes caused permanent functional loss. Endocrine treatment can induce puberty. Because one subject had sinus node dysfunction, they suggested that cardiac and autonomic testing be considered. ⚠︎

### Animal Model

Tischfield et al. (2010) found that mice homozygous for a Tubb3 R262C knockin, a substitution at the same residue, died within hours of birth. The mice had defects in guidance of the anterior commissure, corpus callosum, and cranial nerves, but normal cortical layering. They also showed increased microtubule stability and reduced interaction between Kif21a and microtubules.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

TUBB3, ARG262HIS [c.785G>A; p.R262H]

Tischfield et al. (2010) studied 2 pedigrees with severe CFEOM3, congenital facial weakness, congenital wrist and finger contractures, and lower extremity weakness and sensory loss in the first decade. In these pedigrees they found a heterozygous 785G-A transition in the TUBB3 gene, resulting in an arg262-to-his (R262H) substitution. The mutation arose de novo or from presumed germline mosaicism. None of the TUBB3 mutations in the study was found in more than 1,700 control chromosomes. R262H permitted much more efficient heterodimer formation and microtubule incorporation than R262C. In yeast, it reduced the accumulation of kinesins at microtubule plus-ends and altered microtubule dynamics.

Whitman et al. (2021) identified a heterozygous c.785G-A transition in the TUBB3 gene, resulting in an R262H substitution, in all 14 affected individuals from 13 families. These patients had CFEOM3, facial weakness, joint contractures, intellectual disability, Kallmann syndrome, and early-onset peripheral neuropathy. In the 11 cases (10 pedigrees) with parental DNA available, the mutation had arisen de novo. The cases included monozygotic twins originally reported by Tischfield et al. (2010). ⚠︎

Jia et al. (2022) identified a heterozygous c.785G-A transition in the TUBB3 gene, resulting in an R262H substitution, in a 3-year-old Chinese Han boy (C52). He had CFEOM with ptosis and exotropia, facial weakness, joint contractures, intellectual impairment, cyclic vomiting, and Kallmann syndrome, along with multiple brain malformations.

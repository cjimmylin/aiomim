### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that syndromic congenital fibrosis of extraocular muscles-3A-2 with joint contractures, developmental delay, and peripheral neuropathy (CFEOM3A2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TUBB3 gene (602661) on chromosome 16q24.3.

### Description

CFEOM3A2, also called the TUBB3 R262H syndrome, presents at birth with ptosis, ophthalmoplegia, exotropia, facial weakness, facial dysmorphism and, in most cases, distal congenital joint contractures. Affected individuals later develop intellectual disability, gait disorders with proximal joint contractures, Kallmann syndrome, and a progressive peripheral neuropathy during the first decade of life. Whitman et al. (2021) considered it the most debilitating of the CFEOM-TUBB3 syndromes described to date.

### Clinical Features

Tischfield et al. (2010) studied participants from 3 families who carried TUBB3 R262H or D417H substitutions. All had severe CFEOM3 and congenital facial weakness. All were born with congenital wrist and finger contractures, which the authors suggested reflected maldevelopment of spinal motor neurons. In the first decade of life they developed lower extremity weakness and sensory loss. Electromyography showed a chronic, generalized, predominantly axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy, which had been diagnosed as CMT2 in some subjects.

Tischfield et al. (2010) reported that patients from 2 families with the R262H substitution had severe CFEOM3, facial weakness, and congenital ulnar deviation of the hand with contractures of the thumbs and fingers. Brain MRI showed diffuse hypoplasia of the corpus callosum, agenesis of the anterior commissure, a hypoplastic anterior limb of the internal capsule, and dysmorphic basal ganglia. Individuals with R262H had mild to moderate intellectual, social, and behavioral impairments. The twin of an R262H patient had dysgenesis of both anterior limbs of the internal capsule, apparent fusion of an enlarged left caudate head with the putamen, and dilatation of both anterior horns of the lateral ventricles. R262H subjects also had hypoplasia of the left caudate body and tail. No scan showed cortical dysplasia or evidence of a cortical migration defect. Brain imaging was not reviewed for any D417H subject.

Whitman et al. (2021) described 14 individuals from 13 unrelated families with the TUBB3 R262H variant. All presented as newborns with profound bilateral limitation of eye movements, large exotropia and facial weakness, and 13 had distal joint contractures at birth. Ten had fetal distress, which led to premature delivery in 3, and 3 were small for gestational age or had intrauterine growth restriction. Eight had vocal cord paralysis needing tracheostomy shortly after birth, 9 needed nasogastric or gastrostomy tube feeding in infancy, and 2 had congenital unilateral sensorineural hearing loss. Failure to thrive and short stature were common: 11 subjects were below average height, 13 were below average weight, and 6 had microcephaly. All had facial dysmorphism and facial weakness; 3D photographic analysis of 4 subjects showed reduced facial depth, nasal protrusion, upper facial height, vertical lip dimensions and palpebral fissure length, with normal or increased intercanthal width.

In the cohort of Whitman et al. (2021), all subjects had CFEOM, with severe bilateral, often asymmetric ptosis, hypotropia and large-angle exotropia, and little or no residual eye movement; strabismus surgery gave disappointing results. All had bilateral facial weakness. The 3 subjects who had facial nerve studies had low-amplitude responses and no recordable blink response. All 8 ears tested showed evidence of neural dys-synchrony, and 2 subjects had unilateral congenital sensorineural hearing loss with cochlear and eighth nerve abnormalities. Nine subjects had vocal cord paralysis.

Whitman et al. (2021) reported congenital contractures of distal joints in 13 subjects and acquired contractures in 1. The distal contractures often worsened and more proximal contractures developed over time. Findings included camptodactyly, clubfoot, clinodactyly, syndactyly, fisted thumbs, ulnar wrist deviation, coxa vara or coxa valga, and progressive hamstring and Achilles contractures with leg length discrepancy. One subject had been diagnosed with arthrogryposis multiplex congenita.

All 14 subjects reported by Whitman et al. (2021) had varying degrees of global developmental delay, with speech comprehension and communication better than other domains. Full-scale IQ in the 5 subjects tested at NIH ranged from 17 to 96. The monozygotic twins had autism spectrum disorder. Ten subjects were left-handed, and 3 had seizures or epileptic activity that needed antiepileptic therapy. Gross motor milestones were delayed in all. Of the 9 subjects older than 5 years, 4 learned to walk unassisted and 5 needed assistive devices, and motor function generally worsened by adolescence. Although hypotonia had been reported in all at some point, subjects examined at NIH had proximal or hip-girdle weakness. Some had extensor plantar responses with ankle clonus, which the authors considered consistent with spasticity combined with peripheral neuropathy. ⚠︎

Whitman et al. (2021) found abnormal initial nerve conduction studies in all 8 subjects tested, regardless of age; the youngest was abnormal at age 1 year. Peroneal motor and sural and median sensory responses were absent or of low amplitude, and all conduction velocities were slow and in the demyelinating range. Needle EMG in a 19-year-old showed a chronic neurogenic process in the leg muscles. The authors concluded that the findings supported an early-onset, primarily axonal peripheral neuropathy with secondary demyelination.

Brain MRI in all subjects reported by Whitman et al. (2021) showed marked thinning of the corpus callosum. In the 8 subjects with high-quality imaging, other common findings were asymmetric dysgenesis of the basal ganglia and adjacent lateral ventricles, absent olfactory bulbs and sulci, asymmetric dysmorphic superior cerebellar hemispheres, and small extraocular muscles. Some subjects had Sylvian fissure asymmetry (with polymicrogyria in 1), hippocampal malrotation, decreased white matter volume, or a small pons. In all subjects who had cranial nerve imaging, the oculomotor nerve could not be seen and the facial nerve was severely hypoplastic or could not be seen.

Whitman et al. (2021) found that all subjects had at least 1 feature of Kallmann syndrome. Six of 9 males had micropenis and/or cryptorchidism at birth, and all 8 subjects with informative MRI had absent or hypoplastic olfactory bulbs and sulci. The 3 females who had reached young adulthood had anosmia, primary amenorrhea and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism. Expressivity varied: of the monozygotic twins, one had delayed puberty with low LH, FSH and testosterone, and the other had normal puberty. Decreased bone density and delayed bone age were observed.

Jia et al. (2022) described a 3-year-old Chinese boy (C52) with sporadic CFEOM and the TUBB3 R262H variant. He had right ptosis, exotropia and hypotropia, bilateral limitation of horizontal and vertical eye movements, corneal exposure, and facial weakness. He also had intellectual impairment, congenital contractures, leg length discrepancy, impaired gait, cryptorchidism, cyclic vomiting and funnel chest. MRI at age 4 years showed absent olfactory bulbs and sulci, CN3 and CN7, and hypoplasia of the corpus callosum, brainstem, cerebellum, basal ganglia and thalamus, with asymmetry of the Sylvian fissure and lateral ventricles. He met the diagnostic criteria for Kallmann syndrome.

### Other Features

Whitman et al. (2021) reported that the oldest subject in their cohort needed a pacemaker at age 21 years for sinus node dysfunction with sinus pauses of up to 7.5 seconds causing syncope. Ten other subjects had asymptomatic resting tachycardia (110 to 150 beats per minute) with normal ECGs and echocardiograms. The authors raised the possibility that cardiac conduction problems are related to the peripheral neuropathy or to autonomic dysfunction. ⚠︎

Whitman et al. (2021) reported cyclic vomiting syndrome in 3 subjects, with onset at ages 7, 11 and 15 years; the episodes responded to ondansetron, pizotifen and haloperidol, respectively. One subject developed rapidly progressive chronic kidney disease at age 15 that was ultimately fatal; the authors stated it was unclear whether this was related to the TUBB3 variant.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous TUBB3 R262H mutation arose de novo in the families reported by Tischfield et al. (2010), as sporadic disease or from presumed germline mosaicism, and in all 11 cases with parental DNA tested by Whitman et al. (2021). Whitman et al. (2021) noted that none of their 14 subjects had a family history of the disorder and that no inherited cases of TUBB3 Arg262His had been identified. They suggested that affected individuals may have reduced fecundity because of neurologic deficits and/or hypogonadotropic hypogonadism. Tischfield et al. (2010) found that the D417H substitution, which causes a similar phenotype including congenital contractures, cosegregated with disease in a dominant fashion in 1 pedigree.

### Molecular Genetics

Tischfield et al. (2010) sequenced the TUBB3 gene in probands with CFEOM together with other neurologic symptoms. In 2 unrelated pedigrees they found a heterozygous 785G-A transition, resulting in an arg262-to-his (R262H) substitution. In each case the mutation arose de novo, as sporadic disease or from presumed germline mosaicism; it segregated with the phenotype, was absent from the parents of sporadic individuals, and was not found on more than 1700 control chromosomes. In 1 further pedigree, they found a heterozygous 1249G-C transversion in TUBB3, resulting in an asp417-to-his (D417H) substitution, that cosegregated with disease in a dominant fashion and was not present on more than 1700 control chromosomes. In yeast, heterozygous R262H microtubules had prolonged growth events, reduced rescue frequency, a decreased polymerization rate and an increased depolymerization rate, and both R262H and D417H caused a significant decrease in kinesin accumulation on microtubule plus-ends.

Whitman et al. (2021) identified the heterozygous TUBB3 c.785G-A (R262H) mutation in 14 affected individuals from 13 unrelated families of various ethnic backgrounds, by clinical targeted, exome or genome sequencing or by research sequencing of TUBB3. All parents were clinically unaffected. In the 11 cases (10 pedigrees) in which parental DNA was tested, the mutation had arisen de novo, including in a pair of monozygotic twins. The twins and 1 other subject had been reported by Tischfield et al. (2010).

Jia et al. (2022) performed whole-exome sequencing in 122 Chinese Han patients from 96 families with congenital cranial dysinnervation disorders, confirming variants by Sanger sequencing and using trio or pedigree analysis to establish cosegregation or de novo status. Among 11 CFEOM families with TUBB3 variants, 1 had the R262H mutation (patient C52, with sporadic disease).

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Tischfield et al. (2010) proposed phenotype-genotype correlations for the TUBB3 syndromes. Severe CFEOM3 with facial weakness, developmental delay, moderate to severe corpus callosum dysgenesis and likely a late-onset polyneuropathy predicted E410K, whereas the addition of finger contractures, basal ganglia dysmorphisms, and early-onset polyneuropathy suggested R262H or D417H. Phenotypes limited to CFEOM3 and polyneuropathy often segregated in autosomal dominant families, whereas more severe phenotypes typically arose de novo. Facial paralysis and progressive sensorimotor polyneuropathy occurred only in individuals with R262H, E410K, D417H or D417N. R262C caused relatively isolated CFEOM3, whereas R262H at the same residue caused a severe phenotype; R262H allowed much more efficient heterodimer formation and microtubule incorporation than R262C.

Whitman et al. (2021) stated that the R262H syndrome can be distinguished from the TUBB3 E410K syndrome by congenital, progressive and acquired joint contractures, onset of peripheral neuropathy usually before age 12, impaired gait, auditory dysfunction, and dysmorphic basal ganglia and lateral ventricles on MRI. In contrast, the TUBB3 Arg262Cys variant causes isolated CFEOM3 that segregates in large autosomal dominant pedigrees. Arg262His and Asp417His were the only TUBB3 substitutions reported to cause congenital hand and foot contractures.

Among 11 Chinese CFEOM families with TUBB3 variants, Jia et al. (2022) found R262C in 6 families, E410K in 2, and R380C, R262H and a novel S78T variant in 1 each. Patients with R262C or S78T mostly had isolated CFEOM, whereas all patients with R380C, E410K or R262H had multiple congenital craniocerebral and systemic malformations. Malformations of the basal ganglia, thalamus, brainstem and cerebellum were associated with R380C and R262H, and absent facial nerves and dysplasia of the olfactory bulbs and sulci were associated with R262H and E410K. A 10-year-old boy with R380C (C6) had bilateral ptosis, delayed developmental milestones, intellectual impairment, chest wall malformation and impaired gait, with hypoplasia of the corpus callosum, brainstem, cerebellum, basal ganglia, thalamus and CN3 and enlarged lateral ventricles on MRI; the authors speculated that he might have a sensorimotor polyneuropathy but said electromyography was needed to confirm this. Two unrelated patients with E410K (C21 and C32) had bilateral ptosis, limitation of horizontal and vertical gaze, facial weakness, corneal exposure, intellectual impairment, impaired gait, and absent olfactory bulbs and sulci. Jia et al. (2022) noted that CFEOM patients with TUBB3 variants tended to have syndromic phenotypes, in contrast to those with KIF21A variants. ⚠︎

### Pathogenesis

Tischfield et al. (2010) expressed mutant TUBB3 in rabbit reticulocyte lysate and found that R262H, E410K, D417H and D417N heterodimers formed at moderate yields and co-cycled with native tubulin as efficiently as wildtype. In yeast, the R262H and D417H substitutions both caused a significant decrease in Kip3p and Kip2p kinesin accumulation on microtubule plus-ends, and the authors suggested that kinesin-microtubule interactions are reduced in humans carrying these substitutions. Deleting one copy of TUB2 in yeast caused benomyl sensitivity, whereas the disease substitutions conferred benomyl resistance. They concluded that the TUBB3 syndrome mutations mainly alter microtubule function in a dominant fashion, although partial loss of function could not be excluded for some, and proposed that the progressive axonal neuropathy results from secondary defects in motor protein transport.

Because TUBB3 is expressed in neurons and not in muscle, connective tissue or bone, Whitman et al. (2021) proposed that the contractures most likely result from dysfunction of spinal motor neurons in utero. They suggested that the phenotypic similarity to ECEL1-related distal arthrogryposis may reflect a shared pathway of axon guidance to distal muscles.

### Diagnosis

Whitman et al. (2021) noted that before genetic diagnosis, subjects had been given clinical diagnoses including atypical Moebius syndrome, Carey-Fineman-Ziter syndrome (1 subject) and Marden-Walker syndrome (2 subjects).

### Clinical Management

Whitman et al. (2021) observed that substantial physical therapy from infancy was associated with the best motor outcomes. Some orthopedic procedures led to permanent functional loss after prolonged postoperative immobility; one subject never walked again after foot surgery at age 8. Tracheostomies were removed between ages 7 and 10 years in the oldest subjects. Puberty was induced with testosterone or estrogen. The authors recommended multidisciplinary care, including prevention of corneal exposure and amblyopia and monitoring of bone density.

### ALLELIC VARIANTS

**.0001 CONGENITAL FIBROSIS OF EXTRAOCULAR MUSCLES 3A, SYNDROMIC 2, WITH JOINT CONTRACTURES, DEVELOPMENTAL DELAY, AND PERIPHERAL NEUROPATHY**  
TUBB3, ARG262HIS [c.785G>A; p.R262H]

In patients from 2 unrelated pedigrees with severe CFEOM3, facial weakness, congenital hand contractures, brain malformations and early-onset axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy, Tischfield et al. (2010) identified a heterozygous 785G-A transition in the TUBB3 gene, resulting in an arg262-to-his (R262H) substitution. The mutation arose de novo in each case, as sporadic disease or from presumed germline mosaicism; it was absent from the parents of sporadic individuals and was not found on more than 1700 control chromosomes. In yeast, heterozygous R262H microtubules had prolonged growth events, reduced rescue frequency, a decreased polymerization rate and an increased depolymerization rate, and decreased kinesin accumulation on microtubule plus-ends.

Whitman et al. (2021) identified the heterozygous c.785G-A (R262H) mutation in TUBB3 in 14 affected individuals from 13 unrelated families of various ethnic backgrounds, including the monozygotic twins and 1 other subject previously reported by Tischfield et al. (2010). In the 11 cases (10 pedigrees) with parental DNA tested, the mutation had arisen de novo; all parents were clinically unaffected.

Jia et al. (2022) identified the R262H mutation in TUBB3 by whole-exome sequencing in a 3-year-old Chinese boy (C52) with sporadic CFEOM, facial weakness, intellectual impairment, congenital contractures, impaired gait, cryptorchidism and brain and cranial nerve malformations.

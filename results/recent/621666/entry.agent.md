### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that syndromic congenital fibrosis of the extraocular muscles-3A-2 with joint contractures, developmental delay, and peripheral neuropathy (CFEOM3A2) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the TUBB3 gene on chromosome 16q. The mutation is an arg262-to-his (R262H) substitution.

### Description

CFEOM3A2 is a severe syndromic form of congenital fibrosis of the extraocular muscles. Affected individuals have ptosis, ophthalmoplegia, exotropia, facial weakness and facial dysmorphism at birth, and most also have distal congenital joint contractures. They later develop intellectual disability, gait disorders with proximal joint contractures, Kallmann syndrome (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and anosmia), and a progressive peripheral neuropathy during the first decade of life. Brain malformations include hypoplasia of the corpus callosum and anterior commissure, basal ganglia malformations, and absent olfactory bulbs and sulci (Whitman et al., 2021).

### Nomenclature

Whitman et al. (2021) called this disorder the 'TUBB3 R262H syndrome.' Before genetic diagnosis, patients had received clinical diagnoses that included atypical Moebius syndrome, Cary-Fineman-Ziter syndrome, and Marden Walker syndrome.

### Clinical Features

Tischfield et al. (2010) studied patients with TUBB3 mutations and identified the R262H substitution in 2 pedigrees. All patients with R262H had severe CFEOM3 and congenital facial weakness. They were born with congenital wrist and finger contractures and developed lower extremity weakness and sensory loss in the first decade of life. Electromyography showed a chronic, generalized sensorimotor polyneuropathy that was mainly axonal. Patients with R262H had more severe dysgenesis of the corpus callosum and mild to moderate intellectual, social, and behavioral impairments. On imaging, an R262H patient had agenesis of the anterior commissure and dysmorphic basal ganglia. The patient's twin had dysgenesis of the basal ganglia. ⚠︎

Whitman et al. (2021) described 14 individuals from 13 unrelated families with the R262H mutation. They ranged in age from infancy to 25 years and came from a variety of ethnic backgrounds. Three of them, including a pair of monozygotic twins, had previously been reported by Tischfield et al. (2010). Ten had fetal distress before birth. All 14 presented as neonates with profound bilateral limitation of eye movements, large exotropia, and facial weakness. Thirteen had distal joint contractures at birth, and 1 had acquired distal contractures. Over time the distal contractures often worsened and more proximal contractures developed. Eight had vocal cord paralysis needing tracheostomy shortly after birth. Nine needed tube feeding in infancy, and 2 had congenital unilateral sensorineural hearing loss. Failure to thrive and short stature were common, and 6 had microcephaly. All had facial dysmorphism; 3D photographic analysis showed reduced facial depth, nose protrusion, upper facial height, vertical lip dimensions, and palpebral fissure length. All had CFEOM with severe bilateral, often asymmetric ptosis, hypotropia, large-angle exotropia, and minimal or no residual eye movements. All had global developmental delay of varying degree, with comprehension and communication relatively better than other domains. One subject had an IQ in the average range, and the monozygotic twins carried a diagnosis of autism spectrum disorder. Ten subjects were left-handed. Gross motor milestones were delayed in all, and motor function generally worsened by adolescence. Of the 9 subjects over age 5, 4 learned to walk unassisted and 5 learned to walk with assistive devices.

In the same cohort, Whitman et al. (2021) found that all 8 subjects who had nerve conduction studies had abnormal initial studies. The youngest was abnormal at 1 year of age. Conduction velocities were slow and in the demyelinating range. Needle EMG in a 19-year-old showed a chronic neurogenic process in the leg muscles. Reflex findings were consistent with a combination of spasticity and peripheral neuropathy. All subjects showed one or more features of Kallmann syndrome. Six of the 9 males had micropenis and/or cryptorchidism at birth, and all 8 subjects with informative MRI had absent or hypoplastic olfactory bulbs and sulci. Of the monozygotic twins, one had delayed puberty and the other had normal puberty. The 3 females who had reached young adulthood had anosmia and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism. Three subjects had cyclic vomiting syndrome. Ten had asymptomatic elevated resting heart rates. The oldest subject had a pacemaker placed at age 21 years for sinus node dysfunction. One subject developed fatal chronic kidney disease at age 15; it was unknown whether this was related to the mutation.

Brain imaging in the Whitman et al. (2021) cohort showed marked thinning of the corpus callosum in all subjects. Other common findings were asymmetric dysgenesis of the basal ganglia and adjacent lateral ventricles, absent olfactory bulbs and sulci, asymmetrically dysmorphic superior cerebellar hemispheres, and small extraocular muscles. One subject had polymicrogyria. In all subjects who had cranial nerve imaging, the oculomotor nerve could not be seen, and the facial nerve was severely hypoplastic or could not be seen.

Jia et al. (2022) studied 122 Chinese Han patients with congenital cranial dysinnervation disorders and found 1 patient with the R262H mutation. He was a 3-year-old boy (C52) with no family history of the disorder. His findings included ptosis, exotropia with hypotropia, limited horizontal and vertical eye movements, corneal exposure, facial weakness, and intellectual impairment. He also had congenital contractures, leg length discrepancy, impaired gait, cryptorchidism, cyclic vomiting, and funnel chest. MRI showed hypoplasia of the corpus callosum, brainstem, cerebellum, basal ganglia, and thalamus, with asymmetry of the Sylvian fissure and lateral ventricles. The olfactory bulbs and sulci, oculomotor nerve, and facial nerve were absent. The authors stated that this patient fit the diagnostic criteria for Kallmann syndrome.

### Inheritance

The heterozygous R262H mutation arose de novo in the patients reported by Tischfield et al. (2010) and Whitman et al. (2021). Whitman et al. (2021) found no inherited cases. They suggested that affected individuals may have reduced fecundity because of neurologic deficits and/or hypogonadotropic hypogonadism. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Tischfield et al. (2010) sequenced TUBB3 in probands with CFEOM and additional neurologic symptoms. They identified a heterozygous c.785G-A transition in the TUBB3 gene, resulting in an arg262-to-his (R262H) substitution, in 2 pedigrees. The mutation arose de novo, either as sporadic disease or from presumed germline mosaicism. It was absent in the parents of sporadic patients and was not found in more than 1,700 control chromosomes. Residue R262 was predicted to form a hydrogen bond with D417, and the R262H substitution abolished this bond. In vitro, R262H allowed moderate heterodimer formation, and in HeLa cells the mutant protein was incorporated into microtubules at levels similar to wildtype. In heterozygous yeast models, the corresponding substitution produced long astral microtubules with reduced polymerization rates and faster depolymerization. It also caused a marked decrease of the kinesins Kip3p and Kip2p at microtubule plus-ends. ⚠︎

Whitman et al. (2021) identified the heterozygous R262H mutation in 14 affected individuals from 13 unrelated families. Most were found by clinical targeted, exome, or genome sequencing, and the rest by direct sequencing of TUBB3. In the 11 cases (10 pedigrees) in which parental DNA was tested, the mutation had arisen de novo. All parents were clinically unaffected. ⚠︎

Jia et al. (2022) performed whole-exome sequencing in a cohort of Chinese patients with congenital cranial dysinnervation disorders. They identified the c.785G-A (R262H) mutation in TUBB3 in 1 of 11 CFEOM families with TUBB3 variants.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Tischfield et al. (2010) found that R262C and R262H, which affect the same residue, produced relatively isolated CFEOM3 and a severe TUBB3 phenotype, respectively. They noted that R262H allowed much more efficient heterodimer formation and microtubule incorporation than R262C.

Whitman et al. (2021) compared patients with R262H to those with the TUBB3 E410K syndrome. Both groups had severe CFEOM, developmental delay, facial weakness, progressive peripheral neuropathy, and Kallmann syndrome. Only the R262H patients had congenital and acquired joint contractures, neuropathy beginning typically before age 12, impaired gait, and basal ganglia malformations.

### Clinical Management

Whitman et al. (2021) reported that substantial physical therapy starting in infancy was associated with the best motor outcomes. Results of strabismus surgery were generally disappointing. Males needed testosterone supplementation and females needed estrogen to induce puberty. Bone density studies showed decreased bone density and delayed bone age.

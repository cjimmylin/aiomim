### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spermatogenic failure-104 (SPGF104) is caused by homozygous mutation in the CCDC9 gene on chromosome 19. ⚠︎

### Description

Spermatogenic failure-104 (SPGF104) is a form of male infertility characterized by severe asthenozoospermia, in which fewer than 1% of spermatozoa in the ejaculate are motile, with severely damaged sperm morphology and ultrastructure (Sha et al., 2019).

### Clinical Features

Sha et al. (2019) studied a man with severe asthenozoospermia from a consanguineous family. The morphology and ultrastructure of his spermatozoa were severely damaged.

Khan et al. (2025) recruited 7 large families with clinically diagnosed male infertility from rural Pakistan, 5 of which had a history of consanguinity. In 1 of these families, a genetic cause in CCDC9 was identified.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of SPGF104 in the family reported by Sha et al. (2019) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance: the affected man, from a consanguineous family, was homozygous for a CCDC9 mutation. Khan et al. (2025) likewise identified biallelic CCDC9 missense variants in a Pakistani family with male infertility. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In a man from a consanguineous family with severe asthenozoospermia, Sha et al. (2019) identified a homozygous missense mutation in the CCDC9 gene (g.47763960C-T, NC_000019.9), resulting in a ser109-to-leu (S109L) substitution. In silico analysis predicted the variant to be highly pathogenic. CCDC9 protein levels were significantly low in the patient's spermatozoa.

Khan et al. (2025) performed whole-genome sequencing on 26 individuals (3 to 5 per family) from 7 large Pakistani families with male infertility and identified plausible genetic causes in 5 families, including biallelic missense substitutions in CCDC9 in 1 family. The specific CCDC9 variants were not given in the abstract.

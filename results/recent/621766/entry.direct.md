### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spermatogenic failure-104 (SPGF104) is caused by homozygous mutation in the CCDC9 gene on chromosome 19. ⚠︎

### Description

Spermatogenic failure-104 (SPGF104) is a form of male infertility marked by severe asthenozoospermia, in which fewer than 1% of spermatozoa in the ejaculated semen are motile (Sha et al., 2019). Sperm from an affected man also had severely damaged morphology and ultrastructure (Sha et al., 2019). ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Sha et al. (2019) reported a man from a consanguineous family who had severe asthenozoospermia. His spermatozoa had severely damaged morphology and ultrastructure.

Khan et al. (2025) recruited 7 large families from rural Pakistan with clinically diagnosed male infertility, 5 of them with a history of consanguinity. One of these families carried biallelic variants in CCDC9.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of SPGF104 is consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. Sha et al. (2019) found a homozygous CCDC9 mutation in an affected man from a consanguineous family, and Khan et al. (2025) found biallelic CCDC9 variants in a Pakistani family with male infertility. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

In a man from a consanguineous family who had severe asthenozoospermia, Sha et al. (2019) identified a homozygous mutation in the CCDC9 gene (g.47763960C-T, NC_000019.9) that caused a ser109-to-leu (S109L) substitution. In silico analysis predicted that the variant was highly pathogenic. CCDC9 protein levels were significantly reduced in the patient's spermatozoa.

Khan et al. (2025) performed whole-genome sequencing on 26 individuals (3 to 5 per family) from 7 large Pakistani families with male infertility. In 1 family they identified biallelic missense substitutions in the CCDC9 gene. The abstract does not give the specific variants.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that spermatogenic failure-104 (SPGF104) is caused by homozygous mutation in the CCDC9 gene on chromosome 19q13.32. ⚠︎

### Description

Spermatogenic failure-104 (SPGF104) is a form of male infertility. Sha et al. (2019) described it as severe asthenozoospermia, in which very few spermatozoa are motile and the spermatozoa have badly damaged morphology and ultrastructure. ⚠︎

### Clinical Features

Sha et al. (2019) reported a man from a consanguineous family who had severe asthenozoospermia. Fewer than 1% of the spermatozoa in his ejaculate were motile, and his spermatozoa showed severely damaged morphology and ultrastructure. ⚠︎

Khan et al. (2025) studied a Pakistani family with clinically diagnosed male infertility.

### Inheritance

The transmission pattern of SPGF104 in the family reported by Sha et al. (2019) was consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. The affected man was from a consanguineous family and carried a homozygous mutation in the CCDC9 gene. Khan et al. (2025) also found biallelic CCDC9 variants in an affected Pakistani family. ⚠︎

### Molecular Genetics

Sha et al. (2019) identified a novel homozygous variant in the CCDC9 gene (g.47763960C-T, NM_015603.3) in a man with severe asthenozoospermia from a consanguineous family. The variant resulted in a ser109-to-leu (S109L) substitution, and in silico analysis predicted it to be highly pathogenic. CCDC9 protein levels were significantly reduced in the patient's spermatozoa. The authors suggested that CCDC9 may be a novel pathogenic gene for severe asthenozoospermia.

Khan et al. (2025) performed whole genome sequencing on 26 individuals (3 to 5 per family) from 7 large families from rural Pakistan with clinically diagnosed male infertility. Five of the families had a history of consanguinity. The authors identified plausible genetic causes in 5 of the 7 families (71%). In one of these families, they identified biallelic missense substitutions in the CCDC9 gene. In the other families, they found variants in other genes: a homozygous 10-kb deletion of exon 2 of M1AP, biallelic missense substitutions in SPAG6 and in TUBA3C, and an in-frame hemizygous deletion in the X-linked TKTL1 gene.

### TEXT

A number sign (#) is used with this entry because of evidence that Cornelia de Lange syndrome-4 with or without midline brain defects (CDLS4) is caused by heterozygous mutation in the RAD21 gene (606462) on chromosome 8q24. ⚠︎

### Description

Cornelia de Lange syndrome-4 (CDLS4) is a cohesinopathy caused by alterations in RAD21, a structural protein of the cohesin complex. It causes growth retardation, minor skeletal anomalies, and facial features that overlap those of classic Cornelia de Lange syndrome (CdLS). Compared with CdLS caused by NIPBL or SMC1A variants, the phenotype is milder: facial morphology, limb anomalies, cognition and behavior are less severely affected (Deardorff et al., 2012; Krab et al., 2020). Some individuals with monoallelic RAD21 variants have holoprosencephaly, with or without CdLS features (Kruszka et al., 2019; Krab et al., 2020).

### Clinical Features

Deardorff et al. (2012) reported children with RAD21 mutations who had growth retardation, minor skeletal anomalies, and facial features that overlapped those of CdLS. Their cognitive impairment was much milder than in classic CdLS, which set them apart from children with mutations in NIPBL, SMC1A, or SMC3.

Minor et al. (2014) reported 2 patients from 2 families with atypical CdLS and RAD21 mutations. The first patient had developmental delay, hypospadias, inguinal hernia, and dysmorphic features. The second patient had developmental delay, characteristic facial features, hirsutism, and hand and foot anomalies. The first patient was more mildly affected than the second. ⚠︎

Boyle et al. (2017) described intrafamilial phenotypic variation in a family segregating a RAD21 deletion. The index patient could be classified as having mild CdLS. The patient's mother and 2 aunts carried the variant but did not fulfill the diagnostic criteria for CdLS. The authors suggested that, together with previous reports, this indicated incomplete penetrance of RAD21 variants and that such individuals may be underdiagnosed.

Gudmundsson et al. (2019) reported a 15-month-old boy with developmental delay and distinct CdLS-like facial features. He also had gastrointestinal reflux in early infancy, testis retention, prominent digit pads, and diaphragmatic hernia. The authors concluded that these findings expanded the clinical description of CDLS4.

Dorval et al. (2020) reported a boy with a mild CdLS presentation and a molecularly confirmed novel RAD21 mutation. They compared his features with those of previously described patients carrying other RAD21 intragenic mutations and noted that RAD21 is associated with a milder phenotype.

Krab et al. (2020) reported 49 individuals from 33 families with RAD21 alterations, including 24 previously unpublished cases. The patients came from Australia, Europe, Turkey, Canada and the United States. Full clinical information was available for 29 individuals from 22 families (cohort A), all of whom had CdLS clinical scores of at least 5. Their phenotype was an attenuated form of CdLS compared with that caused by NIPBL or SMC1A variants, with milder facial morphology, limb anomalies, cognition and behavior. The frequent features in cohort A were short nose (23/26), long and/or smooth philtrum (26/29), concave nasal ridge (24/29), thick eyebrows (20/24), long eyelashes (21/26), synophrys (19/28), thin upper lip vermilion (23/29), short fifth finger (23/28), postnatal microcephaly (16/28), and gastroesophageal reflux (13/25). No patient had major limb malformations (0/29) or visual impairment (0/24). Small hands (5/27), small feet (3/27), hirsutism (10/26) and seizures (2/22) were less common than in the NIPBL and SMC1A cohorts. Hearing loss was present in 8/24, heart defects in 9/23, and cleft palate in 6/25. Pregnancies and births were usually normal and prenatal growth retardation was uncommon. Short stature and microcephaly developed after birth. About half of the patients had congenital anomalies such as cleft palate and cardiac anomalies. Unusual findings included vertebral anomalies (clefts and hemivertebrae) and malformations of structures derived from the embryonic foregut. Diaphragmatic hernia, anal atresia or choanal atresia occurred occasionally. Cognition and behavior were also attenuated. Sixteen of 29 patients (55%) had normal or mildly impaired cognition, none had severe or profound disability, and all patients aged 3 years and older used some words. Problems were mainly language-based. Behavioral problems were present in 14 of 25 (56%), mainly anxiety, ADHD, autistic features and obsessive-compulsive behavior. Self-injurious behavior (1/18) and aggression (1/16) were infrequent. ⚠︎

Lei et al. (2020) described a 6-year-old Han Chinese boy, the first child of healthy unrelated parents, who had a de novo t(8; 18)(q22; q21) translocation. He had growth retardation (height and weight -2.0 SD), mild-to-moderate intellectual disability, and left-sided cleft palate. His congenital heart defect comprised a ventricular septal defect, double-outlet right ventricle, and juxtaposition of the aorta and pulmonary artery. He also had thoracic scoliosis, butterfly vertebrae, fifth-finger clinodactyly, fingerpads, umbilical and inguinal hernias, and recurrent respiratory infections. His facial dysmorphism included arched thick eyebrows, long eyelashes, a depressed nasal bridge, anteverted nostrils, large prominent ears, dental malocclusion, and micrognathia. Kabuki syndrome was initially suspected. Multiple exostoses were found at age 10 years.

Goel and Parasivam (2020) reported another case of holoprosencephaly associated with a loss-of-function variant in RAD21, and Kruszka (2020) replied to this correspondence.

### Inheritance

CDLS4 shows autosomal dominant inheritance. Minor et al. (2014) reported the first familial case: an intragenic RAD21 deletion inherited from the patient's mother. Boyle et al. (2017) found a RAD21 deletion in an index patient, her mother and 2 aunts, and suggested incomplete penetrance. Krab et al. (2020) noted that RAD21 variants were often familial (5 of 12 cohort A cases with known inheritance). Mildly affected parents were sometimes diagnosed only after their more severely affected offspring were recognized, so the authors recommended parental testing even when parents appear unaffected. ⚠︎

### Cytogenetics

Wuyts et al. (2002) described a boy with multiple exostoses, hypertrichosis, mental retardation, epilepsy, and brain abnormalities caused by a de novo submicroscopic interstitial deletion of chromosome 8q24. The deleted region overlapped the Langer-Giedion syndrome region and included the EXT1 gene and other genes distal to EXT1. It probably did not include the TRPS1 gene, which lies proximal to EXT1.

Pereza et al. (2015) revised their earlier diagnosis of Langer-Giedion syndrome in a girl with a 7.5-Mb interstitial deletion at 8q23.3q24.13. The deletion included EXT1 but not TRPS1. Because it also included RAD21 and 30 other RefSeq genes, the authors suggested that the correct diagnosis was CDLS4. They compared her with other reported patients who had CDLS4 caused by heterozygous 8q24 deletions.

In the Han Chinese boy with a de novo t(8; 18) translocation, Lei et al. (2020) used nanopore long-read sequencing and the dnarrange pipeline. These showed that the rearrangement was germline chromothripsis: the genome had been shattered into 19 fragments (15 from chromosome 8 and 4 from chromosome 18) that rejoined in random order and orientation. The rearrangement caused 4 deletions on chromosome 8 (456, 1,957, 520, and 3,529 kb) and 1 deletion on chromosome 18 (1,528 kb), all consistent with array results. One of the chromosome 8 deletions contained RAD21 and EXT1 but not TRPS1. Earlier short-read whole-genome sequencing had not resolved the rearrangement, and no pathogenic variants were found in the Kabuki syndrome genes.

### Heterogeneity

Krab et al. (2020) had limited data on 20 individuals from 11 families (cohort B). From these data, they suggested that biallelic RAD21 variants can cause Mungan syndrome and that monoallelic RAD21 variants can cause holoprosencephaly, with or without CdLS characteristics. In several other cases, including cases with sclerocornea, it was uncertain whether the RAD21 variant was involved.

### Molecular Genetics

Deardorff et al. (2012) showed that mutations in RAD21, an integral structural protein of the cohesin complex, cause a congenital phenotype consistent with a cohesinopathy. The mutations acted at the interface between RAD21 and the cohesin proteins STAG2 and SMC1A and impaired the cellular DNA damage response.

Minor et al. (2014) identified RAD21 mutations in 2 patients with atypical CdLS. A patient with mild atypical CdLS had an in-frame intragenic deletion of exon 13. The deletion was inherited from the mother, who had a history of melanoma and other unspecified medical problems, and the authors described this as the first familial case. The second patient had a c.592_593dup frameshift mutation.

Boyle et al. (2017) identified a novel heterozygous 1-bp deletion (c.704delG) in RAD21 in a patient with CdLS. The deletion was predicted to cause a frameshift and premature stop codon (p.Ser235IlefsTer19). The patient's mother and 2 aunts also carried it, and none of them had been suspected of having CdLS or a cohesinopathy before the study. ⚠︎

Gudmundsson et al. (2019) used exome sequencing in a 15-month-old boy with CDLS4. They identified a de novo heterozygous 3-bp deletion in RAD21 (c.1774_1776del) that removed gln592 (Gln592del). The variant was absent from both healthy parents. In silico structural modeling predicted that it disrupts contacts between RAD21 lys591 and SMC1A glu1191 and glu1192, altering the RAD21-SMC1A interface. ⚠︎

Kruszka et al. (2019) reported 4 individuals with holoprosencephaly who had variants in the non-X-linked cohesin genes SMC3 and RAD21. They also found variants in the X-linked cohesin genes STAG2 and SMC1A in 11 other individuals with holoprosencephaly. They concluded that the cohesin complex is an important regulator of median forebrain development.

In 33 families, Krab et al. (2020) found 31 different RAD21 alterations: 7 unique deletions (6 of which also included other genes) and 24 intragenic sequence variants. Thirteen sequence variants were predicted to be truncating (2 nonsense, 2 splice site, and 9 frameshift) and were scattered throughout the gene, and 3 were in-frame deletions. The missense variants tended to cluster at the protein-binding domains. Two variants were recurrent: C585R and R586X. In 9 of 21 index cases with known inheritance the variant was familial. Molecular dynamics modeling predicted that several variants disrupt RAD21 interactions with SMC1A, the SMC1A/SMC3 ATPase head, or STAG1/2.

Lei et al. (2020) studied the Han Chinese boy with germline chromothripsis. Quantitative RT-PCR of his lymphoblastoid cells showed reduced RAD21 and EXT1 expression, while TRPS1 expression was unchanged. The authors concluded that loss of RAD21 and EXT1 explained the phenotype, a likely contiguous gene syndrome combining CDLS4 and atypical Langer-Giedion syndrome. They considered involvement of other disrupted genes, including ZFPM2, unlikely. ⚠︎

Goel and Parasivam (2020) reported a patient with holoprosencephaly and a loss-of-function variant in RAD21.

### Genotype/Phenotype Correlations

Deardorff et al. (2012) suggested that dominant missense mutations in RAD21 cause more severe functional defects and worse structural and cognitive findings than loss-of-function mutations.

Minor et al. (2014) discussed variable clinical presentation within families and low penetrance of RAD21 mutations. They emphasized the clinical value of RAD21 mutation analysis in patients with atypical CdLS and of molecular testing in mildly affected patients.

In 29 patients from 22 families, Krab et al. (2020) observed a trend toward higher CdLS scores and more frequent growth impairment in patients with microdeletions than in those with intragenic variants. Major malformations and cognitive and behavioral problems did not differ between the 2 groups. CdLS scores did not differ between truncating and nontruncating sequence variants. Variability within and between families was striking, especially in cognition and behavior, and in most families it could not be explained by mosaicism.

Lei et al. (2020) compared their patient with 5 previously reported patients who had Langer-Giedion syndrome and deletions that included RAD21 and EXT1 but spared TRPS1. The 6 patients shared arched thick eyebrows (6 patients), long eyelashes (5), a depressed or broad nasal bridge (5), sparse scalp hair (4), and intellectual disability (4 of 6). Only their patient had thoracic scoliosis and a congenital heart defect. Because 2 of 8 previously reported patients with RAD21 variants had congenital heart disease, the authors suggested that RAD21 deficiency might be responsible for the heart disease.

### Pathogenesis

Deardorff et al. (2012) found that RAD21 mutations act at the interface between RAD21 and the cohesin proteins STAG2 and SMC1A and impair the cellular DNA damage response.

Krab et al. (2020) suggested that the relatively mild phenotype indicates that RAD21, unlike other CdLS genes, is not highly intolerant to loss of function. They speculated that RAD21 haploinsufficiency may be compensated more effectively than loss of other cohesin genes. They noted that haploinsufficiency gave roughly halved RAD21 RNA levels without compensatory upregulation.

### Diagnosis

Krab et al. (2020) reported that no RAD21 variant would have been missed using the CdLS consensus criteria for molecular testing. CdLS had been suspected before testing in about 60% of index cases (13/21). The authors concluded that the CdLS score remains a reliable tool and recommended testing parents even when they appear unaffected.

### Animal Model

Deardorff et al. (2012) showed in a zebrafish model that the RAD21 mutations disrupted transcription.
